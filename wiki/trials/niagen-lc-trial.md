Pilot clinical trial of **nicotinamide riboside (NR; Niagen)** at 2,000 mg/day for long-COVID cognitive and somatic symptoms. Cross-over / intra-individual design (each participant served as their own control). Led by **Dr Edmarie Guzman-Velez** (now Rutgers Robert Wood Johnson, previously Mass General). Co-investigator **Dr Rudy Tanzi** (Harvard Medical School / Mass General; founding director of the McCance Center for Brain Health). Disclosed conflict: Tanzi is a long-time consultant to and equity holder in Niagen Biosciences; he was *not* involved in trial execution.

## Design

- Pop: LC ≥ 2 months post-acute, with brain fog + ≥ 2 other symptoms; not hospitalised in acute phase
- Dose: 2,000 mg/day (twice the bottle-recommended)
- 2-week placebo run-in for everyone
- Then either NR for 20 weeks *or* placebo 10 weeks → NR 10 weeks
- Outcomes every 5–10 weeks: NAD+ levels, neuropsych tests (Trail Making B), validated questionnaires (sleep, fatigue, depression, anxiety, cognition)
- 18 participants completed the full 20 weeks; pooled-analysis n = ~43 (10 weeks NR vs own baseline)

## Results

- **NAD+ levels rose ~3-fold within 5 weeks** and sustained
- Direct placebo-vs-NR group comparison: no significant differences (under-powered)
- Pooled-baseline analysis showed significant improvement in:
  - **Fatigue**
  - **Sleep quality**
  - **Depression symptoms**
  - **Trail Making B** (executive function) — improved by 8.4 seconds in 10 weeks (Tanzi: comparable to or better than typical Alzheimer-trial signals)

## Caveats

- Open-access publication; small sample size; pooled analysis was a post-hoc adaptation due to attrition (mostly from re-infection)
- 2,000 mg is *double* the bottle-recommended TrueNiagen dose
- Placebo-controlled efficacy not formally established at this n

## Notes for Eric

The first peer-reviewed RCT-level evidence on this wiki for an oral supplement helping LC. Modest but real signal on fatigue, sleep, depression, executive function. If considering NR self-trial, 1,000 mg/day is the bottle-recommended dose; 2,000 mg/day was the trial dose. Cross-ref ercipedia for any subsequent peer-reviewed evidence.
