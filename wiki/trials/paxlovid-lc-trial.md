# Pax LC — 15-day Paxlovid trial for Long Covid (Yale)

Placebo-controlled RCT testing **15-day extended-dose Paxlovid** in LC, run by Krumholz + Iwasaki at Yale.

## Hypothesis

If [[viral-persistence]] drives LC for some patients, then a longer course of antiviral should clear residual virus and improve symptoms. Standard 5-day Paxlovid course leaves rebound common; 15 days targets fuller clearance.

## Design

- **N = 100** with deep immune phenotyping for *all* participants (not just responders).
- **Dose**: nirmatrelvir + ritonavir (Paxlovid) for 15 days vs placebo.
- **Comparator**: placebo *includes ritonavir alone* (the metallic-taste / potentiator component) — preserves blinding.
- **Outcome window**: 28 days primary; 24-week follow-up after randomisation.
- **Crack the blind early**: ~28 days primary endpoint → notify participants which arm they were on (~90 days after last enrolment) — explicitly because participants asked for this in design consultation.

## Geography + access (decentralised model)

- **41 US states** at recording (expansion ongoing).
- Trusted Medical (Kevin Carr) provides physicians licensed in each state.
- **Anyone in eligible states can apply online**; medical-records-based eligibility verification.
- **All home-based** — medication shipped, blood drawn at participant's home.
- **No clinician-gatekeeping** — patients step forward; pre-screen filters eligibility from records.
- **Compensation for participants' time** — the study explicitly respects research labor.

## Target cohort

- LC patients in the included US states.
- Participants of all backgrounds — explicit equity goal; outreach to underrepresented populations + low health-literacy users.

## Why this design matters

A deliberate prototype of the **digital-decentralised-democratised model** — Krumholz aims to prove the platform works so future LC trials (and trials in other diseases) can be done at speed and scale, without site-by-site logistics or patient-burden.

## Caveats

- N = 100 is small; even if effective on average, the sub-population responder analysis is the more interesting outcome.
- Not all LC has viral persistence — Paxlovid wouldn't help non-persistence sub-cohorts.
- 15-day course not previously safety-tested in non-acute population; metallic taste tolerable at 5 days, less clear at 15.

## Outcome (per #181)

> "We did run a clinical trial of Paxlovid in collaboration with Yale University and Akiko Iwasaki and Harlan Krumholz, who are amazing collaborators — and **it failed**. We are still doing subgroup analysis to understand who it helped, and hopefully why it helped, because a small percentage *did* experience benefit." — David Putrino, ep #181

Putrino's mechanistic explanation for the failure: **paxlovid does not penetrate tissue.** Patients whose LC is driven by SARS-CoV-2 persistence in the gut, muscle, breast, or lymphoid tissue would not be reached by an oral anti-protease that stays in the bloodstream. The implication: paxlovid may help the subset whose LC is driven by *circulating spike* (a small percentage), but the LC field needs **drugs that penetrate immune-privileged tissue** to address the broader persistence hypothesis.
