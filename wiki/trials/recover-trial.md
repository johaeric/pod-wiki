# RECOVER

The US National Institutes of Health's flagship long-covid research initiative. **$1.15B Congressional appropriation.** Lead institute: NHLBI. Coordinated across multiple NIH institutes including NINDS (Koroshetz).

## Cohorts (as of #74, March 2023)

| Cohort | Size | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Acute → followed | 3,635 | Prospective LC vs recovery |
| Long covid established | 6,488 | Cross-section of LC |
| Unaffected controls | 1,000 | Healthy reference |
| Asymptomatic post-covid | 945 | Alternative reference |
| EHR analysis | ~60M | Demographics, variant effects, late outcomes |
| Autopsy | (separate) | Direct organ pathology |

## Tier system

- Tier 1: baseline broad-net testing (everyone)
- Tier 2: deeper testing for symptom-cluster matches
- Tier 3: deepest dive for tier-2 abnormalities

## Mechanism hypotheses (priority list)

1. **Viral persistence**
2. **Autoimmunity**
3. **Immune dysregulation**
4. **Latent virus reactivation** (e.g. EBV)

## Treatment trial strategy

- **Disease-modifying**: antiviral testing first
- **Symptomatic master protocols**: sleep / exercise–PEM / autonomic / cognition

## Findings published already (EHR side, per #74)

- LC less frequent after Omicron than Delta
- Vaccination reduces LC risk
- More severe acute → more LC
- Racial/ethnic minorities with LC more likely to be hospitalised
- ~70 distinct LC symptoms, occurring in clusters
- Rare in children unless severe acute infection

## Avindra Nath cross-reference

- Same intensive small-N protocol applied to ME/CFS, LC, and Gulf War syndrome — tri-condition comparison
