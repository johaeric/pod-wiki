# MRI fatigue imaging study (Mass Gen Hospital + MIT)

Boston-area fMRI research study designed to **map the neural and physiological substrates of LC mental fatigue** — distinct from cognitive symptoms broadly. Co-led by [[daniel-gomez]] (MR physics, Massachusetts General Hospital) and [[ewa-beldzik]] (cognitive neuroscience, Laura Lewis lab MIT).

## Design

Three tasks in the scanner per participant:

1. **15-second breath holds** — vascular/physiological control (separates blood-flow from neural signal)
2. **Simple visual task** — strong sensory-system response; baseline test
3. **Color-word matching task (Stroop-style)** — 12-min cognitive control task; tests prefrontal involvement; behavioural performance measurable; **fatigue should manifest as performance degradation over the 12 minutes**

## Hypothesis

Acute COVID damages the brain via oxygenation lesions visible at autopsy in ICU patients. **Sub-lesional oxygenation impairment** could be the LC fatigue mechanism — too mild for anatomical MRI to detect but visible in functional MRI of blood oxygenation. Compatible with the [[microclots]] mechanism literature.

## Recruitment + timeline

- 30 healthy controls + 30 LC fatigue patients targeted
- ~12-13 controls + 10-11 patients scanned at recording (Jan 2024)
- Data collection complete: July-Aug 2024 expected
- Final analysis + publication: mid-to-late 2025
- Boston area only; transport offered; criteria include MRI-compatible (no metal), severe mental fatigue, no significant lung issues

## Why this matters on the wiki

- The wiki's **first detailed mechanism-targeted neuroimaging study** for LC fatigue specifically.
- **Methodologically novel** — combining vascular control via breath-hold, neural via simple visual, and cognitive via Stroop, in an LC-fatigue-specific cohort, at high-resolution fMRI is not previously combined in the literature.
- If positive, **biomarker candidate** for LC fatigue (potentially diagnostic; potentially treatment-response monitoring).
