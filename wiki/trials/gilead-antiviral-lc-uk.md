# First UK Long-Covid Antiviral Infusion Trial

UK's first targeted antiviral trial for long Covid, led by Mark Faghy (Derby) with David Strain (Exeter), funded by Gilead with **£1.25m** for the initial phase. Starts Easter 2024.

## Design

**Phase 4 tolerability study first** — does the candidate population actually attend?
- 5 consecutive days of IV antiviral infusion
- Day 1: 60-minute infusion
- Days 2–5: 30-minute infusions
- Candidate medication: licensed in the UK for acute hospitalised Covid; data from acute use suggests reduced risk of long-term sequelae
- Drug name not stated on the podcast; can be inferred from Gilead's antiviral portfolio

**Mechanism:** Targets viral persistence (Faghy explicitly credits Amy Proal #103 framing). Hypothesis is that even if active virus is gone, dormant viral reservoirs reactivate on subsequent acute infections and worsen the symptom load. If antiviral can shrink reservoirs, it reduces the dormant-reactivation risk plus current chronic-immune-activation symptom load.

**Patient response:** ~1,200 expressions of interest in a single weekend. Faghy responded personally to every email.

## Severe-patient access

PPI representative Lindsay (severe end of the spectrum) **cannot access** the 5-day infusion protocol. Faghy and team treating this as a job to solve: alternative regimes (different timing, possibly home delivery) being designed in parallel for the bedbound cohort.

## Next phase

Already in active conversations to scale to a randomised national trial as soon as Phase 4 reads out. Estimated 18–24 months to RCT completion, then licensing — a frustration Faghy explicitly names ("people have been living with this for 4 years already").

## Caveats

- **Viral persistence is one of seven mechanisms** (Faghy's framing). The drug will not "eradicate long Covid completely." Expectation management is part of the protocol design.
- Phase 4 success is partly social: testing whether real-world LC patients can attend 5 consecutive days at all.
