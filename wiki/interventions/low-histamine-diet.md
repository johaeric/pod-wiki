# Low-Histamine Diet

A dietary protocol that excludes high-histamine foods (fermented foods, vinegars, aged/cured meats, mature cheeses, beer/wine, canned fish, dried fruits, leftovers, and some fresh foods like tomatoes, avocado, spinach). Used for suspected **Mast Cell Activation Syndrome (MCAS)** where histamine release drives allergic-type, GI, fatigue, and pain symptoms.

⚠️ **Restrictive — needs supervision.** Eliminating too much for too long can:
- Compromise gut microbiome diversity
- Cause new reactions to remaining foods (immune system "tracks" repeated exposures)
- Mask other underlying issues that aren't actually histamine-driven

The standard recommendation: pair low-histamine with **gut-healing work** ("heal-and-seal"), then **reintroduce** to find personal tolerance. The aim is restoring normal diet, not permanent restriction.

## How to interpret the wiki signal so far

Two no-effects (Sally Riggs ep #23, [[jackie-baxter]] aside in ep #21) vs two practitioner recommendations (Moira #21, Tina Peers #38). **Real signal of mixed personal response — and a reminder to set a time-limit before deciding it isn't working for you.** Peers (#38) is the most detailed protocol voice and pairs the diet with H1+H2 antihistamines, mast-cell stabilisers, and background co-morbidity treatment — the diet is foundation, not standalone.

## Dietitian critique (#129, no row)

[[lily-spechler]] in [[129-lily-spechler-lc-dietitian]] argues low-histamine on its own is "cutting down trees instead of asking why the forest is flammable." Histamine is *one* mediator the mast cells release — prostaglandins, leukotrienes, other cytokines also matter. Her preferred mechanism stack: build short-chain fatty acids (butyrate, acetate, propionate) via fibre → SCFAs modulate mast cells and inversely correlate with circulating histamine. Without fibre, gut bacteria feed on the gut lining → permeability → systemic inflammation. The mucosal layer also produces DAO (the enzyme that breaks histamine down). And fibre supports estrogen excretion — estrogen and histamine compete at the same receptor (one reason women dominate LC). Not a count-changing recommendation against; a clinical critique to weigh alongside Peers's pro-low-histamine row.
