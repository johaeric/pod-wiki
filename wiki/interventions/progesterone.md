# Progesterone

Endogenous steroid hormone produced by the ovaries (corpus luteum), placenta in pregnancy, and adrenal glands; small amounts in male testes. Conventional clinical use: HRT, contraception, fertility support, post-menopausal protection. Some neuroprotective applications in traumatic brain injury research.

The **bioenergetic framework** ([[bioenergetic-framework]], [[keith-littlewood]]) elevates progesterone to a primary therapeutic lever in chronic illness, claiming antiviral / aerobic-metabolism-restoring / hypoxia-reversing effects. This claim is **not standard mainstream endocrinology**; the wiki carries it as a hypothesis.

## Specific claims from #45

- **Antiviral** — progesterone receptors found in nasal epithelium; supports macrophage / phagocytosis function.
- **Restores aerobic metabolism** in the cell — Littlewood's central mechanistic claim.
- **Deficient** in: PCOS, PMS, endometriosis, fibroids, heavy bleeding women; older men.
- **Theory of male LC mortality**: men's lower progesterone explains worse acute-COVID outcomes in older male cohorts.

## Mechanism

Progesterone binds the progesterone receptor (nuclear) → genomic effects, plus rapid non-genomic effects via membrane-bound receptors. Modulates GABA-A, NMDA, and oxytocin pathways. Possibly anti-inflammatory via NF-κB modulation.

## Forms

- **Oral micronised progesterone** (Utrogestan in UK; Prometrium in US) — sedating, taken at night.
- **Transdermal cream** — variable absorption, sometimes inconsistent dosing.
- **Vaginal pessary** — fertility / pregnancy use.
- **Injectable** — clinical / research use.

## Caveats

- **Specialist supervision required** for any non-trivial use. OTC creams in unregulated markets are inconsistent.
- **Sedating** at high oral doses; coordinate with sleep schedule.
- **Liver-metabolism** considerations — alcohol, oral contraceptives, B-vitamin status all interact.
- Long-term use in men is poorly studied.

## Cross-ref ercipedia

`ercipedia/` does not have a Long-Covid-specific progesterone page. Mainstream HRT evidence base is solid for menopausal use; LC-specific progesterone evidence is essentially absent (some animal data, no human RCTs).
