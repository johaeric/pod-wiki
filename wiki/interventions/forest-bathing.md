# Forest bathing / nature exposure

The Japanese-origin practice of *shinrin-yoku* — extended quiet time in a forest or natural setting, with attentive breathing and slow walking — operationalised in research as a stress-axis modulator. In Long Covid the relevant claim is more general: any nature exposure, including views from a window or even photos of nature, has been shown in multiple stress-physiology studies to reduce cortisol, sympathetic tone, and inflammatory markers.

## Why it matters in LC

If the [[mitochondrial-dysfunction]] / CDR model is correct, anything that *reduces total cellular stress signalling* should help nudge mitochondria back from Battleship to Powerhouse mode. Forest bathing acts on:

- Sympathetic-tone reduction (parasympathetic shift, [[autonomic-dysfunction]])
- Cortisol reduction
- Slowed breathing rate, often unconsciously
- Visual and olfactory cues that downregulate the alarm system

## Floor

For housebound patients, the literature suggests that even *images* of nature (window views, screensavers) produce smaller but real stress reductions. Useful for severe-fatigue / bedbound floor.
