# Graded exercise / interval training

A **rehabilitation philosophy** that prescribes incremental increases in physical activity, often via short interval training (e.g. 30-second heart-rate elevations followed by recovery, repeated and slowly extended). Standard sports-medicine and physio practice for deconditioning. **Highly contested in the LC / ME-CFS communities** because it can trigger PEM and downward step-function crashes when applied to patients with post-exertional pathology.

> Cross-ref: see `ercipedia/wiki/interventions/get.md` for the trial-evidence picture (NICE 2021 guidance withdrew GET for ME/CFS).

## Why it shows up on this podcast

- It's the *default* thing a fit, healthy first-waver is told by their GP/physio: "you're deconditioned, gradually push." Multiple guests describe trying it before they had heard of PEM and crashing as a result.
- BUT — Anderson (#135/#136) and Spechler (#129) re-frame: **after upstream stability is established (breathing + nutrition + MCAS + autonomic readiness)**, structured movement is essential. The harm comes from skipping the readiness check, not from movement per se.

## The Anderson framing (#135 / #136) — exercise *with* preconditioning

- Use the **RV CAMPING checklist** before adding any structured movement.
- Treat exercise as a prescription — dose, frequency, intensity. Sub-symptom-threshold every time.
- Heart-rate ceiling rule (Workwell-derived): **resting HR + 15–20 bpm**. Train HR to *stay low*, not to climb.
- 3× consistently without crash → 10–20% progression. Not larger.
- **Don't use the Levine / modified CHOPs protocol as published** — too aggressive, designed for the wrong cohort, ~50–60% non-completion. Borrow principles (start horizontal, work toward vertical) only.
- *Sometimes hold the dose for a month* — the body benefits from predictability more than from progression.

## Reading the contradiction

Same intervention category — opposite outcomes. The discriminator is **readiness**:

- "Exercise" applied to a deconditioned-but-otherwise-stable body → standard fitness gains.
- "Exercise" applied to a body with active PEM, breathing dysfunction, untreated MCAS, or metabolic deficit → step-function harm (often permanent baseline drop).

The wiki position aligns with Anderson's: structured movement is essential **after** stability is achieved; before that, it's net-harm. The RV CAMPING checklist is the operational test.
