# Selenium

Essential trace mineral. Cofactor for ~25 selenoproteins, most notably **glutathione peroxidase** (antioxidant defence) and the **deiodinase enzymes** that convert inactive thyroid T4 to active T3. Comes up across the corpus in three distinct framings — mitochondrial stack, thyroid axis, and (failed) hair-loss intervention — but **no patient credits it with personal benefit**.

## Honesty about the evidence here

Three clinician/practitioner mentions, **zero patient `key_to_recovery` or `helped_partial` rows**, and one explicit `no_effect` from [[robert-groysman]] for the LC-hair-loss use case. The strongest positive corpus signal is [[moira-newiss]] (LCP #21) framing selenium as part of the broader mitochondrial-nutrient stack alongside [[coq10]], [[zinc]], [[magnesium]] — and her overall message is **food first, supplements only after gut absorption is optimised**, not "take selenium."

For Eric specifically: selenium sits in the thyroid-axis cluster with [[iodine]]; both are foundational cofactors but neither has a patient recovery row in this corpus. If thyroid dysfunction is in his picture, selenium is a low-risk consideration; if not, it's not high-priority.

## Forms (context, not from corpus)

- **Selenomethionine** — most bioavailable supplement form; standard in nutrition-medicine.
- **Selenium yeast** — high-bioavailability food-derived form.
- **Sodium selenite** — older, less-bioavailable inorganic form.
- **Brazil nuts** — single-best food source; 1–2 nuts per day delivers daily-RDA selenium. Variable selenium content by soil; can overshoot if eating handfuls.

## Use cases (corpus)

### Mitochondrial / antioxidant nutrient stack
**[[moira-newiss]] (LCP #21)** — selenium listed alongside CoQ10, zinc, magnesium as "specific nutrients [mitochondria] need to keep all their complicated system functioning." Food first — optimise diet before supplementing. Selenium is part of the broader frame, not a featured single-agent recommendation.

### Thyroid-hormone conversion (Littlewood — context only)
**[[keith-littlewood]] (LCP #45)** — names selenium alongside [[iodine]] as enzymatic cofactors in thyroid hormone production (specifically the T4→T3 deiodinase conversion). Mentioned-only; he doesn't directly recommend selenium supplementation in the segment.

### Hair loss in long COVID (failed)
**[[robert-groysman]] (LCP #91)** — "there's been some recommendations with things like vitamin E and selenium either topically or orally, but those really haven't shown to respond during long covid." Names selenium specifically as **tried but ineffective** for LC-related hair loss; recommends a vasodilator like minoxidil instead.

## Dosing (corpus)

- None of the three speakers states a dose.
- The standard RDA is ~55 µg/day; nutritional-medicine doses typically run 100–200 µg/day; >400 µg/day chronically is the rough threshold for selenosis.

## Cautions

- **Selenosis at high chronic doses** — chronic intake >~400 µg/day can cause hair loss (ironic given the LC use case), garlic-breath, nail brittleness, GI upset, neurological symptoms. Brazil-nut overdose is a documented case-report driver of this.
- **Drug interactions** — minimal at standard doses; can interact with anticoagulants at high doses.
- **Thyroid auto-immunity** — selenium has small-RCT signal for reducing Hashimoto's antibody titres outside this corpus; both Hashimoto's and Graves' patients should coordinate with endocrinology rather than self-dose.
- **Brazil-nut variability** — selenium content varies 10× by soil region; you can swing from sub-deficient to over-replete by changing brands. Verify supplement labels rather than relying on nuts alone.
