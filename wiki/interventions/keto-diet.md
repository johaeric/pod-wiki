# Ketogenic / Low-Carb Diet

A diet very low in carbohydrates (<50 g/day, sometimes lower) and high in healthy fats, designed to push the body into **ketosis** — burning fat for fuel rather than carbohydrates, producing ketones used by the brain and other organs.

On this podcast it's framed primarily through three lenses:
- **Metabolic flexibility** — your mitochondria can burn fat *and* glucose, smoothing out blood-sugar fluctuations and energy crashes
- **Mitochondrial support** — Naviaux's cell-danger-response narrative argues mitochondria thrive on fat-burn under chronic-stress conditions
- **GI / inflammation** — going low-carb often resolves bloating, gas, GI symptoms, and produces weight stabilisation

⚠️ **Caveat:** if medicated (especially diabetes meds), keto requires medical supervision — drugs may need adjustment. Glucose-lowering effects can be substantial.

## Dietitian critique (#129, no row)

[[lily-spechler]] in [[129-lily-spechler-lc-dietitian]] flags **keto / carnivore as malnutrition risks in LC** specifically — not because they don't have a place, but because LC is energetically expensive at rest (laboured breathing alone burns ~800 extra kcal/day). She's seen patients on these diets eat ~⅓ of their actual metabolic needs and enter measurable fight-or-flight from chronic deficit. Other concerns:
- *Low fibre → fewer short-chain fatty acids* (mast-cell modulators; inversely correlated with circulating histamine).
- *Tryptophan / serotonin / melatonin pathway depends on insulin uptake of competing amino acids → carbs trigger that*; LC patients are already low in serotonin and cortisol, so zero-carb worsens the pathway.
- *Muscle wasting* from chronic deficit reduces cerebral perfusion (muscle is how blood gets to the brain) and mitochondrial mass.

Her practical tooling: track on **Chronometer** (free) — micronutrient levels, not just macros. Match metabolic needs *adjusted for disease state*, not body size. Pair carbs with fat/protein to flatten the blood-sugar curve. Eat by the clock if dysautonomia has muted hunger cues.

Not a count-changing critique — Lily isn't claiming keto/carnivore harmed her or her patients in the structured-trial sense; she's flagging a clinical pattern that's worth weighing alongside the Lorrie Rivers / Sarah Myhill rows above.
