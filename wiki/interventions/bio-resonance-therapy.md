# Bio-resonance Therapy

A frequency-based diagnostic and treatment modality originating in 1970s Germany. Devices (e.g. **Bicom**, **MORA**, **MSAS**) claim to detect "frequencies" associated with viruses, bacteria, fungi, toxins, allergens, and deficiencies — and to deliver corrective frequencies in return. Popular in **Germany, Poland, and parts of central Europe**; rare in UK / US mainstream medicine.

## Wiki position — explicit caveat

> **Mechanism is contested.** Bio-resonance devices are not validated by mainstream evidence. Where peer-reviewed studies exist, results are mixed and often methodologically weak. The most defensible reading: bio-resonance findings (e.g. "EBV reactivation detected") may **prompt useful conversations and downstream confirmatory testing**, but should not be treated as standalone diagnostics.

## How it shows up in podcast testimony

[[lalita]] (#214) used bio-resonance as one input in her second-bout LC workup. The scan reportedly flagged **reactivated EBV + herpes** (both dormant ~30 years prior) and **iron deficiency** (confirmed via bloods → started iron supplementation). The flagged viruses are mechanistically plausible — post-COVID herpesvirus reactivation is documented in the wider literature ([[viral-persistence]] theme) — so in this case the bio-resonance output aligned with a reasonable clinical hypothesis. Whether the device "saw" it via frequencies or whether the practitioner was confirming a known pattern is unverifiable from the transcript.

Lalita did **not** describe bio-resonance treatment as a recovery driver — she described it as **diagnostic input** that informed her supplement choices.

## Caveats

- **Not a substitute for standard diagnostics.** Confirm any actionable finding (e.g. viral reactivation, deficiency) with blood work / standard testing.
- **Practitioner variance enormous.** Quality of clinical reasoning behind the device matters more than the device itself.
- **Cost.** Sessions typically £50-150; multiple sessions common.
