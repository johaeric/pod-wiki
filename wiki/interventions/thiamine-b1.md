# Thiamine (Vitamin B1) / Benfotiamine / TTFD

Water-soluble B vitamin. Cofactor for several mitochondrial enzymes — pyruvate dehydrogenase, alpha-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase, transketolase. **Thiamine** is the dietary form (pork, legumes, fortified grain); **benfotiamine** is a fat-soluble derivative with better tissue penetration (used in diabetic neuropathy); **TTFD** (tetrahydrofurfuryl-disulfide / sulbutiamine-adjacent) is a high-bioavailability form crossing the blood-brain barrier — a community staple in the ME/CFS "high-dose thiamine" protocol promoted by clinicians like Derrick Lonsdale and Elliot Overton.

## Forms (context)

- **Thiamine HCl** — basic OTC form. Low cost.
- **Benfotiamine** — fat-soluble; ~5× tissue bioavailability vs. thiamine HCl; standard in diabetic-neuropathy use cases. Myhill (#118) names this form explicitly.
- **TTFD (allithiamine)** — crosses the blood-brain barrier; the form most associated with the ME/CFS "high-dose thiamine" community protocol. **Not named in the corpus.**
- **Sulbutiamine** — adjacent BBB-crossing analogue marketed as a nootropic.

## Dosing (from corpus)

- **Myhill's stack dose for thiamine/benfotiamine** — not stated in this episode segment.
- **Lorrie's framing** — "high-dose" but no specific number.

The wider non-corpus community protocol (Lonsdale, Overton) commonly cites 500–1,500 mg/day thiamine HCl or 100–300 mg/day benfotiamine, with TTFD typically dosed 50–150 mg/day. **None of these doses are corpus-validated.**

## Cautions

- **"Paradoxical reaction"** — community-reported but not corpus-discussed: starting high-dose thiamine can transiently worsen fatigue / anxiety / GI symptoms as cellular metabolism re-engages. Standard community guidance is to start low and titrate; this is community lore, not corpus-supported.
- **Refeeding-syndrome risk** in severely malnourished patients — thiamine drives carbohydrate metabolism; rapid reintroduction in deficiency can precipitate electrolyte shifts. Edge case for LC patients but worth flagging.
- **Sulphur-sensitivity reactions to TTFD** — some patients with sulphur-metabolism issues (CBS/SUOX) react poorly to TTFD specifically; switch to benfotiamine or thiamine HCl. Community-reported, not corpus-supported.
