# Cognitive Rehabilitation

A targeted clinician-led programme of compensatory strategies for specific cognitive deficits — distinct from [[brain-training]] (computerised attention/processing-speed practice) and from generic CBT-for-cognition.

The wiki's anchor for this is [[090-dr-james-jackson-brain-fog]] — neuropsychologist at Vanderbilt who frames cognitive rehab as **the best-evidenced under-prescribed intervention** for LC cognitive impairment.

## Pioneer / lineage

**Dr Barbara Wilson** (UK) is the named pioneer of compensatory-strategy-based rehab over the last 50 years. The approach has decades of evidence in traumatic brain injury, stroke, and dementia — the hypothesis is the same toolkit applies to LC's "brain injury" presentation.

## What it actually involves

Cognitive rehab is **not "brain games"**. It teaches strategies that compensate for specific deficits:

| Deficit | Example strategies |
|---|---|
| **Executive dysfunction** | Goal Management Training (GMT) — Stop-and-Think, breaking complex tasks into bites, Post-it notes, structured external scaffolding |
| **Processing speed** | Pacing of cognitive load, single-task discipline, avoiding rushed conditions where errors cluster |
| **Memory** | External memory aids, written checklists, structured repetition |

### Goal Management Training (GMT) — anchor protocol

Single best-evidenced approach for **executive dysfunction** across patient populations.

- **Stop-and-Think** — recognise the rhythm of cognitive errors (high stress / hurried / tired / hungry / overscheduled) and train a deliberate pause-and-evaluate at those moments.
- **Automatic-Pilot** — the failure mode label. Brain-injured patients (and stressed healthy people) fly on autopilot and miss critical inputs (Jackson's anecdote: leaving Vanderbilt without the crucial pharmacy pickup).
- **Bite-size tasks** — explicit decomposition of complex tasks before attempting.

## Who delivers it

- **Speech-language pathologists** (SLPs)
- **Occupational therapists** (OTs)

Jackson calls SLPs "an underappreciated group of warriors" in LC. Both can deliver remotely — geographical access is solvable in principle.

## Why it's under-prescribed (Jackson's argument)

- Specialty mismatch: most LC clinics don't routinely refer to SLP/OT for cognition.
- Clinician unawareness — cognitive rehab evidence sits in TBI/stroke literature.
- Patient unawareness — "brain fog" framing hides the fact that there's a clinical pathway.

## Caveats

- **Cognition can drive PEM.** Before starting cognitive rehab, check with treating physician — exertion budgeting applies. SLP/OTs are typically pacing-aware but worth verifying.
- **Brain training (e.g., BrainHQ)** is *complementary*, not equivalent. Cognitive rehab targets specific functional deficits; brain training builds underlying capacity. See [[brain-training]].
- **Antidepressants and anxiolytics** can blunt cognition further — coordinate with physician if cognitive complaints worsen on initiation.
