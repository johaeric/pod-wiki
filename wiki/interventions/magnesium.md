# Magnesium

A base-stack supplement that comes up across multiple clinical and patient contexts in the corpus — POTS adjunct, migraine-frequency reduction, mitochondrial cofactor, sleep aid, restless-legs (dopamine-pathway) adjunct, and transdermal pacing tool (Epsom-salt baths). Cheap, OTC, broad enzymatic role; one of the few supplements that survives the corpus's general "don't take random supplements" caution. Two practitioners ([[carrie-bailey]] #163, [[jacob-teitelbaum]] #167) name **glycinate** specifically as the gentlest oral form and the one they reach for first.

## Forms (corpus mentions)

- **Magnesium glycinate** — [[jacob-teitelbaum]] (#167), [[carrie-bailey]] (#163) — gentlest oral form; "broad enzymatic role"; "helps with everything deficiencies." Carrie names it as one of her two "starter nutrients" alongside B-vitamins.
- **Magnesium (generic, unspecified)** — [[sanjay-gupta]] (#57) for POTS; [[sarah-myhill]] (#118) at 300 mg/day in her base stack; [[david-joffe]] (#143) "for some" with restless legs.
- **Magnesium sulfate (Epsom salts)** — Myhill (#118) at 0.5 kg per bath; transdermal magnesium + sulfate plus detox role (pulls VOCs/pesticides into surface fat layer). [[donna-shaw]] (RA #228) — Epsom-salt baths as part of her pacing-routine that "helped my legs" during recovery.

## Use cases

### Migraine
**[[jacob-teitelbaum]] (#167)**: magnesium reduces migraine frequency; pair with **[[vitamin-b2-riboflavin]] 400 mg/day × 6 weeks** (67–70% migraine-frequency reduction per studies he cites) + food-sensitivity management + early-dose triptans for acute episodes.

### POTS / dysautonomia
**[[sanjay-gupta]] (#57)** — magnesium supplement listed alongside salt-loading, low-carb meals, compression stockings, IV saline as part of his POTS toolkit.

### Mitochondrial / energy
**[[sarah-myhill]] (#118)** — magnesium 300 mg/day in her core ME/CFS base stack alongside vitamin D 10,000 IU (paired with magnesium for absorption) and CoQ10 100 mg. **[[moira-newiss]] (#21)** — magnesium with CoQ10, selenium, zinc as "mitochondrial nutrient support," food first.

### Restless legs / dopamine-pathway sleep disruption
**[[david-joffe]] (#143)** — restless legs are 40%+ more common in women with LC and disrupt sleep onset; the substrate is dopaminergic, and "for some, magnesium is important" alongside iron-status work. New use-case in the corpus — not a generic sleep recommendation but a targeted dopamine/restless-legs lever.

### Foundational deficiency repletion
**[[carrie-bailey]] (#163)** — magnesium glycinate as one of her two "starter nutrients" (with B-vitamins) for LC patients; "helps with everything deficiencies and it addresses everything there." Treats it as floor-level before more targeted work.

### Sleep / general "base stack"
**[[rachel-whitfield]] (#8)** — after years of trial-and-error, kept only D, B12, probiotic, and magnesium of all the supplements she'd tried — the rest "didn't seem to do anything."

### Pacing / recovery routine (Epsom-salt baths)
**[[donna-shaw]] (RA #228)** — Epsom-salt baths as part of the gentle daily routine that helped her legs and supported her pacing arc during ME/CFS recovery.

## Dosing (across corpus)

- **300 mg/day** — Myhill's baseline (#118)
- **Glycinate form** for sleep / calm / general repletion — Teitelbaum (#167), Bailey (#163)
- **Epsom-salt bath** — Myhill (#118) — 0.5 kg per bath; Shaw (RA #228) — recurring routine
- **Migraine prophylaxis pairing** — paired with B2 400 mg (Teitelbaum #167); specific magnesium dose not specified in the migraine paragraph

## Mechanism mentions (educational, not recommendations)

- **Breathing-pattern disorder / electrolyte disturbance** — [[kelly-mitchell]] (#12) notes that low CO2 from chronic hyperventilation can disturb electrolytes including magnesium, calcium, potassium — driving muscle tremors and fasciculations. Magnesium framed as part of the depletion picture, not as a treatment.
- **Diet-quality marker** — [[lily-spechler]] (#129) uses magnesium levels as one of several markers (with folate, B-vitamins) to demonstrate to patients on restrictive diets how nutrient density collapses when carbs are stripped.

## Cautions

- **GI effects** — magnesium oxide / citrate / sulfate can cause loose stools at higher doses (or be intentionally used as a laxative). Glycinate is the gentlest oral form (Teitelbaum, Bailey).
- **Renal impairment** — accumulation risk; not a low-stakes supplement in CKD.
- **Drug interactions** — can chelate certain antibiotics (quinolones, tetracyclines) and reduce absorption of some thyroid + bisphosphonate meds; separate by 2+ hours.

> Cross-ref: see `ercipedia/wiki/interventions/` for any peer-reviewed magnesium evidence — substantial non-LC RCT data exists for migraine + sleep + POTS adjacencies outside this corpus.
