# NAD⁺ / Niacin (vitamin B3)

NAD⁺ (nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide) is a critical coenzyme in cellular energy metabolism — central to the electron-transport chain, mitochondrial oxidative phosphorylation, sirtuin-mediated DNA repair, and inflammatory regulation via PARP. NAD⁺ levels decline with age, with chronic inflammation, and (per emerging research) potentially with post-viral illness.

**Niacin** (vitamin B3) and **nicotinamide** are common oral precursors; **NAD+ IV infusion** and **NMN / NR (nicotinamide riboside / mononucleotide)** are more expensive direct precursors marketed as longevity supplements.

## Why it might help in LC

- Supports mitochondrial ATP production directly (electron-transport-chain cofactor)
- Modulates chronic inflammation via sirtuin and PARP pathways
- Some evidence for benefit in chronic fatigue syndromes and metabolic disorders
- Convergent with the [[mitochondrial-dysfunction]] / Cell Danger Response framework

## What's been said in the podcast

- **[[gez-medinger]] (#46).** On NAD⁺/niacin via Dr [[bindi-venel]] (South Africa). Reports a sustained **~20% energy-envelope improvement plus cognitive clarity**. Maintenance — stops working within 2–3 days if discontinued. Does not credit as recovery factor.

## Caveats

- **Niacin flush** at higher oral doses — harmless but uncomfortable; non-flush forms (nicotinamide, NR) avoid it.
- **Liver toxicity at sustained high doses** of plain niacin — monitor.
- **Cost** — NAD+ IV and NR/NMN are expensive; oral niacin is cheap.
- Form matters — NR/NMN have different bioavailability profiles from niacin or nicotinamide.
