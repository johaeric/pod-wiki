# Electrolytes (balanced supplements / branded drinks)

Balanced electrolyte products — branded sachets and tablets (SiS, Precision 1500 / Precision Hydration, LMNT, Vitassium, Dioralyte-as-electrolyte-source), and the generic "electrolytes" recommendation as a POTS / dysautonomia first-line tool. The corpus consistently pairs them with sodium intake, fluid loading, and compression as the non-drug base of POTS care. This page covers the *balanced* form (Na + K ± Mg ± glucose in solution). For sodium-only on-food loading see [[salt-loading]]; for the SGLT1-targeted glucose+salt formulation see [[oral-rehydration-solution]].

## Forms / brands the corpus actually names

- **SiS / Precision 1500** — [[boon-lim]] (LCP #40) for the morning POTS routine (isotonic tablet in cold flask of water).
- **Generic "electrolyte sachets" / "electrolyte drinks"** — [[claire-taylor]] (LCP #116), [[todd-davenport]] (LCP #141), [[lily-spechler]] (LCP #129, #201), [[sanjay-gupta]] (LCP #57), [[anjali-agarwal]] (LCP #80) — named without a specific brand.
- **Branded examples cited as substitutes for ORS** in the corpus: LMNT, SiS, Precision Hydration 1500 (see ORS page) — flagged as preferred when ORS taste is unpalatable.
- **No mention of LMNT, Liquid IV, Nuun, DripDrop, Trace Minerals, SaltStick, etc. by name in the indexed episodes** beyond the generic substitution note above — patients and clinicians on the podcast use "electrolytes" as a category, not a brand.

## Use cases

### POTS / dysautonomia (primary)

This is the dominant use case in the corpus. Every clinician interviewed on POTS in the wiki frames electrolytes as part of the non-drug base alongside salt loading, fluid loading, compression, and head-of-bed elevation.

- [[boon-lim]] (LCP #40) — morning protocol: isotonic electrolyte tablet pre-dissolved in a cold flask of water from the night before; drink 500 mL while still lying down, let it redistribute 3–5 min before standing. Pair with isometric muscle activation (glutes/calves/quads) before getting upright.
- [[sanjay-gupta]] (LCP #57) — "having lots of salt, having lots of electrolytes, having a lot of water can be very helpful" — listed alongside compression garments and low-carb meals.
- [[claire-taylor]] (LCP #116) — "a lot of patients have worked out electrolytes will be nicer than adding loads of salt to your food" — electrolyte sachets as a palatability-driven substitute for 6–10 g/day on-food sodium loading.
- [[anjali-agarwal]] (LCP #80) — EDS/POTS framing: connective tissue and mast cells both "love water and electrolytes"; recommends constant-availability water bottle with electrolytes to keep sympathetic drive from spiking.
- [[todd-davenport]] (LCP #141) — "hydration using salt, salt supplements, electrolytes, electrolyte drinks" because of documented low plasma volume in ME/CFS — part of circulatory-support stack.
- [[amy-mooney]] (LCP #170) — "electrolyte water" pre-staged as part of the post-shower recovery kit alongside heating pad, robe, power smoothie, sensate-vibration device — pre-position the support, then trigger the activity.

### Blood glucose / dysautonomia coupling

- [[lily-spechler]] (LCP #201) — describes a CGM-tracked case where pre-getting-out-of-bed electrolytes + compression brought the morning glucose-on-standing spike in check. Frames electrolytes as part of dysautonomia support that also stabilises the glucose dysregulation that rides on top of LC autonomic dysfunction.

### As a POTS-stack item in recovery stories

- [[sarah-lotus-path-yogi]] (LCP #114) — listed her POTS lifestyle modifications as "compression and hydration and salt in everything electrolytes and all of that" — these came from peer-patient knowledge-sharing, not her clinic.
- [[carl-robot]] (LCP #176) — "electrolytes every morning... which I realized was meant to help with the dysautonomia. And it kind of did" — partial benefit, not the recovery driver (that was the Simprove probiotic trial).

## Dosing patterns

- **Morning, pre-upright, with the first water of the day** — Boon Lim (#40), Spechler (#201).
- **Throughout the day, as the working fluid carrier** — Agarwal (#80), Davenport (#141).
- **Specific amounts rarely given** for branded electrolyte products; the closest dose-anchored number in the corpus is Taylor's 6–10 g/day **sodium target** (#116), which patients hit via a mix of food salt and sachets.
- **Glucose-containing formulations** (i.e. true ORS, not just Na/K tablets) work fundamentally better for plasma-volume expansion via the SGLT1 mechanism — see [[oral-rehydration-solution]]. The corpus consensus: if you can tolerate the taste, ORS > generic electrolyte drink > salt-on-food alone.

## Cautions

- **POTS sub-phenotype matters.** [[lily-spechler]] (LCP #129) — strong "not blanket advice" warning: electrolytes-with-sodium help the **pooling-pattern POTS** majority but can worsen the **vasoconstriction-pattern** subgroup (ACE2-receptor damage → elevated angiotensin-II → already over-constricted vessels). Same end-state under-perfused brain, opposite mechanism. Reported reactions in her practice include vomiting and an intracranial-hypertension sensation. **Patient feedback during a sodium trial is diagnostic information about subtype, not a sign to push through.**
- **Potassium content** matters for people on [[fludrocortisone]] (florinef wastes K) or, conversely, on spironolactone / ACE inhibitors / ARBs (where K can accumulate). Read sachet labels; the K dose across branded products varies widely.
- **Renal disease** — generic electrolyte loading is not low-stakes in CKD. Defer to clinician.
- **Diabetic patients** — glucose-containing formulations (true ORS, some sport-isotonic drinks) raise blood sugar; sugar-free electrolyte tablets exist.
- **Hyperventilation-driven electrolyte disturbance** ([[kelly-mitchell]], LCP #12) is a *different* problem — chronic low CO2 lowers bicarbonate and can disturb magnesium/calcium/potassium balance regardless of intake. The fix is the breathing pattern, not more electrolytes. Worth ruling in/out before assuming a supplementation gap.

> Cross-ref: see [[oral-rehydration-solution]] for the SGLT1 / Meadow-POTS-study evidence on why glucose + salt in the right ratio is meaningfully more effective for blood-volume expansion than generic electrolyte drinks.
