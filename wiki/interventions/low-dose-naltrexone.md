# Low-Dose Naltrexone (LDN)

Naltrexone (a μ-opioid receptor antagonist) prescribed at **~1.5–4.5 mg/day** — far below the 50 mg used for opioid/alcohol-use disorders. At low dose the proposed mechanisms are **immune modulation, microglial / TLR-4 effects, and reduced neuroinflammation**. Used off-label across ME/CFS, fibromyalgia, MCAS, and now long covid. Often prescribed by chronic-fatigue / functional-medicine specialists rather than GPs.

> Cross-ref: see `ercipedia/wiki/interventions/ldn.md` for the trial evidence picture (pilot RCTs in fibromyalgia / MS / Crohn's; LC RCTs in flight at recording).

## What guests on this podcast report

- Often prescribed alongside antiviral trials, gut-protocols, and other multi-axis approaches — clean attribution is hard.
- Started low (often 0.5 mg) and slowly titrated up.
- Reported benefits: reduced pain / inflammation, "edge off," cognitive improvement; sometimes the first inflection in an arc.
- Side effects: vivid dreams, sleep disturbance early; usually settle.

## Dosing protocol (anchor — Groysman #91)

The wiki's most explicit titration protocol comes from [[091-groysman-qa-pt1]]:

- **Start at 1 mg/day**, titrate **+0.5 mg per week** to **4.5 mg/day** (taken once or twice daily).
- Some protocols start at 4.5 mg directly; conservative start preferred.
- **Minimum 1 month** before assessing effect; some need **3 months**. Slow process — not a few-day judgement.
- **Compounding pharmacy required** (commercial dose is 50 mg). Cost should be **<$100/month**. Reports of $9,000 are price-gouging — flag and shop around.
- **Primary effect: fatigue**. Some brain-fog response. **Won't fix** anosmia/parosmia, GI, anxiety, mood symptoms.
- **Side-effect: opioid reversal** if patient is on chronic opioids — separate dosing in time, or avoid.
- **If LDN itself causes more fatigue, take it at night.**
