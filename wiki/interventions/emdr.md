# EMDR (Eye Movement Desensitisation and Reprocessing)

A trauma-processing therapy developed by Francine Shapiro (1987). The patient briefly recalls a traumatic memory while the therapist guides their eyes (or hands, via tapping) through **bilateral stimulation** — alternating left-right activation that appears to allow the brain to *desensitise* and *reprocess* the memory so that it stops triggering a present-tense danger response.

## Why it shows up on this podcast

In the polyvagal / mind-body / neuroplastic-symptoms framework that increasingly dominates LC recovery stories, **unprocessed trauma is one of the loads keeping the autonomic nervous system stuck in danger mode**. For patients whose chronic-fatigue / LC pattern overlaps with old unprocessed trauma, addressing that trauma is part of the autonomic-stabilisation work.

The clearest articulation on the wiki so far is from Rachel Rock (#177): the bodily sensation while reliving trauma in EMDR was **identical** to her chronic-fatigue feeling — strong evidence that her ANS recorded both as the same alarm state.

## Caveats

- Best done with a therapist trained in EMDR; not a self-administered practice.
- Patients in active polyvagal work (e.g. Safe and Sound Protocol) may need to **pause** EMDR — Rachel paused EMDR while doing SSP and resumed afterwards.
- Trauma material can surface that becomes overwhelming; choose a therapist familiar with chronic-illness contexts.
