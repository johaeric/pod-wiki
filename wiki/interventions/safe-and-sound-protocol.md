# Safe and Sound Protocol (SSP)

A structured 5-hour listening intervention designed by **Stephen Porges** (developer of polyvagal theory). Filtered music delivered via headphones over multiple short sessions (typically 30 min/day for 5 days, or extended pacing). The proposed mechanism: stimulating the auditory pathway in a way that **trains the middle-ear muscles + vagus nerve toward ventral-vagal (rest-and-digest) tone**, increasing nervous-system flexibility — *not* permanent residence in rest-and-digest, but easier movement up and down the polyvagal "ladder".

Delivered only by **certified SSP practitioners** (Unyte / Integrated Listening Systems network). Not free; not available over the counter.

## How it sits in the polyvagal frame

Porges's three states (per Sally Riggs, ep #23):
- **Rest-and-digest** (ventral vagal) — connection, gratitude, acceptance accessible
- **Fight-or-flight** (sympathetic) — panic, no acceptance
- **Immobilization** (dorsal vagal) — heaviness, brain fog, no higher functions accessible

> "If acceptance isn't working, it's not personal failure — your nervous system can't access it from where you are. SSP is a tool to get back up the ladder more flexibly." — Sally Riggs

## Cautions

- People with significant trauma history may find SSP destabilising if delivered too fast — pacing is up to the practitioner.
- Some people find it less powerful than equipment-based vagal-stimulation devices (Alpha-Stim mentioned by Sally as similar mechanism).
- **Vet your practitioner for LC literacy.** Many certified SSP practitioners (chiropractors, yoga teachers, OTs) have no LC experience — Sally Riggs explicitly warns about this in #101. Look for someone who understands that LC patients are predominantly in dorsal-vagal shutdown, not in fight-or-flight.
- **Co-regulation matters.** Should be delivered face-to-face or Zoom with practitioner's face visible — the practitioner needs to re-anchor you if you destabilise during listening. Mask-and-mute setups defeat the mechanism.
- Climbing out of shutdown via SSP routes through fight-or-flight, which is uncomfortable; the wrong scaffolding can dump patients deeper into shutdown for longer.
