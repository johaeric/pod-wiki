# Somatic Tracking

A specific named practice in the **Sarno → Schubiner → Alan Gordon** lineage. Often delivered via the **Curable app** as a guided meditation; can also be done unguided.

## What it is

- Sit with a sensation in the body and *name what it actually feels like*. Not what it means, not what it leads to, not what to do about it. Just the qualitative texture: burning / tingling / shooting / dullness / numbness / warmth / coolness / pressure / movement; widespread vs localised.
- Watch with **mindful curiosity** — not fight, not avoid. Just witness.
- Keep watching as the sensation shifts (it usually does — sometimes dissipating, sometimes moving, sometimes becoming an emotion).

## Why it works (mind-body lineage explanation)

- The brain's danger response is triggered by *fighting* or *fearing* the sensation. Watching it without reacting reduces the danger signal.
- Over time, this **down-regulates the autonomic alarm state** that — in the mind-body / neuroplastic-symptoms framework — generates the symptoms in the first place.
- It also **reconnects** the patient to the body. Many LC / chronic-illness patients have disconnected/dissociated from their body because the body has felt unbearable; somatic tracking is the slow reconnection.

## Distinction from related practices

- **Body scan meditation** is similar but typically more relaxation-oriented.
- **Pain Reprocessing Therapy (PRT)** — Alan Gordon's framework — uses somatic tracking as one of its core practices.
- **Mindfulness of pain** in the Buddhist / MBSR tradition is the spiritual ancestor.
- **Sarno-style TMS work** is the predecessor; somatic tracking is the modernised, neuroscience-grounded technique.

## Caveat

- For some patients, sitting with bodily sensations early in illness is *too much* — overwhelming. Tolin (#166) acknowledges that disconnection / dissociation is a *valid coping mechanism* when the body feels unbearable. Somatic tracking is for when the patient has enough capacity to *return*.
