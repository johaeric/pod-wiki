# Methylene blue

A synthetic dye (methylthioninium chloride) with a long pharmaceutical history — historic medical uses include malaria, methemoglobinemia (the FDA-approved indication), urinary infection treatment, and IV imaging. In recent years it has appeared in **bioenergetic / longevity / nootropic** circles as a mitochondrial electron-transport-chain support compound at low oral doses.

[[keith-littlewood]] recommends it (#45) for restoring physiology in **COVID-injured and vaccine-injured patients**, citing SARS-CoV-2 inhibition papers and an Indian respiratory physiologist using it for TB lung-scarring. No specific patient with a documented outcome is named on-air.

## Mechanism

- **Low dose (≤ 1–2 mg/kg)**: acts as an alternative electron carrier in the mitochondrial ETC, donating electrons directly to cytochrome c — bypasses dysfunctional complex I/III. Mild antioxidant.
- **High dose**: acts as a guanylate cyclase inhibitor and a **monoamine oxidase inhibitor (MAOI)**.

## Interactions and contraindications

**This intervention has serious drug-interaction risks. Do not combine with:**

- **Any SSRI / SNRI / TCA / MAOI / triptans / lithium / tramadol** — risk of serotonin syndrome (potentially fatal).
- **G6PD-deficient patients** — risk of hemolysis.
- **Pregnancy** — fetal harm reported.

Cytochrome-P450 interactions are also extensive; any concurrent prescribed drug regime needs a pharmacist interaction review.

## Caveats

- Off-label / unregulated when sold as a longevity supplement (Littlewood mentions "R&D Labs" as a source). Quality, purity, and dose accuracy vary widely between sellers.
- Stains body fluids blue-green (urine, sometimes mouth). Cosmetically obvious during use.
- No Long-Covid RCT evidence as of recording.
