# Smell training

A structured behavioural intervention for post-viral olfactory dysfunction. The patient sniffs a small set of **strong, distinctive odorants** twice daily for 2–6 months, with active mental engagement (memory recall, anticipation, naming).

The classical "starter four" odorants:
- **Lemon** (citral / citrus)
- **Eucalyptus** (cineole / cool)
- **Rose** (floral)
- **Clove** (eugenol / spicy)

After several weeks, rotate to a new set of four to keep the system stretched.

## Why it works (mechanism)

Smell training is **not a cure — it's a recovery accelerant.** Multiple controlled trials (in young, old, post-viral, post-traumatic, and post-rhinitis patients) show that the smell-training cohort outperforms the no-training cohort on objective olfactory testing at 2-month, 6-month, and 9-month follow-up.

Mechanism (per [[chrissi-kelly]] #50):

- The work happens **top-down** — in the brain — not at the receptor level in the nose.
- Active mental engagement with the smell sends signals **into** the olfactory bulb, preserving bulb volume.
- Olfactory bulb volume strongly correlates with depression — bulb activity protects mood.
- The broader behavioural change — habitually interrogating the environment for smell — keeps the entire neural pathway active during regeneration.

## Compliance is the main failure mode

The single biggest reason smell training fails is that patients try it once or twice and stop. The intervention only works if maintained for **months**.

## Practical setup

- 2× daily, 20–30 seconds per scent
- Engage mentally — recall a memory, name what it should smell like, anticipate
- Keep going even if you can't currently smell it
- Rotate scents every ~12 weeks
- Daily journal of what you can / can't smell helps maintain engagement

## Caveats

- **Not for severe parosmia patients in active disgust phase** — wait for the acute distress to settle, then start.
- **Not a substitute for nutrition support** in parosmia-driven food avoidance.
- **Not a substitute for mental-health support** if olfactory loss is driving depression.
