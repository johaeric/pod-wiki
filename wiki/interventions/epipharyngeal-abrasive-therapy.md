# Epipharyngeal Abrasive Therapy (EAT, "B-Spot Therapy")

A Japanese-developed treatment in which a saline-soaked swab is **abraded against the epipharynx** (the area where the back of the nose meets the throat). Developed by Dr Osamu Hori; mainstream in Japan as treatment for chronic epipharyngitis, and now with **published Japanese case-series for long covid recovery**. Two proposed mechanisms:

1. **Antiseptic** — directly clears any persistent viral / inflammatory load in the nasopharyngeal mucosa (a known reservoir for SARS-CoV-2).
2. **Vagus-nerve stimulation** — the epipharynx is densely innervated by vagal branches; abrasion appears to up-regulate parasympathetic tone (similar mechanism class to other VNS approaches).

Almost no US clinicians offer it as of 2024–2026. In-clinic Japanese practitioners + a home-practice variant taught to patients for ongoing maintenance.
