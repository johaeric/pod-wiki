# Wild / cold-water swimming

Outdoor swimming or cold-water dunking — often in lochs, rivers, or sea. Reported benefits skew **mood, vagal/autonomic, and connection-to-nature** rather than fitness; long-haulers describe it as low-effort, short-duration, and emotionally restorative. Cold thermoregulation does cost energy though, so winter and wetsuit choices matter.

Distinguish from **cold plunge / Wim Hof / contrast hydrotherapy** (more structured, often indoor, often ascribed metabolic/inflammation effects). This page is for the gentle outdoor-swim variant; create a separate page for cold-plunge protocols when they show up.

## Mechanism (added from #49)

Adrian Baker (GP, recovered LC, lifelong cold-water swimmer) names the proposed mechanisms in #49:

- **Endorphin release** — body's natural opiates; analgesia lasts the rest of the day, not just during the swim.
- **Mammalian diving reflex** when face is briefly submerged — pulse slows, breathing slows, parasympathetic shift. Protective reflex.
- **Cold-induced microcirculation** — repeated cold exposure to extremities stimulates new capillary formation, which appears to help arthritic joints (anecdotal — both [[adrian-baker]] and [[leanne-#49]] report relief).
- **Anti-inflammatory** at a systemic level (also anecdotal; the mechanism story is mast-cell + endothelial-tone + parasympathetic shift).
- **Cold-shock-response adaptation** — hyperventilation response disappears after 5–7 consecutive daily immersions.
- **Mental-health + social/tribe** effects — separable from the cold itself but consistently reported.

## Practical heuristics (#49)

- **Time-in-water rule**: ~1 minute per degree Celsius of water temperature, in cold weather.
- **Slow breathing** is the practical training key — slow exhale, head out for first sessions.
- **Do NOT shower hot afterwards.** Skin vasoconstriction is protecting your core; sudden vasodilation (hot shower) drops BP and can faint you. Slowly rewarm with dry clothes, wool hat, warm-but-not-hot drink.
- **Get out of wet kit within 5–10 minutes of leaving the water** — that's when the second cold wave hits.
- **Shivering is normal and can last hours** — not a danger sign by itself.
- **Tow float** for visibility (jet skis, boats) and emergency flotation.
- **Wet suit vs costume**: personal preference; wet suit lets you stay longer.
- **Company matters** for safety + tribe.
- **Read the water before getting in.** Currents change fast (Adrian's own near-miss with a rip current).
