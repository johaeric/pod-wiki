# IV Saline

Intravenous saline (typically 1L isotonic) administered as a clinic infusion to POTS / dysautonomia patients. **Bypasses the gut entirely**, getting fluid directly into the vascular compartment in a way that oral salt + water cannot match for some patients.

Mechanism story: many POTS / LC patients are functionally **hypovolemic** despite normal labs. The autonomic system over-urinates due to ADH dysregulation; oral fluids leave too quickly to support standing.

Effect duration: **24–36 hours** then re-treat. Patients typically need regular weekly or fortnightly infusions.

## Per [[sanjay-gupta-cardiologist]] (#57)

- ~40 of his patients on a regular infusion schedule
- "Wheelchair in, walks out" — color returns, brain fog clears, fatigue eases
- He calls it "the most transformative thing I've found"
- **Not on the NHS pathway**; runs in his private clinic
- He is actively soliciting research collaborators

## Caveats

- Requires a private clinic / IV-trained provider
- IV access may degrade over repeated punctures
- Cost: per-infusion + repeat schedule
- Not formally evidence-graded; observational + clinical experience only
- Gupta acknowledges the lack of formal trials — "patients are telling me they're better, they're not worse, and I haven't harmed anyone"
