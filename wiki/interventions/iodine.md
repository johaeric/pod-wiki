# Iodine

Essential trace mineral. Two distinct corpus framings, both clinician — no patient outcome rows.

1. **Thyroid-hormone synthesis cofactor** — [[keith-littlewood]] (LCP #45) names iodine (and [[selenium]]) as enzymatic cofactors in the T4/T3 production pathway. Mentioned-only; he doesn't recommend iodine supplementation as such.
2. **Antimicrobial / "groundhog regime" multitasker** — [[sarah-myhill]] (LCP #118) calls vitamin C and iodine her **"two favourite multitasking tools"** for the upper-fermenting gut, acute infections, and chronic-infection load reduction. This is a distinctive — and unconventional — framing: iodine as a daily oral antimicrobial, not as a thyroid supplement.

## Honesty about the evidence here

Two mentions, both clinician, **no patient `key_to_recovery` or `helped_partial` rows.** Myhill's recommendation is unusually strong by corpus standards ("everybody" should be on vitamin C and iodine in her "groundhog basic" regime) but it's a single voice; her broader integrative-medicine framework is not echoed by other corpus clinicians. Treat as Tier-C evidence.

For Eric specifically: iodine sits in the thyroid-axis cluster with [[selenium]]; both have non-corpus literature behind them on hormonal-dysfunction LC subsets, but the corpus offers no recovery-story to anchor a personal trial.

## Forms (context, not from corpus)

- **Lugol's solution** (iodine + potassium iodide in water) — Myhill's typical recommended form on her website. **The specific form is not stated in the LCP #118 segment** — only "iodine."
- **Potassium iodide tablets** — pharmaceutical-grade single-dose form, generally for acute exposure prophylaxis (radiation).
- **Iodine in salt** — fortification programmes deliver daily-RDA iodine in iodized table salt in many countries. Eric's salt-loading regimen with **non-iodized** salt may need a separate iodine source.
- **Kelp / seaweed** — food-form iodine; dose is variable and can be very high.

## Use cases (corpus)

### Antimicrobial / chronic-infection-load reduction (Myhill)
**[[sarah-myhill]] (LCP #118)** — vitamin C and iodine as her "two favourite multitasking tools" because they are cheap, OTC, safe, and "contact-kill all microbes." Used for:
- **Upper-fermenting gut / MCAS** — kill off histamine-producing gut microbes; addressing the "inflammatory hole in the energy bucket"
- **Acute infections** — "groundhog acute" protocol: high-dose vitamin C + iodine + fasting
- **Chronic infection load** — "groundhog chronic" for LC, ME, arthritis, cancer

Key practical note from this episode: **don't take vitamin C and iodine at the same time** — one is reducing, one is oxidising; they cancel each other out. Separate by hours.

> "Vitamin C and iodine are my two favorite multitasking tools because they do so much — they contact-kill all microbes." — Sarah Myhill, LCP #118

### Thyroid-hormone synthesis (Littlewood — context only)
**[[keith-littlewood]] (LCP #45)** — names iodine and [[selenium]] as enzymatic cofactors in the hypothalamus → pituitary → thyroid → T4/T3 axis. Mentioned-only; he doesn't directly recommend iodine supplementation in the segment.

## Cautions

- **Thyroid sensitivity** — iodine is biphasic: too little drives hypothyroidism, too much can drive hyperthyroidism or Hashimoto's flares (especially in patients already iodine-replete). Test before chronic high-dose supplementation.
- **Drug interactions** — interacts with thyroid medications (levothyroxine, methimazole), lithium, amiodarone, ACE inhibitors.
- **MCAS / hypersensitivity** — some patients react to iodine specifically; rare but documented.
- **Pregnancy / lactation** — RDA is *higher* in pregnancy; deficiency in this window is the most consequential. Supplementation only with obstetric guidance.
- **Skin staining at concentrated topical doses** — cosmetic only.
