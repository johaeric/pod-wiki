# Radical rest / aggressive rest therapy

A deliberate, usually time-bounded protocol of **near-total rest**: stop exercise, minimise cognitive load, lie flat as much as possible, "do nothing" until the body breaks the inflammatory/post-exertional cycle. Conceptually adjacent to **pacing** but more extreme — pacing is an ongoing posture; radical rest is an active intervention used to interrupt a downward spiral.

Sometimes formalised as **Aggressive Rest Therapy (ART)** in the ME/CFS literature. The term *radical rest* is associated with Fiona Lawson Engel and the LC patient community; it foregrounds that rest must be unapologetic and "rest from doing nothing fancy."

> Cross-ref: see `ercipedia/wiki/interventions/rest.md` for the trial-evidence picture (limited).

## Common reported benefits

- Breaking a push-and-crash cycle
- Headache resolution (recurring on this podcast)
- Restoring some energy floor

## Common challenges

- Feels counterintuitive ("I was supposed to keep moving")
- Loss of identity / worth tied to activity
- Hard to maintain over weeks

| #170 | [[amy-mooney]] | practitioner (OT, US) | recommended | pem, sensory-overload, general-lc | **Anchor of the *aggressive-rest* operationalisation.** Rest is not "doing nothing" — it's *deliberate* sensory + cognitive + somatic deactivation: eye masks, ear plugs / noise cancelling, weighted blanket, vibration tools (Sensate), joint-compression-without-exertion. Two-rest types: **pre-emptive rest before** an activity to lower its cost; **recuperative rest after** with a *pre-arranged support kit* (decisions made before crashing). Three-day delayed-PEM window — give any activity 3 days before declaring it safe. |
