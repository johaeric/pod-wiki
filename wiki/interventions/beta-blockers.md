# Beta blockers

Cardiac beta-adrenergic blockers (propranolol, bisoprolol, atenolol, metoprolol). On this podcast they appear primarily as **first-line POTS treatment** — slowing the postural heart-rate spike that drives the dysautonomia symptom complex. Sometimes also used for migraine prophylaxis or anxiety somatics.

In LC specifically, the picture often looks like: undiagnosed POTS, GP unaware, patient stumbles into the diagnosis online → beta-blocker trial → striking functional improvement. Amy Small's case (ep #5) is the canonical version of this pathway on this podcast.

> Cross-ref: see `ercipedia/wiki/interventions/beta-blockers.md` for the trial-evidence picture.

**Eric note**: Eric is on **ivabradine** (a different HR-control drug, often used when beta-blockers aren't tolerated or where pure HR control is wanted without the broader sympathetic blockade) — see project `CLAUDE.md`. The two drugs occupy adjacent niches in POTS pharmacotherapy.

## Common reported benefits

- Resolution of postural tachycardia
- Reduced breathlessness on stairs
- Less brain-fog from the cardiovascular component
- Improved exercise tolerance up to a clear ceiling

## Common cautions

- Bradycardia / hypotension if dose too high
- Cold extremities / fatigue side-effect
- Some POTS patients tolerate ivabradine better
