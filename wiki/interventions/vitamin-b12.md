# Vitamin B12 (Cobalamin)

Water-soluble B vitamin, methylation cofactor, essential for nervous-system function (myelin synthesis), red-cell production, and homocysteine clearance. The corpus has two patient `helped_partial` rows — one of them ([[sarah-lotus-path-yogi]] on LCP #114) explicitly attributes peripheral-neuropathy improvement to **high-dose B12 injections** prescribed by [[sarah-myhill]] — plus two clinicians who routinely find B12 low in LC patients and supplement.

The unusual thing about B12 in this corpus: clinicians and patients converge. Bailey (#163) is one of the few voices who flags it as **more important than vitamin D** for LC ("nobody's bringing in the C and the lysine and the zinc — it's like D D D D D"). Groysman (#92) finds B12 routinely low. Two recovered/recovering patients ([[rachel-whitfield]] #8 and [[sarah-lotus-path-yogi]] #114) both kept B12 in their long-term stack after extensive supplement-culling.

## Forms (corpus mentions)

- **High-dose injections** — [[sarah-lotus-path-yogi]] (LCP #114) via [[sarah-myhill]] for peripheral neuropathy. Form not specified in the transcript (Myhill generally uses methylcobalamin and hydroxocobalamin); paired with "other co-factors."
- **Oral / sublingual high-dose tablets** — [[carrie-bailey]] (LCP #163) recommends 5,000 µg daily. Calls out US RDA of 50 µg as "way too low" — "you would think you're getting enough B12 but in essence don't look at that part to figure that out." Notes there is **no toxic upper limit**.
- **Generic oral B12** — [[rachel-whitfield]] (LCP #8) — one of four supplements she kept in her cull (vitamin D, B12, probiotic, magnesium).

## Use cases

### Peripheral neuropathy / post-vaccine neurological symptoms
**[[sarah-lotus-path-yogi]] (LCP #114)** — developed peripheral neuropathy after COVID vaccinations (pins-and-needles, numbness spreading from feet up). [[sarah-myhill]] (whom she saw clinically) prescribed B12 injections specifically for this. Sarah's framing: viruses and vaccinations can wear away the myelin sheath; **high-dose B12 plus cofactors can rebuild it, but you may feel worse before better**. She names B12 deficiency as one of her identified "root causes" alongside gut microbiome problems and leaky gut.

> "Through the really high doses of B12 and other co-actors it can feel worse before it gets better." — Sarah, LCP #114

### General LC supplementation (clinician baseline)
**[[robert-groysman]] (LCP #92)** — "I look at most of the B vitamins, B12 and six are often low; occasionally we find B12 high and some of the other B vitamins occasionally low." Tests, then supplements.

**[[carrie-bailey]] (LCP #163)** — frames B12 as "a special nutrient that is so critical for your nervous system, for your liver, for your brain to operate." Argues the field is over-fixated on vitamin D and under-fixated on B12 + C + zinc + lysine. Recommends 5,000 µg/day.

### Recovered-patient base stack
**[[rachel-whitfield]] (LCP #8)** — after years of trial-and-error, kept only D, B12, probiotic, and magnesium of all the supplements she'd tried — the rest "didn't seem to do anything." Implicit positive signal — she kept it because she felt it was doing something.

## Dosing (across corpus)

- **5,000 µg/day oral** — [[carrie-bailey]] (#163). "There's no toxic upper limit."
- **Injection (Myhill protocol)** — [[sarah-lotus-path-yogi]] (#114). Dose not specified in the transcript; Myhill's published protocols typically use methylcobalamin or hydroxocobalamin at high mg-range doses for neurological indications. Verify dose with the prescriber.
- **Generic OTC dose** — [[rachel-whitfield]] (#8). Specific dose not stated.

## Cautions

- **Injection-related risks** — bruising, infection, bleeding, rare nerve injury at injection site (mentioned generically by [[robert-groysman]] LCP #76 in the context of any injection, including B12 shots and flu shots).
- **Methylation status matters** — patients with MTHFR variants may do better on methylcobalamin than cyanocobalamin (not discussed in corpus, but relevant context).
- **Underlying-cause workup** — sudden B12 deficiency may signal pernicious anaemia, ileal disease, or PPI/metformin interaction. A clinician should investigate why levels are low, not just supplement.
- **"Feel worse before better" in neuropathy contexts** — Sarah (#114) explicitly notes this; can be alarming if not anticipated.

> Cross-ref: see `ercipedia/wiki/interventions/` for any peer-reviewed B12 evidence — substantial non-LC evidence exists for cobalamin in pernicious anaemia, neuropathy, and homocysteine reduction outside this corpus.
