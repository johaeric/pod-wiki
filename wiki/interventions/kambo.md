# Kambo (frog-poison ceremony)

A traditional Amazonian ceremony in which the secretion of the *Phyllomedusa bicolor* (giant monkey frog) is applied to small surface burns on the skin. Produces a short, intense physiological response: vasodilation, vomiting, often diarrhoea, sometimes screaming or crying release, transient weakness. Practitioners frame it as a detoxification + emotional / trauma-release modality.

> Wiki position: **logged because a recovered patient names it as a late-stage emotional-release tool**, not because the underlying claims are validated. **Caution flag**: powerful, ceremony-only, contraindicated in unstable cardiovascular or autonomic conditions, and *not advisable* during active bedbound or PEM-prone phases — vomiting + dehydration + sympathetic-spike intensity can push a fragile system over a cliff. Considered only as a late-stage / post-recovery tool by the one guest who has named it.
