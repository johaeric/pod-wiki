# Acetyl-L-Carnitine (ALCAR / L-Carnitine)

An amino-acid derivative essential for transporting long-chain fatty acids into mitochondria for beta-oxidation. **Acetyl-L-carnitine (ALCAR)** is the acetylated form, which crosses the blood-brain barrier more readily than plain L-carnitine and is favoured for neurological/cognitive/nerve-pain indications. **L-carnitine** (un-acetylated) is favoured for cardiac and muscular indications. Both forms appear in the corpus; clinicians do not always disambiguate (and auto-captioning frequently mangles the spelling — e.g. "acetal carnitin," "Asel cantin," "acetel ALC cartine").

The signal is **clinician `recommended`** — no patient in the corpus credits carnitine as a top-3 recovery factor.

## Forms (corpus mentions)

- **Acetyl-L-carnitine (ALCAR)** — [[jacob-teitelbaum]] (#167) for severe nerve pain, paired with [[alpha-lipoic-acid]]. [[sarah-myhill]] (#118) — 1 g/day in her mitochondrial base stack.
- **L-carnitine (cardiac form)** — [[jacob-teitelbaum]] (#55) in his cardiomyopathy/myocarditis recipe. Likely "acetyl-L-carnitine" given his broader practice, but the cardiac use case is the L-carnitine indication.
- **Dietary source** — Myhill (#118) — "if you're a vegetarian you definitely [need to take] acetyl-L-carnitine because that is rich in meat." Heads-up for plant-based LC patients.

## Why this comes up

Three distinct framings, all from clinicians:

1. **Mitochondrial fatty-acid transport** — [[sarah-myhill]] (#118): one of the "top three" mitochondrial supplements alongside CoQ10 and magnesium. Without carnitine, mitochondria can't burn the fatty acids being delivered to them.
2. **Cardiac repair after COVID** — [[jacob-teitelbaum]] (#55): part of his cardiomyopathy/myocarditis recipe (B vitamins + magnesium + CoQ10 + acetyl-L-carnitine). Standard non-LC literature: carnitine deficiency is well-documented in heart failure.
3. **Nerve pain / neuropathy** — [[jacob-teitelbaum]] (#167): paired with [[alpha-lipoic-acid]] as the "natural-remedy" starting layer for severe nerve pain before escalating to his >12-medication pain protocol.

## Use cases

### Mitochondrial fatty-acid transport (ME/CFS base stack)
[[sarah-myhill]] (#118):

> "The bare minimum of raw materials we need for mitochondria… magnesium 300 mg plus vitamin D 10,000 IU for its absorption, coenzyme Q10 100 mg, vitamin B3 niacinamide 1500 mg, acetyl-L-carnitine 1 g… D-ribose 5 g as a rescue remedy."

Same sequencing rule as CoQ10: fix upper-fermenting gut first, otherwise gut bacteria outcompete absorption.

### Post-COVID cardiac
[[jacob-teitelbaum]] (#55) — cardiomyopathy/myocarditis recipe: "B vitamins, magnesium, CoQ10, acetyl-L-carnitine — a recipe that will dramatically improve cardiac function." Cardiac role here likely benefits from un-acetylated L-carnitine more than ALCAR, but Teitelbaum doesn't disambiguate in this episode.

### Severe nerve pain / small-fibre neuropathy
[[jacob-teitelbaum]] (#167):

> "The natural remedies are lipoic acid and acetyl-L-carnitine."

Pair-recommendation with [[alpha-lipoic-acid]]. Starting layer before escalating to his pain-medication sequence.

## ALCAR vs L-carnitine — quick guide

The corpus doesn't make this distinction sharply, but standard pharmacology:

- **Neurological / cognitive / nerve-pain** indications → **acetyl-L-carnitine** (crosses BBB)
- **Cardiac / skeletal muscle / energy** indications → **L-carnitine tartrate or fumarate**
- **Both forms** raise systemic carnitine pools; ALCAR also adds an acetyl group used in acetylcholine synthesis (mild stimulant feel reported anecdotally).

## Cautions

- **Thyroid interaction** — L-carnitine can blunt thyroid hormone action at high doses (relevant in hypothyroid LC patients). Not raised in corpus.
- **TMAO / cardiovascular debate** — gut conversion of carnitine to TMAO has been linked to cardiovascular risk in some observational data. Not raised in corpus, but mentioned in adjacent literature.
- **Gut-first sequencing** — Myhill's rule (#118): handle upper-fermenting gut before supplementing, because gut bacteria are "hungry for the mitochondrial supplements" too.
- **Fishy body odour** — high oral doses can cause trimethylaminuria-like effects in some people.
- **Form matters for indication** — see ALCAR vs L-carnitine above.
