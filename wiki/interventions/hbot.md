# Hyperbaric oxygen therapy (HBOT)

Treatment delivered in a pressurised chamber where the patient breathes near-100% oxygen at 1.5–3 atmospheres absolute. Standard medical indications include decompression sickness, carbon-monoxide poisoning, non-healing wounds, radiation injury, certain infections.

In Long Covid, HBOT has emerged as an off-label intervention with **moderate but accumulating evidence** for fatigue, cognition, and tissue oxygenation. The proposed mechanisms are:

- Forced oxygen dissolution into plasma (higher tissue oxygen delivery despite microclot impairment)
- Stimulation of mitochondrial biogenesis (some animal evidence)
- Anti-inflammatory and angiogenic effects
- Possible direct effect on amyloid microclot dispersion (Pretorius/Kell-adjacent hypothesis)

## What's been said in the podcast

- **[[saskia-mulder]] (#48).** "HBOT has been a big one for helping me with lots of symptoms on my brain and my chest." Reports `helped_partial` benefit. No specific protocol stated (number of sessions, ATA depth).
- **[[masha-makeeva]] (#96).** Anchor clinician + recovered-patient endorsement. Names HBOT as central to her own ~80% recovery and a routine recommendation in her practice. Specific protocol observations:
  - **20-60 sessions** typical — depends on prior baggage (autoimmune history etc.).
  - **First-session benefit common** (energy + sleep).
  - **Worsening fatigue in first 3-4 sessions is positive sign** — consistent with mitochondrial turnover (clearing damaged → biogenesis of new). Don't schedule activities post-session early.
  - Mechanism framing: forced O₂ dissolution → mitochondrial repair + new biogenesis + inflammation reduction.
  - Names [[gez-medinger]] and "Dr Tom" as practitioner-patients who used HBOT and benefited.
- **[[amir-hadanny]] (#153).** Anchor RCT-evidence episode — pairs with Makeeva.
  - **Aviv LC RCT (≈90 patients, sham-controlled).** Subjective + objective MRI / fMRI / SPECT / cognitive-battery improvements; sham group was effectively blinded (50/50 patient guesses post-trial). Improvements correlated between subjective and objective. Three publications.
  - **18-month follow-up sustained gains** — direct counter to "feels good, fades" critique. Mechanism repair, not symptom-suppression.
  - **"Covid horns"** imaging signature: orbital-frontal lobes + temporal poles + hippocampi metabolic deficit — the cribriform-plate viral entry mapping. Patient selection by SPECT first.
  - **Established LC (>6mo) needs 60 sessions, not 40.** Daily 5 days/week × 2 hrs/session.
  - **Oxygen fluctuations** (cyclic 100% O₂ + air breaks within session) are mechanistic key — interval training for mitochondria. Mild-HBOT without on/off cycling probably weaker.
  - Four healing-a-wound components: energy + trigger + stem cells + angiogenesis. **New blood-vessel growth = the rate-limiter** (~2 months minimum).

## Caveats

- **Cost and access.** Often £100+/session in private clinics; courses are typically 30–40 sessions. Mild HBOT (1.3 ATA in soft chambers) is cheaper than hard HBOT (2.0+ ATA in hospital chambers) but with weaker effect.
  - **MS Charity UK access**: ~£15/session — flagged in #96 as the cheapest LC-relevant access pathway in the UK. Worth investigating if HBOT becomes a candidate intervention.
- **Travel cost.** Sessions are typically daily; patients pay in time + transport + PEM exposure.
- **Fire / safety considerations** in the chamber — no electronics, specific clothing, no oils/cosmetics.
- **Ear pressure issues** common in the first few sessions.

## Cross-ref ercipedia

A small Israeli RCT (Zilberman-Itskovich 2022, *Scientific Reports*) showed cognitive and fatigue improvements in LC patients after 40 sessions. The Aviv RCT (Hadanny et al.) extends this with three publications including objective MRI/fMRI/cognitive-battery readouts and **18-month sustained-gains follow-up**. Mainstream UK clinics increasingly offer LC HBOT courses but it is privately funded.
