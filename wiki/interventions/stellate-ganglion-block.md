# Stellate Ganglion Block (SGB)

Local-anaesthetic injection around the cervical sympathetic track (C6-C7, or C4 + C6 in modified protocols) to temporarily block sympathetic outflow on one side of the head/neck/upper chest. Originally developed for PTSD (Eugene Lipov); applied to LC from early 2021.

## Anchor: #76 Robert Groysman

The wiki's primary source. ~1,800 SGBs performed; modified C4+C6 protocol; ultrasound-guided (anti-fluoroscopy-only).

## What it's for in LC

| Symptom | Anecdotal response (Groysman, #76) |
|---|---|
| **Parosmia / anosmia / phantom smells** | Often immediate within 30 seconds of block |
| **Brain fog** | Typically improves; transient post-block exhaustion common |
| **Fatigue** | Often improves |
| **Anxiety / insomnia / depression / PTSD** | Strong responder population |
| **Tinnitus** | Small N — one patient cleared after one block |
| **Other dysautonomic symptoms** | Variable |

Effective in patients **up to 27.5 months post-covid** at recording.

## Mechanism (Groysman's framing)

Resets the sympathetic–parasympathetic balance in long covid (which is hypothesised to be locked in sympathetic dominance). Effect outlasts the local anaesthetic by weeks–years (mechanism unclear — only ketamine works similarly).

## Procedure must include

- **Ultrasound guidance** (not fluoroscopy-only — soft tissues invisible)
- **Local anaesthetic only**, no steroid
- **Horner's syndrome on treated side** (≥3 of 5 signs) confirms real block: droopy lid, small pupil, red eye, stuffy nostril, flushing/hoarseness
- Right side first by default (80% right-dominant)
- **Never bilateral simultaneously** — would block recurrent laryngeal nerve bilaterally → airway emergency
- 3-day or 5-day protocols for non-responders; pulse-radiofrequency for "snap-back" cases

## Cautions

- **Stable POTS**: caution — SGB can destabilise (acrobat-on-ladder framing). Not contraindicated but warned.
- Common side effects: post-block fatigue (especially in brain-fog patients) for hours to days; transient hoarseness; transient stuffy nostril.
- Rare serious complications (intra-arterial → seizure; hematoma; nerve injury) — operator-skill dependent.

## Common failure mode

> "Most 'failed SGBs' weren't actually SGBs — the local was misplaced into the brachial plexus. Without Horner's, no SGB happened."

Reddit / Facebook success rates of 20-30% for parosmia likely reflect mis-placed blocks, not true failure rates. With correct placement (real Horner's), success is much higher.
