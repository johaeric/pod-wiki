# Isolation / Muscle-Activation Exercises

A specific category of strength training where each exercise targets **a single muscle or muscle group** rather than recruiting many muscles in one movement. Standard usage in injury rehab and strength-training warm-ups; **proposed by [[rachael-rebound-athletic]] in #168 as the correct movement modality for LC reconditioning** (the first component of her [[baseline-formula]]).

## Examples

- **Clam** (side-lying, single-muscle = gluteus medius) — vs. squat (multi-muscle, compound, high-energy)
- **Glute bridges** (gluteal isolation) — vs. lunge (compound)
- **Scapular squeezes / row variations** (rhomboid + middle-trap isolation) — vs. press-up
- **Hip-flexor activation** (psoas / iliacus, often horizontal) — vs. running-up-stairs

## Why they fit LC reconditioning

- **Energy efficiency** — single-muscle movements demand less ATP than compound movements. Given the mitochondrial-dysfunction substrate in LC, isolation is the lowest-energy form of strength training that still drives adaptation.
- **Stabiliser focus** — many isolation moves target stabilising muscles (hip flexors, scapular stabilisers, deep core). Useful for **deconditioning recovery + injury prevention** when patient eventually progresses to compound work.
- **Often horizontal / mat-based** — friendly to POTS / orthostatic-intolerance patients who can't tolerate sustained vertical posture.
- **Slow, controlled, mindful** — supports Mind-Body reconnection where higher-intensity training would trigger fear / PEM associations.

## Distinct from

- **Walking + gentle yoga** — different movement modalities, complementary not substitute.
- **Graded exercise therapy** — GET prescribes graded *load* on the same compound-movement substrate. Isolation exercises change the substrate to one mitochondria can absorb.
- **Compound strength training** (squats, deadlifts, presses) — appropriate later in recovery once capacity is rebuilt.
