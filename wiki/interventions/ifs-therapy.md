# Internal Family Systems (IFS) therapy

Therapeutic model developed by Dick Schwartz (book: *No Bad Parts*) treating the mind as a system of "parts" — protectors, exiles, the Self — and working to bring parts into harmony rather than fighting them. Different vocabulary from brain-retraining; same operative goal of regulating an over-protective nervous system.

[[jenny-adams]] (#145) names IFS as her **post-recovery dysregulation tool**: "What does this part need? Can the protectors move aside?" Concrete example — drives off in a huff after an argument, parks up, runs an IFS exercise, gets home regulated, can have the conversation properly.

## Why it shows up on this podcast

- Adjacent to [[mental-self-harming]] ([[sam-munslow]] #34) — both work the inner-critic loop.
- Adjacent to [[curable-app]] / [[alan-gordon]] PRT — both treat dysregulation as the source.
- Adjacent to **[[mind-body]]** broadly — IFS is one of several vocabularies for the same phenomenon.

## Wiki position

Logged as `helped_partial` from a recovered patient who uses it post-recovery rather than as the load-bearing recovery driver itself. **Pattern**: IFS is more often a recovery-deepener / relapse-prevention tool than a recovery initiator on this wiki.
