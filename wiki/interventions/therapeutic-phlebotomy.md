# Therapeutic phlebotomy ("bloodletting")

The deliberate withdrawal of a unit (~pint) of whole blood as a therapeutic intervention. Mainstream indications: haemochromatosis (iron overload), polycythaemia vera, porphyria cutanea tarda. **Off-label use for post-viral / vaccine-injury symptom relief is controversial and pre-evidence.**

This page exists because it appears as a single guest's self-reported **key_to_recovery** outcome in #84 — and because the wiki's policy is to log *every* `key_to_recovery` claim, not just the ones with mainstream support.

## What Pover reports (#84)

- Discovered accidentally after a routine large blood draw — felt dramatically better afterwards.
- Hired a private phlebotomist to come to her house and remove a pint of blood.
- Reports going from "zombie on the sofa" to talking normally during the procedure itself.
- Functional level moved from ~zero to ~30%.
- Now also receives traditional Chinese **acupuncture-based bloodletting** from a practitioner.

## Theory she's working with

- Damage to the nervous and lymphatic systems means waste products accumulate between sessions.
- Removing blood is a physical reset of the metabolic-waste load.
- Cross-ref distantly to [[microclots]] / [[helapheresis]] (Pretorius, Khan) — same family of "stuck circulation needs to be physically cleared" intuitions but a different mechanism (whole blood vs apheresis-filtered).

## Caveats

- **n=1**, no peer-reviewed evidence for this indication.
- Pover gets pushback online for promoting it.
- Risks: anaemia if frequent, infection from non-clinical settings, syncope, electrolyte disturbance.
- The Chinese-acupuncture-bloodletting variant is a different practice with different risk profile.
- Wiki policy: **log it, don't endorse it.**
