# CoQ10 (Coenzyme Q10 / Ubiquinol / Ubiquinone)

A mitochondrial cofactor and electron-transport-chain component. Every clinician in the corpus who builds a mitochondrial-support stack includes it. The signal is **clinician `recommended`** across the board — no patient in the corpus credits CoQ10 as a top-3 factor in their recovery arc. Patient outcome data is thin; this is part of the floor of mitochondrial stacks, not the lever.

## Why this comes up

- **Mitochondrial base stack** — [[sarah-myhill]] (#118): CoQ10 100 mg/day alongside magnesium 300 mg + vitamin D 10,000 IU + niacinamide 1500 mg + acetyl-L-carnitine 1 g + D-ribose 5 g as the "bare minimum raw materials" to feed mitochondria.
- **Cardiac repair** — [[jacob-teitelbaum]] (#55): part of his cardiomyopathy/myocarditis recipe (B vitamins + magnesium + CoQ10 + acetyl-L-carnitine) for post-COVID heart involvement.
- **Mitochondrial resilience under load** — [[david-putrino]] (#181): CoQ10 alongside oxaloacetate and NAD+ as "techniques to make mitochondria more resilient to overexertion" — borrowed from elite-athlete recovery protocols.
- **Acute-COVID fatigue lever** — [[leo-galland]] (#191): "I really like coenzyme Q10. A lot of people respond well to it." But carries a substantial caution on form (see below).

## Forms (and the Galland caveat)

[[leo-galland]] (#191) makes the most precise point in the corpus:

> "There are two different forms of CoQ10. The oxidized form called **ubiquinone** — the original supplement — and the reduced form called **ubiquinol**. There's this idea that ubiquinol is much better. It's not really true. If you have too much ubiquinol, it actually screws up the electron transport chain. The ratio of ubiquinone to ubiquinol is very important for the beginning of the electron transport chain."

This is the corpus's main counterweight to the marketing push for ubiquinol as "the better form." Galland reaches for it only when he can identify a specific problem area — not as a default.

## Dosing (across corpus)

- **100 mg/day** — [[sarah-myhill]] (#118) baseline in her ME/CFS mitochondrial stack
- **100–600 mg/day** — [[leo-galland]] (#191), range for acute-COVID fatigue. Galland favours the lower end and prefers ubiquinone over ubiquinol.
- **Dose unspecified** — [[jacob-teitelbaum]] (#55, cardiac recipe), [[david-putrino]] (#181, athletic-style mitochondrial support)

## Use cases (across corpus)

### Mitochondrial / ME-CFS base stack
[[sarah-myhill]] (#118) — CoQ10 100 mg is one of the "top three" mitochondrial supplements alongside magnesium and vitamin D. Important caveat from Myhill: *don't supplement until you sort out upper-fermenting gut*, because gut bacteria are "hungry for the mitochondrial supplements" too and will outcompete absorption.

### Post-COVID cardiac
[[jacob-teitelbaum]] (#55) — for cardiomyopathy / myocarditis: "B vitamins, magnesium, CoQ10, acetyl-L-carnitine — a recipe that will dramatically improve cardiac function." Available via his free Cures A-Z app.

### Resilience to overexertion (PEM-adjacent)
[[david-putrino]] (#181) — frames CoQ10 / oxaloacetate / NAD+ as "things that enhance the Krebs cycle and help take out the trash." Borrowed from athletic-recovery science as scaffolding for LC mitochondrial support. He does not explicitly endorse CoQ10 for PEM prevention but groups it in the same toolkit.

### Acute-COVID fatigue (Galland)
[[leo-galland]] (#191) — "When people have fatigue with acute COVID, I really like coenzyme Q10." But: directed use, not scatter-gun; ubiquinone form preferred; watch ratio with ubiquinol.

## Cautions

- **Form matters** — Galland (#191) on the ubiquinone/ubiquinol ratio above. Megadosing ubiquinol can disrupt the electron transport chain.
- **Gut-first sequencing** — Myhill (#118) — fix upper-fermenting gut before supplementing mitochondrial cofactors, or gut bacteria outcompete absorption.
- **Anticoagulant interaction** — CoQ10 may reduce warfarin's effect (not raised in corpus, but standard caveat).
- **Statin-induced depletion** — well-documented outside the corpus; not raised by guests here.
