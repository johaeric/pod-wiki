# Nattokinase

A fibrinolytic enzyme isolated from *natto*, a traditional Japanese fermented soybean food. **Breaks down fibrin** — the protein structure of blood clots — and is taken in supplement form as an oral fibrinolytic.

Popular in LC circles since the [[microclots]] hypothesis emerged (Resia Pretorius, Douglas Kell). Often paired with **serrapeptase** and **bromelain** in "fibrinolytic stack" protocols, sometimes alongside low-dose aspirin or anticoagulants in clinician-managed protocols (cf. Asad Khan's triple-therapy stack, LCP #37).

## Caveats

- **Bleeding risk.** Stop before any surgery (usually 1-2 weeks). Caution alongside aspirin, anticoagulants, or other fibrinolytics — discuss with a clinician.
- **Soy allergy.** Nattokinase is derived from soy.
- **Dosing varies wildly.** Common range 2000–4000 FU (fibrin units), 1-2× daily on an empty stomach.
- **Evidence in LC is patient-anecdote-level**, supported by mechanism plausibility from the microclots hypothesis rather than RCT data.
