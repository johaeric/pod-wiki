# 2-day cardiopulmonary exercise test (CPET)

The closest thing the wiki has to a **reproducible objective biomarker for PEM**. Two consecutive maximal CPETs on a stationary bike with mask gas-exchange measurement, 24h apart, designed to capture both pre- and post-PEM physiology.

## What the test produces

- Day 1: max CPET → identifies **ventilatory anaerobic threshold (VAT)** — the point where aerobic metabolism breaks down and gas-exchange shifts.
- Day 2: same protocol 24h later — by which time PEM has been provoked.
- **Healthy / deconditioned controls** replicate or improve on day 2 (motor learning).
- **PEM patients** cannot replicate — VAT drops, submaximal workload falls. The drop is **unfakeable** because gas-exchange physiology cannot be voluntarily manipulated.

## Why this matters

- It demonstrates objectively that PEM is a real metabolic-physiological event, not a complaint or deconditioning.
- Functional consequence: post-PEM the patient's ceiling has *literally* dropped — activities possible day 1 may be impossible day 2.
- It is the methodology against which other proposed PEM biomarkers should be validated.

## Limitations

- Highly specialised — few centres globally; expensive equipment; trained operators.
- Deliberately provokes a crash, so deployment requires clinical judgement and informed consent.
- Mark Faghy (#120, Derby) has developed a **2-day spread protocol** (more time between the two tests) to reduce iatrogenic harm in fragile patients.

## Where on the wiki

The diagnostic mirror of [[post-exertional-malaise]] and the foundation under [[workwell-foundation]]'s exercise-prescription guidance (Anderson #135/#136 cites Workwell's resting-HR + 15–20 bpm ceiling as derived from this physiology).
