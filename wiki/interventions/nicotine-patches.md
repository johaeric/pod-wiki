# Nicotine Patches (transdermal)

Off-label use of transdermal nicotine (the smoking-cessation patches) for LC, based on a receptor-competition hypothesis: SARS-CoV-2 spike protein binds and silently blocks **nicotinic acetylcholine receptors (nAChR)** across the body; nicotine, with ~30× higher affinity, displaces spike over weeks to months.

Anchor episode: #130 ([[marco-leitzke]]).

## Critical dosing rules (Leitzke #130)

- Start at the **lowest commercially available** patch (~7–7.5 mg/24h).
- Reduce dose by **covering** (gauze + tape), **NEVER by cutting** — cutting destroys the gel matrix and dose becomes uncontrollable.
- First-line: 1.75 or 3.5 mg/24h for 3–5 days to habituate, then escalate cautiously.
- Avoid going above ~14 mg routinely; 21 mg "not advisable" in this population.
- **Long, not high.** Time on the patch matters more than dose intensity. Weeks-to-months is the expected scale.
- Expect a **flare in the first ~7 days** — when nicotine displaces spike from receptors, the freed antigen looks like an acute infection to the immune system. This is the desired mechanism, not a sign to stop.

## Safety / interactions

- **Vasodilatory**, not vasoconstrictive (raises intracellular NO in endothelium) — opposite of the smoking-myth concern.
- UK NICE (2013) statement supports up to 5 years of transdermal use without health risk.
- Approved adjunct in stable angina; cardiac risk profile is well-characterised.
- **Do not combine with MAO inhibitors** (Leitzke flag).
- Other LC supplements/meds can continue — Leitzke argues against piling on antivirals (Paxlovid) or antibiotics, both of which may blunt the immune system's recognition of freed virus.
- Co-carcinogenic potential is uncertain; Leitzke's argument is that for severe disabling chronic illness, weeks-to-months use is a reasonable risk-benefit trade.

## Wiki position

- **No recovered-patient testimony in this episode.** Logged as `recommended` from the clinician/researcher hypothesis. Watch for the German RCT and follow-up patient-recovery rows.
- Cheap, accessible, globally available — sits alongside [[salt-loading]] and [[cold-water-exposure]] as a low-cost trial candidate, but with a **clearer mechanism story** than most.
