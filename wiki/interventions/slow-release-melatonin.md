# Slow-release melatonin (low-dose)

**2 mg slow-release at 7 pm** is the protocol Joffe (#143) gives to "virtually all my long COVID people." Pharmaceutical-grade, prescription-only (Australia: TGA-approved); slow release matches normal melatonin physiology — peak at ~9 pm, decay over the night. Resets the desynchronised circadian clock that LC characteristically produces (low serotonin → low melatonin → drift).

> Cross-ref: see `ercipedia/wiki/interventions/melatonin.md` if/when added — for trial evidence (not yet on this wiki).

## Why this dose / form

- **Dose**: 2 mg only. Joffe is explicit — "this idea that more is better is silly." Low dose tracks endogenous melatonin levels; higher pharmacological doses don't add benefit and may interfere with the next night.
- **Slow-release**: matches the physiological release curve. Immediate-release peaks too fast and crashes; slow-release sustains through the night.
- **Timing 7 pm**: peaks at 9 pm — when SWS pressure peaks — modelling normal entrainment.

## Adjacent benefits

- **Reduces violence in REM behaviour disorder** — patients can fall out of bed acting out dreams.
- **Endothelial protection at low dose** (similar mechanism class to statins) — relevant given LC endotheliitis. Joffe is co-author on a paper with Razer / Hill / Putrino.

## Wiki position

Logged as **clinician-recommended** for LC sleep dysfunction on the strength of Joffe's clinical experience. **Eric-relevant**: cheap, well-studied, low risk; raise with his clinician if he ever runs into LC-attributable sleep disturbance.
