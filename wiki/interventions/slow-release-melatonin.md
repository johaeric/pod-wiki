# Melatonin

The pineal hormone that signals "night" — synthesised from serotonin via tryptophan, released in response to dim-light signals from the suprachiasmatic nucleus. In LC the entire serotonin→melatonin pipeline is dysregulated (low serotonin demonstrated in the literature, downstream low melatonin assumed), producing the desynchronised circadian clock LC patients describe as "wired and tired."

Two clinicians in the corpus recommend supplementing melatonin for LC sleep, with **markedly different dose and form preferences**. The headline framing is [[david-joffe]]'s (#143) **2 mg slow-release at 7 pm** as a circadian-reset tool — explicitly *not* a pharmacological knockout dose. [[jacob-teitelbaum]] (#167) similarly reaches for **sustained-release** formulations (he names EP120 in North America) as one of several "circuit breaker" sleep tools. The mechanism mentions (#129 [[lily-spechler]]) help anchor *why* — the serotonin→melatonin axis is broken in LC, which is also why bare-tryptophan dietary strategies and serotonin-supporting interventions sit in the same conceptual cluster.

## Asad Khan low-dose slow-release protocol (Joffe anchor)

**[[david-joffe]] (LCP #143)** is the corpus anchor for the low-dose slow-release framing. Protocol given to "virtually all my long COVID people":

- **Dose**: 2 mg only. Joffe is explicit — "this idea that more is better is silly." Low dose tracks endogenous melatonin levels; higher pharmacological doses don't add benefit and may interfere with the next night.
- **Slow-release**: matches the physiological release curve. Immediate-release peaks too fast and crashes; slow-release sustains through the night.
- **Timing 7 pm**: peaks at ~9 pm — when SWS pressure peaks — modelling normal entrainment.
- **TGA-approved, prescription-only in Australia.** Pharmaceutical-grade; quality assured.

Adjacent benefits Joffe names:

- **Reduces violence in REM behaviour disorder** — LC patients with RBD can fall out of bed acting out dreams; melatonin damps that.
- **Endothelial protection at low dose** (similar mechanism class to statins) — relevant given LC endotheliitis. Joffe is co-author on work with Rezayat / Hill / Putrino touching this.
- **Time DLMO (dim-light melatonin onset)** as a research tool: Joffe argues the "low morning cortisol" finding in LC may partly be a *measurement artefact* — patients' circadian clocks are shifted forward, so a 7 am draw catches what is biologically 3 am. He has discussed running DLMO saliva curves with David Putrino to disambiguate.

## Teitelbaum sustained-release framing

**[[jacob-teitelbaum]] (LCP #167)** lists sustained-release melatonin alongside chamomile (loose-leaf, not bags), Benadryl/diphenhydramine, and his "Revitalizing Sleep Formula" herbal mix as components of his SHINE-protocol sleep stack. He specifically names **EP120 sustained-release melatonin** as a North-American product. The framing is "circuit-breaker that goes offline" sleep dysfunction in fibromyalgia / ME/CFS / LC — supplement plus herbs plus formula, no single hero.

## Mechanism / circadian context (educational, not protocols)

**[[lily-spechler]] (LCP #129)** uses the serotonin → melatonin → cortisol cascade to explain why LC patients sleep poorly even when they aren't taking anything. Long-haulers have demonstrably low serotonin (a biomarker being explored); melatonin is downstream; cortisol and melatonin sit in a yin-yang relationship. Her use of this is dietary — she argues *for* moderate carbohydrate intake to support tryptophan crossing into the brain, *against* the strict no-carb regimes some LC patients adopt. She does not recommend melatonin supplementation directly; she uses the pathway as the argument for not stripping the dietary precursors.

**[[david-joffe]] (LCP #143)** extends the mechanism: circadian disturbance in LC has multiple contributing layers — dysbiosis (a paper out the day of the interview), dopaminergic injury (under-discussed compared to serotonin), low light hygiene from inactivity, and frank pineal entrainment failure. Melatonin treats the entrainment layer; other layers need other tools.

## Wiki position

Logged as **clinician-recommended** for LC sleep dysfunction on the strength of two independent clinicians (Joffe LC-specialist, Teitelbaum ME/CFS integrative). No patient `key_to_recovery` rows — melatonin is not the lever recovered patients credit, it's a sleep-floor tool clinicians prescribe alongside the deeper work. **Eric-relevant**: cheap, well-studied, low risk at 2 mg slow-release; raise with his clinician if he ever runs into LC-attributable sleep disturbance. **Avoid the high-dose 5–10 mg over-the-counter pills** the corpus warns against by implication — both clinicians who recommend it specify **low dose + sustained release**.

## Cautions

- **More is not better** — Joffe explicit; higher doses don't improve effect and may worsen next-night sleep architecture.
- **Form matters** — immediate-release peaks and crashes; sustained-release matches physiology. Most OTC melatonin in the US is immediate-release 5–10 mg, well above what these clinicians recommend.
- **Fear-response amplification in mind-body-syndrome / insomnia cases** — [[nora-rodden]] (LCP #211) cites a community member's story of how sleep-supplement advice (including melatonin) *worsened* her insomnia by ratcheting performance anxiety around sleep. Not a pharmacological harm signal; a behavioural one. Worth noting for patients whose insomnia is downstream of dysregulated stress response rather than circadian shift.
