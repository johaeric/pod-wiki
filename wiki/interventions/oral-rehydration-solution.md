# Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS)

A glucose + salt + (often) potassium sachet dissolved in water. UK brand: **Dioralyte**. US brands: **Pedialyte**, **Normalyte**, **Vitalyte**. Originally developed for diarrhoea/vomiting recovery; repurposed for chronic hypovolemia in ME/CFS / LC / POTS.

## Why it's different from salt-on-food

The stomach contains a **sodium-glucose co-transporter (SGLT1)** that actively pulls salty solution into the bloodstream when glucose and sodium are present in the right molar ratio. This means:

- **Salt-on-food** delivers sodium loosely; absorption is partial and delayed.
- **ORS** delivers sodium *plus* the glucose that activates SGLT1 — the stomach pulls the entire salty volume into the bloodstream efficiently.
- Outcome: 1 L of ORS over 30 minutes can match 1 L of IV saline for blood-volume expansion in POTS patients (Meadow study, cited in #139).

## Typical protocol (Ussher's, #139)

- **1.5 L/day** as the daily working dose.
- Drunk over a couple of hours, not all at once.
- **Most of daily fluid intake should come from ORS**, not from water-alongside-ORS. Water dilutes the effect.
- During a crash: a single litre over 1–2 hours can restore enough functional state to walk around within 30–60 minutes (described as "near-Biblical" by Ussher).

## Mechanism story

Hypovolemic thirst centre fires in response to low blood volume. RAAS axis is suppressed in ME/CFS / LC, so angiotensin-II-driven salt craving is absent — patient drinks plain water (only signal firing), worsening dilution → hyponatremia. ORS delivers the sodium the patient cannot taste-crave, in a form the stomach can absorb efficiently. Combined with hydration, restores plasma volume.

## Caveats

- **Not blanket advice.** Spechler's #129 caveat applies: in vasoconstriction-pattern POTS (ACE2-receptor damage / elevated angiotensin-II), additional sodium can worsen things. Patient feedback during a sodium trial is diagnostic information about the subtype, not a sign to push through.
- ORS taste is mildly unpleasant for many; some patients find branded electrolyte drinks (LMNT, SiS, Precision Hydration 1500) more palatable. Look for similar glucose:sodium ratios.
- Sugar content matters for diabetic patients — branded sugar-free formulations exist.

## Eric-relevance

- The mechanism explains the consistent pattern Eric and others have noticed: hydration alone isn't enough; salt+water is better; **ORS is significantly better than salt-on-food** for the hydration leg of the POTS stack.
- Useful experiment: try 0.5–1 L Dioralyte before/during a heat day or after a near-crash and observe.
- Pairs naturally with [[fludrocortisone]] (RAAS replacement at the aldosterone leg) and [[salt-loading]] — same sub-phenotype caveat (pooling-pattern POTS only).
