# HRV Biofeedback

Tech-augmented breathwork: while practising slow paced breathing, watch your **heart rate variability** elevate in real time on a wearable or app display. The breathing is the active ingredient; the live HRV trace is a motivational and accountability layer that helps people stick with the practice and dial it in.

> Cross-ref: see [[breathwork]] and [[diaphragmatic-breathing]] for the mechanism. HRV biofeedback is the same intervention with an instrumented loop.

## What it actually does

- Paced exhale-extended nasal breathing (typical 4-6 / 6-8 cadence) → vagal-brake engagement → heart rate falls on exhale → HRV waveform elevates.
- The wearable shows the elevation in real time → the practitioner gets immediate, visible reinforcement.
- Repeated practice trains the autonomic nervous system to recover the relaxation response on demand.

## What it doesn't do

- The data on its own changes nothing. Wiles is emphatic on this.
- Watching your HRV without pairing breathwork or other regulation tools won't move it.
- HRV is not a normative metric — comparing your absolute number to anyone else's is meaningless. Only personal trend over time is useful.

## Specific platforms / tools mentioned

- **Hanu Health** — continuous (every-second) HRV wearable + coaching, co-founded by Wiles (#82). Designed specifically for biofeedback rather than fitness use.
- General wearables (Apple Watch, Fitbit, Garmin, Aura, Whoop) — also report HRV but typically as a once-daily summary metric, less useful for in-the-moment biofeedback.

## When NOT to use it

- In a severe crash where any cognitive load is too much — fall back to passive interventions (vagus-nerve TENS, lying flat, supported breathing) and re-introduce when capacity returns.
- Don't use a low reading as a self-fulfilling prophecy ("HRV is down so I can't do anything today"). Treat it as data, not a verdict.
- Don't check it more often than you can usefully act on the result.

## LC-specific signal

- Wiles reports LC patients in his clinic have **15-30 minute HRV recovery times after a walk** versus 2-5 minutes in healthy adults — useful pacing signal.
- Stuck-sympathetic LC patients show chronically suppressed HRV; gradual recovery as the autonomic state heals.
