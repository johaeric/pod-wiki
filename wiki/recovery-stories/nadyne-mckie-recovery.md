# Nadyne McKie's recovery arc

A **practitioner's-perspective recovery + ongoing-management story**. Nadyne explicitly **does not frame her recovery as binary**: she sees fatigue-based illness as something she will manage for life. This puts her recovery in a different category from the "full recovery" stories on the wiki — operationally substantive (no longer LC-disabled; working clinically; running a private practice; raising kids; published a book) but ongoing-vigilance-required.

Her son's recovery is included here because it's the secondary recovery arc Nadyne discusses on these episodes.

## Nadyne's own arc (high-level)

- **Caught covid → bedbound period → diagnosed with LC.** Specific dates not detailed on-air.
- **Yoga therapy + psychotherapy + breathwork** (her own professional toolkit, applied to herself): the integrated mind-body approach she now teaches clients. Was the leave-shavasana person before LC; now able to rest properly.
- **The hardest part of recovery for her was learning to rest.** Frames rest as a practice, not a passive default. Acknowledges still slipping back into "plate spinning" patterns and feeling Whispers of fatigue when she does — uses these as signals to dial back.
- **Co-authored a book** on convalescence + self-compassion in fatigue-based illness with **Fiona Agombar** ([[fiona-agombar]]). Released the week before #155 was recorded.
- Now identifies as managing the after-effects rather than fully cured. *"I think you can recover, but you are always aware of the after-effects."*

## Her son's arc

- **Post-COVID LC** in a school-age child; long period off school, phased return, energy-management as the central principle.
- Was supported by an excellent **NHS paediatric consultant** who actively believed and advocated. This is highlighted as the load-bearing piece of *external* support — a sharp contrast to the paediatric gaslighting many parents on the wiki describe.
- Logistics + advocacy: dropping sport-day (against school pressure), ignoring being put into the long-distance run, phased return as an ongoing structure.
- Recovered ~2022. Still has Whispers under stress; uses the toolkit (rest, breathing, awareness).

## What she identifies as load-bearing (own and son's)

1. **Believing the body** (and the child's body) — listening to symptoms early prevents the deep crash patterns.
2. **Rest as practice** — gentle yoga, breathwork, acceptance of mid-day downtime; deliberately re-skilling the nervous system into ventral / parasympathetic.
3. **NS-to-NS coupling** — when the carer is regulated, the child's substrate for recovery is improved. Caregiver self-care is operationally essential, not optional.
4. **An advocate inside the medical system** (her son's paediatric consultant). External validation is non-trivial.
5. **"Just for now" framing** — keeps the door open between acceptance + hope.

## Distinctive framings

- **Screen apnoea** as a daily energy-drain (#155).
- **Rest = horizontal, eyes/ears closed, no Netflix** — practical operational definition (echoes [[149-eleanor-stein]]).
- **"You can't pour from an empty cup."** Recurring across the episodes; specifically applied to caregiver-of-LC-child.
- **Movement, not exercise.** Frames exercise as often a distraction-from-emotion mechanism; LC removes the distraction and forces the encounter. (Same operative move as [[085-fiona-agombar-yoga-therapy]] and [[129-lily-spechler-lc-dietitian]]'s "movement, not exercise.")
