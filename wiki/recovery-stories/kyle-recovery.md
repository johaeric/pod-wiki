# Kyle's Recovery (full, ~2 years; meditation + identity-letting-go)

South African; got Covid October 2020; ~2 years severely ill; full recovery via meditation + welcoming-discomfort practice + identity-letting-go.

## Long-covid course

- **Acute (October 2020)**: mild Covid; lingering symptoms.
- **Months 3–12**: symptoms worsened *with* his attempts to fix them. *"The more seriously I tried to take it, the more serious the symptoms got."*
- **Year 1**: stopped exercising, dropped university, moved home, life shrank.
- **Rock bottom**: tried supportive doctor, then a Physio with pacing strategies → big crash → loss of hope.

## What did not help (notable counter-evidence)

- **Low-histamine diet** — created food-fear; restrictive; no benefit. *"Beliefs formed that these foods were a threat — and then I got reactions to them."*
- **Pacing** — fed perfectionism + fear. He couldn't ever do it perfectly; every imperfect rest produced a crash → reinforced the fear → kept him stuck.

These are valuable counter-rows on the wiki. The interventions aren't bad in themselves — they failed *for him* because his nervous system was in a fear state and the framing of these interventions amplified rather than calmed it.

## What helped — load-bearing

**Meditation / contemplative practice** as the load-bearing recovery work. Buddhist-adjacent influence (he mentions cave-retreat traditions). Two operative techniques:

1. **Welcoming discomfort** — *"What am I resisting?"* sitting with sensations rather than distracting from them. Distraction (podcasts, books) made symptoms worse over time because resistance compounded; welcoming the discomfort released the body's danger response.
2. **Crash → flare-up reframe** — same sensations, different label, different nervous-system response. Going to the gym → sore tomorrow ≠ illness.

**Identity letting-go** as the meta-shift. Both pre-illness identities (athlete, student, social guy) and the *sickness identity* that formed during illness needed to be let go. *"Life stripped me of all the escapes and identities until there was only one way left to go."*
