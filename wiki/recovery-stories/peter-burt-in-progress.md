# Peter Burt — recovery in progress (UK)

Director of a contracting company; husband; father of two teenage sons. Caught Covid early in the pandemic, hit hard physically (oxygen sensors at home, twice came close to the call-the-hospital threshold).

## Pre-illness pattern

The familiar perfect-storm setup: high-multitasking work + endurance hobbies (50–70 mile road bike rides at weekends) used as stress-coping mechanism. Identity tied to being the action dad / main breadwinner / hardest worker in the room. Childhood with broken family units → "men don't cry" upbringing + learned not to be seen.

> "If it hadn't have been Covid, then it would have been something else eventually. That's quite a scary thought."

## Phase 1 — push-through (~2 years)

Returned to work after 5 weeks at 2 hr/day. Cycled crash-and-burn-and-recover for 2 years, telling himself the man he wanted to be needed to keep showing up. Brief upswing in May 2023 (legs had power again) followed by his **biggest crash that summer** — woke up one morning and the small reservoir of recent improvement was gone. Cried for hours. Lowest point: thoughts of being better off not here.

## Phase 2 — the two pivots

1. **Breathwork.** Found Patrick McKeown / James Nestor on this podcast. Started with a [[bolt-score]] of 2. First time in 2 years he could actively change his own state. Used the "beer-count" analogy with his wife — baseline 1–2 beers, every conversation/walk adds beers, breathing brings the count down.
2. **Therapy with "Amy".** After years of his wife and best-friend boss suggesting it, he agreed at his lowest point. Two crystallising sessions:
   - *"You're denying people the opportunity to do for you what you love doing for them."*
   - *"You're grieving the man you used to be."* Eventually: he doesn't want that man back.

## Where he is now

Recovering, not recovered. Still working through residual symptoms; learning to ask for help; learning to receive. Wanted to come on the podcast specifically to put men's-mental-health on the LC map.
