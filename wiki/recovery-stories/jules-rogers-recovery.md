# Jules Rogers — full LC recovery (UK; Mind-Body + HRT route)

UK mother of two; recovered after a ~3-year arc that combined diet experimentation, fatigue coaching, **Mind-Body therapy** (the inflection), **life-coach training** (the closing 20%), and **HRT** for the perimenopausal overlap.

## Backstory

- 2000 (age 18): post-glandular-fever CFS; recovered spontaneously, ~1 year.
- April 2020: covid in UK first lockdown; LC fatigue ~2 weeks later.
- "Perfect storm" lead-in — emotionally stressful job + family stressors → sustained sympathetic dominance pre-illness.

## What turned things around

1. **Diet experiments** (~1 year) — low-histamine, raw-foods phase, then **elimination diet with a nutritionist** (May–Sept 2021). Elimination diet phase = first time she felt notably well. Significant.
2. **Pamela Rose 3-week fatigue-recovery course** — useful tools, especially the *"it's just for now"* framing. Not the answer.
3. **Mind-Body therapy** — ~8 sessions over 3 months. **80% of recovery.** Learned to:
   - Associate symptoms / emotions with thought patterns.
   - Take specific actions that change the symptom outcome.
   - Self-regulate between sympathetic and parasympathetic states.
4. **Studying as a life coach + Mind-Body practitioner** — the closing 20%. The training itself was therapeutic; gave her brain back; restored confidence after severe brain fog.
5. **Walking with mantras** — "walking makes me strong, walking makes me healthy" on repeat to override the conditioned fear-of-PEM neural pathway.
6. **HRT** for perimenopause — late addition; **big shift for brain fog specifically**. Hadn't planned ever to take HRT; her research found supportive evidence in some perimenopausal LC patients.

## What she'd tell past Jules

- You can recover. Hold the belief.
- Recovery is teaching. Use what you learn to reshape life.
- Trust yourself; nobody else knows your body as well as you'll know it by the end of this.
- Drop the competitive-perfectionist "I'm going to be the best at recovery" framing. It's an obstacle, not a tool.

## Recovery character

Not a single "yay-I'm-better" moment. The realisation came through repeated activities done without the fear-of-PEM internal monologue, then experiencing benign post-activity tiredness as normal fitness fatigue rather than as a PEM warning.
