# Workwell Foundation

US non-profit specialising in **two-day cardiopulmonary exercise testing (2-day CPET)** in ME/CFS — the classic methodology that demonstrated reduced anaerobic-threshold reproducibility on day 2 in PEM patients. They publish exercise-physiology guidelines for ME/CFS that reframe "exercise" around **anaerobic-threshold ceilings** rather than progressive overload.

## What's relevant for LC

- Anderson (#136) sources her **resting HR + 15–20 bpm ceiling** rule from Workwell's guidelines.
- The principle: in PEM-prone patients, the anaerobic threshold is significantly lower than in healthy controls. Crossing it during activity triggers PEM and doesn't produce conditioning gains. So train *below* it.
- This re-frames "training" as practising HR-staying-low under load, not as practising HR-climbing-higher under load.

## Wiki position

Logged as `recommended` from a clinician's reference (Anderson #136). Workwell guidance underpins the modern PEM-aware exercise-prescription tradition; it's the closest thing to a published evidence-base for the "go below threshold, not above" approach.

## Lineage

The 2-day CPET methodology was developed by Stacy Stevens, Mark VanNess, Christopher Snell, and Jared Stevens (the original Pacific Fatigue Lab), who founded the Workwell Foundation as the operational continuation. [[todd-davenport]] (#141) joined the lab in 2005 as a consultant and remains scientific advisor.
