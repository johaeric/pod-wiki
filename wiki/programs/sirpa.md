# SIRPA — Stress-Induced Recovery Practitioner Approach

A practitioner-delivered mind-body framework rooted in the **John Sarno / Howard Schubiner** lineage (TMS — tension myoneural syndrome) but extended to include nervous-system / autonomic dysregulation in chronic-fatigue and post-viral conditions. Practitioners are typically clinicians (physios, OTs, nurses, psychologists) who add SIRPA training to their existing scope.

Core thesis: **chronic symptoms are often driven by sustained sympathetic overdrive whose origins sit upstream of the presenting illness** — in childhood patterns, personality types (perfectionist, over-responsible, helper), unprocessed trauma (small-t and large-T), in-utero / trans-generational stress. The body keeps the score; symptoms are signals.

Treatment is multi-layer: identifying the upstream pattern + somatic / breath / mindfulness work + cognitive / emotional processing of suppressed feelings.

## How it relates to other frames on this podcast

- **Polyvagal theory (#23 Sally Riggs)** — same observation about the autonomic nervous system, different vocabulary; SIRPA is more practitioner-delivered, polyvagal more theoretical.
- **NS-loop / neuroplasticity (#20 Amy Anker)** — different vocabulary again; same insight that the nervous system can stay stuck in symptom-producing patterns even after the original injury heals.
- **Long Covid Breathing / Breath Cycle / Buteyko** — sits *under* SIRPA in the model: breath is one of the levers SIRPA practitioners use to influence the upstream nervous-system state.

## Caveats

- Practitioner-dependent — quality varies.
- Trauma-informed pacing matters; pushing past tolerance can destabilise.
- Single mention so far on this podcast.
