# Brain-retraining programs — which to consider

You asked about ANS Rewire and similar programs (DNRS, Gupta, and others in that family). I read the program pages for ANS Rewire, DNRS, Gupta, Primal Trust, Lightning Process, Mickel Therapy, SIRPA, ATNS, CFS Health, and the Curable app, plus the neuroplasticity theme page that ties them together. I also re-read your overview in `ercipedia/wiki/overview.md`.

Below is what the wiki actually says, organised by program, with the strongest signals first. **Lead caveat: this is podcast testimony — anecdote and clinician opinion. Cross-check ercipedia for any peer-reviewed evidence before deciding.**

## Bottom line first

- The strongest signal in the wiki is for **ANS Rewire** (Dan Neuffer's program). Two patients name it as primary in their recovery, including one whose presentation closely matches yours (POTS plus dysautonomia plus brain fog).
- The Curable app is the cheapest credible entry (~50 dollars a year) and has three patients crediting it as primary. Lower stakes, lower commitment.
- Gupta and DNRS have no recovered-patient "this was the primary thing" rows on this podcast yet. They have clinician recommendations and supportive endorsements, but no key-to-recovery testimony.
- Lightning Process has two recoveries but both used it late, when patients were already 90 percent or close.
- All of these presuppose that structural drivers have been ruled out. They are not substitutes for the autonomic, MCAS, or microclot medications you are already on.

## ANS Rewire (Dan Neuffer)

The clearest signal in the wiki for someone with your profile.

- Two patients credit it as the primary factor in their recovery
  - Joanna Rayl in episode 56 — two-and-a-half years of LC with dysautonomia, fatigue, brain fog, MCAS, and pain. Took medical leave, full daily commitment, near-full recovery within months. Notes that the leave was what made full commitment possible.
  - Natalie Gold in episode 189 — recovered POTS, brain fog, anxiety, dysautonomia over two years. Found Joanna Rayl's story first, then committed. Long-standing psoriatic arthritis, chronic migraine, and neck pain also resolved alongside the LC. She specifically credits the small daily videos, the education-first approach, and Dan's encouragement to adapt the protocol rather than follow it rigidly.
- One partial-help row — a teenager with paediatric ME slash CFS went from 40 percent functional capacity to 80 percent over eight months on ANS Rewire. Substantial but not full recovery.
- Five recommended rows from clinicians and recovered figures — including Dr Jacob Teitelbaum twice, Gez Medinger, and the founder Dan Neuffer himself. Zero null and zero harmed rows.

What makes ANS Rewire stand out for your situation specifically:

- Dan explicitly rejects the brain-training-purist position that says "stop all biomedical treatment." He calls his approach somato-neurological and frames it as combining upstream nervous-system retraining with targeted treatment of secondary dysfunctions and somatic body work. This is the wiki's clearest articulation of why brain training and your pharmacological pillars are not in conflict.
- He flags multi-sensory triggering — sounds, smells, light, cold, heat, barometric pressure setting off symptoms — as a heuristic for dysautonomia-rooted post-viral illness. Worth asking yourself whether that pattern fits.
- He actively discourages buying the program until you have watched the four free intro lessons. If the dysautonomia frame does not resonate, the program will not work — engagement requires belief in the model. This is structurally honest in a way most paid programs are not.

## Curable app

The pragmatic low-cost option. About 50 dollars a year.

- Three patients credit it as primary — Julie Black in episode 109, Rachael in episode 127, and Lizzie in episode 165. Two more name it as a meaningful adjunct.
- Originally a chronic-pain app, but commonly used by LC, ME slash CFS, and fibromyalgia populations as the in-the-moment fear-and-flare reframing layer.
- Rachael in episode 127 chose Curable specifically because she could not afford face-to-face Gupta. The wiki treats Curable as the affordable proxy for the same brain-retraining family.
- Considered low-stakes — try it first if cost or commitment is a barrier. If it works, great. If not, you have lost very little.

## Gupta Program (Ashok Gupta)

Reasonable reputation but the wiki does not yet have a recovered-patient credit for it.

- Zero key-to-recovery rows on this podcast.
- Three clinician or host recommendations, including Dr Tina Peers in episode 38 who reports "30 percent improvement" in her patient cohort.
- One patient mentioned considering it but went with Curable instead due to cost.
- The wiki note is explicit — "when a recovered patient credits Gupta as primary, the count moves." It has not yet.

Read this honestly: Gupta might be excellent and the absence of testimony might just be sampling. But on the wiki as it stands, the signal for ANS Rewire is stronger.

## DNRS — Dynamic Neural Retraining System (Annie Hopper)

Mixed picture. Three clinician recommendations, but the only patient outcome on record is a null result with a useful warning attached.

- Sally Riggs in episode 101 — psychologist, herself a long-hauler, mostly recovered. Her note: "I did the dynamic neural retraining system. I think it's meant to be done in three days, and I think I did the whole thing in one day." She frames this as a perfectionism-driven approach to brain retraining that creates the very nervous-system pattern the program is trying to undo. Her own recovery came through polyvagal-theory work and the Safe and Sound Protocol.
- This matters for you because you track meticulously and may have perfectionist tendencies. If you do choose any brain-retraining program, the wiki's implicit warning is — pace yourself through it the way you would pace any other rehab. Doing it harder, faster, or more rigidly is counterproductive.

## Lightning Process (Phil Parker)

Two recoveries, but both patients were already most of the way home before doing it.

- Rachel Whitfield in episode 8 — credits LP as primary, but had booked an unaffordable work fortnight before doing it, which created the urgency.
- Jamie in episode 186 — did LP at around 90 percent recovered, after Primal Trust and other foundational work. Her view: LP was useful as a final-stretch tool, possibly harmful if used early because of the three-day intensive format.
- Dr Sarah in episode 144 — partial help at around 30 percent recovered. Surfaced self-limiting beliefs but was not her primary recovery driver.

Bottom line — controversial, three-day format is expensive and intense, and the pattern is "useful late, risky early." Probably not the first thing to try, given your recent crash.

## Primal Trust (Dr Cathleen King)

One recovery on record, with a presentation close to yours.

- Jamie in episode 186 — used Primal Trust while bedbound. Did the lessons from bed even though the program is meant to be done standing. Light-visualisation exercises plus gentle walking radius expansion plus the self-talk reframe "this is just a hypersensitive nervous system."
- Indication tags: POTS, PEM, sound and light sensitivity. Profile-relevant for you.
- Single mention so far, so the signal is thin compared to ANS Rewire. But the use case — bedbound or post-crash, low-effort entry — is worth knowing about.

## Mickel Therapy

Worth knowing as a potential closer rather than a starter. One recovery on record — Claire's son in episode 208, who closed the final 20 percent gap with three Mickel sessions at age 21 after years on ANS Rewire. Different model — symptoms read as the body signalling overstepped emotional boundaries — and a small practitioner network. Not where I would start.

## ATNS slash symptomatic.me (Dr David Clarke)

Not a paid program but a free framework worth a look. Dr Clarke has treated 7,000-plus neuroplastic-symptoms patients and the website carries a 12-item self-assessment questionnaire and a short documentary. Useful diagnostic on-ramp before paying for anything — does the neuroplastic-symptoms frame fit your case at all? Adverse Childhood Experiences and personality patterns are the central levers in his model, which may or may not feel relevant to you.

## What this means for your decision

Things specific to you, drawn from your overview:

- POTS plus PEM plus possible MCAS — closely matches the profile that ANS Rewire patients on this podcast started from. Natalie Gold in episode 189 is the closest single match.
- You are already on the pharmacological pillars these programs are designed to integrate with — ivabradine, mestinon, florinef, antihistamines. Dan Neuffer's somato-neurological framing explicitly says these stay in place. Do not stop your medications to try a brain-retraining program.
- Your recent April 2026 PEM crash from the renovation matters. Brain-retraining programs require sustained daily practice — typically 30 to 60 minutes a day for two months on ANS Rewire, six months on DNRS. Starting one mid-crash is the wrong move. Wait until you are stable.
- You are on 50 percent sick leave. Joanna Rayl noted that taking medical leave was what made full commitment to ANS Rewire possible. Your time-budget is more favourable than most patients'.
- You track meticulously — flag for yourself the Sally Riggs warning. Doing a brain-retraining program perfectionistically can be counterproductive. The point is to teach the nervous system that nothing is dangerous, not to grade yourself.

If I were sequencing this for you, the wiki's signal points toward:

1. Watch Dan Neuffer's four free ANS Rewire intro lessons. Test whether the dysautonomia-rooted-illness frame resonates. If it does not, no program in this family will work and you can stop here without cost.
2. While you are doing that, take the ATNS questionnaire on symptomatic.me as a second-opinion frame test.
3. If you want a low-stakes trial first, the Curable app at around 50 dollars a year is the wiki's recommended cheap entry. Some patients fully recovered using only Curable.
4. If the frame resonates and you want a structured program, ANS Rewire has the strongest signal in the wiki for your profile. Wait until you are stable from the April crash before committing — the daily commitment is real.
5. Treat any program as additive to your current stack, not a replacement. Your medications stay. Pacing stays.

## Caveats and disclaimer

- Based on podcast testimony — this is anecdotal evidence, not clinical proof. Cross-check against `ercipedia/` for peer-reviewed evidence before acting.
- Patient testimonials skew positive — non-responders disengage and do not appear on recovery podcasts. Take all signal-counts with that bias in mind.
- All these programs presuppose structural drivers have been excluded. You have had a tilt test and standard workup, which covers the main bases.
- None of these is a substitute for autonomic, MCAS, or microclot medications when those are indicated. Discuss any change with your clinicians at Karlavägen.
