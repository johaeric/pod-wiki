# Episodes to listen to — curated for Eric's profile

A living, ranked list. Episodes added as conversations surface them. Each entry: why this one, what to listen for, and how long it takes.

Profile recap (so future-me can sanity-check additions): POTS plus PEM plus possible MCAS, on ivabradine / mestinon / florinef / antihistamines / Bufomix. Recent April 2026 PEM crash from renovation. 50 percent sick leave. Tracks meticulously via Apple Watch + covid_tracker app. Decision-making about brain-retraining programs and HRV tracking devices.

## Tier 1 — Listen first

These three are load-bearing for the brain-retraining decision.

### #189 — Natalie Gold (53 minutes, videoId `cA_23ERNOns`)

**Why**: Closest profile match in the entire podcast. Severe POTS-presentation — could only be upright 5 minutes, then 90 minutes to recover. Recovered over 2 years on ANS Rewire. Also resolved 16 years of psoriatic arthritis and chronic migraine alongside the LC.

**Listen for**:
- The "stuck personality patterns" framework — controller, perfectionist, helper, worrier, achiever
- "Don't do the Dan Neuffer program — do the Natalie Gold program" — adapt rather than follow rigidly
- The "small-t trauma" point — nervous-system dysregulation can come from a good childhood with subtle wounded messages, not only from big-T trauma
- The repetitive-thoughts loop ("you should buy salami") and how she worked through it

### #56 — Joanna Rayl (52 minutes, videoId `pF5yeYcoSak`)

**Why**: The full-medical-leave commitment story. PhD student, took paid leave, full daily ANS Rewire commitment, full or near-full recovery within months. Names the leave as "the most important decision I made besides finding the program." Your time-budget question.

**Listen for**:
- The eight pieces of advice at the end — worth taking notes on, especially "don't set recovery deadlines," "movement not exercise," "rest is the message your body is asking for"
- How she found ANS Rewire — searched "full CFS recovery," found CFS Unraveled, the light-bulb moment
- Failed paths in year 1 (cardiology, allergist, immunologist, PT made it worse) — the false-trail patterns

### #102 — Dan Neuffer (62 minutes, videoId `BKNX_t2acGw`)

**Why**: The founder explains the model in his own voice. Listen **before** paying for anything.

**Listen for**:
- The multi-sensory triggering heuristic — sounds, smells, light, cold, heat, barometric pressure all flaring symptoms. If that pattern fits you, the frame fits. If not, the program won't work.
- The "multilateral" / "somato-neurological" position — explicit that brain-retraining sits alongside, not instead of, medications. The bit that matters when you're on a pharmacological stack.
- His advice to watch the four free intro lessons before buying. Treat episode #102 itself as the free preview of the preview.

## Tier 2 — Tools and tracking

### #82 — Jay Wiles on HRV (53 minutes, videoId `wzVF8XxgOWM`)

**Why**: The anchor episode for HRV in long covid. Board-certified HRV-biofeedback specialist, co-founder of Hanu Health. Most credentialed HRV voice on the podcast. Listen before buying any HRV device.

**Listen for**:
- "**Relative not normative**" — only your own baseline trend matters, never compare your number to anyone else's. The single most useful framing.
- "**Data is motivation only**" — breathwork is the active ingredient; the device is for accountability and dialing in the practice.
- The **15–30 minute post-walk HRV recovery time** in LC patients vs 2–5 minutes in healthy adults — concrete pacing signal you can use.
- His own COVID HRV crash: 110 ms baseline → 14 ms, took 3 months to recover even though acute symptoms cleared in days.
- Don't use a low reading as a self-fulfilling prophecy.

### #86 — Harry Leeming on Visible (39 minutes, videoId `IXw5NIw4goQ`)

**Why**: Visible-app deep-dive. Free tier captures morning HR/HRV via phone camera; paid Visible Plus uses a deliberately screenless Polar chest strap. The clearest counter-frame to the Apple Watch hypervigilance trap.

**Listen for**:
- Why the Polar wearable has no screen — explicit design choice against hypervigilance entrenchment
- "Digital biomarkers" — what they are, why they matter for getting LC taken seriously by clinicians and policymakers
- The Imperial College menstrual-cycle study using Visible data (not directly relevant to you but illustrates the data infrastructure)
- The deliberate decision *against* hour-by-hour tracking — actionability and energy budget both argue against it

The decision tree this list supports:

1. Start with **#189** — does Natalie's story resonate? If yes, you're probably in the dysautonomia-rooted cluster these programs target.
2. Then **#56** — the commitment shape. Could you do that with your sick leave?
3. Then **#102** — does Dan's frame fit your symptoms? The multi-sensory trigger heuristic is the self-test.
4. Independently, **#82** — frame your HRV practice before buying any device.
5. **#86** — if you want a wearable, Visible is the wiki's strongest signal; this is the deep-dive on it.
