# Post-exertional malaise (PEM)

The defining symptom of ME/CFS and a near-universal feature of severe Long COVID: **a delayed, disproportionate worsening of multiple symptoms 12–72 hours after exertion** (physical, cognitive, or emotional), lasting days to weeks. Unlike normal fatigue, PEM does not respond to rest within a single recovery cycle and *does not improve* with graded activity — pushing through it deepens the crash.

The **International Consensus Criteria (2011)** name this **postexertional neuroimmune exhaustion** — a more accurate term than "malaise" but unwieldy in everyday use. Davenport (#141) prefers it because it captures both the exhaustion and the suspected neuroimmune mechanism.

> Cross-ref: see `ercipedia/wiki/symptoms/pem.md` for the trial-evidence picture.

This is one of Eric's defining features (POTS + PEM, see project `CLAUDE.md`). Pages on PEM are unusually load-bearing.

## What it actually is (per Davenport #141)

- Multi-system, multi-symptom: **not just fatigue**. Fatigue + cognitive dysfunction + sleep impairment + widespread pain + numbness/tingling + headaches + breathlessness + palpitations + GI/GU disturbance.
- Triggers include **physical, cognitive, and environmental** exertion. A short iPad video can be enough; chemical/sensory exposures count too.
- Lag time **24–72h** typically; sometimes shorter ("I thought I got away with it, then got hit 12 hours later").
- Recovery period prolonged. **Functional ceiling drops** post-crash.
- Variation is the only constant — between people, days, phases of recovery.

## Mechanism — the plug-in hybrid car analogy (Snell → Davenport)

- **Aerobic metabolism** = the gas motor: long-term, charges the battery, lets you do prolonged things.
- **Glycolytic / creatine-phosphate** = the battery: short bursts only.
- In PEM the gas motor is broken or unreliable. You over-rely on the battery, can't charge it, get stuck at the side of the road = a crash.
- At the cellular level: bioenergetic depletion (chemicals used to regenerate energy molecules become depleted; recovery time-lag depends on which ones and how depleted).

## What helps (per podcast testimony)

- [[pacing]] — the orthodox response: stay below the energy ceiling, never push.
- [[heart-rate-pacing]] — quantitative pacing using a wearable or chest strap.
- **Lying supine, feet up** — increases cardiac return during the post-exertion window.
- **Compression** (lower-extremity + abdominal) — supports blood return; addresses documented low plasma volume in ME (Davenport).
- **Hydration + salt + electrolytes** — Davenport names ORS / electrolyte drinks as crash-care basics.
- **Pre-prepared crash care plan** — see [[bateman-horne-center]] template.
- **Warning-sign list** — patient-specific (cold, sweating, palpitations, head heavy, struggling to talk) — once you can name yours, you can intervene before crashing.

## What harms

- [[graded-exercise]] / interval training applied without PEM awareness.
- "Push and crash" cycles — common before patients learn the term.
- "Beware the clinician who has you hop on the treadmill on a good day" (Davenport #141).

## Diagnostic / objective measure

- [[2-day-cpet]] — the wiki's gold-standard PEM biomarker. VAT drop on day 2 is unfakeable; differentiates PEM patients from deconditioned controls. Limited availability.
- Mark Faghy (#120) has a 2-day spread protocol to reduce harm.

| #191 | [[leo-galland]] | clinician | **Relative-ischaemia model.** Re-frames PEM as *post-exertional agony* (the word "malaise" understates it). Implication: blanket mitochondrial supplements can fail because the bottleneck is **oxygen delivery**, not enzyme capacity. Address microcirculation first; avoid oxidative therapies (HBOT, ozone) without antioxidant pre/post-loading. Some patients also experience cognitive→physical or physical→cognitive *crossover* crashes — overdoing one domain crashes the other. |
