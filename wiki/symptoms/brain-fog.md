# Brain Fog

The most consistently named cognitive symptom across the podcast: word-finding difficulty, slowed processing, working-memory failure, "alien-in-my-head" sense, breakdown of high-level discourse organisation. Often paired with — and easily confused with — fatigue.

## The "brain fog → brain injury" reframe (added from #90)

[[090-dr-james-jackson-brain-fog]] argues the term **brain fog** is a dual-failure label: too vague (50 patients = 50 definitions) and actively minimising for patients with severe deficits. He prefers **brain injury** as the umbrella, then sub-classifying:

- **Processing speed** — wheels turning slowly
- **Memory** — episodic and working
- **Executive function** — planning, multitasking, strategy

Why specificity matters: [[cognitive-rehab]] targets a specific deficit. "Brain fog" gives clinicians no rehab target, which is part of why so few patients are referred for the well-evidenced rehab pathways.

The strongest external evidence on the podcast is [[018-louise-cummings-cognitive-linguistic]]: a 142-participant study identifying robust deficits in **verbal recall, verbal fluency, and discourse informativeness** in LC patients vs healthy controls. ME/CFS comparison group matched controls on most measures except letter fluency — preliminary evidence that LC brain fog is not pure-fatigue-driven.

## 6-month follow-up findings (#122)

Cummings's 41-patient follow-up at ~21 months post-infection ([[122-louise-cummings-cognitive-linguistic-followup]]):
- Most language tasks showed **spontaneous improvement** — but **verbal recall remained significantly impaired**.
- On confrontation naming, scores looked similar to controls but with marked **cognitive struggle / inefficiency** to reach the target word. Score parity hides the laborious-effort signal.
- Cognitive issues outlasted physical symptoms in many participants. **Cognitive demand was the dominant blocker to returning to work** — only 1 of 37 returned to their pre-Covid role.
- Currently writing up a global 4,432-adult survey looking for **brain-fog subtypes** linked to employment outcome.

## Mechanisms hypothesised on the podcast

- **Hypoperfusion via low CO₂** (Kelly Mitchell #12, Wibbs #17): hyperventilation → vasoconstriction → reduced cerebral blood flow → brain fog
- **Sympathetic dominance / autonomic dysfunction** (Wibbs #17, Amy #20): NS in fight-or-flight diverts blood from the prefrontal cortex
- **Direct viral / inflammatory neurological insult** — referenced but not mechanistically detailed in episodes 1–20

## What helps (across episodes)

- **[[cognitive-pacing]]** — the lever the wiki most strongly endorses. Treat screens, reading, conversation as energy expenditure.
- **[[cognitive-rehab]]** — clinician-led compensatory-strategy programme (SLP/OT). Goal Management Training (GMT) for executive dysfunction (#90). The wiki's "best-kept secret" intervention for cognition.
- **[[brain-training]]** — computerised attention/processing-speed practice (e.g., BrainHQ). Complement to rehab (#90).
- **Real rest** — eyes closed, low stimulation, breath focus
- **Breathing rehab** — addressing the CO₂ side of the mechanism
- **Walking outdoors** — Amy ep #20 named bilateral movement specifically
- **[[stellate-ganglion-block]]** — Groysman (#76) reports often-immediate parosmia + brain-fog improvement; Q&A in #91/#92 covers practical questions
- **Time** — multiple recovered guests describe gradual but real cognitive return

## What doesn't help / harms

- **TV / scrolling as "rest"** — actively counterproductive (Cummings #18, Cass #19, AJ #13)
- **Pushing through cognitive demand** — same logic as physical PEM. Cognitive crashes are a thing.
- **Standard cognitive screens** that come back "normal" — give false reassurance because they don't probe high-level discourse / pragmatics (Cummings #18)
