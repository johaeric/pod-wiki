# GI dysfunction in Long Covid

Cluster covering the GI / motility / functional-gut symptom set in Long Covid: persistent diarrhea, constipation, nausea, vomiting, abdominal pain, bloating, reflux/heartburn, dyspepsia, faecal incontinence, rumination, food intolerance, IBS-like presentations.

The clinical framing introduced by [[mohammad-bashashati]] (#43) is **post-COVID Disorders of Gut-Brain Interaction (DGBI)** — the modern term for what was historically called "functional GI disorders." Defined as chronic GI symptoms ≥ 3 months meeting DGBI criteria, onset ≥ 6 months post-infection, with prior COVID-19, and no pre-existing DGBI of the same kind.

## Acute-phase prevalence

Up to ~1/3 of acute COVID has GI symptoms (Bashashati cohort: 45% diarrhea, 35% nausea±vomiting, 30% abdominal pain). Other documented complications: GI bleeding, pancreatitis, mesenteric ischaemia, motor-plexus damage, GI necrosis from hypoperfusion.

## Post-COVID prevalence

- ~9% develop DGBI at 6 months in mixed-population cohorts (India/Bangladesh, n=288)
- 66% of patients with chronic GI symptoms ≥ 6 months post-COVID meet DGBI criteria (Bashashati case series, n=164)
- 40% of post-COVID DGBI patients did **not** have GI symptoms during the acute phase

## Mechanism (per Bashashati)

ACE2 is highly expressed in gut epithelium. SARS-CoV-2 directly infects gut cells → secretory changes + low-grade inflammation + microbiota shift. The enteric nervous system (~200–600M neurons) responds with altered motility, hypersensitivity, neurotransmitter changes. Cross-talk to CNS via vagus → centralised symptom amplification. Stress and anxiety amplify the loop.

Frames it as a member of the post-infectious DGBI class. **Walkerton, Ontario (May 2000)** — E. coli + Campylobacter water contamination — is the precedent: 36% post-infectious IBS at 2 years; majority showed eventual spontaneous remission long term. This is the optimistic prognostic anchor.

## Diagnostic workflow

DGBI is **exclusion-based**. Workup:

1. Bloods, stool (calprotectin, parasites, infection)
2. Endoscopy / colonoscopy + random biopsies if indicated
3. SIBO breath test
4. Thyroid function
5. Imaging if needed
6. *Then* Rome IV criteria classification

## Treatment toolkit

Symptom-led, layered. Day-to-day variability often warrants PRN dosing.

| Cluster | Tools |
|---|---|
| IBS-D | Antispasmodics, neuromodulators (low-dose TCA / SSRI), antidiarrhoeals, antibiotics if SIBO |
| Functional dyspepsia | PPIs, neuromodulators, dietary triggers (caffeine, acid) |
| Constipation | Fibre, hydration, exercise |
| Faecal incontinence | Fibre, antidiarrhoeals, pelvic-floor biofeedback |
| Rumination syndrome | Diaphragmatic breathing, mindfulness, virtual-reality biofeedback, neuromodulators |
| Bloating | Rule out *H. pylori* / coeliac / SIBO; low-FODMAP diet, low-salt |
