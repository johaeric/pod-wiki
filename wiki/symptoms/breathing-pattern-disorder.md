# Breathing-Pattern Disorder

An umbrella term for chronic dysfunctional breathing — most commonly **upper-chest, mouth, accessory-muscle breathing** with elevated rate and reduced tidal volume. End result: **chronic mild hyperventilation** → low CO₂ → multiple knock-on effects: vasoconstriction (cold extremities, brain fog), Bohr-effect inversion (haemoglobin holds onto O₂ harder; pulse-ox reads 99 % but tissues are O₂-starved), depleted bicarbonate (impaired lactate clearance → PEM-like heavy legs), electrolyte disturbance (tremor, twitching).

Symptom set is near-identical to LC's general symptom set: dizziness, fast HR, air hunger, fatigue, chest discomfort, balance loss. **74 %** of LC patients had low end-tidal CO₂ in a paper Kelly Mitchell cites in #12.

⚠️ **Compatible with**, not replacement for, other LC mechanisms — viral persistence, MCAS, mitochondrial dysfunction etc. can co-exist with a breathing-pattern disorder. The pattern can persist as a habit even after the upstream trigger resolves, which is why it deserves its own intervention pathway.

## How to spot it

- Chronic mouth-breathing
- Visible upper-chest movement / stillness in the belly during quiet breathing
- Sigh / yawn frequency
- Frequent throat clearing
- Air-hunger panic when trying to slow the breath / breathe through the nose
- Low BOLT score (<25 sec)
