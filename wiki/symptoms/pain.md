# Pain

The wiki's **anchor entry on pain in LC**. Built primarily from #53 ([[deepak-ravindran]]).

## Prevalence

- **>70%** of LC patients in Ravindran's Berkshire clinic score ≥6/10 on aches/pains in muscles or joints (Yorkshire Rehab Screen).
- Pain is roughly as common as fatigue and breathlessness — but **systematically under-counted in research** because most clinics don't use pain-specific questionnaires.
- Common manifestations: muscle/joint aches, "coat-hanger pain" (cervico-thoracic), low back pain, headache, **new-onset migraine**, worsening of pre-existing migraine.

## Mechanism (Ravindran's three-pain framework)

| Type | Mechanism | LC role |
|---|---|---|
| Nociceptive | Tissue damage; chemicals → nerves → brain. NSAIDs/paracetamol/opioids work short-term. | Minor — only when pre-existing arthritis flares post-COVID. |
| Neuropathic | Nerve damage (e.g. diabetes, chemo, sciatica). Gabapentin / amitriptyline / duloxetine work. | Minor — only if a discrete nerve is injured. |
| **Nociplastic** | Central sensitization. Nervous + immune system stay sensitized; **scans clean, blood inflammation markers normal**; drugs and injections poorly effective. | **Dominant LC pain mechanism.** Same biology as fibromyalgia, ME/CFS, chronic migraine, IBS. |

> "More than 90% of my patients are not coming up with anything sinister or anything structurally damaging on scans — that means their pain is reversible nervous-system hypersensitivity." — Ravindran, #53

## What helps (per #53)

The therapeutic lever for nociplastic pain is **calming the immune + autonomic nervous systems**, not analgesia at the symptom level:

- **Sleep** (foundational)
- **Nutrition** — anti-inflammatory, whole foods
- **Mind–body** — yoga, tai chi, Pilates, mindfulness, **yoga nidra**, relaxation
- **Gentle physical activity** (paced; PEM caution)
- **TENS** machines
- **Cold water** (with PEM caution; see Adrian Baker #49)
- **Hydrotherapy** / gravity-unaided water therapy
- **CBD / cannabis** — patient self-reports, NHS-unavailable
- For pre-existing arthritis flares: standard analgesia + steroid injections may genuinely help

## Teitelbaum's alternative framing (LCP #167)

[[jacob-teitelbaum]] splits LC pain into ~7 named categories — distinct from Ravindran's nociplastic-only framing. Not all chronic LC pain is nociplastic; different pain-types respond to different interventions:

- **Muscle pain** (the bulk of fibromyalgia / LC pain) — low-energy muscles stuck in shortened position; 100s of tender knots.
- **Brain pain** — chronic pain → brain amplification → makes any pain worse. Standout new recommendation: **[[pea-luteolin|PEA (palmitoylethanolamide) 1,200–2,400 mg/day]]**, cites a recent disc/compression-pain study showing ~70% pain reduction at 3 months. Use a high-absorption / micronised form.
- **Migraine** — see dedicated [[headache-migraine]] page. B2 riboflavin 400 mg + magnesium + early-dose triptans.
- **Nerve pain** — multi-step medication sequence (lipoic acid, acetyl carnitine, then gabapentin/amitriptyline etc.) in his book chapter.
- **CRPS/RSD** — separate protocol.
- **Mast-cell sensitivities** — PEA also helps here.
- **Curamin (curcumin-based)** as starter for any LC pain.

These framings are **complementary, not contradictory**. Ravindran is talking about the *dominant* LC pain pattern (nociplastic); Teitelbaum is talking about all the *sub-types* you may need to distinguish. In practice: assume nociplastic until you have evidence otherwise, but don't ignore the sub-type-specific levers when they fit.
