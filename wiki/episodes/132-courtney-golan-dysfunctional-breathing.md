# Episode 132 — Rosalba Courtney & Hadas Golan — Dysfunctional Breathing in LC

Two breathing-therapy clinicians published a 5-patient case series in 2024 (with Jeremy Wolfberg, now at MGH) on **Integrative Breathing Therapy (IBT)** in LC — measuring biomechanics, biochemistry (capnometry), and symptom questionnaires before/after a structured 6-week protocol.

- **Rosalba Courtney** — Australian osteopath; PhD on dysfunctional breathing parameters, measurement, and clinical relevance; founder of IBT.
- **Hadas Golan** — speech-language pathologist (Boston University Medical Center, ENT department); upper-airway / voice / VCD specialist; trained in Buteyko + IBT.

## What dysfunctional breathing is (Courtney's three-axis frame)

Dysfunctional breathing = breathing that doesn't fulfil its functions, with three independent dimensions:

1. **Biomechanics** — pattern (upper-chest vs diaphragmatic), rate, accessory-muscle dominance.
2. **Biochemistry** — CO₂ / pH / oxygen handling. Hyperventilation = low CO₂.
3. **Psycho-physiological** — perception of breathing (hyper- or hypovigilant), distress signals carried in breath.

A breathing pattern disorder is one slice; hyperventilation another; the third — distorted *perception* — gets missed by purely mechanistic frameworks.

## Why LC patients have unusual hyperventilation

Courtney's working hypothesis: in LC, hypocapnia often presents *without* obvious tachypnea or hyperpnea. The likely cause is **mitochondrial under-production of CO₂** (internal respiration failure), not over-breathing. So forcing reduced-volume breathing (one-wind-and-poo Buteyko-style) makes them feel *worse* — they aren't low because they over-breathe; they're low because they under-produce.

Implication: respect that the dysfunctional pattern is a compensation for upstream pathology (vascular, mitochondrial, microclot-driven hypoxia) — work *gently*, not aggressively.

## Hadas's findings (the case series)

- **Population**: 5 consecutive LC referrals to her ENT clinic (not cherry-picked) with confirmed dysfunctional breathing on assessment.
- **Baseline measurements** (in person; capnometry + manual assessment of respiratory motion + Nijmegen + SEBQ + Dyspnea Index questionnaires):
  - **Profoundly low end-tidal CO₂** in many cases — into the 20s mmHg, lower than her asthma cohort baseline (30–32).
  - Respiratory pattern often **fast and shallow** to the point that initial palpation took prolonged contact to feel anything; some patients had effortful, irregular breathing.
- **Protocol** (over 6 weeks): nasal-function work → mind-body / posture / awareness → gentle diaphragmatic breathing → addressing hyperinflation/hyperventilation → resonant-frequency (cadence) breathing → pacing of speech & physical effort. Group classes (1 hr × 6 weeks) + individual sessions for measurement-driven personalisation.
- **After 6 weeks**: improvements across all three dimensions — biomechanical, CO₂, symptoms; many patients chose to repeat the 6-week course before progressing.
- Hadas now treats ~80 LC patients in this protocol; says she sees consistent benefit when patients stay long enough (often 12+ weeks).

## Why "less is more" in LC breathing

Both speakers stress: **the technique is not the treatment** — the personalisation around the patient is. The IBT model is structured assessment → tailored protocol → reassess. No one-size-fits-all. Reduced-volume Buteyko-style work is too aggressive in many LC patients; they often start with **slow / cadence breathing** before any reduced-volume work.

Group format adds a major dimension: validation, peer support, reduced isolation. Hadas reports the group classes effectively functioned as support groups in their own right — with measurable clinical improvements alongside.

## What's next

Authors want a larger pilot RCT with a control group, ideally with an MRI / pathophysiology readout (what *changes* in the brain / vascular / mitochondrial axis when breathing improves). Courtney also wants to expand IBT practitioner training. They are looking for research-collaboration partners.
