# #69 — Fiona Lowenstein — Body Politic founder, journalist, advocate

Fiona Lowenstein is one of the **architects of the global Long Covid patient movement**. They founded [[body-politic]] (originally a queer-feminist wellness collective in NYC pre-pandemic) and pivoted it in March 2020 into what became the world's first major LC support group. Their March 2020 New York Times op-ed about not recovering from Covid is, to their knowledge, *the first mainstream-media article describing what is now called Long Covid* — 2,000 people signed up overnight to join the support group via the link in the article.

## What Fiona built

- **[[body-politic]] support group** — moved from Instagram chat → WhatsApp → Slack within 48 hours. Slack was chosen for the channel-by-topic structure and security. Currently 14,000+ active users; ~40,000 cumulative signups.
- **[[patient-led-research-collaborative]] (PLRC)** — formed inside Body Politic from a "Data Nerds" channel; ran the first survey of LC symptoms in May 2020. Now an independent, funded organisation.
- **[[long-covid-sos-uk]]** — formed inside Body Politic by UK members who recognised each other regionally and split off into their own group.
- **The Long Covid Survival Guide** (book, The Experiment, 2022) — Fiona edited; chapters by recovered/recovering patients and a few aligned clinicians ([[david-putrino]], [[akiko-iwasaki]]). Designed deliberately as **non-prescriptive** ("Choose Your Own Adventure" health framing), and as a "support group in book form." Includes a chapter by [[lisa-mccorkel]] on how to read research papers as a patient.

## Notable framings

- **Internalised ableism** is something Fiona explicitly names — they had to unlearn it post-illness, and considers they're still in process. This is a wiki-load-bearing concept for the [[advocacy]] theme.
- **Race + class privilege in care access**: Fiona acutely names that they got hospital admission in March 2020 NYC partly because they wore a Yale sweatshirt to the ER, partly because they had a long-standing PCP relationship, while Black women presenting with the same symptoms at the same time were turned away as "anxiety" — and some did not survive. This is logged on the [[healthcare-gaslighting]] page as racialised gaslighting at the acute-care entry point.
- **Loneliness as the trauma trigger** — *"the first step is just knowing you're not alone — that's the thing that makes it survivable."* Pre-Sabrina-and-Twitter contact, Fiona felt completely isolated; the first FaceTime with a fellow patient felt like a "homecoming."
- **Workflows designed by chronically-ill people** — the book itself was produced by ~25 chronically-ill writers using async Google Docs, soft deadlines, expected flares, and explicit communication about bad days. *"It made me feel like we're so creative — chronically ill people should really be put in charge of designing workflows for chronically ill people."* This is a [[work-and-disability]]-relevant cultural argument.
- **Quick fixes don't exist** — book deliberately frames recovery as long, multi-modal, and individual. *"It's okay to not go to a million medical appointments because it might cause you to have a PEM crash from doing that."*
