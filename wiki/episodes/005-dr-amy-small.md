# #005 — Dr Amy Small

**Guest**: Dr Amy Small — GP partner in East Lothian (10 years); occupationally acquired covid in April 2020; long-hauler. Now **clinical advisor at Chest Heart & Stroke Scotland**. Lost her GP partnership at 6 months. Bristly-energetic advocate, BMA elected representative, very active on social media.

This episode is **highly relevant to Eric** (POTS-defining): Amy's clinical picture is POTS-dominant, and she credits beta blockers as essentially curing it.

## Recovery status

Substantial. POTS controlled on beta blockers; pacing structures the day. Working at CHSS in a clinical-advisor role; not back to GP partnership.

## Symptom picture

- Atypical acute phase (headache only, no smell loss, mild fever) → cough at day 6.
- Persistent fever for **4+ weeks** that nobody could explain.
- Severe dyspnoea at week 3; A&E sent her home.
- After half-a-day return to work at month ~6: **bedbound for 10 days**, jaw too fatigued to chew cereal.
- POTS-pattern: tachycardia, breathlessness on stairs, slurred speech / brain fog / weak legs on over-exertion, temperature spikes when overdoing.

## What helped, what didn't

### Beta blockers — "such a turning point" (key_to_recovery)

Took an out-of-date beta blocker before a stressful work meeting (left over from her dying father's prescription). Noticed striking improvement: walked stairs without breathlessness, less ache, clearer thinking. Got her GP to formally start her on a beta blocker for **POTS**.

> "My condition is basically cured through medication, and I am able to function, I am able to work."
> — Amy, ep #5

Classification: **key_to_recovery**. This is a clean N=1 case where targeted POTS pharmacology unlocked function.

### Pacing — foundational

After her catastrophic post-return-to-work crash, contacted a friend with ME/CFS, learned about pacing, bought a book, applied it rigorously. Doesn't claim it cured anything — but became the floor without which everything else would have collapsed.

> "I would love it if every person, four weeks post-covid, could be told: do you feel better? Fine. If not — here, learn about pacing. The earlier we get that in, the better."

Classification: helped_partial (she did not name it as the single key factor — beta blockers were that — but she does treat it as essential infrastructure).

### Other things tried

- **Nutritionist + supplements** — helped_partial, "better diet."
- **Osteopath / lymphatic drainage** — mentioned_only, "at least I felt like I was doing something."
- **Antibiotics** (covid assessment centre, week 2) — no_effect.

## Strong claims about LC care

- **Multidisciplinary expert clinics are essential** (she avoids the words "long covid clinic" because politically toxic in Scotland). Pure rehab without investigation = poor outcomes; pure investigation without rehab = same.
- **Pacing must be taught at week 4 post-covid** — every patient, default.
- **Beta blockers and POTS recognition are systematically missed** in primary care.
- **Hospitalised-vs-community funding gap is unjust** — community patients have relapsing-remitting illness and PTSD-from-isolation that the funding model ignores.
- **Healthcare-worker LC** is disproportionate; she felt "betrayed" — caught at work, lost partnership, had to seek help from the **Cameron Fund** (medical charity for GPs).
- Whole-body MRI scanning is bad practice — incidental findings cause more harm than good. We need to learn *who* needs which scan.
