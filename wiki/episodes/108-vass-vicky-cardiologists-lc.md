# #108 — Vass + Vicky: UEA cardiologists on long covid risk factors

UK cardiologists at the University of East Anglia (UEA): **Prof Vassilios "Vass" Vassiliou** (consultant cardiologist + Professor of Cardiology) and **Dr Vasiliki "Vicky" Tsampasian** (cardiology registrar, research fellow, on a PhD in cardiac imaging). They published a **meta-analysis / systematic review of LC risk factors** during the recording window — covering **41 studies, ~860,000 patients** — that the wiki should treat as the highest-evidence summary of who is at higher risk of developing long covid.

## Anchor finding: meta-analysis risk factors

Identified risk factors (relative-risk language — *no one has zero risk*):

- **Older age**
- **Female sex** (~50% higher risk than men)
- **Higher BMI** (overweight / obese)
- **Smoking**
- **Hospitalization / ICU admission during acute COVID** (more severe acute → more LC)
- **Pre-existing comorbidities**: coronary artery disease, chronic kidney disease, diabetes

**Modifiable protective factor:**

- **Vaccination roughly halves the risk of long covid.** The wiki's clearest single-number summary of vaccination-as-LC-prevention. Subsequently confirmed by other studies.

## Operational caveats they flag

- **Risk is population-level, not individual-level.** Any individual without these factors can still develop long covid (Jackie's own arc is the example raised in the episode).
- **Long covid is a clinical diagnosis** (NICE / WHO): persistent symptoms ≥3 months, not attributed to other diseases. **Tests can come back normal in long covid** — that doesn't refute the diagnosis. Tests determine *management* and *prognosis*, not *whether* a patient has it.
- **Mechanism heterogeneity.** Long covid is caused by multiple mechanisms (autoimmune, microclots, viral persistence, exaggerated inflammatory response, endothelial dysfunction). One pill is unlikely to address all of them.

## Cardiac-specific framings worth surfacing

- **Palpitations + chest pain + small scarring** are the cardiac symptom triad in LC patients
- **"Coat hanger pain" / chest pain** can map to autonomic dysfunction (compatible with [[sally-riggs]] #101's same observation from polyvagal angle)
- **Cardiac MRI** can detect small scarring and rule in / out structural problems
- **Normal cardiac tests are reassuring, not dismissive.** A patient with palpitations + normal echo + normal Holter monitor + normal MRI = same symptoms but **prognostically lower-risk**. Prognosis improves; symptoms still need management.
- **The vast majority of LC patients with palpitations improve substantially within 3–4 months**, with or without targeted treatment. Within 1–18 months for a fuller arc.

## Treatment implications they raised

The risk-factor pattern points to candidate trials:

- **Hormonal therapy / HRT** for the female-overrepresentation pattern (mentioned by Jackie as already in clinical use)
- **Nicotine-related interventions** (the "nicotine patch protocol" floating around in patient communities) for the smoking-overrepresentation pattern — early-stage, no RCTs yet
- **Vaccination of currently-LC patients** — RCTs running; some patients report symptom improvement post-vaccine
- **Standard cardiovascular risk-reduction** (weight loss, exercise as tolerated, smoking cessation) reduces both *future LC risk* and downstream cardiovascular outcomes

## Anchor public-health argument

Vass argues UK booster eligibility is **too narrow**. Specifically: **asthmatics under 65** are not eligible despite higher LC risk. The clinical risk-benefit for them favours vaccination access. The episode's clearest policy critique.

> "If our study influences a small proportion of people in the UK and across the world to start living a healthier lifestyle — cut down smoking, reduce weight, eat healthily, exercise, get vaccinated if unvaccinated — that would be quite a bonus." — Prof Vassiliou, ep #108

## Reassurance framing for LC patients with palpitations

Worth lifting out as a useful framing for any patient with cardiac symptoms in LC:

- Normal tests don't dismiss your symptoms. They de-risk your prognosis.
- Doctors prefer to find normal tests *because* it means a better long-term outcome — not because they don't believe you.
- Most palpitations in LC improve substantially with time, even without targeted treatment.
- The "I want my test to come back abnormal so it's something" feeling is understandable but counter to your interest. *Symptoms + abnormal tests* = same symptoms, worse prognosis.

## Eric-relevance

- **Risk-factor profile**: Eric is male, non-smoker, normal BMI — only the "no protection from prior comorbidity" factor was elevated. The meta-analysis would predict him as *low* baseline risk. That he developed severe LC anyway is the personal-level / population-level mismatch the episode names.
- **Vaccination cuts LC risk ~50%** is the clearest single-number policy finding for any conversation Eric has about reinfection prevention.
- **Cardiac-MRI is the appropriate test to rule in/out structural cardiac involvement** if Eric ever has unexplained palpitations or chest pain. Echocardiogram + Holter cover the dynamic-electrical question; MRI covers the structural question.
- **Prognosis framing**: most LC palpitations resolve within 3–4 months → 1–18 months → years for the tail. Eric is past most of this; the framing is useful retrospectively rather than prospectively.
