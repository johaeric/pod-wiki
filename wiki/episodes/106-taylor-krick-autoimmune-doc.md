# #106 — Taylor Krick: functional-medicine framing for LC, with mold as the standout mechanism

US chiropractor (Salt Lake City Utah) practising **functional medicine** at the Washington Wellness Center; host of *The Autoimmune Doc Podcast*. Brings a **mechanism-first, lab-test-driven, multi-mechanism** framing for LC that is meaningfully different from anything else on the wiki — because his prior specialty (mould toxicity, autoimmunity, ME/CFS) means LC presented to him as a recognisable cluster of mechanisms rather than a new disease.

## The "anti-niche niche" framing

Krick chose autoimmunity as his clinical specialty *because* it's a non-specialty: gut, hormones, toxins, stress, trauma all feed autoimmunity, so an autoimmune practice ends up addressing the same multi-system mechanisms LC presents. He treats LC patients identically to autoimmune / mould-toxic / Lyme patients — same labs, same supplements, same lifestyle work. Long covid is a *case profile* for him, not a new clinical category.

## Stage model of autoimmunity

A useful frame to add to the wiki:

1. **Silent autoimmunity** — autoantibodies present (e.g. myelin basic protein antibodies in pre-MS), no symptoms, no MRI lesions
2. **Symptomatic autoimmunity** — symptoms (numbness, vertigo, tunnel vision in the MS example), MRI still clean, formal diagnosis withheld
3. **Diagnosed autoimmunity** — symptoms + diagnostic-criterion-meeting findings (e.g. demyelinating lesions on MRI for MS)

Most LC patients sit in stages 1–2 across multiple antibody profiles. The research literature shows **>100 SARS-CoV-2 antibody cross-reactivities** with human proteins (thyroid, gut-barrier junction proteins, neurological receptors), most not commercially testable. Compatible with Proal's #103 framing of multiple downstream mechanisms.

## Krick's mechanism stack for LC (in his order of priority)

Lab-driven assessment; he runs a 90-minute first appointment where he "dumps the puzzle pieces and flips them face up." Mechanisms he actively tests for:

1. **Low-hanging fruit on basic labs** — anemia (no oxygen to brain), low thyroid, elevated liver enzymes, low vitamin D, low omegas, poor blood sugar control, elevated CRP. Won't fix LC but blocks recovery if missed.
2. **Mitochondrial dysfunction** — underlies fatigue, PEM, exercise intolerance, new-onset blood sugar drops. Tested via the **Organic Acid Test (OAT)**.
3. **Neurological / vagus nerve dysfunction** — sympathetic dominance, "wired and tired" pattern, vestibular issues, dizziness, vertigo, tinnitus.
4. **Gut → neurotransmitter axis** — clostridia metabolites inhibit dopamine → noradrenaline/adrenaline conversion. Detectable via OAT urinary metabolites. **This is Krick's most distinctive standout finding** in LC patients — he describes it as the single most impressive lab pattern he sees.
5. **Mast cell / histamine activation** — particularly in the amygdala, drives fear-based responses + hypervigilance.
6. **Mould toxicity** — see anchor section below.
7. **Autoimmune cross-reactivities** — measurable for some antibodies, not for most.
8. Hormones, gut dysbiosis (even without GI symptoms), methylation defects.

## Anchor finding: mould toxicity as the standout LC mechanism

**The strongest single claim in this episode and worth surfacing as a wiki anchor.** Krick reports that out of *hundreds* of LC patients tested, **mould burden is the most common standout finding** — sometimes known to the patient (basement flooded; lived in apartment with damp), sometimes invisible until labs show high yeast / candida / fungal colonisation.

> "Mould piled up the logs and poured gas on them. Covid came and dropped a match." — Taylor Krick, ep #106

The wiki has not previously had a strong mould-as-LC-mechanism voice — only oblique hints. Krick provides the clearest articulation:

- **Mould → glutathione depletion** (the master antioxidant); covid-sequelae literature shows low glutathione in LC patients
- **Mould → mast cell activation**; LC patients with mould burden present with much heavier MCAS/histamine load
- **Mould → mitochondrial dysfunction, sinus colonisation, gut imbalances, autonomic dysregulation** — same downstream cluster as LC
- **The "perfectly healthy 30-year-old who got severe LC while their less-healthy parent didn't"** is the typical pattern: prior subclinical mould burden in the younger person plus more vigorous immune response = worse LC.

Common patient-side cues he hears: pipe burst weeks before COVID; ripped out basement carpet; multi-flat / apartment building; long basement habitation. **Worth a wiki theme page** for the wiki to track future mentions.

## Anchor lab: the Organic Acid Test (OAT)

**Krick's most-recommended single test for LC patients.** Mosaic Labs (formerly Great Plains Lab); urinary metabolites; **76 markers**:

- Gut dysbiosis (clostridia, candida, fungal colonisation indicators)
- Mitochondrial markers (Krebs cycle metabolites, fatty acid oxidation)
- Neurotransmitter metabolites (the dopamine→noradrenaline/adrenaline conversion-block pattern)
- Methylation defects
- Glutathione status proxies

Krick: "literature-controversial; not gold-standard for anything; clinically the most useful single test I have for LC."

## Vagus nerve TENS

His most-viewed YouTube video is on **vagus nerve stimulation via TENS device** (100k+ views). Used at the tragus (ear) location. Same family as breathwork, cold thermogenesis, yoga — different doors to the same downstream goal of vagal-tone restoration. Compatible with — and complementary to — Sally Riggs's #101 polyvagal frame, but operating via electrical rather than auditory stimulation.

## Treatment philosophy — sniper, not machine gun

Cross-cutting principle worth surfacing on the wiki:

> "I see people taking 20 good things at minuscule doses. We take 4 things, all aimed at the same mechanism, in high doses. People are shooting a bear with a BB gun." — Taylor Krick, ep #106

- **Don't supplement without lab evidence.** "I don't put anyone on Nattokinase unless their D-dimer is elevated or they have a clotting history."
- **Don't supplement without targeted dose.** Sub-therapeutic doses of 20 things ≠ therapeutic dose of 4 things.
- **Stepwise, not all-at-once.** First grade (gut), second grade (detox), third grade (mitochondrial). Sequenced; outcome-tracked at each step (subjective + objective lab improvements).
- **Education first, supplements second.** Time goes into learning your own mechanism via podcasts, lab interpretation, history-taking. Trial-and-error with the right clinician compresses 2–3 years of self-experimentation to 2–3 months.

## Eric-relevance

- **Mould-as-mechanism is a candidate Eric should at least screen for**. Pipe-burst-style triggers + multi-flat / damp building exposure are the patient-side cues. If he's never been clinically screened for mould burden, the OAT is the single highest-value test in this episode for him.
- **Neurotransmitter-via-gut pathway** — if Eric has wired-and-tired anxiety + fatigue alongside POTS/PEM, the OAT clostridia / dopamine-conversion pattern is worth screening. Compatible with his existing dysautonomia framing, just upstream of it.
- **High-dose targeted supplementation** as a treatment principle — counterweight to the sub-therapeutic-many-things pattern that is common in patient self-experimentation.
- **Sniper-not-machine-gun** is operationally usable: any time Eric is considering a new supplement, the test is "what mechanism am I aiming at, and what evidence do I have that this is the mechanism?"
- Vagus nerve TENS is a candidate at-home practice if his existing breathwork / cold work plateaus.
