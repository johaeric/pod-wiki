# LCP #215 — Overwhelm 101: Why Your System Says "Too Much" (and What to Do Next)

Jackie host-solo, 20:21. **Short, dense, prescriptive.** A standalone synthesis of polyvagal / nervous-system framing applied to the very common LC experience of "everything is suddenly too much." Practical, with a clean 7-tool in-the-moment list.

## Central reframe

**Overwhelm is not a mindset failure — it's a capacity issue.**

> *"Your nervous system has a certain bandwidth. In long covid or ME/CFS, that bandwidth is often reduced. Not permanently, not irreversibly, but for now your system has less space to process input. Things that used to feel natural — a conversation, a decision, a noise — can suddenly feel like too much."*

Jackie maps three nervous-system states (echoes polyvagal theory, Riggs LCP #23 framing):

1. **Regulated baseline** — ventral vagal
2. **Fight-flight** — irritability, brain fog, panic, urge to escape, crying "for no reason," everything happening too fast
3. **Shutdown / dorsal vagal** — frozen, disconnected from body, no energy, can't connect with others, avoidance

> *"This is a physiological state. Your body saying 'I can't take in any more information right now.' That's not a failure. It's communication."*

## Why overwhelm happens *in recovery*

- **System already working hard** managing symptoms, regulating energy, processing uncertainty
- **Poor breathing** makes the system work harder than it needs to
- **Threat-detection more sensitive** post-illness — the body interprets more inputs as danger
- **Invisible task load**: planning rest, pacing, monitoring symptoms, navigating appointments, advocating for yourself, explaining yourself to others
- **Emotional content**: hope, fear, grief, longing, frustration — *"these all take up emotional space and we often struggle to process these emotions because they can feel uncomfortable and we tend to disconnect from our body rather than lean in."*

## The 7 in-the-moment tools (Jackie's curriculum)

The most concrete actionable list from the episode — worth carrying out as a card:

1. **Name what's happening** — *"I'm feeling overwhelmed. My system is overloaded. This feels like too much for me right now."* Reduces internal pressure to push through.
2. **Orient to environment** — eyes on three things around you (color, shape, texture); reach out and touch them. Tells the nervous system *"we're here, we're safe enough."*
3. **Reduce input** — dim the lights, step away from screens, pause the conversation, close eyes, sit down or lie down. *"This isn't avoidance. It's regulation."*
4. **Micro pause** — **one soft breath out, not a deep breath.** Longer exhale; pay attention to the diaphragm. Distinct from "take a deep breath" — that's the wrong instruction in overwhelm.
5. **One point of contact** — hand on chest, belly, or collarbone. Feel warmth and pressure. The hand-on-heart safety cue.
6. **Shrink the moment** — *"just this moment, just this next step. I don't need to do everything. I just need to do the next tiny thing."*
7. **Step away from problem-solving brain** — *"Not now. First we regulate, then we think."* Don't try to fix / analyse / catastrophise during overwhelm. Return to safety first; cognition comes back online after.

## Longer-term what-helps

- **Pacing** — predictable rest, not pushing beyond capacity
- **Boundaries** — *"ruthlessly put yourself and your healing first. Oxygen mask first."*
- **Co-regulation** — being around people who feel safe, who don't put extra expectations on you
- **Meeting sensations with curiosity not fear** — somatic tracking, mindful practices
- **Micro moments of joy** — "tiny sparks that widen capacity"
- **Letting go of perfection** — *"good enough is enough"*
- **Reducing internal pressure** — especially the *"shoulds"* (Jackie cross-refs Amy Davies #212 on internalised pressure)
