# #44 — Moira Newiss — How do we make our energy?

Second appearance of [[moira-newiss]] (cf. #21 for the foundational nutritional-therapy episode). This episode is a **deeper dive on mitochondrial energy production** and the practical lifestyle levers that reduce cell-stress signalling. The clinical thread builds directly on the [[mitochondrial-dysfunction]] theme as it stands in the wiki.

## ATP basics — Newiss's framing

Every cell makes its own energy. The basic chain:

1. Carbohydrates → glucose; fats → fatty acids; protein only in last-resort starvation states.
2. **Glycolysis** in the cytoplasm.
3. **Krebs (citric acid) cycle** in the mitochondrial matrix.
4. **Oxidative phosphorylation** + **electron transport chain** along the inner mitochondrial membrane.
5. Proton gradient → ATP synthase → **ATP** (adenosine triphosphate).

Quote-worthy scale numbers:

> "You make your body weight in ATP in a day. The chemical process is happening thousands of times a second in every cell." — Newiss, ep #44

Cells with high energy demand (brain, heart, skeletal muscle) hold thousands of mitochondria each. Vitamins / minerals act as **enzyme cofactors** at every step — the reason diet is upstream of energy.

## Powerhouse vs Battleship — Naviaux's CDR

Newiss explicitly invokes **Dr Robert Naviaux's "Cell Danger Response"** model. Mitochondria function dual-mode:

- **Powerhouse mode** → make ATP, support normal energy.
- **Battleship mode** → cellular defence, downregulate energy production, signal alarm.

Mitochondria sense the *total* stress load (mental, emotional, physical, blood-sugar, sleep, environmental, viral, medication) and switch modes accordingly. Chronic fatigue / Long Covid = stuck in Battleship mode. The healing cycle has to **complete** for return to Powerhouse — which often it doesn't, hence persistent illness.

Mitochondria are also **endocrine signalers** — they produce stress hormones (steroid synthesis in the adrenal glands), so they are simultaneously sensors *and* signal-emitters in the stress response.

## The iceberg / tipping-point model

Cellular stresses accumulate silently — you "look fine" externally — until a stressor (the virus) tips the iceberg. Pre-COVID load axes named: poor diet, sleep deprivation, blood-sugar instability, sedentary work, stress, gut-health issues. This is the framework Newiss uses to explain why two people exposed to the same SARS-CoV-2 dose end up with very different LC trajectories.

## Practical levers (Powerhouse-restoration toolkit)

### Diet (her primary lever)

- **Real food first.** Outer aisles of the supermarket; if it has 10 ingredients on the label, skip it.
- **Rainbow plate.** Easy starting point: one new colour per day.
- **Easy zero/low-cook foods**: berries, boiled eggs (pre-cooked from supermarket), tinned oily fish, nuts, hummus, leafy greens. She has a **severe-fatigue meal-plan download** with shopping list and no-cook recipes (chia pot, smoothie).
- **Protein** every meal (animal or plant: hummus, beans, lentils).
- **Healthy fats**: olive oil, oily fish, egg yolks. Drop seed/vegetable oils (sunflower, etc.) — pro-inflammatory.
- **Drop ultra-processed foods.** Especially refined carbs, sweeteners, emulsifiers, preservatives.

### Keto / low-carb (specifically)

- Ketosis: ≤50 g/day carbs (sometimes ≤20 g) → liver makes ketones (acetoacetate, β-hydroxybutyrate).
- **Anti-inflammatory:** ketone metabolism produces fewer free-radical byproducts than glucose metabolism.
- Therapeutic precedent: epilepsy (especially paediatric), now type-2 diabetes, increasingly studied in Alzheimer's / dementia.
- Cleaner-teeth feel + clearer mind + reduced brain fog as secondary signals you've gone keto without testing.
- **Ancestral framing.** UK climate → ~9 months/year would have been low-carb/fat-dominant pre-agriculture; we're metabolically built for fat-burn.
- Athletic-performance evidence: fat-adapted endurance athletes (cycling Glasgow→Bristol fasted in 5 days at the recent Public Health Collaborative conference she attended).
- Test ketosis: urine dipsticks (Amazon) or breath ketone meters. Not necessary if symptoms-led.

### Other levers

- **Reduce toxin exposure**: skin-care, cleaning products (use bicarb / vinegar), air fresheners (most are bad), hair dyes — all sense-able by mitochondria.
- **Medication review.** Some common drugs are mitotoxic (statins, fluoroquinolones, metformin in some). Discuss with GP — never stop unilaterally.
- **Blood sugar testing** if not done; address pre-diabetes before it slides.
- **Movement, gently.** Even bedbound: clench hands, move legs — signals to mitochondria "it's daytime, we're alive." Avoid GET; respect [[pacing]] envelope.
- **Hydration.**
- **Breathwork, yoga, meditation, mindfulness.** Reduce stress-axis signalling.
- **Forest bathing / nature exposure.** Cited studies: even *photos* / window-views of nature reduce stress-response markers.

## Eric-relevant signal

- **Toolkit episode** — no specific patient outcome, but the **CDR model** is the working clinical hypothesis Eric should have at the back of his mind for any new LC intervention. If the intervention plausibly *reduces total cell-stress signalling*, it's compatible with the model.
- **Keto carries a `helped_partial` precedent** from #23 ([[sally-riggs]]) — for GI symptoms in particular, not POTS. Worth a controlled, supervised trial only if the medication stack (ivabradine, mestinon, fludrocortisone, antihistamines) is reviewed first — dehydration risks with keto + fludrocortisone is a real consideration.
- **Forest bathing / nature exposure** is a free, low-cost lever. Convergent with [[gratitude-and-forgiveness]] and the autonomic-regulation toolkit ([[boon-lim]] #40).
- The **"clenching your hands" minimum-movement** advice is useful for true bed-day floor-level baseline.
