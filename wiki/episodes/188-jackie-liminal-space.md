Jackie's solo essay on the **late phase of recovery** — the liminal space between "still sick" and "fully well" — and how to move through it.

## Three phases of her own recovery

1. **Denial / pushing through** — getting worse
2. **Educating + building strategies** — still crashing, but learning
3. **Gentle building** — strategies in place, exploring what's possible

## Tools for the liminal phase

- **Body works list** — write down every time the body responds well. Counters negativity bias; pulls you up on bad days
- **Celebrate small wins** — small things look big to current you, that's fine
- **Bring play in** — playful state = safer nervous system; childishness as therapy
- **Mute support groups** when ready — algorithm feeds you what you don't need
- **Suzy Bolt's [[fern-program]]** — used herself near end of recovery to address fear of reinfection
- **Visualisation** — see yourself doing the dream

## Important framings

- *"Healing isn't a destination, it's a soft place to land"* — easing pressure on the nervous system by embracing journey over destination
- "Is this normal?" as a constant check during the liminal phase — re-learning what normal feels like
- Cites Dan Neuffer: people often **stuck at 80%** — that's the point to start *gently challenging* yourself
- Cites Dr Kathleen King (via Raelan Agle): people get stuck because they keep trying to *fix* rather than starting to *re-engage with life*
- After recovery: identity void — didn't fit with the still-ill, didn't fit with the never-ill. Therapist + Michelle Irving's work helped. Recovery is rebuilding *who you want to be now*.
