# #101 — Sally Riggs returns: polyvagal theory, SSP, and her new e-course

Follow-up to [[023-sally-riggs-psychologist-long-hauler]] from ~3.5 years into Sally's arc. **Status update: "more or less recovered"** — a step beyond the "substantial recovery" framing of #23. The episode is split between (a) her clearest articulation yet of polyvagal theory for an LC audience, (b) her **Safe and Sound Protocol** anchor reaffirmed with stronger attribution, and (c) the launch of her LC-specific 3-module e-course.

## Polyvagal anchor — refined

Restates Porges's three-state model with a critical LC framing the wiki should preserve: **most of "calm your nervous system" content online is built for people who already live in rest-and-digest. LC patients are predominantly in dorsal-vagal shutdown, which is the opposite condition.** Sally argues the standard advice (meditation, gratitude, "I am safe" affirmations) cannot work upstream of state — those are *outputs* of rest-and-digest, not inputs to it.

> "Telling yourself you're safe is never going to work. Cuing your body for safety has to be *somatic* — perception, body-position, sensation. The words are irrelevant; the doing and the cuing is what does it." — Sally Riggs, ep #101

## Sally's own warning-signal mapping (replicable)

A practical pattern worth lifting out for any LC patient learning state-tracking:

- **Chest pain** = first sign of fight-or-flight onset for her (likely MCAS-component)
- **"Coat hanger pain"** (across the upper back, shaped like a hanger) = *past the point of no return* — sympathetic→shutdown transition incoming; can no longer redirect
- **The work**: catch the early sign, complete the sympathetic cycle (movement, breath, orienting), don't let it tip into immobilisation

Each patient needs to map their own warning signals. Jackie's is breath rate accelerating. The principle generalises.

## Safe and Sound Protocol — strengthening attribution

Sally now describes SSP as **"really the first thing that kickstarted my recovery"** (September 2021). The mechanism explanation she gives in #101 is the wiki's clearest:

- 5 hours of frequency-manipulated music (middle frequencies = human-speech band)
- Stretches the vagus nerve "like an elastic band" — short bursts (2–10 min) of stretch + recoil
- Auditory pathway → middle-ear muscles → vagus nerve → ventral-vagal tone
- Re-simulates *infant attachment soothing* (mother sing-song voice → nervous-system regulation) — many adults who didn't get full attachment co-regulation as babies use SSP for that re-do, except now self-administered.

**Practitioner caveats** (important — promoted to the SSP page):
- Must be delivered by a certified practitioner (Unyte network)
- Many certified practitioners (chiropractors, yoga teachers, OTs) **don't know LC** — Sally explicitly warns to vet for LC literacy
- Face-to-face / Zoom-with-visible-face is needed for **co-regulation** — practitioner re-anchors you if you destabilise during listening
- Going through fight-or-flight to climb out of shutdown is **uncomfortable** and can dump people deeper into shutdown without the right scaffolding

## Sally's e-course (3 modules)

Launched alongside this episode. Built specifically for LC, not adapted from chronic-pain or generic chronic-illness courses (which she critiques explicitly — "every sentence in mine has long covid in it").

1. **Sensitivities** — polyvagal explainer, trauma context, **track your nervous system, not your symptoms** (counter-recommendation to mainstream LC clinic advice). Five obstacles named: perfectionism, chronic fatigue, self-criticism, insomnia, [fifth blanked].
2. **Strategies** — one polyvagal practice per day; can't progress until practiced. Includes orienting, weighted-blanket positioning, self-hugging, and others. Designed to be doable from total bedbound shutdown ("if you're still in bed 24/7, you can still do this course").
3. **Support** — life-architecture redesign so the post-recovery setup queues nervous-system regulation by default.

Format accessibility: video / audio-only / transcript options for light-, sound-, or visual-sensitive patients; 5–10 min lessons.

## Anchor strategy: orienting

The technique she demonstrates in real time:

> "Look around. Take in everything. What's behind you, under the desk, above you — those are subconscious safety cues. Don't judge ('that's dirty, I should put it away') — just *see*. Watch for the body's response: a sudden deep breath, a yawn, the stomach softening — those are the signs your nervous system has accepted the safety signal."

Mechanism: **neuroception** — the subconscious physiological perception system that decides "safe / not safe" upstream of cognition. Hypervigilant nervous systems fixate on threat cues; orienting forces broad sweep that lets safety cues land.

## Sally's own DNRS history (new disclosure)

> "I did the dynamic neural retraining system. I think it's meant to be done in three days, and I think I did the whole thing in one day. That was well before I learned about my nervous system." — Sally Riggs

Self-deprecating but useful — illustrates the perfectionism-driven approach to brain-retraining programs that *creates the very nervous-system pattern the program is trying to undo*. A DNRS warning the wiki should propagate.

## Eric-relevance

- **Polyvagal frame > generic acceptance/mindset advice** — Sally's strongest claim. Eric is more likely to be in mixed sympathetic-shutdown than rest-and-digest, so any "just accept it" advice is upstream-blocked.
- **Warning-signal mapping** — Eric should map his own (heart-rate spikes? breath rate? specific body-pain signal?). Sally's chest-pain → coat-hanger-pain progression is hers; the *practice* of mapping is portable.
- **Orienting** is a free, do-anytime, no-equipment polyvagal entry point — the cheapest test of whether somatic-cuing-over-cognition lands for him.
- **SSP** is a candidate intervention if cheaper somatic strategies plateau, but it's gated behind a vetted certified practitioner.
- The e-course is built for LC severity ranges; if Eric ever evaluates a polyvagal program, this is the wiki's most LC-specific candidate.
