# #173 — Dr Diana Driscoll — Healing PoTS and Dysautonomia

US optometrist who got post-viral dysautonomia/POTS, was disabled for 12+ years, had two affected children, was repeatedly told she was "perfectly normal, just more aware of her body." Reverse-engineered her own treatment, founded **POTS Care** clinic and **Genetic Disease Investigators**; holds 5 patents in the space. Recovered fully and stays well.

## The Driscoll model of inflammatory POTS

Driscoll views POTS as a **presentation, not a disease** — the heart's response to a deeper inflammatory problem affecting the cardiovascular system. Her commonalities-first algorithm:

1. **High intracranial pressure** — secondary to inflammation; often missed on MRI (no swollen optic nerve), but disabling. Treated with diuretic-class medication (acetazolamide-style).
2. **Parasympathetic / acetylcholine deficiency** — found via her own studies that nicotine patches normalised symptoms, proving receptors worked, so it wasn't autoimmune receptor blockade. She built **Parasym Plus** to support acetylcholine production (nicotine itself is too inflammatory long-term). First-line in her protocol.
3. **Oxidation from chronic inflammation** — drives anxiety, OCD-flavored hyperarousal, "buzzy fear," fragile blood vessels. Built **NAC Max** (recycles glutathione). Carries it daily during recovery.

> "POTS is the heart's response. The label has done us no favors — the actual problem is the inflammatory tendency, and that's what should be tracked and treated." — Diana Driscoll, ep #173

## Distinctive claims

- **70–80% female skew explained** by estrogen surges being inflammatory to blood vessels — male patients' arc is shorter on average.
- **Hot flashes vs inflammatory episodes** — inflammatory episodes look like hot flashes but include tachycardia and panic spike (often misread as menopause).
- **Genetic propensity** is real — she's running gene work to identify why some patients can't handle this inflammatory pattern, so recovered patients can stay well after future viruses/triggers.
- **Recovery feels fragile for ~2 years**, then becomes stable. She is now "probably the most active person of any age I know."
