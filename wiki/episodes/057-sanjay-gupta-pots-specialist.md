# #57 — Dr Sanjay Gupta — the POTS Specialist

[[sanjay-gupta-cardiologist]] is a UK cardiologist based in York with a YouTube channel and a private clinic. **>1000 active POTS patients.** This is the **anchor POTS clinician episode** for the wiki — and the most directly Eric-relevant episode so far.

## Core thesis

POTS is the wrong name. It's a man-made label tied to an arbitrary HR threshold (>30 bpm postural rise). The right frame is **dysautonomia** — and the dysautonomia umbrella covers POTS, CFS, fibromyalgia, "appropriate sinus tachycardia," IBS, FND, LC. Same litter, possibly the same puppy.

> "POTS, CFS, long covid, fibromyalgia, appropriate sinus tachycardia — they could all be the same puppy, or different puppies from the same litter. But they're definitely from that same litter."

## Mechanism story

- The autonomic nervous system has two contrasting sides: **flight-and-fight** vs **rest-and-digest**
- Dysautonomia patients are **simultaneously tired and wired** — over-active flight-and-fight + suppressed rest-and-digest
- Persistent adrenaline surges → racing heart, GI dysfunction, sleep fragmentation, sensory hypersensitivity
- **POTS or LC = a post-viral unmasking of underlying genetic dysautonomic vulnerability** — only ~10% of COVID patients develop LC; severity of acute infection doesn't predict it; many patients describe pre-illness "I wasn't quite right but I thought it was me." Vaccine can also unmask.

## Diagnostic stance

- **Clinical diagnosis, not test-driven.** Don't fail patients on the 30-bpm threshold.
- "I'm here to treat the patient, not treat a test."
- FND patients seen by neurologists very likely include undiagnosed POTS.

## Four-pillar treatment (do all simultaneously)

### 1. Lifestyle

- **Salt 6–10 g/day (~2 teaspoons extra)** — without salt, increased water just makes you urinate more. Salt holds water in the system.
- High fluid intake
- **Compression garments** for the legs — make the body smaller relative to blood volume
- **Low-carb / high-protein meals**, smaller and more frequent — carbs pool blood in the gut (postprandial PEM-like flares)
- **Magnesium supplement**

### 2. Physiotherapy

- Build leg muscle strength to support venous return on standing
- **Start recumbent** if needed; build up gradually
- "Strong but consistent and pitched at the right level" — empathic physio. Not GET.
- Deconditioning from PEM-induced bedrest is itself a problem; don't avoid movement entirely

### 3. Medications (Gupta's stack — Eric-relevant)

| Med | Role | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Ivabradine** | HR control, preferred over beta-blockers | Pure HR-slowing, no BP drop, no sympathetic blockade |
| Beta-blockers | Second-line HR control | If ivabradine not tolerated |
| **Midodrine** | Vasoconstrictor — pushes blood up | Acts like internal compression stockings |
| **Fludrocortisone (Florinef)** | Fluid retention | Volume support |
| **Mestinon (pyridostigmine)** | Rest-and-digest enhancer | "Gentle drug; lots of people like it" |
| DDAVP (desmopressin) | Pill-in-pocket for shifts/events | Originally a bedwetting drug; retains fluid for one event |
| **LDN** | Anti-inflammatory | Useful adjunct |
| Clonidine | Adrenaline antagonist | For the "wired" phenotype |
| **Antihistamines (H1+H2)** | MCAS overlap | Many POTS patients have MCAS |

> "Medications are a bridge to a destination, not the destination. They break the vicious cycle so you can muster strength for self-management."

### 4. IV saline — the transformative outlier

- 40 of his patients on regular IV saline
- "Wheelchair in, walks out" pattern
- Effect lasts **24–36 hours**, then re-treat
- Mechanism: bypass the gut, get fluid directly into vascular compartment
- **Not on the NHS pathway**; he runs it privately
- *"Patients are telling me they're better. They're not worse. I haven't harmed anyone. Why wait for someone else's research?"*

### 5. Patient advocacy (Gupta's pillar four)

- Writes to workplaces / schools / universities about accommodations
- Supports PIP (UK disability benefit) applications
- Supports blue-badge (parking) applications
- "Reuniting the patient to their identity"

## Severity

> "It is more unsettling and seems more dangerous than it actually is."

Over 1000 patients across years, no observed POTS-attributable heart failure / heart attacks / deaths. The racing heart is debilitating but **not life-threatening**. The threat is to quality of life. Useful for Eric's reassurance.
