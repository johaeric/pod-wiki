# #90 — Dr James Jackson: Brain Fog, Cognitive Impairment & Brain Injury

US neuropsychologist at Vanderbilt Medical Center; pre-pandemic spent ~20 years studying cognitive outcomes of ICU survivors. Pivoted into LC after running ICU-COVID-survivor studies → realised non-hospitalised LC patients had similar (sometimes worse) cognitive trajectories. Runs **5 LC support groups/week, ~100 patients/week** at Vanderbilt — explicit programme model. Author of *Clearing the Fog: From Surviving to Thriving with Long Covid — A Practical Guide* (US + UK).

## The wiki's anchor "brain-fog → brain-injury" reframe

Jackson argues the term **brain fog** is dual-failure: (1) too vague — "you could sample 50 LC patients and get 50 definitions"; (2) actively minimising — patients feel dismissed by it. He prefers **brain injury** as the umbrella, then sub-classifying:

- **Processing speed** — wheels turning slowly
- **Memory** — episodic and working
- **Executive function** — planning, multitasking, strategy

Why the specificity matters: cognitive rehab targets a specific deficit. "Brain fog" gives clinicians no rehab target.

## The signature claim: cognitive rehab is the wiki's "best-kept secret"

Jackson, self-described "Johnny one-note": [[cognitive-rehab]] is consistently effective for LC cognitive impairment but vastly under-prescribed in both US and UK. He cites **Dr Barbara Wilson** (UK pioneer; compensatory strategies). Two specific named programmes:

- **Goal Management Training (GMT)** — best-evidenced approach for executive dysfunction. Core technique: **Stop-and-Think** — recognise "high stress / hurried / tired" as the rhythm where cognitive errors cluster, train interrupt-and-evaluate at those moments. **Automatic-Pilot** as the failure-mode label.
- **Brain training** (e.g., **BrainHQ**) — distinct from cognitive rehab; computerised attention/processing-speed training. Evidence base growing; complementary to rehab.

Delivery: speech-language pathologists or occupational therapists. **Remote delivery viable** — no geographical bottleneck *if* clinicians offer it.

## Other practical signals in the episode

- **ADD/ADHD medications** — under exploration for LC cognitive symptoms (overlapping phenotype). Logged as `mentioned_only` — Jackson doesn't strongly endorse or claim outcomes.
- **Long Covid clinics (~195–200 in US)** as a starting point — get a diagnosis + a plan; "people are empowered by a plan".
- **Cognitive crashes are real** — cognition can drive PEM. Pace cognition like physical activity.

## On acceptance, OCD, vulnerability-modelling

Jackson discloses his own **OCD diagnosis (2018)** and the year of resistance before he could "accept" it. Frames acceptance not as resignation but as **"in this moment, here is where I am — what can I do?"**. Mirrors the same arc several wiki recovery stories describe (#7 Bolt, #20 Anker, #34 Munslow). His clinical move: **deliberately disclose own struggles to patients** — it gives them permission to take their masks off. Echoes [[081-liza-dileo-thomas-patient-experience]]'s communication theme.

## On mental health + LC

Argues against the **"it's just anxiety / it's all in your head"** clinician antipattern (cf [[healthcare-gaslighting]]). Patients learn to never disclose mental-health struggles to clinicians who once dismissed them. Jackson's reframe: **untreated mental health worsens physical symptoms via the bidirectional cycle** — managing mental health is itself a physical-symptom intervention. This is consonant with [[078-lucy-gahan-psychology]] and [[023-sally-riggs-psychologist-long-hauler]].

## "Frank, the Ardent Patient Advocate"

Jackson cites a Vanderbilt patient ("Frank") who flags **brain fog as actively insulting** to severely affected patients — anchors the language argument experientially.

## Eric-relevant intake

- **Reframe**: if Eric ever feels his cognition is "fine, just foggy", remember Jackson's reclassification — processing speed / memory / executive function are distinct levers and respond differently.
- **GMT's Stop-and-Think** is portable without infrastructure: catch the rhythm of errors (hurried / tired / stressed) and inject a deliberate pause. Cheap, no clinician needed.
- **BrainHQ** as a candidate self-experiment if a cognitive training step is ever desired (US-based platform; computerised; trial-supported).
- **Vanderbilt support-group model (~5 groups/week, ~100 patients/week)** is a useful operational reference if Eric ever evaluates community-side LC infrastructure.
- The OCD analogy isn't directly relevant to Eric's clinical picture but the **acceptance-as-stance-not-resignation** reframe is — same point #75 Jackie made about her own arc.
