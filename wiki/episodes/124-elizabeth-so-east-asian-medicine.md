# Episode 124 — Dr Elizabeth So — East Asian Medicine & Acupuncture

Elizabeth So is a NYC-licensed Doctor of Acupuncture & Chinese Medicine (DACM, Lac, NCCAOM Diplomat). She prefers the term **East Asian medicine (EAM)** over TCM — her training spans Chinese, Japanese, Taiwanese and Korean techniques. Recovered from her own LC using EAM principles after standard care didn't work.

**EAM toolkit covered in this episode:**
- *Acupuncture* — needle thinner than a hair pushes cells apart (rounded tip). Mechanisms: local immune/circulatory response; ANS regulation (parasympathetic shift); strong neuroplastic effect (cites NYU stroke-rehab residency); dermatome → organ innervation; electromagnetic conductivity hacking.
- *Chinese herbal formulas* — multi-herb balanced combinations, not single herbs.
- *Gua sha* — scraping technique with stone tool (her favourite is a jar lid). For LC specifically she uses it to break up "biological debris" / blood stasis in lungs and accessory breathing muscles. Diagnostic: if nothing comes to surface, blood-stasis is not the issue.
- *Cupping*, *moxibustion*, medical massage, Eastern nutritional guidance.

**Framework she uses for LC:**
- Long Covid presents very differently in each patient because each person's "internal landscape" is unique. EAM's principles function like ecology principles — the body is its own ecosystem; you can't predict every fire but you understand how the system rebalances.
- Sequela-to-febrile-disease is a built-in EAM concept thousands of years old. So treating LC as post-viral sequela is native ground.
- LC mechanics distinct from other post-viral states: higher tendency toward blood-stasis, viral-persistence-style chronic patterns. *"Long Covid amplifies whatever you already had."* Past migraines come back; past irregular periods come back; past asthma comes back.
- Treatment combines a foundational/root component (rebalancing the ecosystem) + branch component (current symptom flare).

**Integration stance:** Western and Eastern medicine describe the same body in different vernacular. She sees herself as the translator. Worked at an integrative inpatient hospital in Taiwan — herbs prescribed alongside Western meds with full understanding of interactions. Will refer out when the case isn't her lane.

> "Even though East Asian medicine has never treated long Covid before, we have the baseline principles of how that ecosystem functions."
> — Elizabeth So, ep #124
