# #68 — Dr David Saperstein — Links in Long Covid, MCAS, Dysautonomia, EDS & more

A clinical neurologist who **starts as a neuromuscular specialist (small-fibre neuropathy), drifts into autonomic, then into MCAS + EDS + POTS** as he realised the same patients kept showing up across his clinic. Now treats the cluster as a single integrated picture, and applies that toolkit to LC. The episode is a strong overview of the **MCAS / dysautonomia / EDS-hypermobility "trifecta" / "pentad" / "septad" pattern**.

## The clinical framework

- **Small-fibre neuropathy (SFN)** — the autonomic nerves are damaged. Standard EMG/NCS misses it; a **skin-punch biopsy** with specialised staining is needed. SFN explains a lot of the dysautonomia + neuropathic-pain phenotype that gets dismissed as "non-specific symptoms."
- **The trifecta**: hypermobility (EDS spectrum) + dysautonomia + MCAS. Very common to find all three together.
- **The pentad**: trifecta + GI symptoms + immune issues.
- **The septad**: pentad + 2 more (sometimes auto-immune, sometimes anxiety).
- **The hypermobile phenotype** as the underlying thread — Saperstein hypothesises that genetically-predisposed connective-tissue patients are more susceptible to dysregulation following a viral hit.
- **Pre-LC history matters** — most LC patients had subtle hypermobility, sensitive stomach, easy hives, fainting episodes as a kid. They compensated, lived their lives. Then Covid (or vaccine) tipped them over.
- **"Control board with knobs"** metaphor — turn the right knobs (which depend on the patient) until things settle.

## Notable distinct claim: Long Covid POTS *presents* differently

A claim other dysautonomia specialists don't all share: **post-COVID POTS patients describe the racing heart rate as the dominant symptom**, while non-COVID POTS patients describe orthostatic intolerance / dizziness as dominant. Tilt-table results look the same. He's not sure what it means yet — could be acute-phase artefact (LC patients are diagnosed sooner because they know a trigger date) or could be a real subtype distinction.

## Treatment toolkit (clinical, MCAS-leaning)

Antihistamine cocktail (his typical starting prescription):
- **H1 blocker, twice daily, often double the standard dose** — Zyrtec / Allegra / Claritin / Xyzal. He emphasises the **higher-than-OTC-recommended dosing** and the **twice-daily schedule** (not the once-daily that the labels say).
- **H2 blocker, twice daily** — famotidine 20–40mg or ranitidine. Histamine-2 receptors are throughout the body including brain; blocking H2 amplifies H1 effect and helps brain-fog.
- **Mast-cell stabilisers**:
  - [[sodium-cromoglicate|cromolyn]] (oral)
  - [[ketotifen]]
  - **[[quercetin]]** (supplement, antioxidant, mast-cell stabiliser) — 250–500 mg, sometimes higher; can interact with thyroid meds — caveat noted

Sympathetic-nervous-system blunting:
- Beta blockers (or ivabradine where available)
- Increased salt + fluids
- Compression

Behavioural / NS-regulation:
- **Brain-retraining programs** (he names DNRS-style as "neural retraining"). Useful when the sympathetic alarm is broken — *"the alarm is going off but there's no fire."* Coexist with the alarm while you also treat the upstream drivers. → cross-ref [[dynamic-neural-retraining-system]].
- Meditation
- Small-fibre-neuropathy autoimmunity — research-stage; if confirmed, IVIG / immunotherapy may be the targeted treatment (cross-ref [[066-lauren-stiles|Lauren Stiles #66]]).

## The mast-cell ↔ sympathetic feedback loop

Important framing: mast-cells release mediators that stimulate the sympathetic nervous system. The sympathetic system in turn activates mast cells. **You usually have to treat both sides of the loop** — treating one alone leaves the other to keep firing. This explains why patients who respond beautifully to e.g. midodrine alone are rarer than those who need an MCAS layer + an autonomic layer + a NS-regulation layer.

## Diagnosis caveat

MCAS is hard to lab-confirm. Tests: serum tryptase, urine N-methylhistamine + prostaglandin D2 in 24-hour collection, sometimes intestinal biopsy showing increased mast-cell counts. **Often presumptive** — diagnose by symptom pattern + therapeutic response. Don't wait for a positive lab result to start the antihistamine cocktail.
