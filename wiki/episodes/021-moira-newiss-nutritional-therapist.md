# #021 — Moira Newiss — Nutritional Therapist

**Guest**: **Moira Newiss** — nutritional therapist (Institute for Optimum Nutrition London, BANT registered, Public Health Collaborative ambassador). Based on Scotland's west coast near Oban. Specialises in burnout / chronic fatigue / long covid in professionals. Implies own past experience ("I've been there myself") but doesn't name a recovery story.

A whirlwind tour of nutrition's role in LC. Practitioner-as-educator episode, not a recovery account. **No new evidence-log rows for interventions earn `key_to_recovery` or `helped_partial`** — Moira speaks as a clinician, so most of her named interventions land as `recommended`.

## Mechanisms named (her shortlist)

Five LC mechanisms she watches for, all sharing **inflammation** as common factor:
1. Mast Cell Activation (MCAS)
2. Neuroinflammation (microglial priming)
3. Dysautonomia
4. Microvascular damage / microclots
5. Metabolic dysfunction
6. Mitochondrial dysfunction → **cell danger response** (Naviaux frame: mitochondria sensing too much stress, switching to defensive mode → fatigue)

Plus: gut microbiome / **gut-brain axis** (70 % of immune system, 70 % of serotonin made in gut, vagus nerve), leaky gut / intestinal permeability, viral persistence, autoimmunity.

## Dietary playbook

- **Anti-inflammatory whole-food diet**: rainbow plate of fruit/veg, healthy oils (olive, coconut, oily fish), nuts/seeds; avoid ultra-processed foods (>5 ingredients = processed).
- **Low-histamine diet** for suspected MCAS — useful for some, restrictive, **needs supervision**. ⚠️ Jackie reports trying it, didn't help her, returned to bananas + tomatoes (`no_effect` aside, host).
- **Low-carb / ketogenic** diet for blood-sugar stability + mitochondrial flexibility — **with medical supervision**, especially if diabetic/medicated.
- **Elimination diets**: only under supervision; risk of over-restriction → reactivity → vicious circle. Do gut-healing first ("heal and seal"), then reintroduce.
- **Mitochondrial nutrient support**: CoQ10, selenium, zinc, magnesium. Food first, supplements only after gut absorption is optimised.

## Sleep / circadian

- Eat within daylight hours; last meal 3–4 hr before bed
- Bright light first thing (sunlight or SAD lamp — she uses one)
- Avoid bright screens before bed
- We've lost ~2 hrs of sleep in <100 yrs of electricity — biology hasn't caught up

## How to read this for Eric

Moira's practitioner framing (no recovery story, no patient outcomes) means everything is `recommended`. Useful as a *checklist* of dietary axes worth considering with his clinicians: low-carb/keto stabilises blood sugar (Eric is on florinef → already managing aldosterone/sodium); CoQ10 + magnesium are low-risk. **Low-histamine diet is the one with potential payoff for Eric** given his empirical antihistamine trial — but evidence elsewhere on this podcast (ep #23 Sally Riggs, also no-effect for her; Jackie no-effect) is mixed.
