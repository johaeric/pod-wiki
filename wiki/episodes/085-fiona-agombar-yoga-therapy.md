# #85 — Fiona Agombar on Yoga Therapy for Long Covid

Yoga therapist (Viniyoga tradition), author, "rest activist." Had ME for ~15 years from 1990 (post-burnout from running a busy PR business + glandular fever); used wheelchair at worst; recovered fully via gentle remedial yoga. Specialises in fatigue conditions; teaches a teacher-training course for LC yoga therapists.

## Yoga vs yoga therapy — distinction

- **Yoga**: group classes, generic.
- **Yoga therapy**: 1:1, individualised. History-taking; tailored practice for that person's pattern. **Hardcore physical yoga is generally contraindicated for LC / chronic fatigue.**
- For LC/CFS Agombar's tools: Mantra, breathwork, relaxation, very gentle movement (almost always supine). Working with **Prana** (energy flow) rather than postures.
- Look for a yoga therapist who is **trauma-informed** and emphasises breathwork.

## Mechanism framing

- LC + ME + chronic fatigue family: post-viral; in yoga terms, Prana is stuck or depleted.
- Western analogue Agombar likes: **Terry Wahls** on mitochondria as the cellular root of disease.
- Both yoga (Prana) and Wahls (mitochondria) say: *don't label or give power to the disease*; treat the underlying state and many downstream conditions improve.

## On not giving the diagnosis power — done compassionately

- "We don't deny how devastating LC is. But the mind tends to project: *this will always be like this*. Compassionate present-moment attention notices fluctuation, lets symptoms come and go without anticipatory dread."
- Anticipatory dread keeps the nervous system in fight/flight, which keeps symptoms loud. Breaking that loop is much of the work.

## Trauma framing

- Two layers: (1) most people in modern Western society carry pre-existing trauma; (2) chronic illness is itself traumatic — uncertainty, fear, hospitalisation, the social dismissal pattern.
- Symptoms can re-trigger trauma response. The body remembers.
- Yoga therapy works to gently unlock the trauma response — slow, self-compassionate, with a **safe-resource** anchor (a felt sense of safety like the softness of one's hands or an image of a loved one) the practitioner can return to if breath-work itself becomes triggering.

## Why breath-focus can be triggering — and the workaround

- LC patients with **air-hunger trauma** can find nasal/diaphragmatic breathing instantly retriggers panic.
- Other trauma patterns (e.g. having been held down) are also triggered by being still + closed-eyes + breath-focused.
- Solution: build the **safe resource** first; never force; allow safe touch (own hands) instead.
- This is a real reason to seek a trauma-informed yoga therapist over a generic class.

## On Buteyko-family practices

- Strong overlap with breathwork on the podcast. **One specific point of difference**: Agombar excludes **breath-holds / control pauses** for LC patients until near-recovery. Too much for the cohort. Coherent / *Sama* breathing only.

## On societal context — the rest-activist position

- Rest is socially scripted as laziness; convalescence is a lost concept (cf. [[030-gavin-francis-recovery]]).
- Society's signals (sugar/processed food, news cycle fear, drink/Netflix as "rest", productivity-as-worth) are systematically unhealthy for the nervous system.
- Recovering from LC is also a chance to **rebuild a life on different lines** — not to return to the same pattern that may have contributed to vulnerability.
- Echoes patterns already in the wiki: Bolt #7/#77, Suzy Bolt's "5% stretch", Gahan #78's "self-loss" → "reclaiming life".

## The takeaways from her in-progress book (with Nadine Mai, due 2024)

1. **Check your breath 3-4× daily.** Hand on chest, hand on belly. Is it slow? Lower? Through the nose?
2. **One Yoga Nidra a day, ideally two.**
3. **Notice if you're in stress-response.** Pause. Pacing as a continuous practice, not a phase.
4. **Self-compassion as game-changer** alongside breath. Without it, progress gets sabotaged by overcommitment, perfectionism, rushing.

## On forgiving the dismissers

> "I have family members who, to this day, deny or downplay my experience. I've come to forgive that — they're responding to the so-called normal of our society. **The Myth of Normal** by Gabor Maté is the book."

This is a useful frame for Eric: the dismissal isn't necessarily individual cruelty; it's a culture defending its own assumptions. Compassion *outward* is part of the practice, not just compassion inward.

## Eric-relevant intake

- The trauma-informed-yoga-therapy framing **explains why Eric's earlier breathwork attempts may sometimes back-fire** — and gives a vocabulary (safe resource, anchor) for the workaround.
- "One Yoga Nidra a day, ideally two" is a concrete dose recommendation that aligns with Jackie's #64 self-declared top-3 (yoga nidra at #2).
- The **forgiveness-of-dismissers** framing pairs with [[040-boon-lim-autonomic]]'s autonomic-state regulation via gratitude/forgiveness — different vocabularies, same lever.
- The Buteyko-style **breath-hold caveat** is a useful caution: Eric should not treat all breathwork as interchangeable; the holds are specifically what to skip while still recovering.
