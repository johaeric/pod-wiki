# #95 — Dr Élaina Gauthier-Mamaril: Long Covid, Zombies & Crip Prophecies

Edinburgh-based interdisciplinary research fellow at the Centre for Biomedicine, Self and Society. Philosopher of disability; ME/CFS since age 10 (now 31 — 21 years of lived experience). Identifies as **queercrip** (cyclical / episodic / invisible illness — "we don't fit the gender-binary of disability"). The wiki's first dedicated **disability-studies / philosophy-of-LC** anchor episode.

Recording corresponds to her Edinburgh Fringe show *"Long Covid, Zombies & Crip Prophecies"* (Cabaret of Dangerous Ideas, 8th + 22nd Aug 2023, New Town Theatre).

## The headline thesis: LC as a "mass disabling event"

Drawing on disability-justice activists (Leah Lakshmi Piepzna-Samarasinha) — frame LC not as an individual misfortune but as a **mass disabling event**:

- **1.9 million** in private households (UK ONS, March 2023) — Care Homes / prisons not counted.
- **40,000+ children**.
- Repercussions for years to come.

The frame demands a societal response, not just a medical one. Her work places LC in conversation with **post-polio survivors** (still alive, still adapting, often still working) and **HIV** (vaccine + cure changed disease trajectory but didn't address chronic-illness needs). Lessons from past post-viral histories matter precisely because society wants to "move on."

## Zombie as method (the most provocative move)

She deliberately uses the zombie metaphor against the typical "kill or cure" framing of disability. Why zombies:
- They come in **hordes** (mass-disabling resonance).
- They look like **people you know who've been changed**.
- They're seen as having **nothing to teach** — yet disabled communities have **deep practical wisdom** about uncertainty, adaptability, patience, fortune-reversals.

This is a deliberate "refusing respectability politics" move — she's reclaiming a figure able-bodied society uses to dehumanise the disabled and using it to highlight the **knowledge stored in disabled bodies**. The Stephanie Foo *What My Bones Know* parallel: disabled people's adaptation skills made many of them well-prepared for the chaos of pandemic lockdown.

## Cripistemology — the wiki's name for "lived experience as expert knowledge"

Johnson + McRuer's term **cripistemology** ("crip + epistemology"): there's specific *knowledge* stored in disabled lived experience that is not derivable from the able-bodied position. It maps cleanly onto the wiki's [[083-ryan-prior-journalist-author]] phronesis framing — and adds the philosophical apparatus.

**Operational consequence**: clinical research with patient involvement isn't sufficient if patient input is filtered through "doctor language." Cripistemology insists the **patient's own framings are the data** — not just material to translate into clinical idiom.

## NICE guideline backlash (a critical signal worth flagging)

Notable real-time observation about the **2021 NICE guideline change** for ME/CFS:
- The change moved from **graded-exercise + CBT** to **pacing-based** care.
- Patient-led advocacy fought hard for this — patients reported GET / CBT had been **harmful** in many cases (no-one cured; some actively worsened).
- **Backlash from clinicians** committed to GET + CBT-as-therapy — they argue the change harms patients.
- This pattern (committed clinicians vs patient lived experience) historically has caused harm — analogue cited: post-polio paediatric muscle overwork in the 1950s.

Cross-ref [[graded-exercise]] and [[me-cfs-parallels]].

## On the queercrip identity move

A key political distinction:
- **Queer ↔ LGBTQ binary refusal** — queer = not in the binary.
- **Crip ↔ permanent-disability binary refusal** — queercrip = cyclical / episodic / invisible.

She's deliberate that this is a **political identity claim, not a medical one** — and acknowledges it's a privilege of her location/education/presentation that she can claim it. People in many global contexts can't make the political move.

She had to fight her institution to keep the word "crip" in the show title — they argued it was a slur. Her position: **reclamation parallels "queer"**. (Caveat: don't impose the word; ask. But also don't be paralysed by discomfort.)

## Methodology — podcasting as scholarship

Her main research output is a **6-episode mini-series** called *Massively Disabled*. Deliberate methodological move:

- **Narrative non-fiction format** (not interview); voiceover + cut-and-paste; ~15-30 minute episodes.
- **Full reference list + transcript** per episode → citable like an academic paper.
- **Limit episode length to ~30 min** — accessibility and listener attention; not lecture-format.
- Argues: **academic podcasting legitimises podcasting as scholarship** + reflects her own disabled-cognition reality (writing dense academic papers is hostile to her brain).

This is the wiki's first reflection on **format-as-political-act** in LC research — pairs with [[054-elizabeth-dreicer-consuli]]'s research-infrastructure framing and [[097-harlan-krumholz-listen-paxlovid]]'s digital-decentralised study design.

## Two practical handles she names

1. **"I don't know how to say this — can I ask?"** as the antidote to "I don't know what to say so I won't say anything." Naming the uncertainty is better than silence around disability/illness.
2. **"You don't look that sick" is more harmful than wrong words.** People with cyclical/invisible illness expend their best energy *to be visible-as-functional* in public — being told they don't look sick punishes that effort.

## Eric-relevant intake

- The **mass-disabling-event frame** is reusable for political conversations about LC (when the topic is healthcare-system / society-level, not personal-clinical).
- **Cripistemology** is the philosophical vocabulary for what the wiki itself is doing — patient + tech-collaborator extracting structured knowledge from lived experience. Eric's project sits inside this tradition whether he's named it that or not.
- The **NICE guideline 2021 backlash** is an active political fight worth being aware of when LC + ME/CFS coverage in mainstream UK press flares up.
- The **post-polio cohort framing** (still alive; adapted; working) is a useful long-horizon counter-frame to the "we'll find a cure soon" Twitter pattern. Many will live with this for decades.
