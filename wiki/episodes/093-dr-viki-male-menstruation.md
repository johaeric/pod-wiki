# #93 — Dr Viki Male: Research into Long Covid & Menstruation

UK reproductive immunologist at Imperial. Best-known for the early peer-reviewed work showing **~1% of vaccinated people had a later-than-usual period; ~4% had heavier — back-to-normal next month**, defusing some of the misinformation around vaccine-period effects.

Pulled into LC-period research by [[gez-medinger]] (~18 months prior to recording) reaching out after her vaccine work — many of his LC interviewees were noticing menstrual-cycle ↔ symptom patterns.

## Two-strand research programme (just-launched at recording)

### Strand 1 — Visible-app collaboration (large N, low burden)

Symptom-pattern research using app data (with user opt-in). Prior survey with ~450–500 LC menstruators found:

- **70%** noticed a pattern between symptoms and cycle.
- **>3/4 of those who noticed a pattern** said symptoms were worse in **the run-up to and during the period** (i.e., the late luteal phase + menstrual phase).

The pattern matches the [[hormonal-dysfunction]] theme already on the wiki. Visible-app integration scales N up enormously and reduces participant burden. Confirms / refutes the survey's pattern with ecological-momentary symptom-tracking.

### Strand 2 — Wilco substudy (deep N, high burden)

In collaboration with [[danny-altmann]] at Imperial (Wilco study — auto-immunity-in-LC immune-system characterisation). [[wilco-imperial]] takes blood at multiple points across one cycle from N=30 LC women living near Hammersmith Hospital — characterises immune-system shifts cycle-by-cycle.

Goal: tie the symptom pattern to immunological mechanism — analogous to MS/IBD's known cycle-coupling.

## The MS / IBD analogue (the immunology framing)

Male's foundational claim: **the second half of the menstrual cycle is biologically a "mini-pregnancy" rehearsal**:
- Progesterone-dominated, prepared for embryo implantation.
- Immunological state shifts (analogous to pregnancy-state shifts).
- Multiple sclerosis improves in pregnancy + the second half of the menstrual cycle; **worsens in the days leading up to and during periods**.
- IBD: balloon-distension study showed IBD patients had ↑ pain only during their period; non-IBD controls didn't. Demonstrates cycle ↔ disease specificity.

LC patients reporting *the same pattern* as MS suggests either an immune mechanism or autoimmune contribution. Pairs with [[067-decodeme]]'s ME/CFS GWAS framing and the broader autoimmunity-in-LC hypothesis.

## Why this matters clinically

Two practical levers it could justify (pending RCT confirmation):
- **Hormonal contraception** to flatten cycle-driven symptom oscillation.
- **HRT** for perimenopausal LC women.

Male flags the **LC ↔ perimenopause symptom overlap** as something multiple GPs are now noticing — sometimes "just treat the perimenopause first" can substantially help quality of life. Open question: does LC trigger / accelerate menopause, or do the symptom sets just resemble each other?

## On the quiet PLRC (Patient-Led Research Collaborative) review

Lisa McCorkell's group published a Frontiers in Rehabilitative Science review covering LC + ME/CFS + menstruation — Male calls it a brilliant collation but says "the answer is actually still not that much" — i.e., the literature is thin enough that her studies are filling foundational gaps.

## On scientific accessibility / patient involvement

Notable methodological observation:
- Information sheets for the substudy had to be cut to single-screen length because LC patients can't process 3 screens of text — Male had to **negotiate with the Ethics Committee** to keep the brief format.
- This is a small example of why LC research benefits from patient involvement: ethics templates designed for healthy participants impose participant-hostile burden on the very patients they're meant to enrol.

## Cohort size + timeline

- Wilco substudy: N = 30 ideally; recruitment just started; first data return TBC — Male wouldn't put a date on results.
- Visible study: large-N, less burdensome — results expected sooner.

## Eric-relevant intake

- Doesn't directly apply to Eric's clinical picture but the **autoimmune-cycle-coupling immunology** is a reusable mechanism frame for understanding why some women's LC symptoms predictably worsen in the late luteal phase. Useful background for clinician conversations involving any female family/friends with LC.
- The **Wilco substudy** is a useful cross-link to flag when Imperial autoimmunity-in-LC results emerge in 2026.
- The **GP-level "treat perimenopause first"** observation is the kind of pragmatic operational signal worth remembering for support-group / advice contexts.
