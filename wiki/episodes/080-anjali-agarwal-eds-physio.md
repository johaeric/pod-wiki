# #80 — Anjali Agarwal: EDS, Hypermobility & Long Covid

Consultant physiotherapist based in Hyderabad treating muscular-skeletal patients, women's health, pulmonary rehab, EDS/hypermobility — and a growing long-covid telehealth caseload. Co-director of Long Covid Physio advocacy.

## Core thesis: the EDS/POTS/MCAS trifecta is over-represented in long covid

- The triad of **POTS + MCAS + hypermobile EDS-like connective-tissue features** appears together repeatedly in her LC patients.
- Two patterns: (a) people who already had EDS find LC has dramatically amplified everything; (b) people with no prior hypermobility now report joints dislocating, skin too elastic, TMJ popping out on a yawn, etc.
- Frames covid as **attacking connective tissue** — connective tissue is what holds you together; viral damage there explains why a single virus produces symptoms across so many systems.
- ~80% of her female LC patients now report POTS-pattern symptoms; many are also hypermobile and many flare around their menstrual cycle.

(This is a third independent statement of the trifecta clustering — converges with [[038-dr-tina-peers-mcas]] and [[068-dr-david-saperstein]].)

## Why hypermobility produces POTS — physiology

- Connective-tissue laxity → **hypermobile blood vessels** that don't recoil/contract well.
- On standing, vessel walls expand, ligaments + soft tissue can't pump blood up against gravity → orthostatic intolerance / POTS.
- Adds a structural explanation on top of the autonomic/baroreflex story — a different layer than [[040-boon-lim-autonomic]]'s baroreflex framing.

## Why MCAS sits at the centre

- MCAS damages connective tissue when it flares.
- Mast-cell instability → flares → connective-tissue mis-behaviour → joint instability + POTS triggering.
- "Father of the problem is the mast cell." Control mast-cell triggers and a lot of the other symptoms calm down.
- Notes a striking **paediatric MCAS** signal: 12–14-year-olds reacting to perfumes, dyes, food, sound, lights — parents reporting the child was never reactive before.

## EDS — what to look for

- Painful joints, repeat dislocations (kneecap can buckle 7-10× per day in severe cases), scoliosis, uterine prolapse, stretchy skin, chronic GI issues, MCAS, fibromyalgia-pattern pain, migraines, sleep apnoea, dental crowding/gum mobility, **CCI (cranio-cervical instability)** at C1/C2.
- Often presents at **puberty** (12-14) or with menstrual cycles.
- Beyond joint hypermobility: structural hypermobility of internal tissues (gut, uterus, blood vessels) — not just on the Beighton score.

## Treatment / lifestyle scaffold (her four pillars)

1. **Connective tissue care** — hydration, electrolytes, collagen-rich foods, avoid hyperextension postures. Same logic for LC patients without formal EDS dx.
2. **Lifestyle / circadian rhythm** — fixed sleep and meal times; **digital detox after 7 pm** (EMF + blue-light hits mast cells); plants/greenery in the home.
3. **Trigger control** — food triggers (high-histamine, processed, food dyes), environmental triggers (perfume, chemical-laden clothing on others, EMF), interpersonal stress triggers. Personalised trigger map, not generic histamine list.
4. **Structure / posture** — neck-on-spine alignment as the fulcrum; broad-base footwear; ergonomic desk/computer setup; compression garments; avoid hyperextension and cervical-forward postures.

## In-the-moment crash protocol

When a flare is starting (joint about to dislocate, mast-cell escalation, sympathetic surge):

1. **Sit or lie down** — anywhere, even mid-flight, mid-mall. Don't try to push through.
2. **Belly breathing, eyes on belly button** — abdominal breathing while imagining the unstable joint settling back to centre. Stops sympathetic-overdrive cascade. Joint relocation often happens spontaneously.
3. **Hydration + electrolytes** — connective tissue needs water. Dehydration is the enemy of EDS *and* POTS — they share this lever.
4. **Small protein snack** — never go hungry; hypoglycaemia is its own crash. Carry a tiffen box.
5. **Don't fear the dislocation** — fear itself drives the next sympathetic surge that drives the next dislocation. EDS patients learn to relocate joints themselves.

## Exercise framing — "find your bar"

- Exercise releases histamine — too much will trigger MCAS → PEM → connective-tissue instability → cascade.
- Goal: **never overdo**. Each person's safe-zone bar is different.
- Don't follow generic "get back to running" advice — that's the strongest known catalyst for crash in this cohort.
- Mobility, gentle yoga, breath-led movement before any cardio.

## On hormonal cycles

- Hypermobile women report **POTS spike + joint instability spike + MCAS flare** during periods every cycle.
- Real signal that LC + hormones + connective tissue are interlocked — supports the wiki's [[hormonal-dysfunction]] theme already populated by [[026-jackie-maybin-menstrual-health]] and [[045-keith-littlewood-hormones-thyroid]].
