# #74 — Walter Koroshetz on the NIH RECOVER Initiative

Director of the **National Institute of Neurological Disorders and Stroke (NINDS)**. Pre-pandemic worked on ME/CFS research with Avindra Nath. Now leads the NIH's $1.15B Congressional appropriation for **RECOVER** (Researching COVID to Enhance Recovery).

## Scope

- $1.15 billion Congressional appropriation
- 14,000+ participants enrolled (and growing)
- "Designed to be the backstop should there not be a quick answer"

## Cohorts

| Cohort | Size | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Acute** (newly infected → followed for years) | 3,635 | Compare who develops LC vs not, prospectively |
| **Long covid** (already affected, vs recovered controls) | 6,488 | Cross-section of established LC |
| **Unaffected** | 1,000 | Healthy controls |
| **Asymptomatic post-covid** | 945 | Different reference point |

Plus a separate **electronic health record cohort of ~60M individuals** in the US (run by a partnered group), and an **autopsy study** for direct organ analysis. Designed to follow people for decades — to capture late effects (Alzheimer's, cancer-rate changes).

## Tier 1 / 2 / 3 testing

- Tier 1: broad netcasting — all enrolees get baseline panel
- Tier 2: more intensive testing if specific symptom cluster emerges
- Tier 3: deepest-dive testing for tier-2 abnormalities

## Four leading mechanism hypotheses (RECOVER's priority list)

1. **Viral persistence** (live, dormant, or particles in tissue continuing to stimulate immune)
2. **Autoimmunity** (immune response overflows → antibodies against self-antigens)
3. **Immune dysregulation** (immune system "revved up" and not resetting)
4. **Latent virus reactivation** (EBV, etc. — common after any infection, can flare with covid)

> "Different mechanisms could be operating in different patients."

## Treatment trials approach (two-pronged)

| Strategy | Examples |
|---|---|
| **Disease-modifying** | Antivirals first (testing whether residual virus is driving symptoms); other mechanism-targeted agents |
| **Symptomatic master protocols** | Sleep, exercise/PEM, autonomic, cognition — each a master protocol allowing parallel/sequential drug testing |

> "One is what we call disease-modifying treatments to get at the underlying biology. The other is symptomatic — improving the sleep disorder, the POTS, the cognitive trouble, the exercise intolerance."

## Avindra Nath's parallel work

- Clinical Director of NINDS intramural program (NIH's own research hospital — largest in the world).
- Originally designed an intensive small-N protocol for ME/CFS; publishing soon.
- Then **applied the same protocol to LC** (and to **Gulf War syndrome**, with the VA) — a tri-condition comparison study.
- Importance: small-N + deep phenotyping in parallel with RECOVER's broad cohorts.

## Key quotes

> "We thought this would be an easy problem. People all over the world studying it; someone will come up with the answer quickly. If it's not an easy problem, RECOVER is the backstop."

> "**About 70 different symptoms occur in clusters** — nobody has just one. We've found an autonomic cluster, a cognitive cluster, an exercise/breathing cluster, plus sleep variations."

> "The symptoms in LC are very common occurring without covid. Don't blame *everything* on long covid before you've ruled out hypothyroidism, etc. — many of those things can be treated."

> "**Less frequent after Omicron than Delta. Less frequent if vaccinated. More frequent in severe acute disease and in racial/ethnic minorities.** Pretty consistent across variants in symptom flavour. Pretty rare in children."

## Caution on "graded exercise" framing

Koroshetz directly addresses ME/CFS community concerns: RECOVER's exercise protocols **build PEM monitoring into the design** — if you develop signs of PEM you don't progress to the next step. Pulmonary support also part of the protocol for those with persistent breathing issues.

## Eric-relevant intake

- The RECOVER mechanism shortlist is **the single most authoritative consolidation of "what we don't yet know"** in the wiki — useful for grounding any single mechanism (microclots, brainstem, MCAS) as one possibility among several.
- 70-symptom-cluster framing matches Eric's experience.
- The **"don't blame everything on long covid"** advice is specifically Eric-relevant: thyroid, vitamin D, etc. should remain checked.
