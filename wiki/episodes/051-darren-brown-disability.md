# #51 — Darren Brown discusses Disability

Short follow-up segment with [[darren-brown]] (HIV/cancer physiotherapist, Long Covid Physio co-founder). A focused conceptual primer on **disability models** and what "rehab" means for an episodic illness. Distilled for wiki use.

## Key takeaway

Disability is not a body-part impairment (biomedical model) and not just an environmental barrier (social model) — for fluctuating illnesses like LC, the right frame is **episodic disability**.

## Disability frameworks — historical arc

1. **Biomedical model** (classical, oppressive when used alone): disability = an impairment of a body part or function (e.g. amputation). Ignores environment.
2. **Social model** (1970s–1990s): the *environment* disables the person, not the body. Powerful for rights advocacy but misses lived physical impairments.
3. **WHO ICF** (International Classification of Functioning, Disability and Health, 1990s+): disability is the *interaction* between health condition, body impairments, activity limitations, social participation, and environment. Most widely used today; still imperfect.
4. **Episodic Disability Framework** ([[kelly-o-brien]], University of Toronto, originating in HIV research): adds the dimensions of **fluctuation, unpredictability, uncertainty, worry about future health**. Disability waxes and wanes; a person may not identify as disabled on good days and clearly is on bad days.

> "It fluctuates and changes... an interaction between a health condition, any impairments, any limitations to activities, difficulties in social and community participation, and the environment."

## Why episodic-disability matters for LC

- LC, ME/CFS, HIV, cancer, chronic low-back pain — all relapsing-remitting. The classic "can you walk 100 metres?" benefits-test snapshot **systematically misclassifies** people whose disability is real but episodic.
- Brown uses the **Episodic Disability Questionnaire** in his clinical work to support patients' benefits assessments — has found it instrumental in getting people the support they're entitled to.
- A Canadian Institute of Health Research-funded study is currently running (40 qualitative interviews across UK / Ireland / Canada / US) to test whether the framework fits LC and to adapt the questionnaire.

## Rehabilitation — important re-framing

Brown is explicit: **rehab is not synonymous with exercise**. After ME/CFS-era harm from GET, "rehab" became a dirty word; he wants it reclaimed:

- Education, skills training, equipment (e.g. shower stool for orthostatic intolerance), home adaptations, modifications
- **Pacing** is a rehab intervention
- Symptom monitoring to prevent relapses and flareups
- Person-centred, individualised, risk-stratified — explicitly **not** a one-page exercise prescription
- **PEM/PESE must be excluded before any consideration of physical activity**

> "Safe rehabilitation does not mean giving exercise to everybody."
