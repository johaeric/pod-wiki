# #024 — Alec Finlay — "I Remember"

**Guest**: **Alec Finlay** — Edinburgh-based poet & artist. **Has had ME for ~30 years** (since age 21) and developed long covid in the pre-lockdown wave of March 2020 — third year at recording. Commissioned to design **Scotland's national Covid Memorial** in Pollok Country Park, Glasgow. Walks ~150 m max — relies on supported delivery & friends; does the work of the memorial collaboratively.

This episode is **not about treatments**. It's about the politics of post-viral illness and patient solidarity. No intervention or program evidence-log entries.

## The memorial

- Pollok Country Park, Glasgow — old wooded estate. Multiple groves rather than a single artwork.
- **Tree supports**: simple oak posts shaped vaguely like human figures, each "supporting" or being supported by a particular tree, modelled on photos of real people in poses of physical support. Hannah Devereux is the photographer.
- All inscribed *I remember*. QR codes link to audio of memories, read by **Robert Carlyle** from a Vancouver studio (broke down repeatedly during recording).
- The "I remember" form (after American artist Joe Brainard) is a single sentence. Hundreds of memories submitted: bereaved, recovered, still-ill, angry, funny.
- Memories painted on A4 paper, scanned to National Library of Scotland for permanent archive, printed in a book (May 2022), buried in wooden boxes at the site.
- **Multiple languages**: English, Polish, Romani, Urdu, Japanese, Chinese, French.
- Wildflower planting — seeds from the park itself.
- **23 March 2022** — first national day of remembrance; minute's silence at noon.
- Formal opening **27 May 2022**.
- Dream of satellite memorials Scotland-wide pending funding.

## The argument

Two threads run through the episode:

**1. Pain-as-empathy across identity (Elaine Scarry).** Pain in someone else's body can only be *believed in* — and we believe through our own experience of pain. He pushes against the "competitive view of pain" he sees in current culture: an "I remember" form is open to all, including humour, including the "well-meaning" who never got ill — every memory has validity.

**2. ME ↔ LC parallel.** Decades of ME being neglected directly shaped how unprepared the medical system was for LC. He frames the LC community as inheriting an existing scandal:

> "If we'd spent 30 years researching ME, we may have medicine for long covid now." — Alec

> "There's almost a deliberate distortion of what the illness was. To live that medical scandal twice — all I can do is say this is what it was like." — Alec

He also describes a moving moment with a bereaved family member who "broke the rule": couldn't pose with a tree, found a broken stump, said "that's me, I'm broken." A support post will be made by that stump.
