Dan Neuffer (founder of [[ans-rewire]], author of *CFS Unraveled*) returns for the first of a two-part conversation with Jackie on the role of **belief** in recovery. Continued in #198.

## Why belief matters

- Belief shapes behaviour, and recovery is built on consistent behaviour
- Without belief in a treatment, you won't follow through long enough to see effects
- Belief also shapes what you *notice* — the reticular activating system filters reality through belief
- Even hope-based belief improves quality of life independent of physical recovery

## Acceptance vs resignation

- **Resignation**: "this is as good as it gets, I'll learn to live with it"
- **Acceptance**: "I am ill *for now*, and that gives me space to act"
- The "for-now" qualifier is everything. The medical model often pushes resignation in disguise: "you have an incurable illness, the healthy response is to accept it." That's logical only if the premise (incurable) is true — and recovery stories show it isn't.
- Bias warning: humans build evidence to support pre-existing beliefs. If your belief is "I won't get better," you'll find evidence everywhere.

## Three layers of evidence

1. **Rational** — understand the science: ANS dysfunction, neuroplasticity, why recovery is biologically possible
2. **Social proof** — recovery stories. One isn't enough. Hundreds change the calculation.
3. **Personal proof** — your own progress. Even tiny gains. Track them or you'll forget.

## Notes for Eric

A useful pairing with #198 (the practical "how to build belief" half). Dan's framing of resignation-vs-acceptance is sharper than most — worth holding when the wider chronic-illness culture nudges patients toward "make peace with it" before they've exhausted options. The reticular-activating-system framing is a clean answer to "why optimism matters even when nothing has changed yet" without veering into magical thinking.

## Key quote

> "If your belief is that this illness is incurable, then resignation is the healthy response. But beliefs should be based on evidence — and the evidence shows people recover."
> — Dan Neuffer, ep #197
