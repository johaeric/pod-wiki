# #008 — Rachel Whitfield's recovery

**Guest**: [[rachel-whitfield]] — leadership trainer (NHS-adjacent), single mum, runner/cyclist; NLP training. Got covid December 2020 from her daughter; **fully recovered May 31, 2021** after the [[lightning-process]] training in early May.

This is the **first explicit Lightning-Process recovery story** on the podcast and the first full recovery arc indexed. Take seriously but read with the standard caveats: N=1, brain-retraining frame, observer-expectancy strong.

## Recovery status

Recovered (since 31 May 2021). Now does running, cycling, parenting, work; cautious about returning to her pre-covid over-commitment pattern.

## Symptom picture

- Atypical acute (Dec 2020): brain fog and a striking **dread-anxiety** ahead of fatigue. No fever-cough-smell loss.
- Felt better, returned to running and work, **crashed ~10 days later** ("hit a wall").
- Boom-bust pattern flattened to "just bust" over weeks.
- New symptoms appeared and migrated: pins-and-needles, burning back, throat, ribs, grey-skinned, swollen purple toes.
- **Insomnia** as worst single symptom — pressure-about-sleep made it worse; eventually broken with mirtazapine + giving up the fight.

## Theoretical framing she adopted

> "It's autonomic. The body went into fight-flight to fight the virus and never switched off — because my stress bucket was already full."

Sources she draws on:
- **Kyle Davies — *The Intelligent Body*** (autonomic dysfunction model)
- **James Nestor — *Breath*** (PTSD-pattern dysfunctional breathing maps to LC symptoms)
- **Nicole Sachs — *Cure for Chronic Pain*** podcast (TMS / mind-body)
- **Alex Howard — Optimal Health Clinic** (maladaptive stress response; free 3-hour video series) — see [[optimal-health-clinic]]
- **Anthony Greenfield — *Five Forces of Change*** (routine + purpose + connection in chronic illness)
- **Dr Boon Lim** ([[boon-lim]]) interviews

## What helped — her own ranking

### Lightning Process — pivotal (key_to_recovery)

3-day Zoom training (week of 9 May 2021) + ongoing 1:1 coaching. Key reframe: **"you have enough energy; your bucket is leaking — because the fear-of-overdoing keeps firing fight-flight"**. She walked an unintended extra 0.1 mile and started dipping; the coach reframed that as the leaking-bucket pattern, and the dip resolved.

> "I'd become a genius at anxiety, a genius at worry, a genius at being sensitive."
> — Rachel reframing what the LP coach pointed out, ep #8

Got HR to 150 on a bike, dread came 36 hours later, knew what it was, walked through it without crashing — that was the threshold-crossing moment.

### Stress reduction (cancelling work for 6 weeks)

Cancelled all work at the bottom of her crash. Felt better immediately. Realised it was the *anticipatory* stress driving symptoms, not the work itself.

### Outsourcing everything

School run, cleaning, cooking — let people help. "Asking for help was incredibly humbling."

### Breathwork

Saw a breathing physio remotely; was a mouth-breather without realising. Learned diaphragmatic / nasal breathing; uses an app daily; uses it as a flare-rescue technique.

### Yoga + meditation (Yoga Nidra)

Joined a recovery yoga class run by a recovered long-hauler. Continues weekly. Yoga Nidra named as the only thing that switches her brain off.

### Acceptance

Borrowed Suzy-Bolt-style framing (and named it independently): once she could stop fighting, recovery began.

## What didn't help

- **Restrictive elimination diets** — tried low-histamine briefly; got confused; warns explicitly against extended elimination protocols. Stuck with rainbow diet + hydration.
- **Vitamin supplements** — took everything anyone mentioned ("rattled"); now takes only D, B12, probiotic, magnesium; doesn't think the others did anything.

## Notable quotes

> "It wasn't even boom-and-bust — it was just bust."

> "Good days are the most dangerous days... I had to learn the energy narrative wasn't the right one anymore. I had enough energy. I had a leaking bucket."

> "Fear of the relapse can create the relapse."

> "Listen to your partner. Mine knew it was stress before I did."
