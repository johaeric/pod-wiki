# #46 — Gez Medinger — Patient Advocate, Researcher & Author

The most prominent **patient-advocate-with-research-platform** voice on the podcast so far. Medinger is a London-based filmmaker who caught COVID March 2020 (untested) and never recovered. He had previously been ill for a year after glandular fever in 2000, which gave him a working "this might be the same again" frame faster than most. He now runs an ~80-film LC YouTube channel interviewing experts, and is co-authoring **The Long Covid Handbook** with Prof Danny Altmann (Imperial immunologist), to be published by Penguin (~late 2022).

This episode is a state-of-the-personal-journey check-in plus high-signal practical advice from someone who has interviewed dozens of LC clinicians and researchers. Episode is high-density — the wiki should treat it as a meta-summary of much of what other guests have said.

## Recovery status

**Not recovered.** Improving but still significantly limited. MCAS-dominant presentation in 2020; less so now but still present. Dysautonomia without classic POTS. Cognitive function specifically vulnerable to listening / Zoom / phone calls (more than to TV or driving). Crashes after high-effort work like the manuscript revisions described in the episode.

## What helped (Medinger's actually-tried list)

- **Antihistamines + low-histamine diet** — *the* breakthrough, after speaking with [[tina-peers]] (#38). Summer 2020. "Absolutely critical to managing my symptoms." Still a daily anchor.
- **Dysautonomia management** — pacing, salt, fluids, breathwork. The other major lever.
- **NAD⁺ / niacin (vitamin B3)** — via Dr Bindi Venel (South Africa). Reports a sustained ~20% boost to his energy envelope + cognitive clarity. **Maintenance** — if he stops for 2–3 days, the envelope shrinks back. Doesn't claim recovery.
- **Probiotics** — "the probiotics do help" — he doesn't expand.
- **Vitamin C + D** — takes them; doesn't claim observable LC-specific benefit.

He explicitly distinguishes his pattern from the "every supplement cures someone" pattern: **LC exposes random pre-existing deficiencies; throwing supplements at it is "pinning the tail on the donkey blindfold."** Convergent with [[joachim-gerlach]] #41 and [[keith-littlewood]] #45 [[polypharmacy-warning]].

## What harmed

- **Tilt test (with [[boon-lim]])** — crashed him for **6 weeks**. He notes the diagnostic information was already known from a basic sit-to-stand. The video he produced afterward was useful, but the personal cost was high. Worth flagging as a `harmed` row for the diagnostic procedure — sometimes the test itself is exertion-equivalent for severe disautonomia.

## The 4-category framework

Medinger's working model for splitting LC symptoms (echoes other guests' frames):

1. **Metabolic** — mitochondrial / bioenergetic
2. **Mast cell** — MCAS, histamine, allergy-style
3. **Dysautonomic** — autonomic nervous system, POTS-spectrum
4. **Vascular** — clotty blood, poor tissue oxygenation
5. **Organ impairment** (separate overlay) — actual lung / cardiac damage
6. **PEM overlay** on all of the above

He says learning to *separate* his own symptoms into these categories was a key inflection — once you can tell "this is mast cell" vs "this is autonomic" vs "this is PEM-overlay," you can act differently.

## The 50% rule

His recurring practical-pacing rule for good days:

> "Aim to do 50% of what you think you ought to be able to do. If you get away with that, try 60% next time. If you don't, you won't have blown yourself up the way you would on 100%." — Medinger, ep #46

Convergent with all the pacing guests — but the framing as a probabilistic safety margin is useful.

## On the long-term prognosis

Cites a paper by John Patel/Pailoor (he didn't name it cleanly on-air; described as published via Oxford University Press) on a Canadian SARS-1 cohort of ~50 originally-ICU-severe patients still ill **19 years later**. Argues this means LC recovery is not a given — and that we should not assume time alone will fix this for everyone. **The implication: if you've been ill 2+ years without improvement and you don't change anything, expecting random improvement at year 3 is unlikely.** This is the harshest practical claim of the episode.

## On gaslighting (self and external)

> "Long Covid is the ultimate head wreck. It takes what you think you know about yourself and strips it all away so you can't trust your own faculties anymore." — Medinger, ep #46

Two specific patterns:

- **Self-gaslighting** — questioning whether the bad day is "in my head" because the good day let you function.
- **External gaslighting** — the trolling that comes with public LC advocacy (the "James Blunt strategy" of staying classy). Specifically references friction with a faction of the ME/CFS community over a film he made with [[sally-riggs]] on physiological-stress / autonomic / mind-body framings. Medinger's position: this debate is real but "everyone draws the line slightly differently" and the rhetoric can itself trigger autonomic flare.

## On invisibility

> "People only see you when you're burning your nous to keep up with normal life. They don't see you crash into the barriers as soon as they walk away." — Medinger, ep #46

Important angle the wiki should keep — even other LC patients fall into the "they look fine therefore they're not as ill" trap.

## Eric-relevant signal

- **NAD+/niacin** as a maintenance "20% energy envelope" intervention is worth investigating. Eric is not currently on B3; baseline blood B-vitamin panel may be useful. Cross-ref ercipedia for evidence weight before trial.
- **The 50% rule** is a clean, ready-to-use heuristic.
- **The 4-category framework** is a useful mental sorting tool — most of Eric's symptoms cluster into autonomic + MCAS-overlay + occasional PEM. Recognising which is which on any given day is worth practising.
- **Tilt test as `harmed`** is a personally useful cautionary note: Eric has confirmed POTS via tilt; a *repeat* tilt test would carry the same crash risk for diagnostic information he already has.
