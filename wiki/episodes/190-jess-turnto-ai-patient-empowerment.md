Jess (return guest, see ep #140) — founder of **Turn2**. Former ME/CFS sufferer, mother of twins with complex medical needs. Discusses AI's role in patient research and empowerment.

## Frame: tech has agendas

- **Facebook/Instagram/TikTok** — agenda is watch-time / dopamine retention
- **Google search** — time-to-click; advertiser revenue
- **YouTube** — watch-time
- **AI chatbots (current)** — relatively *unmonetised* right now, so optimisation is closer to "give the best answer." This will change.

## Practical use of AI for patient research

- Use the **big four foundation models**: ChatGPT (OpenAI), Claude (Anthropic), Gemini (Google) — and reasoning variants where available
- **Always ask for referenced answers** — "answer with evidence and sources"
- Be aware of **confirmation bias** — models people-please. Push back: "no, I'm actually not interested in that, give me X instead."
- *Patient experience data is undervalued by formal medicine* — RCTs intentionally exclude messy real-world variables
- For sensitive material: turn off training-on-data in settings, redact identifiers from blood reports etc.
- Anecdote: AI flagged her son's mouth lump as "likely abscess, see dentist within 3 hours" — turned out to need emergency dental surgery.

## Turn2 — new product

- AI personal-health-sidekick that reads ~1M words/week across LC content (research, expert podcasts, patient discussions) and surfaces the 12 things relevant to *you*
- Deep dives return parsed view: 10 things patients say + 10 things experts say + 10 recent papers, all referenced
- Costs ~$2/week (less than they spend on AI credits); scholarships available
- Mobile app available; community-tailored daily updates

## Key quote

> "We don't believe we should give vanilla answers to health questions. There's not one answer — there are studies, patient experiences, expert insights. If we make the answer generic we lose all the human stories."
> — Jess, ep #190
