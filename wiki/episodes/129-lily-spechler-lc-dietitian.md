# Episode 129 — Lily Spechler — The Long Covid Dietitian

Lily Spechler is a US-registered dietitian (RD) who recovered from her own LC and now specialises in LC nutrition. Co-launched the **Energy Expansion Project** with Dr Meg Anderson (PT) — autonomic-rehab + nutrition group program based on a modified Levine/CHOP protocol.

**Her central, repeated claim: malnutrition is the most common pattern she sees in LC.**
- LC is energetically expensive at rest. Laboured breathing alone burns ~800 extra kcal/day. Tachycardia, chronic inflammation, orthostatic intolerance all add baseline energy cost.
- Patients eat less because they're moving less → enter energy deficit.
- *Weight gain* can itself be a sign of malnutrition: body strips muscle (metabolically expensive) and stores fat (energy reserve under perceived starvation). Muscle loss → reduced cerebral perfusion, worse glucose handling, fewer mitochondria.
- Personally added 1,000 kcal/day to her own diet (after researching COPD nutrition) — became a recovery turning point.

**Concerns about restrictive diets in LC:**
- *Low-histamine* — only addresses one mediator (histamine), ignores prostaglandins, leukotrienes, cytokines. "Cutting down the trees doesn't ask why the forest is flammable."
- *Carnivore / keto* — risks malnutrition, eliminates fibre. Fibre matters because:
  1. Short-chain fatty acids (butyrate, acetate, propionate) modulate mast cells; SCFA inversely correlates with circulating histamine.
  2. Without fibre, gut bacteria feed on the gut lining → permeability → systemic inflammation.
  3. Mucosal layer is the primary site of DAO production (the enzyme that breaks histamine down).
  4. Fibre supports estrogen excretion. Estrogen and histamine compete at the same receptor — partly why women dominate LC.
- *Zero-carb diets* — carb intake triggers insulin → triggers tryptophan uptake into brain → serotonin → melatonin. LC patients are often low in both serotonin and cortisol. Stripping carbs worsens that.

**What she suggests for almost everyone:**
1. *Track on Chronometer* — free, shows micronutrient gaps not just macros.
2. *Match metabolic needs* — use disease-state adjustments, not just body size.
3. *Stable blood sugar* — pair carbs with fat/protein; avoid refined-carb spikes ("don't make the pancreas do the airport-and-dry-cleaning-and-10-page-paper-in-one-day").
4. *Eat by the clock if appetite cues are gone* — dysautonomia can suppress hunger signals; metabolic needs don't pause.
5. *Electrolytes are not blanket advice.* Pooling-pattern POTS may benefit from sodium loading. Vasoconstriction-pattern (e.g. ACE2-receptor damage → angiotensin-II elevation) gets worse with sodium — vomiting, intracranial-pressure feeling.

**Mass-cell framing:** address the environment, not just the symptom. Build SCFAs, protect gut barrier, support DAO production, stabilise blood sugar, meet metabolic needs.

**Pacing ≠ inactivity.** Strong stance: deconditioning is itself a risk; once stable, gradual movement is essential.

> "The number-one trend I've seen with long-haulers is malnutrition. Don't out-human yourself."
> — Lily Spechler, ep #129
