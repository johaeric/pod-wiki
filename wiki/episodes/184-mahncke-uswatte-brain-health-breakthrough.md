Henry Mahncke (neuroscientist, CEO of Posit Science / Brain HQ) and Gitendra "G" Uswatte (rehab psychologist, UAB) discuss their **CI Cognitive Therapy** clinical trial for Long COVID brain fog.

## The intervention — three components

1. **Brain HQ "double decision"** — software-based cognitive processing speed training (bottom-up)
2. **In-clinic everyday-task training** — calendar setup, online shopping, emails, grocery lists, organised via the psychology technique of *shaping*
3. **Transfer package** — goal-setting, self-monitoring, problem-solving to bridge clinic-gain → real-life gain

Done outpatient, 30 hrs over 2–4 weeks.

## Trial results (small RCT, n=7+7, Birmingham AL)

Compared to usual care (essentially nothing in their region):
- **80% of those for whom returning to work was relevant did so** in the CI Cognitive Therapy arm; 0% in usual care
- Large improvements in everyday activities, fatigue, brain fog, depressive symptoms

A second confirmatory study is ongoing. They are also pairing with **vagus nerve stimulation** in newer work — hypothesis is VNS reduces chronic neuroinflammation including in the brain.

## Mechanism framing

- LC brain fog ≈ slowed cognitive processing speed, similar to "chemobrain" and post-stroke patterns
- LC damage to brain tissue is *diffuse* (capillary/microvascular), not focal like a stroke — so rehab strategy is to recruit healthy tissue via plasticity, not "heal damaged tissue"
- Brain HQ improves underlying signal quality; the everyday-task training scaffolds gains into real-world use; the transfer package makes it meaningful

Brain HQ is available at brainhq.com (14 languages, free daily exercise; subscription for full). The other two components currently require in-person at UAB or local cognitive rehab clinicians; telehealth version under development. Phone: 205-934-9768.
