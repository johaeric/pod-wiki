# #013 — AJ — "Finding My Sparkle"

**Guest**: AJ — friend of [[jackie-baxter]] from a hill-walking community in Forfar, Scotland. Sustained a brain injury in March 2018 sledging during the "Beast from the East" snowstorm — diagnosed with **post-concussion syndrome (PCS)**. Three-and-a-half years post-injury at recording. Founder of [findingmysparkle.co.uk](https://findingmysparkle.co.uk) and active on Facebook/Instagram raising PCS awareness.

**This is not a long-covid story.** AJ does not have long covid. The episode is included for **cross-condition wisdom** — chronic-illness adaptation lessons that map directly across to the long-covid presentation: sensory hypersensitivity, fatigue, cognitive limitation, unpredictability, partner-relationship strain, identity loss, the grieving of the old self. Treat outcomes here as parallel evidence — not classified into the long-covid intervention buckets, but real signal to read across.

## Why she's on the long-covid podcast

The symptom overlap is striking:
- **Sensory hypersensitivity** — bright light, sudden noise, unexpected touch trigger involuntary "pinging" limb movements (a movement disorder triggered by the brain injury)
- **Fatigue** that is non-physical and resists "push through" attempts
- **Cognitive load = real load** (screens drain like physical activity)
- **Unpredictability** — good day / bad day with no clear cause
- **Identity loss** — type-A, motivated, fit person becomes someone unable to do daily tasks
- **Partner-relationship strain** — the dignity-loss of needing help dressing, eating, navigating space

Jackie picks this up explicitly: "I'd never thought reading a book could leave me tired. AJ told me, three years ago, that it could."

## The "Happy List"

The single most exportable concept from this episode. A friend asked AJ early-on, "What are you doing every day that makes you happy?" — and she realised she had no answer. So she made a list and stuck it on her fridge. Items are *small*: hot chocolate in a favourite mug, lavender candle, Sunday cuppies with family, sitting in a particular chair with a blanket looking out at greenery.

> "On a really bad day you might not remember what makes you happy. The list is on the fridge so you don't have to remember." — AJ, ep #13

Jackie notes she has since recommended this idea to others.

## Practical tips (cross-applicable to long covid)

### What helped — pain
- **Paracetamol + ibuprofen rotation** (max daily dose). Reluctant to take long-term meds, but found over-the-counter alternation insufficient and eventually accepted regular pain meds. (Cross-ref: long-covid pain protocols vary.)
- **TENS machine** — distracting low-grade stimulation that displaces the worse pain while in use. Helps in the moment; pain returns when off.
- **Acupressure mat** — paradoxical: painful at first, then warmth and pain-relief settle in.

### What helped — sleep / nervous system
- **Restorative yoga + yoga nidra** (online, post-lockdown). Yoga nidra reliably puts her into deep sleep. Combined with weighted blanket + eye mask.
- **Flotation tank** — sensory-deprivation. Best sleep without medication; she'd weekly-subscribe if there were one nearby. Restricted by travel cost.
- **Meditation app — Insight Timer** (Bethany Auriel-Hagan as preferred teacher).

### What didn't help
- **Trying to stick to a rigid daily schedule** — set herself up for failure when she couldn't keep it. Replaced with a flexible weekly task list, no day-assignments.
- **Pushing through.** "I tried to hack my way through my brain-injury recovery. The wrong personality for it."

### Pacing wisdom
- **Rest before you need to.** Once the bad day arrives, you've already overshot.
- **Screen time = active brain time.** Lying in bed scrolling Instagram drains as much as a task. "Anything to do with screens really can take a lot out of you and you might not realise that at the time."

## On partners (Jackie has rare overlap question)

Jackie asks AJ about partner-guilt — feeling bad that JP's life has been changed. AJ's framing:
- The patient often pushes the partner away ("you didn't sign up for this, you can leave"). The partner stays anyway. This needs many open conversations.
- "I'm still valuable as a human being. We're equal. He's here because he loves me, not from loyalty."
- *Find new things you can do together.* JP bought a kayak; new shared adventures replaced lost ones (hill-walking).

## On the medical journey

- 3 months of being signed off two weeks at a time with no help — "every time I thought *that's the date I'll be better*."
- Then physio + OT (5 months in). Bath board, perching stool. Walking aid (still uses crutches).
- Severe sensory hypersensitivity episode landed her in hospital; 60-week wait for neurology.
- Postcode lottery for chronic-illness rehab access — explicitly named.
