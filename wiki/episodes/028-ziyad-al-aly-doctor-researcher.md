# #028 — Ziyad Al-Aly — Doctor & Researcher

**Guest**: **Dr Ziyad Al-Aly** — nephrologist + clinical epidemiologist (USA, St Louis VA system). Big-data researcher whose **2021 Nature paper** ("High-dimensional characterization of post-acute sequelae of COVID-19") was among the **first formal evidence pieces** on long covid, drawn from millions of US veteran records. One of the most-cited researchers on LC.

This is a *systems-level* episode. No new intervention rows. Updates several themes.

## Key claims (well-evidenced from his big-data work + emerging autopsy data)

**1. Long covid is multifaceted / multi-organ, not respiratory.**
> "Some people present as fatigue + brain fog. Some as blood clots, stroke. Some as new-onset diabetes — kids with no family history. We need to reframe COVID as a *systemic* infection, not a respiratory one." — Al-Aly

**2. It can affect anyone.** Children → 90-year-olds; athletes; men; women; all ethnic/socio groups. He pushes back hard on "more common in women" generalisations because it depends *which* sequela you're looking at:
- Brain fog / neurocognitive decline → more in elderly
- Skin / hair issues → more in younger adults
- New-onset diabetes → seen even in 9–10 year-olds with no risk factors

**3. Viral persistence is real.** Refers to a 44-person autopsy preprint (NIH) finding SARS-CoV-2 in brain, lung, kidney, liver tissue **months after** the initial infection. Mechanism candidate for the multi-organ sequelae.

**4. Vaccination reduces but does not eliminate LC risk.** His own preprint (n≈33,000 breakthrough infections) — risk **slightly lower** than unvaccinated infection, **not dramatically**. Israeli study (smaller) had been more optimistic; Al-Aly thinks his bigger dataset is closer to truth. Vaccines are *one* protective layer; masks + ventilation still matter.

**5. Omicron is not "mild" in LC terms.** Even if per-case severity is lower, Omicron infects vastly more people → far larger absolute LC burden. Cautions explicitly against the "let it rip" narrative.

**6. Reinfection compounds.** Hard to study cleanly because LC fluctuates; people who get COVID again may simply reactivate existing LC vs add a fresh layer. But repeated infections fare worse than single infections.

## On the medical-system response

Compares LC patient advocacy to **Larry Kramer-era HIV activism** — credits patient movements as the reason LC research happened at all:

> "These are the Larry Kramers of long covid. Without the patient advocacy movement, we wouldn't be having this conversation." — Al-Aly

Sharp critique: governments fixate on the "tip of the iceberg" (acute cases / hospitalisations / 30-day deaths), ignoring the much larger long-tail post-viral burden. Calls for a Manhattan-Project-scale long-covid effort, not lip-service.

ME/CFS lesson — neglected for decades; the cost is now visible in how unprepared the system was for post-viral LC.

## Quotes

> "Most policymakers focus on the tip of the iceberg. The aftershock will last for generations." — Al-Aly

> "We had 100 years to study post-viral illness and we ignored it. Let's not waste this crisis." — Al-Aly

## How to read this for Eric

The single most important *epidemiology* episode in the wiki so far. Reinforces:
- Viral persistence is a credible mechanism (worth tracking the antiviral RCT pipeline in ercipedia)
- Reinfection avoidance still matters even for vaccinated people with existing LC
- Eric's POTS+PEM picture sits inside a much bigger multi-organ landscape — clinically he's well-defined, but the underlying biology is shared with many other LC presentations
