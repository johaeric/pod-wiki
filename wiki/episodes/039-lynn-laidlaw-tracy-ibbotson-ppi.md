# #039 — Lynn Laidlaw & Tracy Ibbotson — PPI & Research Involvement

**Guests**:
- **Lynn Laidlaw** — Central Scotland; lives with autoimmune rheumatic disease + inflammatory arthritis overlap. **4-year diagnostic odyssey** before getting biologic-level treatment. Patient researcher; led a co-produced shielding study (Grant-funded). Forced retirement at 50 — the involvement world rebuilt her professional identity.
- **Tracy Ibbotson** — PPI Lead, University of Glasgow Medical School. ~8 years in PPI, came in via frustration with patient information sheets. Runs PPI groups for the medical school including the **Glasgow LC PPI group**.

The wiki's **anchor episode for [[patient-and-public-involvement]]**. Not a treatment episode; a research-infrastructure episode. Pair with Faghy #36 (Derby) for the practical-application companion.

## What PPI is, distilled

**PPI** = doing research **with** people who have lived experience, not for them or to them. Not the same as participating in research (taking the drug, filling the questionnaire). PPI shapes the research itself: priorities, design, outcomes that matter, recruitment, dissemination.

> "We're limited by our imagination. Patients can be involved at any point in the research process — and should be involved in all parts." — Laidlaw

> "It's a way of working, not a method. Co-production is values-driven: power-sharing, equality, trust, inclusivity." — Laidlaw

## Why it matters — concrete examples they cite

- **Asthma charity priority-setting**: raised funds intending to commission cure-research. Patients said: "you're already doing cures; we want quality-of-life research, here and now." The fund was redirected accordingly.
- **HIV/AIDS** drove the modern PPI movement — "no decision about me without me." LC patient advocacy is in that lineage.
- **Long-covid term itself was coined by patients** (Elisa Perego on Twitter, May 2020).
- During pandemic, PPI in research applications dropped from ~70-80% to 20% — exposing how shallow the embedding really was.

## Patient-side perspective — Lynn's odyssey

- **4 years**, ~12 second opinions across UK; told symptoms were "significantly psychological."
- Health-literate, resourced, family-supported, multi-organ-disease pattern that the single-organ specialist system can't handle ("multidisciplinary merry-go-round").
- The phrase "they can't prove it but it's hard to disprove" from her own GP — a microcosm of LC patients' experience.
- Had to **learn not to hope** — "to curate my symptoms because doctors couldn't cope with the list" — the learned strategy of strategic self-presentation. PPI work emerged from "I don't want anyone to go through what I went through."

## Researcher-side perspective — Tracy's evolution

- Started 2015 in primary-care research; first complaint was 25-page patient info packs that nobody reads.
- Now teaches alongside PhD students + masters students + medical students — embedding PPI early, before researchers ossify.
- The "AI = artificial insemination, not artificial intelligence" anecdote — example of jargon that the non-jargon group catches in 30 seconds and the research team hadn't.

## Power, tokenism, and what to avoid

- **Don't bolt PPI on at submission deadline.** Mandates produce tokenism.
- **Don't extract.** Transactional involvement (helicopter in, helicopter out) is harmful.
- **Resource it properly**: pay people, cover travel/Wi-Fi/tech, schedule with breaks for chronic-illness reality. PPI at the researchers' convenience under-represents marginalised communities.
- **Goldilocks problem**: Lynn observes that experienced patient contributors get rejected as "too research-literate; not patient-like enough" — yet inexperienced contributors get rejected as not knowing enough. Both moves preserve power.
- **The only power Lynn says she has is the power to walk away.**

## What the LC research field has done well

- Strong patient advocacy from the start (term coinage, prioritisation).
- LC Scotland is "very engaged" with researchers — Tracy says patients are pushing the agenda hard, including rejecting graded exercise therapy as a research priority.
- Paper-writing models like "patient first author" emerging in some LC papers.

## How to find/get involved (for listeners)

- **People in Research** website — UK projects advertising for public contributors.
- **NIHR / health research authority** networks (England); equivalent network in Scotland.
- Long Covid Support / Long Covid Scotland — local routes.
- Twitter — Lynn's primary channel ("90% of what I'm involved in came from there"), with the caveat that Twitter itself becomes work.

## Quotes

> "No one will care more about research than the people living with diseases. If I didn't think I could help, I wouldn't waste researchers' time — because it's too important." — Laidlaw

> "Get researchers when they're young. Indoctrinate them, and then PPI becomes the normal way to do research, not a bolt-on." — Laidlaw
