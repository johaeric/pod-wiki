# #50 — Chrissi Kelly — AbScent & Parosmia

The wiki's **anchor episode for olfactory dysfunction in Long Covid**. Chrissi Kelly is the founder of [[abscent]] — a UK charity supporting people with smell loss and parosmia. She lost her own sense of smell in 2012, started a Facebook group in 2015, registered as a charity in May 2019. Membership grew from ~1,500 in February 2020 to ~85,000 by mid-2022.

This episode introduces the clinical taxonomy, mechanism, prognosis, and practical management for olfactory dysfunction post-COVID.

## Vocabulary (the clinical layer)

| Term | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Olfaction** | Smell, served by the olfactory nerve in the nose (CN I) |
| **Gustation** ("true taste") | Salty/sweet/sour/bitter/umami — receptors on the tongue, served by gustatory nerves (CN VII, IX, X) |
| **Trigeminal** | Pain/temperature/menthol/chili sensation in mouth+nose — served by CN V |
| **"Taste"** (colloquial) | The blend of all three — properly **flavour** — perceived in the mouth as the swallow pumps a small puff of air retronasally up to the olfactory cleft |
| **Anosmia** | No smell |
| **Hyposmia** | Reduced smell |
| **Parosmia** | Distorted (usually disgusting) smell triggered by an actual odorant — coffee smells like sewage |
| **Phantosmia** | Smell hallucination without a trigger — like olfactory tinnitus |
| **Olfactory perseveration** | Term coined by AbScent trustee [[simon-gane]] — a smell that starts as triggered (parosmia) and persists for hours/days even after the trigger is gone, becoming untriggered |

## Acute COVID effects

Early variants impaired all three nerves — olfactory + gustatory + trigeminal. Hence the YouTube videos of people eating raw chili peppers without flinching, drinking straight vinegar without reacting. **Later variants seem to spare gustation and trigeminal more** and present primarily as olfactory dysfunction.

## Mechanism

The olfactory epithelium has stem cells and is one of very few continuously regenerating neural systems in the body. SARS-CoV-2 appears to:

1. Damage olfactory neurons directly
2. Impede the *regeneration* of new neurons (the more concerning piece)

Parosmia is interpreted as **aberrant regeneration** — the regrowing nerves are making bad connections, like reconnecting a telephone exchange and stuffing the wires into the wrong holes. Statistically, parosmia patients show **slightly better long-term outcomes** than pure hyposmia patients — counterintuitively, distortion is a sign of activity.

**Olfactory bulb volume** is strongly correlated with depression. When smell signal stops, the bulb shrinks → low mood progressively sets in. **Smell training works partly by sending top-down activity into the bulb**, preserving its volume even when bottom-up signal is poor.

## Prognosis

- Recovery is real but slow.
- Many cases improve over 6–18 months.
- **Recovery beyond 2.5 years is documented.** Don't accept "after a year you have to live with it."
- Children: typically don't articulate smell loss but go off their food / refuse favourite foods. Recovery is usually ≤ 6 months in children — stem-cell regeneration is faster.

## Smell training (the only evidence-based intervention)

The intervention with research support. **Not a cure — a recovery accelerant.** Studies show smell-training cohorts perform measurably better on objective olfactory tests than control groups at 2-, 6-, and 9-month follow-up.

**The work happens in the brain**, not in the nose:
- Sniff a smell (lemon, eucalyptus, rose, clove are the classical "starter four")
- *Think* about the smell — recall any associated memory
- Search for what should be there
- Repeat 2× daily

**The bigger lever: keep interrogating your environment for smell** — coffee before drinking, outside on the way out the door, laundry. Patients who lose smell quickly stop trying — that's the wrong direction.

Compliance is the single biggest problem. Patients try once or twice and give up.

## The food / nutrition red flag

The most clinically consequential point of the episode. **Parosmia patients drop high-protein foods preferentially**, because animal proteins (meat, eggs, fish) contain the aroma compounds most likely to trigger disgust.

This leaves them on bland carbs — high calorie, low protein, low micronutrient. Risk of **protein-energy malnutrition** at exactly the time they need it for recovery.

**Solution:** pharmacy-grade meal replacement drinks (the kind given post-oral-surgery — *not* gym-style protein shakes which lack the full vitamin/mineral profile). **Huel** mentioned by name (unflavoured + cinnamon + oat milk is Kelly's tip — artificial chocolate / strawberry / banana flavours are often disgusting to parosmia patients).

## Quality-of-life impact (often underestimated)

> "It's like looking in the mirror and there's no reflection there. Our sense of smell is completely bound up with our sense of self." — Kelly, ep #50

- Family meals collapse — patient becomes the cook who can't cook
- Partner intimacy disrupted — partner smells disgusting
- Self-soothing rituals (tea, bath, candles) all involve smell
- **Anxiety surrounds the loss**, then a slow drift into depression
- Children with parosmia refuse food, lose weight, become hard to manage
- Babies with affected parent become hard to feed

Kelly frames it as "anxiety enveloping the whole thing" — and the medical system's typical response (ENT looks in nose, says "looks fine, go home") leaves patients without help.

## AbScent's five pillars of support

1. **Peer support** (forums, in-person groups)
2. **Clinical support** (rhinology, ENT)
3. **Food support** (managing the altered eating relationship)
4. **Practical advice** (smell training, smell-lock management)
5. **Mental health support** (external — important, often missed)

## Practical food tips for managing smell loss

- True-taste balance (sweet+sour, sweet+salty)
- Add **trigeminal stimulation** (chili, ginger, mint, peppercorn, menthol)
- Texture variety (nuts in salads)
- Temperature contrast (warm + cool)
- Visual appearance — never white food on a white plate

## Eric-relevant signal

- Eric does not currently report olfactory dysfunction. If it ever appears as a new symptom: **smell training daily, don't accept "after a year you have to live with it," watch nutrition.**
- The **olfactory-bulb-volume / depression link** is mechanistically interesting — yet another "sense input → brain volume → mood" pathway worth holding alongside the autonomic + mast-cell + microclot frames.
- **AbScent** is the resource to flag for any friend/family member with persistent COVID smell loss. Free Facebook group, free YouTube smell-training instructions, Sniff app.
