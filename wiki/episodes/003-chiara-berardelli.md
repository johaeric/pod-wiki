# #003 — Chiara Berardelli

**Guest**: Chiara Berardelli — singer-songwriter; former GP (left general practice ≈2017). First-wave long-hauler (March 2020). Closes the episode with her song *Tyrannosaurus*, named for the recurring monster-headache that defined her illness.

## Recovery status

Improving at time of recording. Two to three weeks of "feeling better than I have in ages" — wary, not declaring recovery. Tentative pacing toward more activity for next year, holding off on new work commitments.

## Symptom picture

- Recurring **acute viral-like episodes** monthly (March, April, May 2020) — thought it was repeated unrelated viruses for months.
- After a 3-day cycling holiday in late August 2020, **catastrophic crash**: persistent headache for most of October ("felt like I'd been poisoned").
- Headache so dominant she nicknamed it Tyrannosaurus.
- No classic taste/smell loss. No cough. Mild dyspnoea on stairs only.
- **Mood crashes** synchronised with acute spells — distinct from her past depression but felt similar.
- **Insomnia** during crashes.

## Healthcare experience

- GP unhelpful: told her **long covid was only for "shortness of breath and chest pain" patients**.
- Neurologist diagnosed **"chronic menopausal migraine"** because she's "a woman of a certain age". She rejected this — the headaches weren't migraines, and she'd asked specifically about long covid (out of his specialism). Prescribed migraine tablets she didn't take.
- Antibody test repeatedly unavailable; eventually accepted she didn't need confirmation to manage symptoms.
- "The medical profession is geared up to make a diagnosis about something that you can do something about, not about something that you can't do something about."

## What helped, what didn't

### Stopping all exercise (radical rest)

After the August cycling crash, then a January hill walk and an April bike ride both triggered re-crashes, she **stopped exercising entirely**. Headaches improved within weeks. Classification: helped_partial.

> "I had to stop, because it's not worth it. It definitely is affecting it... that's when things, the headaches, started to improve."
> — Chiara, ep #3

### Daily yoga

Specifically the **"yoga for headaches"** category of online videos. ≈15–20 minutes a day for several months at her worst. Two reported benefits:
- Felt like she was *doing something* even when bedbound-adjacent.
- "It's the voice of self-compassion every day. Sometimes you just need to hear it."

Stopped doing it daily once she felt better — explicitly notes this as a relapse risk. Classification: helped_partial.

### Counselling (private, weekly online)

Started ≈mid-2020 for unrelated reasons; became central to managing long covid. Specifically reframed her need-for-diagnosis as unnecessary ("it's real if *you're* feeling it — it doesn't matter what everyone else thinks"). Helped with acceptance, self-criticism, patience. Classification: helped_partial.

### Wild swimming

Started 2020; continued into December in a wetsuit. Realised cold thermoregulation was costing her energy and stopped over winter; resumed April. Not really exercise — "you don't really swim, you just dunk". Mood + sense-of-perspective benefit. Classification: helped_partial.

### Alcohol cessation

Stopped almost entirely "to see if it helped." Doesn't claim a clear effect; has continued mostly off it. Classification: mentioned_only.

### What didn't help

- Pushing through with exercise — repeated harm pattern.
- Doctor visits (apart from one helpful nurse later).
- Bloods — clear, useful only to rule out other things.
- Facebook support group — mixed; the post about a 282-day headache *worsened* her mood early on.
