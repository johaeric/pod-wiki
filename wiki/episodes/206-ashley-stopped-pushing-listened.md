Ashley — got severely ill in early lockdown phase (March 2020). Bedbound and bouncing between alternative protocols for years. Now married, recently planned and danced 5 hours at her wedding.

## Acute and chronic phase

- Severe LC; bedbound for extended periods
- Positive lab tests for Lyme disease, mold, multiple latent viruses → went down those treatment paths
- Alternative-medicine treatments (herbs, antivirals, etc.) reduced severity but never resolved it; remained bedbound

## The pivot

- Realised: husband in same household didn't get sick, others with positive tests aren't ill — *why is my body unable to recover?*
- Hypothesis: stuck in survival mode → body unable to heal
- Initial skepticism — "is this all in my head?"
- Resolved by hearing recovery stories of others who described similar pre-illness sensitivities

## What helped — Ashley's recovery framework

- **Brain retraining** — initial 6 weeks gave a small but real shift
- **Visualizations**
- **Somatic therapy with a coach** — this was the inflection. The coach taught her how to *listen to the body's language*, not the mind's language. She framed it as learning a new language entirely.
- **EFT tapping** — for processing limiting beliefs
- **Listening to body's needs daily**
- **Pacing within window of tolerance** — but with the body as the guide, not the clock

## On recovery as continued process

- Initial recovery happened fast (months) once the somatic work clicked — went from bedbound to walks, friends, garden
- A second wave of recovery (a year+ later) involved trauma processing and was equally important
- Now married, working out of the house, fully functional life
- "Recovery is more layered than you think — even when you feel recovered, more comes up"
- "Healing doesn't mean life is perfect. It means knowing yourself enough to meet yourself where you're at"

## Notes for Eric

This is a useful intermediate-stage recovery story. Two key takeaways: (1) **somatic therapy** as a distinct modality from brain-retraining or breathwork — the framing of body as a *separate language* with its own grammar that has to be learned is unusual and concrete; (2) the warning that protocol-based alternative-medicine routes (Lyme, mold, viruses) can take you to "less severe but still bedbound" without finishing the job. Need-listening is what closes the gap. Pair with #211 (Nora Rodden) for similar nervous-system-driven recovery framing.
