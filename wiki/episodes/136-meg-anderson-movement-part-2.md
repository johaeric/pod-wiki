# Episode 136 — Dr Meg Anderson — Movement Part 2: How To

The practical follow-up to #135. Once preconditioning is in place (RV CAMPING checklist), how do you actually start moving?

## Diagnostic question 1: do you have PEM?

- PEM = post-exertional malaise. *Delayed* fatigue / symptom flare following activity, out of proportion to the activity, with characteristic onset window (often 12–48 hr) and characteristic duration (often days, sometimes a week+).
- PEM does **not** mean you can never exercise. It means you need to know **your** version: when does it start, when does it peak, when does it end. That window is what you build pacing around.
- Variants:
  - **PEM** (delayed fatigue/exhaustion).
  - **PESE** (post-exertional symptom exacerbation): broader symptom flare — GI, breathing, heart, neurological tingling.
  - **PENE** (post-exertional neuroimmune exhaustion): flu-like (chills, fever, headache, body aches).
- The 2024 **Appleman et al.** paper measured muscle samples in LC patients after maximal exertion-induced PEM: amyloid deposition, catabolic breakdown, structural muscle changes. Maximal exertion is dangerous in PEM. *Sub-maximal, sub-symptom-threshold* movement is the safe range — and is what the protocol must stay inside.

## The exercise-as-prescription frame

Treat exercise like a medication: **dose, intensity, frequency**.

- **Dose**: subsymptom-threshold every time. Stop when symptoms start increasing >2 points on a 0-10 scale.
- **Frequency**: minimum 3×/week — body craves consistency.
- **Intensity**: changes day-to-day to keep the dose constant. Same routine, different effort, depending on autonomic state.

Day-to-day variability is the rule, not the exception. Hormonal cycle (luteal phase ↑ symptom burden) → reduce intensity, keep frequency. Same activity, less effort, more calories.

## Anderson's heart-rate guideline

- Sourced from the [[workwell-foundation]] guidelines for ME/CFS — **no more than 15-20 bpm above resting heart rate** during activity.
- Translation: if your resting HR is 70, target ceiling is 85-90 during movement. *Lower than what feels possible.*
- Most LC patients shoot up to 110-120 just standing up. Goal is to **train HR to stay low** during exertion, not to push HR higher. Opposite of conventional fitness training.
- Often requires **down-regulation before activity** — legs up the wall, breathing exercises, snack — to bring HR low *before* starting.

## Why she doesn't use the Levine protocol / modified CHOPs

- Designed for general POTS, not LC / ME/CFS / MCAS / hypermobility — the original studies excluded those populations.
- Too aggressive at start; too aggressive in progression.
- ~50–60% non-completion rate when applied broadly.
- Doesn't build space for PEM monitoring.
- **Borrows the principles** anyway: start horizontal, work toward vertical.

## Progression rules

- **3× consistently without a crash → consider 10–20% progression.**
- Some weeks she tells patients to *hold* even when ready — body benefits from predictability more than from progression.
- 10–20% increase (not bigger) — keeps the experiment fine-grained enough to scale back if PEM follows.

## When NOT to exercise (absolute)

- Active acute infection (with or without fever).
- Any fever.
- Active PEM crash.

When emerging from a crash or infection, **don't restart where you left off**. Roll back ~1 month of progress and rebuild — but rebuild fast (days/weeks rather than the original months).

## The sub-symptom rule

You will *never* be at zero pain / zero fatigue when you start moving. The bar is:
- "Window" baseline (less crashes, smaller fluctuations) achieved → ready.
- Increase >2 points on 10-point scale during activity → stop.
- Slight elevation in symptoms after activity is *normal and healthy*; elevation that disrupts daily routine is PEM and you went too far.

## Hormonal cycle management

- Luteal phase = higher symptom burden, body using more calories, lower threshold. Same routine, less effort to hit the same heart-rate target. May need extra calories.
- *Don't take the week off.* That breaks consistency. Adjust intensity, not frequency.
