# #011 — Iain on being a partner to someone with chronic illness

**Guest**: Iain — musician/teacher and old friend of [[jackie-baxter]]. His partner Cal (non-binary, they/them) has lived with chronic fatigue + mental health issues for years. Iain caught covid Christmas 2020 but did not develop long covid. Cal does not have long covid; the episode is about *partnering with someone who has a chronic condition*, applied to long-covid Partners.

This is a **carer's-perspective** episode. No interventions or symptoms to classify against the long-covid wiki — the value is in the relational/caregiver framing and in Iain's tips for partners of newly chronically ill long-haulers.

## Why this episode matters here

When somebody develops long covid, their partner often becomes an overnight carer to a previously healthy person — *during a pandemic*. Iain's relationship started with Cal already chronically ill, so he has years of trial-and-error around boundaries, communication, and being a "team" without dynamic sliding into carer/patient. Jackie names that this is what's been happening with her own partner. Eric is in the same dynamic — this content is relational, not medical, but it is the kind of thing that quietly makes or breaks a long-covid life.

## Key framing — the "team" reframe

> "We're a team. It is not me positioned against Cal and the illness, it's the two of us dealing with that together." — Iain, ep #11

Knock-on effects:
- The well partner doesn't unilaterally "take care of" the ill one — both are working, just on different things.
- The ill partner's effort to manage symptoms is real labour that should not go "unpraised, unsung."
- Both partners need their own space and own outlets — caring without being engulfed.

## Iain's practical tips

1. **Put on your own oxygen mask first.** A burnt-out partner helps no one. Build in time off, separate friends, board-game nights — Cal actively *encouraged* Iain into this from the start.
2. **Concentric circles of support.** Patient at the centre leans on partner; partner leans on a wider ring. Don't make the patient the *only* place a frustrated partner can vent — find someone else for that.
3. **Have a backup plan, always.** "We were going to see a film. We can't. Here's what we'll do instead — drive somewhere nice with ice creams, podcast on, no walking." Avoids the doom-spiral when plans collapse.
4. **Pre-arranged signals.** If Cal needs to leave a social situation but can't say it, hand-squeeze pinky = "get me out." Used once, but the *existence* of the system reduces social anxiety.
5. **Be the social buffer.** The well partner is often the one who reads the room earlier than the ill one. Watch and call time when needed.
6. **Allow ranting.** "It's okay for it to be terrible now and then." Bottling up frustration spirals; venting on schedule defuses it.
7. **You are not signing up for the easy version.** "If you're committing to a long-term relationship and someone gets a chronic illness — you're a team to work *through* things, not just through the lovely parts."

## On guilt (both sides)

Cal feels guilty for limiting Iain's life. Iain feels powerless when Cal crashes. Both are addressed by:
- naming the feelings explicitly (no martyrdom)
- explicit boundary-supporting work (Iain gets to keep going out; Cal is helped to find their own people for those evenings)
- "I love the time I spend with Cal — even on the bad days." Direct acknowledgement that the partnership is *still wanted*, not endured.

## On the medical/benefits system (UK)

Iain is direct about Cal's experience navigating chronic-illness benefits in the UK:

- DWP yearly assessments for ESA / PIP / Universal Credit are designed adversarially; they will weigh "you turned up on time" against you.
- **Take someone with you to assessments.** Friend, family, advocate. Not optional.
- GPs sometimes refer to homeopathy / unevidenced alternative therapies; Iain calls this out as charlatanry — money spent on things with no evidence base.
- Citizens Advice, unions, online disability communities — use them.

## On finding a partner-support community
