# #027 — Dr Jenny Ceolta-Smith — The Aftermath of Leaving Work

**Guest**: **Dr Jenny Ceolta-Smith** — occupational therapist (England), specialism in **vocational rehabilitation**; Vocational Rehabilitation Association member; researcher on DWP employment support; ex-DWP / Job Centre OT. **Has long covid since March 2020**; **lost her own job October 2021** (technically agreed-to-leave, eight-page formal complaint to her university went unanswered). Member of the **Long Covid Support Employment Group / @longcovidwork**.

Practical employment-and-benefits episode. No interventions, but very high signal on the *system* around LC patients leaving work.

## Headline advice (paraphrased from her group's legal counsel)

**Do not resign.** Common dilemma: wait to be dismissed, or resign first?
- General advice from national law centres: if you resign first, it can be harder to subsequently raise disability-discrimination claims (no reasonable adjustments offered, etc.) via ACAS conciliation or employment tribunal.
- Myth busting: dismissal does not destroy future employability. Employers do hire people with LC histories.

## The system map (England-focused, with Scotland caveats)

- **ACAS** — pre-tribunal conciliation, free helpline, **3-month-less-1-day** time limit from alleged discrimination event
- **National Law Centres** (e.g. GM Law collaboration) — webinar on self-represented tribunal process available
- **Equality Advisory Service** — free letter templates / helpline
- **Unions** — first port of call if you have one
- **Home insurance with legal cover** — genuinely useful; insurer assesses if your case has >51 % chance of success
- **Equality Act** — reasonable adjustments framework; common LC ask is *work-from-home* refused

Documentation discipline — every meeting bounced back in writing: "thanks for confirming today that…". Especially important when cognitive dysfunction makes recall unreliable.

## Benefits playbook

- **New Style ESA** — non-means-tested, **£74.70/week**, up to 1 year, while still officially employed and on sick pay. Easy claim, fit-note + medical questionnaire.
- **PIP (Personal Independence Payment)** — non-means-tested; **nightmare process** but worth fighting; high success rate at appeal. Backdated to claim date. Unlocks council tax reductions, blue badge, vehicle tax exemption, travel discounts. Not affected by working.
- **Universal Credit** — for those eligible
- **Carer's Allowance** — partner-side
- **Permitted Work** — under ESA, up to 16 h/week / £150/week without losing benefit. ⚠️ Jenny was caught out: a single 6-hour above-£150 month → benefit suspended, no decision-maker response. **Check rules in detail before exceeding limit.**

Where to get help:
- GP surgery well-being / welfare workers
- Social-prescribing link workers
- Long Covid clinics with benefits support
- Housing-association welfare-rights workers
- Citizens Advice
- Long Covid Support Employment Group (releasing benefits resource pack March 2022)

## On her own grievance

Sent eight-page complaint to vice-chancellor + HR director + dean shortly before leaving. **Five months later, no reply.** Says she hasn't fully closed the chapter. Honest reflection that ACAS had recommended she raise a grievance earlier in the process; in retrospect she would have, even at the time it felt detrimental.

## Quotes

> "If you don't ask, you don't get. The benefits system should be there to support people. Stigma's stopping a lot of claims that would succeed." — Jenny

> "Three months notice with no income and no work was a sentence. I'm not sure I've come to terms with my employer's response yet." — Jenny

## How to read this for Eric

Eric's situation is different (Sweden, not UK) so the specific bodies don't transfer. The procedural lessons do: document every meeting in writing; raise grievances *before* leaving; check legal cover on insurance; advocacy work can substitute for lost professional identity.
