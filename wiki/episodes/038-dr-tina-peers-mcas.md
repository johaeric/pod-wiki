# #038 — Dr Tina Peers — MCAS & Long Covid

**Guest**: **Dr Tina Peers** — qualified 1983. UK GP → 24-year contraception + reproductive-health consultant for Surrey → menopause specialist. Diagnosed her own daughter's MCAS in 2016 after years of unexplained illness; that opened the MCAS lens for her clinical practice. Started LC clinic **1 Nov 2020** off the recognition that LC symptoms matched MCAS. Organised the **June 2021 MCAS + LC online conference** (still available at lc-treat.com; **Lawrence Afrin** keynote highly recommended). Now expanding to a multi-doctor clinic.

The wiki's **most consolidated MCAS-treatment protocol episode**. Anchors the [[mcas]] symptom page. Pair with #37 Khan for the patient-side, and ercipedia for the mechanistic / RCT picture.

## What MCAS is — Peers's framing

- **~17.5% population prevalence** (her estimate — large because mast cells underlie or contribute to IBS, fibromyalgia, asthma, eczema, rosacea, psoriasis, EDS, endometriosis, POTS, chronic headaches, depression, MCAS-style allergic conditions, and more).
- Recently characterised: first cases described 1980s, named 1990s, three case reports in journals 2007. Most doctors weren't trained in it.
- **Three-aetiology framing**: environmental, genetic (>50 *KIT* mutations across domains), viral, epigenetic.
- Mast cells = "the bouncers" — populated wherever the body interfaces with environment (mucosa, skin, gut, airways) and around nerves and lymphatics. When dysregulated, they release a cytokine storm + heparin + elastase, producing inflammation, hypersensitivity, and tissue effects.
- **Hypothesis**: severe acute covid (and post-acute LC) is enriched for people with prior undiagnosed MCAS — abnormal immune response to the virus.
- **In her LC clinic, ~98% of patients have a pre-existing MCAS history** when carefully reviewed; 2% have no prior signal but respond very fast to antihistamines + low-histamine diet.

## The treatment protocol (her sequence)

**Foundation**:
1. **Low-histamine diet** — she follows the Swiss SIGHI list. Often subjective improvement within 3 days.
2. Boost the methylation cycle / immune system with vitamins + minerals identified by genetic profiling.
3. **Filtered water** (charcoal sticks ~£12/6 months); avoid plastics; minimise EMF (Wi-Fi off at night, no phone in bedroom — explicit MCAS-stabilisation reasoning, not woo).

**Pharmacological add-ons** (one at a time, 2-week trial each):
1. **Type 1 antihistamines** — loratadine 10 mg → titrate to 3×/day; or cetirizine; or fexofenadine 120 mg (180 mg with prescription).
2. **Type 2 antihistamines** — famotidine 20 → 40 mg twice daily; or ranitidine.
3. **Mast-cell stabilisers** — ketotifen, rupatadine; **sodium cromoglicate** specifically for gut MCAS (epigastric tenderness signal).
4. **LDN** — both an MCAS stabiliser and cytokine-effect dampener.
5. **Montelukast** 10 mg/day (leukotriene inhibitor; ~30% develop depression, monitor).
6. **Diazepam** — small dose (0.5–1 mg twice daily). Notes: MCAS patients do **not** become addicted because the diazepam is bound to mast cells, not brain; can stop without withdrawal.
7. Specialist drugs (cromolyn, immunosuppressants) for refractory cases.

**Background co-morbidities to clear**:
- **Mold / mycotoxins** — urine mycotoxin panel; treat actively.
- **EBV reactivation** — the covid virus seems to reactivate it.
- **Lyme** — can be reactivated.
- **Any other infection** — UTIs, fungal, even tooth infections — anything keeping mast cells stimulated.

**Adjuncts she's now using or considering**:
- **Arc Microtech device** — micro-current device worn on the arm 3 hours/day for 6 weeks. Originally developed for racehorses → jockeys → human use. Boosts ATP. ~95% of her patients report some improvement (pain, headache, sleep, brain fog, fatigue).
- **Gupta program** — neuroplastic retraining. "30% improvement" per her patients on average.
- **Possible gut SARS-CoV-2 persistence** treatment — recent stool studies showing virus may be replicating in gut. Antibiotic candidates: metronidazole, vancomycin, amoxicillin (per a published study); could also try natural antimicrobials.
- HBOT and apheresis: her patients who travelled (Germany, Cyprus) "feel better for a few days then it catches up" — under-impressed.
- Ozone therapy: limited data; one Cyprus clinic uses it.

## Diagnostic limits

- **Serum tryptase** is normal in MCAS (only elevated in mastocytosis) → useful only to exclude mastocytosis.
- Chilled-sample tests (chromogranin A, prostaglandin D2, plasma histamine, heparin, leukotrienes; chilled 24-hour urine) are **rarely available in UK labs** — chain-of-cold breaks them.
- Newer cytokine-panel companies on a single blood sample — Peers exploring.

## Vaccine-triggered LC

Names this carefully and clearly: a subset of her LC patients had no covid before vaccination. Mechanism she names: mRNA enters non-deltoid tissues (ovaries, testes, liver, heart, brain, spleen); cells then produce spike protein internally → autoimmune attack on own cells in MCAS-prone individuals. Treatment is the same as virus-triggered LC.
