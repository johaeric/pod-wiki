# #91 — Q&A with Dr Groysman, Part 1

Listener-submitted Q&A with Dr Robert Groysman ([[076-dr-robert-groysman-sgb]] anchor episode). Wide-ranging — covers SGB-adjacent topics (dysautonomia, vagus, tinnitus, brain fog, palpitations), plus questions outside SGB (LDN dosing, spike protein, microclots, hair loss, iron deficiency, exercise/pacing). Part 2 is [[092-groysman-qa-pt2]].

## Dysautonomia testing options when tilt-table is negative

For listeners whose tilt-test is negative but orthostatic intolerance is severe:

- **Heart rate variability test** (ideally Holter monitor over 24h)
- **QSART** (quantitative sudomotor axon reflex test) — sweat response to electrical stimulus
- **Sweat litmus paper** for sympathetic sudomotor function

But Groysman's bottom line: **history + symptoms are usually enough** for diagnosis. Don't chase indirect tests that won't change treatment.

## Vagus nerve — dysfunction, not damage

LC vagus is dysfunctional, not anatomically damaged. Evidence: imaging (MRI, ultrasound) shows **thickening of certain segments** of the vagus, which impairs function. Thickened nerves don't conduct as well. Recovery expectation: **functional, with time and treatment**, not surgical.

Contextual reminder of vagus footprint: tongue-back / swallowing / peristalsis (full GI), heart-rate-lowering, bronchial calibration, salivation/mucus, anti-inflammatory tone, anxiety regulation.

## Tinnitus mechanism (speculative)

No specific mechanism studies in LC tinnitus. Groysman's two best-fit hypotheses:

1. **Auditory-nerve neuropathy** — same inflammatory pattern as trigeminal/vagal nerve involvement, but the brain interprets auditory-nerve irritation as ringing rather than pain/numbness.
2. **Vagal involvement** in the auditory canal — partly supports why **VNS often reduces tinnitus**.

## Spike protein — agnostic on persistence and clinical relevance

Groysman is **non-committal** on the persistent-spike-protein hypothesis:

- Some patients have circulating spike many months post-infection (or post-vaccine); some don't.
- No good clinical test for spike persistence at present.
- **Nattokinase** and **serrapeptase** (and **intermittent fasting / autophagy**) suggested as candidate clearance approaches but evidence in vivo unclear.
- "Important to know that not every single person has circulating spike proteins."

## Hibernation hypothesis — agnostic

No definitive evidence SARS-CoV-2 hibernates like herpes/Hep B/Hep C/HIV. Postmortem persistence is documented (in deceased LC patients) but that's residual/persistent virus, not active reactivation cycles.

## LC + medically-induced menopause

Groysman acknowledges:
- Menstrual cycle changes during LC
- Possible (uncertain-permanence) infertility signal
- Premature menopause in some women

But disclaims direct mechanism knowledge — points to ongoing studies (PLRC menstruation review; Imperial / [[dr-viki-male]] in [[093-dr-viki-male-menstruation]]).

## Iron deficiency anemia and the ferritin trap

Important diagnostic point:
- **Ferritin is also an inflammatory marker**, so LC chronic inflammation **falsely elevates ferritin** and can mask iron-deficiency anemia.
- Don't accept "ferritin is normal" as ruling out iron deficiency in LC.
- LC patients commonly have low iron from gut dysbiosis (poor absorption) + increased utilisation.
- "Too long for it to be long covid" — Groysman explicitly rejects this gaslighting frame: **"there is no time-limit on long covid"**.

## LDN dosing (anchor practical detail)

[[low-dose-naltrexone]]: anchor protocol explicitly stated:

- Start **1 mg/day**, titrate **+0.5 mg per week** to **4.5 mg/day** (once or twice).
- Some protocols start at 4.5 mg directly.
- **Minimum 1 month** before assessing effect; some need **3 months**.
- **Compounding pharmacy required** (commercial dose is 50 mg). Cost should be **<$100/month**. Reports of $9,000 are price-gouging.
- Effect is **fatigue** primarily; some brain-fog response. Won't fix anosmia/parosmia, GI, anxiety.
- **Side-effect profile**: main concern is **opioid reversal** if patient is on chronic opioids (separate them in time).
- If LDN causes more fatigue, take it at night.

## Hair loss

Direct viral disruption of hair-growth cycle (each follicle has anagen/catagen/telogen phases — chronic inflammation interrupts).

- Topical **vitamin E** + **selenium** generally not effective in LC hair loss.
- **Minoxidil** (vasodilator) worth trying.
- **GHK-Cu** (copper peptide gel/foam) — vasodilator, Groysman prescribes it for hair loss.
- **Topical steroid** if autoimmune component suspected.

## Increased sensory sensitivity

Groysman doesn't have strong patient pool data on whether SGB helps multi-sensory hyperacuity (light/sound/smell). Likely sympathetic-overdrive related (fight-flight pupil dilation, hearing acuity). Refreshingly admits **"I don't know"** when he doesn't.

## Shortness of breath + palpitations (without structural cause)

Three functional mechanisms named:
1. **VQ mismatch** (perfusion ↔ oxygen demand), autonomic-controlled
2. **Heart rate response inadequate** for activity
3. **Bronchial dilation from sympathetic overdrive** — counter-intuitively *more* air to move = perceived shortness of breath

For palpitations: sympathetic overdrive → faster, harder, more irritable beats → premature beats. SGB and VNS can both target this. Jackie adds a host caveat: **breathing-pattern disorder** (not addressed in this Q&A) is also a major mechanism.

## Orthostatic intolerance / POTS — symptom management vs root cause

Symptom management (the "what to do today" layer):
- **Hydration + salt** (oral or IV)
- **Compression stockings**
- IV **saline** for severe POTS

Root cause: **fix the dysautonomia**, then orthostasis improves. Groysman's tools: **VNS** or **SGB**.

## Exercise + post-COVID — the controversial answer

Listener question: does exercise harm everyone with post-COVID?

Groysman's answer: **"I think it would be helpful in all people"** — but with explicit pacing caveats:

- Pace yourself. Don't push to breaking point.
- Movement, not exercise, sometimes — "if all you can do is walk to another room, that's fine."
- Build up *very gradually* — extra steps, not extra miles.
- Deconditioning happens in **as little as a week**. Body is made to move.
- **Build tolerance over time** — listen to body for fluctuating capacity.

Jackie reinforces with her own experience: **redefining "exercise" as "movement"** + adding [[breathing-pattern-disorder]] context. Important caveat that this doesn't mean "everyone go running tomorrow"; it means within-tolerance graded movement.
