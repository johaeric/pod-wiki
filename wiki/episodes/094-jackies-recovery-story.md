# #94 — Jackie's Long Covid Recovery Story

The host's own recovery episode — interviewed for the first time by her partner **Mally** ("Maly" in transcript). Jackie acknowledges she's recovered. **Seventh full recovery on the wiki.**

Combines a chronological recovery narrative with retrospective synthesis — what helped, what didn't, what surprised her, how she found out she was recovered.

## Pre-LC profile

- Hill-walking + cycling + running enthusiast (how she met Mally).
- Ed-sector job (Scotland); long commute; people-pleaser; "always saying yes" to everything.
- Self-described: "didn't know how to sit still."

## Onset (March 2020)

- Acute COVID confirmed by paramedics — borderline-hospitalisation severity. Wasn't admitted because so many were sicker.
- 5–6 weeks bedbound effectively.
- Apparent improvement at ~3 months; thought "that's the end of that."
- **Crash at month 3.5** — long-covid proper hit and lasted three years.

## Symptom profile during LC

- **Fatigue** beyond anything previously experienced
- **Severe breathing difficulties** (later identified as [[breathing-pattern-disorder]])
- **Tachycardia + palpitations** (post-shower HR took 30+ min to settle)
- **COVID toes** (black toes early)
- **PEM** "from basically anything"
- **Intermittent sleep + mild GI**
- All of which Jackie now retrospectively groups under [[dysautonomia]]

## What didn't help

- **Pushing through.** She is the wiki's most articulate "I-tried-pushing-and-it-made-me-worse" voice. Tried more exercise, tried less, tried a tiny bit less, all of it too much.
- **Working through it.** Worked from home for ~12 months before going on sick leave. In hindsight: should never have been working at all.
- **Hopping between supplements.** Notes she "depleted my bank account" with no observable benefit.
- **Cutting things from her diet** in various permutations — no perceptible effect.
- **Nattokinase** — no benefit; gave her "completely insane periods."
- **Frequency machine** (an unspecified bioelectric device) — no effect.

## What helped — the "Big Three"

### 1. Breathwork (the foundational intervention)

Jackie identified that her breathing pattern was "completely dysfunctional" — would have struggled to be much worse. Two distinct phases:

- **Re-learning to breathe.** Fixed the dysfunctional pattern → directly fixed breathlessness, indirectly fixed palpitations.
- **Using breath to influence the autonomic nervous system.** Once the pattern was fixed, she used breath as an active autonomic-regulation tool — slowing heart rate, dampening palpitations. *"This was just such a powerful tool and I got really obsessed with breathing."*

She is currently completing **breathing-instructor training** to teach this.

### 2. Yoga Nidra + gentle yoga (for "real rest")

Pre-LC Jackie didn't know how to rest. Yoga Nidra was the only modality that genuinely **switched her brain off**.

- "My brain was a hamster wheel — going round and round and round."
- Yoga Nidra found a way through.
- Gentle yoga gave her a way to move her body without depleting it.

This was the inflection where she understood that **rest isn't earned — everyone needs it; healthy people too**.

### 3. Cold water (had to come after the breathwork)

Sequencing matters: she explicitly says she would have hyperventilated if she'd tried cold water before fixing her breathing. With breathing repaired:

- First dip: very brief; "felt like I'd been plugged into a socket" afterwards.
- Pattern: each session a bit better; effects lasted longer over time.
- She's never had PEM from cold water or swimming in cold water — flag for the [[mark-harper]] **water-buoyancy-as-PEM-protection** hypothesis (#87).
- Joined a local swimming group → social benefit + Mally didn't have to come every time → confidence to leave the house alone.

## Other interventions that helped

- **Counselling** (started after [[suzy-bolt]] told her in Oct 2021: "you can't put all of that on Mally"). She is **still seeing her counsellor** at recording. Big help with anxiety, trauma, perfectionism.
- **Letting go of chores.** Mally took everything over. *"All the things that needed doing got done."*
- **Going on sick leave** (~18 months in). Felt like giving up; in hindsight should have done it from the start.
- **Career break** (start of 2023) — removed the work-related stress that she hadn't realised was so destructive. Inflection point.
- **The Fern programme** with [[suzy-bolt]] (~March 2023) — joined when she was already 90–95% recovered. Useful in two ways: (1) consolidated knowledge she already had into a structured tool; (2) **named her perfectionism** and how it was driving her recovery-perfectionism.

## Other things tried (lower impact)

- **Safe and Sound Protocol** — found useful (consistent with #23 [[sally-riggs]]).
- **Vedicinals-9** — "did help my gut a bit" but not a huge improvement (#41 [[joachim-gerlach]]).
- **Journaling** — useful occasionally, especially for stress/anxiety; not a daily practice.

## Mental health: the under-iceberg

A major theme: as physical symptoms cleared, **a load of underlying mental-health stuff surfaced** — anxiety, trauma, the perfectionism. Pre-LC she had been so physical-symptom-focused she hadn't been aware of it. She's emphatic this is real and needs treating, not dismissed.

## On acceptance

The same arc as #75 Jackie-3y-reflections, #07 Bolt, #20 Anker, #34 Munslow, #89 Short, #90 Jackson:
- Initial reaction: refused outright. *"I refuse to accept this."*
- Reframe that worked: **acceptance of the moment, not the always.** "This moment is awful, but this moment will pass."
- Once she stopped fighting: things started getting better.

## On finding out she was recovered

Recovery announcement happened **during an interview with [[lorrie-rivers]]** for the holistic-healing summit. Lorrie asked how she was doing. Jackie was mid-answer, said something like "actually I think maybe I'm not really ill anymore but I wouldn't say I'm recovered…" — and caught herself doing the perfectionism thing again.

The realisation: **no-one is 100%**. Not the same person as pre-LC, but maybe **better** in some ways: trauma processed, perfectionism understood, fewer frantic obligations, more boundaries.

After the interview: walked through to Mally — "I'm recovered" — Mally: "I know."

## Brain catch-up

Important meta-observation: **brain-body asynchrony goes both ways.**
- Early in LC: brain ahead of body — "I should be able to do this" → push → crash.
- Coming out of LC: body ahead of brain — body fine, brain still says "be careful, last time we tried this, we crashed". Re-learning to trust body takes time.

## "Jaxter 2.0" — what's different

- More respect for her own health.
- Boundaries (a word she'd previously only associated with maps).
- Listens to her body now — *"no-one else knows your body better than you do."*
- Fundamentally still the same brain ("always on; firework idea generation"), but now remembers to **turn it off sometimes**.
- The breathing-instructor training is a deliberate identity-shift — turning lived experience into a way to help others.

## Advice to her past self

1. **Keep an open mind.** The things that helped most were the things she'd have dismissed at the start.
2. **Recovery is not linear.** "Speed bumps" framing — slow you down but don't stop you.
3. **Ask for and accept help.** "Once I allowed Mally to take over stuff and went on sick leave, that was when I started to pull things together."
4. **Be kind to yourself.** [[mental-self-harming]] is real.
5. **Belief in recovery.** Without it, the dark places get very dark.

## Eric-relevant intake (host-side)

This episode is unusually high-fidelity for the wiki because the host shares Eric's UK setting + structural similarities (high-functioning, busy, type-A pre-illness, severe breathing dysfunction, dysautonomia spectrum). Specific signals:

- **Breathwork → autonomic regulation as a tool, not just calm** — the cognitive shift from "breathing exercises" to "actively use breath to influence HRV / palpitations" is operational and Eric-relevant.
- **Sequencing rule**: fix breathing before cold water, otherwise the cold-shock breath response will hyperventilate you. Eric already swims, so this is informational.
- **Counsellor-not-partner principle**: Suzy Bolt's "you can't put all that on Mally" → portable to Eric's context.
- **Career break as inflection** — separate work-stress-removal from full work-cessation; the loss of *worry about not coping* matters as much as the work-load reduction itself.
- **Brain-body asynchrony in recovery** — useful frame for what Eric may experience as he improves: body permits more before brain trusts more.
- **Perfectionism as recovery-blocker** — same lever Jackson #90, Short #89, Munslow #34 each name from different angles.
