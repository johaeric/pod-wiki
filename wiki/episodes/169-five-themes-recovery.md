# #169 — Five Essential Themes for Long Covid Recovery

Host-solo Q&A episode. Jackie answers two listener questions: *"What is the one treatment / habit that leads to recovery?"* and *"What is the common link between recovery stories?"* — plus a third on brain training. Her answer: **there is no one thing**, but there are five recurring themes she's pulled from interviewing hundreds of recovered patients.

## The five themes

### 1. Consistency

- *Daily, sustainable* practice — breathwork, journaling, therapy, acupuncture, whatever works for the individual.
- The "roots underground" metaphor: the plant looks the same on the surface for weeks; the work is happening below ground.
- **Consistency ≠ pushing.** It's finding what you can do *every single day* and doing it.

### 2. Mindset

- Positive thinking alone won't heal anyone. Patronising to claim otherwise.
- But *belief in recovery* matters: belief that things can get better even just a little bit.
- Cites [[144-dr-sarah-recovery]]'s "stress-bath vs healing-bath" framing — the cells' chemical environment affects what they can repair into.
- **Open-mindedness** is part of mindset: many things that helped Jackie were things 5-years-ago-Jackie would have dismissed (breathwork, cold water, mind-body framing). Doctors don't always know; this doesn't mean the answers don't exist.

### 3. Understanding

- Until Jackie understood what was going on in her body, she was doing all the wrong things.
- **Understanding the nervous system** was her own light-bulb moment — let her know which strategies might work and how to recognise effect.
- Example: a breathing exercise that *induces* panic isn't helping; a slightly-less-intense version might hit the sweet spot.
- Understanding triggers is part of this — what activates you, can you reduce/mitigate?
- *Knowledge reduces fear.* "Even if there's not a lot we can do about it, understanding what is actually going on can bring down the sheer terror — and that itself helps the nervous system."

### 4. Build your strategies

- A *list*, a *toolkit*. Different strategies for different states.
- Medications are strategies — including bridges (cf. [[057-sanjay-gupta-pots-specialist]] explicitly framing meds as bridges).
- Hormones, acupuncture, HBOT, diet, breathwork, etc. all candidate strategies.
- **Don't dismiss the small things** — most recovery stories on this podcast are stacks of consistent small things.
- Ask: *Does it feel good in my body?* If not, probably not helping.
- *Mental health is a strategy*. Therapy is recommended; mental-health support helps everything else work better.

### 5. A healing environment

- What you put **into** your body: food, supplements, hydration, *and air*. (Plug for the breath being the fastest biochemistry-changing tool.)
- What you **consume** elsewhere: people around you, media, support groups, news. Are you scrolling LC support groups full of multi-year-illness stories? Mute them. Less news. 
- **What you tell yourself** — internal narration matters. Not dismissing emotions; choosing perspective.
- Ask: *What does my body need right now?*

## The overarching theme: empowerment

- Even amid bodily chaos, focusing on *what we can do* is empowering. There's a lot we can't, but what *can* we?
- 1% better is better than 0%. 1%s add up.

## Brain training Q&A

- Most (not all) people on this podcast who recovered did *some* form of brain training as part of their recovery — Gupta, ANS Rewire, DNRS, Curable.
- It's rarely the whole story. Often a mindset-shift or a transition-out tool. Jackie's own use was the *transition-out* version (~3 years in).
- **Curable app** is the common low-cost option (~$50/year); not affiliated.
- The most expensive program isn't always the right fit. Find what fits *you*.
