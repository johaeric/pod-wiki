# #98 — Charlotte Hammond: Long Covid Recovery

UK recovery story — 30-year-old, Birmingham → Buckinghamshire (back with parents). **Onset Oct 2021**, recovered to "no longer LC" within ~14 months but with residual capacity reduction. **Eighth full recovery on the wiki.**

## Pre-LC profile

- Head of English + Modern Foreign Languages at a UK secondary school.
- Working in a "12-hour-day" school culture; manager had had a heart attack and not taken a break — culture of not taking breaks.
- Pre-LC nervous system already in a stressed state.
- Self-described "Type A; not great at giving herself a break."
- Owned her own house, lived alone, kept cats.

## Onset (Oct 2021)

- Caught COVID at school — colleague got it a few days before her, reasonable certainty of school-source.
- "Half-term" timing — initial gutting was "missing a week of fun things"; turned out longer.
- Acute COVID moderate — never considered hospitalisation; mainly fatigue + breathing difficulties.
- Tried to return to work after ~2 weeks; **3 days back made her significantly worse** — the wiki's clearest "this is the moment I knew I was done" pattern.
- Decided early she couldn't keep the job.

## The crash + life reorganisation

- Living alone in Birmingham; friends mostly in 20s, didn't know what to do; older friends elsewhere.
- "Trying to do everything herself, kept getting iller."
- ~4 months in: **moved back to Buckinghamshire to live with parents.** Rented out her house; financially survived; "best decision."
- Improvement started after the move + the support system — the social-isolation-relief was the inflection.

## What helped — the recovery stack

### 1. A private doctor who listened (the most important first step)

Got referred to a private doctor who **had had LC himself**:
- Treated whole person, not symptom-by-symptom.
- Recognised LC as a genuine illness — implicit validation she hadn't received elsewhere.
- Prescribed supplements + **antihistamines** ([[antihistamines]]).
- Gave her **permission to celebrate small wins** — a critical re-framing for someone in Type-A mode.
- Helped her find Baseline faster than NHS could.

### 2. Diet — sugar-free + gluten-free

Adopted **gluten-free + sugar-free** diet. Reports clear benefit. (Note: not lectin-free / Plant-Paradox-style as in #96; broader anti-inflammatory diet.)

### 3. Cold water + cold showers ([[cold-water-exposure]])

Joined the wiki's growing cold-water-helps cohort. She specifically mentions cold showers as the entry-point — accessible without water access.

### 4. Antihistamines (MCAS-adjacent)

Prescribed by her private doctor. Whether she fits a strict MCAS profile or a histamine-sensitive sub-cohort isn't clarified. Cross-ref [[037-dr-asad-khan]] [[038-dr-tina-peers-mcas]] [[068-dr-david-saperstein]] for the H1+H2 cocktail framing.

### 5. Therapy via a charity (mental-health iceberg)

Got therapy via a UK charity — sessions at **£25/session** based on household income. Says ~20 sessions over the recovery arc; calls it crucial.

> "If you're talking to a therapist, you can properly vent. Having someone with no vested interest is so important. My parents — much as they love me — were upset for me every time I got worse. That puts pressure on you."

This is the wiki's clearest articulation of the **partner-or-family-can't-substitute-for-therapy** principle (cf #94 Jackie's Suzy-Bolt-said-the-same to her).

### 6. Family support + parents on board

Parents believed her. Could see what worked + didn't. **Living with people who back you up matters** — same lever Eric has via Mally (cf [[094-jackies-recovery-story]] [[011-iain-partner-with-chronic-illness]]).

### 7. The unspecified medication that "sped it up"

She references a medication she can't disclose details of that **sped up** her recovery from a slow upward trajectory to a faster one. *"Had I not been able to had not been very fortunate and it kind of sped up then I would have got there but just got there a bit more slowly maybe."* Logged for completeness; not actionable since unidentified.

## On the recovery → not 100% pre-LC question

> "I would class myself as not having LC anymore but I'm not as fit as I was. My body's taken a battering."

Specific residuals:
- **Sensory sensitivity** — can't tolerate large groups for as long as she used to; sounds/lights bother her more.
- **Lower stamina** — building strength back is its own phase post-LC.

Frames recovery as having two phases: clearing LC, then rebuilding capacity. The second phase has its own arc.

## On "Mental change is the bigger gift"

> "I'm so much more mentally resilient now. I am a totally different person. I know myself better. I think I've got so much out of it."

Names being more comfortable with emotions in herself + others. *"I felt more naturally that I knew what to do if somebody is upset in front of me. I feel more connected to other people."*

## On the body-listening problem after recovery

Important and resonant with [[094-jackies-recovery-story]]:

- During LC: didn't listen to her body enough.
- Post-LC: now listens **so much** that the smallest twinge can trigger "is the LC back?"
- Tension: trust the body to recover vs the trauma-trained vigilance.

The wiki's clearest articulation of **post-recovery hypervigilance as a trauma residue worth addressing**.

## Advice to her past-self

- **Quit your job.** (But she wouldn't have listened.)
- **You're not as alone as you feel.** People love you; you love them — it just feels like that right now.
- **Everything is temporary** — you can't imagine being not-ill when you're so ill, but you won't be ill forever.
- **Get therapy.** The charity-rate channel made it accessible.

## Eric-relevant intake

- **The "partner-can't-substitute-for-therapist" principle** — Charlotte's articulation is independent of Jackie's #94 articulation. Two recovery stories now name this lever from different angles.
- **Sugar-free + gluten-free diet** as helpful — different shape from #96 Makeeva's lectin-free framing; same anti-inflammatory direction. Eric's diet calibration could plausibly include sugar-free as a self-experiment if not already.
- **Cold showers as entry-point** — for anyone who can't access cold water. Eric already swims.
- **Post-recovery hypervigilance** is a real phase — Eric should expect it and not pathologise it.
- **"Body has taken a battering, capacity rebuild is its own phase"** — useful framing for the post-LC recovery curve. Healing the LC ≠ being pre-LC fit.
