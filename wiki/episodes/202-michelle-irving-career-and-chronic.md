Michelle Irving (Australia) returns ~100 episodes after her first appearance (#100). She's been navigating chronic illness for 20+ years and now coaches women through career and chronic illness. Since the last episode, she has launched **Chronic Illness at Work** — a corporate arm teaching organisations how to support people with long-term health conditions.

## The Career and Chronic Map — five stages

1. **Off-ramp from work** — medical leave / bedbased phase; one job is to take care of your health. Identity dissolution starts here. Skills: emotional map (covered in #100), clinician boundaries, asking for and receiving help.
2. **On-ramp back to work** — testing capacity; volunteering; side hustles; interviews; returning to old job. Carry forward what you've learned about what you want.
3. **The new** — even returning to the same job is *new*. Recognising newness gives permission to relax and treat re-entry as learning. Skipping this stage produces self-blame.
4. **Test and redesign** — you spend ~80% of your working life here. Test capacity, redesign, test, redesign. Continual not finite.
5. **Authentic leadership** — emerges naturally from having navigated complex terrain with sovereignty.

Stages aren't strictly linear — you can move back and forth between them.

## Sovereignty over self-sacrifice

- Most women (and many men) are conditioned to self-sacrifice; chronic illness forces a stop
- The discomfort isn't wrong — it's deconditioning of self-sacrifice patterns
- Boundaries from a *grounded* state ("there is no flexibility") work; battle-state boundaries ("there is no flexibility!" arms-crossed) don't

## Communicating capacity

- Most patients overstate by communicating *optimal* capacity rather than baseline
- Tool: **Circle of Wellness** — work out optimal vs baseline, communicate from baseline so others can plan

## "Chronic confidence" — for both sides of the conversation

- Patient with the condition needs confidence to say what they need
- People-leader needs confidence to have the conversation
- Educational gap is enormous; her corporate work fills it
