# #48 — Saskia Mulder — Long Covid & a visit to South Africa

A heavy, unusually candid two-year patient story. Saskia Mulder is an Irish psychiatric nurse who caught COVID at work in March 2020 (she was a deputy manager on an over-65 acute psych admissions ward; the ward was the first in their hospital to take COVID patients, and PPE was inadequate). 27 months in at recording. Not recovered.

This episode is the **anchor patient case for late-stage triple-therapy treatment via the Stellenbosch route** (the South African branch of the Pretorius/Kell microclot network). It's also the wiki's most explicit account so far of **LC-driven suicidality**, and Mulder's decision to start an SSRI is treated as a critical inflection point.

## Recovery status & timeline

- **Mar 2020** — caught COVID at work after caring for the first ward COVID patients in inadequate PPE
- **Mar 2020 (week 1–10)** — acute illness, told over the phone "if you can't hold breath 4s call 999," not admitted
- **~6 weeks** — "relapse" with high-temp, sore throat, breathing difficulty; antibiotics, then antibiotics again for what was diagnosed as pneumonia
- **Months 1–9** — relentless deterioration: post-viral myalgia, pleurisy, costochondritis, severe dysautonomia (not yet named), nausea, daily severe headaches, sinus infections, tachycardia
- **~Month 8** — CT scan with contrast dye triggered chest flare → suspects MCAS reaction
- **Month 9** — first formal LC diagnosis from a private respiratory consultant; started oral steroids
- **Month 10** — felt slight improvement, walked in cold winter forest, **4-month catastrophic relapse**
- **Months 16–17** — active suicidality with developing plan; started SSRI; ideation stopped within days
- **Month 24** — flew to South Africa for triple-therapy via Pretorius's lab
- **Month 27 (recording)** — 9 weeks of triple therapy completed; off it now due to anaemia from heavy bleeding

## The South Africa trip — practical detail (the heart of the episode)

Why South Africa not Germany: the Mülheim (Jaeger) waitlist is 6+ months and reportedly opaque. Stellenbosch booked her in **3 weeks** for the Pretorius-collaborator route.

**Logistics that worked:**
- Booked through the South African route after a fellow LC patient recommended it
- Travelled with her son (essential — could not have managed cases alone)
- Night flight: pressure change at altitude relieved her crippling head pain mid-ascent
- Wheelchair / fast-track support at Heathrow + Cape Town airports
- Hotel in Cape Town; lab in Stellenbosch ~45 min by Uber (£20 each way)
- Money goes much further than in Mülheim — flight is the expensive piece

**The clinical encounter:**
- Met Dr Resia Pretorius personally at the lab
- Doctor (Mulder calls him "Jako"; likely Dr Jaco Laubscher) spent meaningful time with her and her son
- "First doctor in two years to ask me to tell him all my symptoms"
- Walked through microclot mechanism with a vascular model
- 3-day blood-test → results review:
  - **Microclots: grade 2** (out of higher possible — moderate)
  - **Hyperactive platelets** (less severe than worst seen but clearly abnormal)
- Prescribed triple therapy + sleep, anti-MCAS, and (critically) **menstrual-cycle suppression** (which she didn't get and ended up needing)
- Personal mobile number for weekly WhatsApp progress updates

> "He said: just contact me with updates every week. In all my years as a nurse I've never had an experience like that with a doctor over here in the last two years." — Mulder, ep #48

## Triple therapy outcome (Aug–Oct 2022)

Stack: **clopidogrel 75 mg/day + apixaban 5 mg BD + aspirin 75 mg/day**.

**Improvements:**
- Deeper sleep
- Better circulation (hands/feet temperature regulation)
- Reduced sensory hypersensitivity (louder noises tolerated)
- Fewer food intolerances *during* treatment (suggests MCAS-vascular overlap)
- Less limb pain (the touch-equivalent-of-pain symptom — clothes feel painful)
- Friends/family observed a sharper, more present Saskia

**Setbacks:**
- Severe iron-deficiency anaemia from very heavy bi-monthly menstrual bleeding induced by anticoagulation (her cycle had already shifted to 2-week intervals before South Africa; the apixaban amplified bleeding)
- Stopped at **9 weeks** because (a) she'd run out and (b) she was too physically depleted to fly to Germany for follow-up bloods
- Reverted to lumbrokinase + serrapeptase as natural-blood-thinner maintenance
- Major caveat: at ~24 months in, the recovery ceiling on triple therapy is plausibly lower than for someone starting at 6 months. Other patients she met in SA who started at 6mo were "doing really really well."

## Mulder's framing of the result

> "I think we have to be realistic. There's a lot of people with a lot of hope and a lot of money — this is not the magic cure. It's a massive part of the puzzle. But unless we get our blood flow and circulation right we're not going to get better." — Mulder, ep #48

> "I've given my body a good blast of what I'd call my chemo to my cancer." — Mulder, ep #48

## Mental health — the most important section

The episode's most consequential disclosure. **Worth reading before any LC patient or carer dismisses LC-related mental-health concerns as soft.**

- ~16–17 months in, repeated relapses, no validation, daily intractable headaches → developed active suicidality with a forming plan.
- As a psychiatric nurse, she **recognised the danger of the relief she felt from having a plan** ("the patients who get a sudden lift have made the decision").
- Disclosed to two close nurse friends over coffee. They insisted she call her GP.
- Her hesitation: "I didn't want to give the doctors the pleasure of saying 'didn't we tell you it was in your head.'"
- GP put her on a **mild SSRI**. Suicidal ideation stopped within days. Has not returned.

> "If I could say anything today to people listening who are struggling and worried about the stigma of mental health: it was the best decision I made at that time in my life." — Mulder, ep #48

She explicitly references LC patients who have died by suicide. The wiki should treat this as a load-bearing part of LC clinical care, not an adjunct.

## Eric-relevant signal

- **Stellenbosch route** (Pretorius's collaborator network) is a real alternative to Mülheim — 3-week waitlist vs 6+ months, much cheaper, same diagnostic and similar protocol. *If* Eric ever considers triple therapy, this is the lower-friction option.
- **Triple therapy late-stage outcome shape**: improvements in sleep, circulation, sensory, food tolerance, limb pain — **not recovery**. Two patient cases now (Khan #37 + Mulder #48), both `helped_partial`. Convergent improvements pattern.
- **Anaemia from menstrual bleeding** is a documented hazard of triple therapy in pre-menopausal women — Eric's not at risk for this specific issue but Eric's antihistamine + autonomic stack would need a careful interaction review with anticoagulation.
- **Mental-health red flag.** If Eric, or anyone Eric cares about with LC, ever reaches the dark headspace Mulder describes, **the Mulder pattern (delay → admit → SSRI within days → ideation stops) is the right one to remember**. Don't wait. Don't worry about stigma. The medication works for this specific use case in LC.
- **Cranial sacral therapy** is a new datapoint — placeholder page created.
- **HBOT** finally has a patient case logged (`helped_partial`).
- **Lumbrokinase + serrapeptase** as a self-managed natural blood-thinner stack post-prescription-anticoagulation is a reasonable maintenance idea for some patients (do not extrapolate to Eric).
