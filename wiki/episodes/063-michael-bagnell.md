# #63 — Dr Michael Bagnell — Long Covid and the brain

Dr Bagnell is a US-based functional neurologist with 33 years' clinical experience, specialising in **brain-based health care** — i.e. asking which brain region's dysfunction may explain a presentation. The episode is a wide-ranging argument that **brain stem dysfunction** is the central nervous-system substrate of much of Long Covid's symptom set, and that there are concrete, low-invasive rehabilitation tools to retrain it.

## The brain-stem hypothesis

Bagnell aligns with [[avindra-nath]]'s NIH ME/CFS work: SARS-CoV-2 binds preferentially to ACE2 receptors, which are densely expressed in the **brain stem** (alongside cortex, amygdala). The brain stem houses the autonomic nervous system. Damage / inflammation here would explain the entire dysautonomia symptom list — orthostatic intolerance, tachycardia, blood pooling, GI dysmotility, breathing irregularity, temperature dysregulation, sleep disruption, sensory sensitivity. He frames this as the **30,000-foot view** that subsumes the three competing camps (microclots, mitochondrial dysfunction, dysautonomia) — the three may share an upstream brain-stem-inflammation cause.

Cross-ref: he cites a paper *"Persistent brain stem dysfunction in long Covid: a hypothesis"* — sharable as a reading.

## Diagnosis at home (POTS-style)

He recommends Eric-applicable home testing:
1. Lie down 3 minutes, take HR + BP with a wrist cuff (saves baseline).
2. Stand up rapidly (near a wall), retake HR + BP.
3. **Heart rate jump >30 bpm** = POTS-pattern dysautonomia. (Or large BP swing + symptoms = orthostatic intolerance.)
4. The COMPASS-31 self-rating questionnaire is downloadable from his website for free.

## The rehab toolkit

Brain-stem rehab is **not linear** (one symptom = one drug). Brain regions have many inputs and outputs. He emphasises a "non-cookbook" approach. Tools, ranked low-invasive → invasive:

- **Eye movements** — horizontal/vertical/fast/slow tracking, convergence work (vision therapy)
- **Gargling** (strong, water hitting back of throat) — vagal stimulation via cranial nerves
- **Singing / phonating** — vagal stimulation
- **Cross-crawl movements** — knee-to-opposite-hand, lying or standing — activates lower brain stem (heart/gut control region)
- **Cold-water immersion** — start with face dunks (10–20 sec), progress to cold-shower-on-head. *"Believe me when I say these things are worth your trial."*
- **Primitive reflex integration** — Moro reflex (startle) is the most clinically relevant; if it's re-emerged, upper brain stem is destabilised. Check via "do you startle easily?" Worth googling and addressing if positive.
- **Vagal nerve stimulation devices** — gammaCore (cervical, FDA-approved for migraine/cluster headache, rentable), or the cheap clip-on aural devices. Mechanism: retrograde activation up to cortex (mood) and downward to gut (microbiome). **Contraindicated** if pacemaker, neck stent, or very low BP.

He stresses: **monitor HR/BP during rehab**. A 45-min PT slot may need to stop after 7 min for 15-min rest. Push past that and you crash. This is the same pacing principle Eric already runs.

## "Slow medicine" framing

A repeated theme: medicine is moving too fast. Empathy is the first casualty when time runs out. He argues for a "slow medicine" movement modeled on Italy's slow-food movement — *"are you caring for the patient properly?"*

## Pre-existing vulnerabilities ("perfect storm")

Concussion history, ADHD, anxiety, OCD, depression — these all suggest pre-existing brain-stem destabilisation. Compensation worked pre-Covid; viral inflammation tipped it over. Logged as: most LC patients had *some* pre-existing neurologic compensation, just well-hidden.

## Eric-relevance

- Confirms the brain-stem / autonomic frame Eric is already operating under (POTS, mestinon, ivabradine).
- **Practical actionables**: gargling, primitive-reflex check, cross-crawl, cold-shower-on-head are all at-home, free, low-risk.
- **gammaCore vagal nerve stimulation** worth flagging for Eric — rentable in US, evidence in migraine/cluster headache, plausible mechanism for autonomic regulation.
- **Vision therapy / eye-tracking issues** — if Eric notices reading difficulty or convergence weirdness, vision therapy is the angle (not stronger glasses).
