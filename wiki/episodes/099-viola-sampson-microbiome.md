# #99 — Viola Sampson: Long Covid & the Microbiome

UK-based registered microbiome analyst; practitioner-side recovered ME/CFS patient (10+ years ill from age 19). After recovery via cranial-sacral therapy + psychotherapy + herbalism, persisted lifelong gut symptoms (lectin-night-shade-gluten-dairy reactive) eventually drove her to the microbiome literature → started experimenting on herself + clients in 2015 → quietly launched practice 2018 → now leads a group practice (The Microbiome Group) with multi-practitioner case discussion daily.

The wiki's deepest microbiome anchor — significantly extends [[gut-health]] and [[probiotics]] beyond previous coverage.

## Microbiome 101 (consolidated)

The microbiome is **everywhere a body meets its environment**: gut, oral, skin, respiratory, vaginal. Most LC discussion is about the **gut microbiome**, and within that mostly **bacteria** — but it includes:
- **Bacteria** (best-studied)
- **Yeasts / fungi** (e.g., Candida)
- **Archaea**
- **Single-celled organisms** (amoebae)
- **Viruses** — the **virome**, including bacteriophages that police bacterial populations

The gut as **continuous with soil** and planetary-health systems. Beneficial yeasts, bacteria, and viruses keep the ecosystem in dynamic stability.

## Two key microbiome states for LC

The most important practical distinction:
- **Pro-inflammatory microbiome** — gram-negative bacteria releasing LPS; leaky-gut → systemic inflammation → fatigue, brain fog, joint pain.
- **Anti-inflammatory microbiome** — beneficial bacteria producing **short-chain fatty acids** (e.g., butyrate) → local + systemic anti-inflammatory effects + nervous-system support.

LC tends to push the microbiome toward pro-inflammatory; pre-LC bacterial state matters for severity / vulnerability.

## Predisposing factors that worsen LC microbiome impact

She names several risk factors that make LC more likely to cause significant gut dysbiosis:

- **Slow transit time / constipation / bloating / IBS-C** — pre-existing.
- **PPI use** — suppresses stomach acid → small-intestinal bacterial overgrowth (SIBO) → IBS-like symptoms.
- **Antihistamines that suppress stomach acid** (H2-blockers like famotidine) — same SIBO risk as PPIs.
- **Antibiotic exposure within 6 months** of COVID infection.
- **ADHD** (associated with histamine picture).
- **MCAS** — mast cells line the gut; specific bacteria stabilise mast cells.

## Why gut testing matters

The Microbiome Group uses **whole-microbiome testing** (e.g., **Biomesight**) — tests *thousands* of bacterial species, not just 30-40 like many functional-medicine practitioners. Because:
- Only a fraction have known function.
- Diverse species means subtle states matter.
- Specific therapeutic strains can be matched to gut profile.

## The intervention warnings (a critical lever)

Sampson is **emphatic about not aggressively de-microbiome-ing yourself**:

- **First do no harm.** Never use broad-spectrum antimicrobials on a microbiome you don't understand.
- **Berberine, oregano essential oil** — common in functional medicine — can severely damage beneficial bacteria.
- **You can't recolonise extinct species.** Once a beneficial species is wiped out from your gut, it doesn't come back via probiotics (probiotics are transient — persist for days to weeks).
- **The "weed-seed-feed" protocol from functional medicine doesn't make ecological sense** — you can't re-seed without fecal microbiota transplant (FMT).

This is the most important wiki signal for any LC patient considering aggressive gut-protocols. Cross-ref [[070-lorrie-rivers]] (anti-parasitic-protocol minority position) — **#99 Sampson is the anti-aggressive-antimicrobial counter-voice**.

## What to actually do — the "feed beneficial bacteria" model

The wiki's strongest practical microbiome guidance comes from this episode:

### Prebiotics > Probiotics for long-term shifts

- **Probiotics** = live bacteria → transient, do good in passing through, don't persist.
- **Prebiotics** = fibre / compounds that *feed* beneficial bacteria → **create longlasting large-scale shifts.**

### Specific named probiotics + their evidence base

| Strain (commercial product) | What it's for | Histamine-friendly? |
|---|---|---|
| **L. rhamnosus Rosell-52 + B. longum R0175** (Optibac Every Day — note: not "Extra Strength" or "Max") | Anxiety / stress / nervous-system regulation; calming effects | ✓ Yes |
| **L. reuteri DSM 17938** (BioGaia Protectis) | Slow transit / constipation / SIBO-adjacent / post-meal-slump symptoms | ✓ Generally |
| **L. rhamnosus GG (LGG)** (Culturelle) | Allergies, viral infection prevention + length reduction, **degrades histamine in the gut**, reduces blood inflammation markers | ✓ Yes — actively helpful |

### Specific named prebiotic — GOS

- **Galactooligosaccharides (GOS)** — feeds **bifidobacterium**, the genus most consistently low in chronic-fatigue / LC patients.
- Bifidobacterium interfaces with **gut-brain axis** + autonomic nervous system.
- Brand: **Bimuno** is one option; **Vivomixx Healthcare GOS** is "the better version."
- **General-tolerance**: usually positive across patients; minority of constipation/bloating-presenting patients may not tolerate.

## Microbiome ↔ inflammation ↔ autonomic system loop

The bidirectional cycle:
- **Gut state → vagus nerve → brain state.**
- **Vagus tone → reduces gut inflammation.**
- Beneficial bacteria → SCFA (esp. butyrate) → systemic anti-inflammatory + nervous-system support.

This is the wiki's clearest synthesis of why **gut + autonomic + immune are inseparable** in LC. Cross-ref [[gut-health]], [[autonomic-dysfunction]], [[mcas]].

## On viral persistence in the gut microbiome

- Lancet (2023, week before recording) literature review: **viral persistence in children's intestines** found in 2-3 cases.
- Appendix: post-acute viral persistence is documented.
- The **virome** angle is under-studied; bacteriophages and persistent SARS-CoV-2 remnants both relevant.
- Microbiome Group case-study (Aug-Sept 2022, N=6) was Sampson's first formal LC microbiome intervention study — informed her current practice.

## Mediterranean diet + polyphenols + olives

The diet most consistently associated with healthy long-term outcomes is the **Mediterranean diet**:
- Olives, sauerkraut, kimchi, yogurt (if histamine-tolerated).
- **Polyphenols = colour + bitter flavour** in food → fed via brightly-coloured plants + bitter tannins.
- **Olives** specifically as a "low-spoon" gut-feeder — keeps in a jar, no prep, high polyphenol density. *"Like you're tending an incredible ecosystem."*

## On not crash-dieting your way back

Restrictive diets (gluten-free + dairy-free + nightshade-free etc) often signal **microbiome repair is needed**. Sampson's progress from "could only eat pure protein + plain rice" to "I eat everything now, including croissants and pizza" was driven by microbiome work. **Don't stay restricted longer than necessary** — joy of food matters; mental load of restriction is real.

## Cranial-sacral therapy

Important to note for cross-referencing: Sampson credits her own ME/CFS recovery centrally to **cranial-sacral therapy** (vagus-nerve stimulation through touch). Trained as a CST practitioner for 15 years specialising in chronic fatigue before her microbiome pivot. Not the focus of this episode but anchors a lineage.

## Eric-relevant intake (high-density)

- **Optibac Every Day** (L. rhamnosus Rosell-52 + B. longum R0175) — specific probiotic recommendation for nervous-system regulation. Histamine-friendly. Non-prescription. Worth flagging for self-experiment. **Distinct from "Extra Strength" and "Max" variants** — get the "Every Day" SKU.
- **L. rhamnosus GG (Culturelle)** — anti-allergy, histamine-degrading, viral-infection-prevention. Particularly relevant given Eric's reinfection concern + antihistamine use.
- **Bimuno / GOS prebiotic** — feeds bifidobacterium, generally well-tolerated, supports nervous-system regulation. Low-spoon trial candidate.
- **Don't take broad-spectrum antimicrobials on the gut without testing.** This rules out a particular category of "what should I try" experiments — useful boundary.
- **Mediterranean diet + polyphenols** — Eric's general direction probably already aligns; **olives as low-spoon gut-feeder** is a specific operationalisable signal.
- **Antihistamine-as-PPI-analogue** — H2-blockers (famotidine) suppress stomach acid → SIBO risk. Eric uses Pepcid p.r.n., so this is a real-time consideration if intake increases.
- **The "ecosystem you tend" framing** — direct counterweight to the "kill the bad bugs" functional-medicine narrative. Worth holding when Eric encounters that framing in popular LC content.
