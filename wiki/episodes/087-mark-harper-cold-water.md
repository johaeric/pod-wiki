# #87 — Mark Harper on Cold Water for Long Covid

Anaesthetist (Brighton + Norway, half each); came to cold-water research via his original PhD on **preventing perioperative hypothermia** (people getting cold during surgery → more complications). Started open-water swimming when his pool closed → noticed remarkable post-swim wellbeing → noticed the surgery-stress-response and the cold-adaptation literature were describing the same thing. Wrote *Chill: The Cold Water Swim Cure*; runs ChillUK clinical-trial-backed introductions to cold water.

## Core claim

**Cold-water exposure as a stressor — in moderation — reduces baseline inflammation and trains the autonomic nervous system to swing more flexibly.** Same physiology as the surgery stress-response except controllable; can be applied to many post-viral / chronic-inflammation conditions.

## Mechanism (Harper's three layers)

### 1. Acute parasympathetic — face-in-water diving reflex

- Cold water on the face stimulates the **trigeminal nerve** → **parasympathetic** activation → heart rate drops.
- He demos this in schools: hands in iced water → no big effect; face in iced water → measurable HR drop after a brief delay.
- Evolutionarily this is the diving reflex (babies hold their breath under water).
- **Lowest-cost LC-applicable entry point: bowl of cold water on the face**. No swimming required.

### 2. Adaptive sympathetic attenuation — repeat exposure

- First few times in cold water: big sympathetic response (gasp, hyperventilation, HR ↑, BP ↑).
- After ~6 exposures (water <20°C, can be spaced over days, doesn't need a heroic dose): the response **attenuates**. Resting HR drops slightly. Resting BP drops slightly.
- Mapping to LC: the wiki's recurring picture of "stuck sympathetic overdrive" gets a directly trainable countermeasure.

### 3. Anti-inflammatory effect

- Severe covid is in large part an **inflammatory / quasi-anaphylactic** response.
- Adaptation to cold pushes inflammation from "pathological" toward "physiological" levels.
- Same mechanism family as exercise (also anti-inflammatory) — known to reduce risk of severe covid and improve vaccine effectiveness.
- For fibromyalgia (where they have data) and emerging signal for LC, cold adaptation reduces symptoms.

## Six rules (his book)

1. **Get your body in first**, then the face. (Sequence matters — don't trigger parasympathetic + sympathetic simultaneously.)
2. **Know how to get out** before you get in (Agnes Allen's rule: anything's easier to get into than out of).
3. **Get warm afterwards.** Skin water cools you fast; dry, dress, get out of the wind.
4. **Go in warm** — body warmed from the inside (exercise, food).
5. **Don't go alone in open water.**
6. **Make sure you know where you're going in.**

## "Minimise discomfort, maximise fun" (his condensed version)

- Wear shoes, gloves, swim hat — they don't reduce the benefit (the benefit is from body-trunk exposure).
- Forget wetsuits — too much faff for the dose required; a rashvest if you need warmth.

## How long, how cold (LC-specific)

- **Two to three minutes is enough.** Just long enough that the breath comes back.
- Water doesn't need to be especially cold — **anything below 20°C works**.
- Doesn't need to be daily — **once a week × 6 weeks works**. Even cold showers count (less effective, but measurable: one workplace study showed cold-shower group took 6 fewer sick days).
- The "1 minute per degree" rule he calls out as nonsense: too many variables (sleep, hormones, hydration, recent food, recent exertion) for a fixed dose to be right.

## On the morning-vs-afternoon temperature regulation problem (relevant to LC)

- Harper acknowledges LC patients have abnormal thermoregulation; some days even mild cold feels intolerable.
- Listen to the body. Days when it feels too cold, get out sooner.
- Doesn't necessarily wreck things — just shortens the dose.

## On post-exertional malaise (PEM)

- Jackie raises that she has *never* triggered PEM from cold water (despite triggering it from many other things including brushing teeth).
- Harper offers two hypotheses: (a) cold itself, (b) **water buoyancy supports the body, removing the postural / muscle exertion that triggers PEM in walking, etc.** This is also why swimming as exercise is uniquely friendly to chronic illness — the water carries the load.

## On the mental health side

Strong claim:
- ~60% of depression cases are inflammation-related — and respond to anti-inflammatories.
- Cold-water swimming → reduced inflammation → mental-health benefit not separable from the physical-health benefit.
- Pairs with **Jill Bolte Taylor's "step to the right"** framing: getting into cold water suspends the left-brain anxious narrative; you re-emerge in the right-brain present-moment state.
- Plus the **integrated package** of outdoor swimming: blue therapy + green therapy + social + autonomy + exercise — all independently health-promoting.

## "ChillUK" — the program

- 8-swim / 8-week (or 4-week intensive) programmes started in North Devon, expanding.
- Confidence-and-fun framing, not boot-camp.
- ~60% of participants continue after the programme.
- Other groups he names: Mental Health Swims (Meetup-style), Blue Tits.

## Quotes

> "Exposure to cold as a stress is good — like a workout. Don't pull the muscle. That's hypothermia."

> "1 minute per degree is wrong because anything that attaches a specific number is wrong — there are too many variables."

> "If you get out and you want to go back in, you've done it right."
