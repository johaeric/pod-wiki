# #109 — Julie Black: full LC recovery in ~3–4 months via Suzy Bolt + Alan Gordon's *The Way Out* + the Curable app

Scottish recovered LC patient — caught COVID early July 2022, recovered to "I don't have long covid anymore" within ~3–4 months of starting the **Curable app**, and is back competing in **endurance horse-riding** (50 km competition two weeks before recording, second-overall team result). **10th full recovery on the wiki, and the wiki's clearest single articulation of the mind-body / pain-reprocessing-therapy route to LC recovery.**

## Pre-illness context (anchor)

A model "perfect storm" lead-up the wiki should preserve as a representative pattern:

- **Cognitively demanding science-advisory role**, supportive workplace but constantly intense; "this is getting out of hand, isn't sustainable"
- **Endurance-riding event organising** — sitting at her computer evening after evening, plus cycling out to plan routes
- **Maintaining her own and her horse's competitive fitness**
- **Helping plan a friend's wedding**
- **Massive Garden** that "the list of things needing done never ends"
- **Probably perimenopausal** (40s, never addressed)
- **Acute trauma** ~1 month before COVID: her horse got stuck in a bog, fire-rescue + vets at the scene, came close to euthanasia. Horse fully recovered; Julie did not have time to process it. Waking nightmares persisted up to LC onset.
- **Looked after partner with severe acute COVID for a week** before catching it from him.

The wiki's pattern across recovery stories ([[094-jackies-recovery-story]], [[098-charlotte-hammond-recovery]], [[107-raelan-agle-recovery-stories]]) is consistent: virus = match, the prior load = the kindling. Julie articulates this most cleanly.

## Symptom profile

>20 symptoms on her list. Most striking:

- **Severe insomnia** — *zero sleep* for several weeks, hallucinatory; adrenaline waves "coursing through" her body
- **Breathlessness** even just sitting up in bed to eat
- **POTS-pattern**: heart pounding lying down; tachycardia + near-syncope on upright. Used a potty in the bedroom for ~1 month because she couldn't safely make it to the bathroom.
- **Histamine intolerance** — "fizzy feeling traveling through blood vessels" in body and head, dramatically resolved by low-histamine diet
- **Brain fog** ("doesn't seem like quite the right term — out of space and time")
- **Sensory overstimulation** — could not read, watch TV, listen to music
- **Daily panic attacks** (had never had one in her life)
- **Suicidal thoughts** very early in the arc — see anchor section below.

## Anchor: suicidal-ideation framing in early LC

> "I got so depressed I had suicidal thoughts. Looking back this was quite early on — people have been dealing with this for years and I'm sure they've had those thoughts. But it's quite scary how quickly they came on for me." — Julie Black, ep #109

Julie articulates a critical wiki signal Jackie also picks up: **the mental-health load of LC is under-discussed because patients fear the "it's just anxiety" gaslighting that follows.** The wiki's [[mental-health-and-suicidality]] theme is reinforced by this episode — early suicidal ideation is normal in LC, not a sign of psychiatric primary cause, and not stigma-loaded.

## What didn't work

- **Every supplement Under the Sun**, expensive premium brands, no isolatable benefit
- **Doom-loop Facebook groups** — "the kind whose main message is 'you will never recover; just learn to manage it; trying to get better will make you worse'." She believed it; planned her life as a disabled person; thought about whether to sell the horse or learn to enjoy him from a wheelchair. **Treated as harm by her own framing.**

## What worked — the recovery stack

In rough chronological order:

1. **Low-histamine diet** (early, from a Facebook tip) — dramatic relief of the "fizzy" body sensation + brain fog ("could have ended up much worse without that advice"). Did not address the other symptoms.
2. **Validating doctor** (not her GP) — couldn't *cure* but ran the right tests, ruled out structural pathology, validated her experience, explained dysautonomia umbrella in a way that re-framed many of her symptoms. The negative-test results became the *foundation* she could build a mind-body approach on (she could fully commit *because* there was no detectable damage).
3. **[[suzy-bolt-recovery|Suzy Bolt's yoga recovery program]]** + her Facebook group — breathing classes (in-bed initially), **yoga nidra** (the first thing that allowed her to *mentally* rest), very gentle yoga from bed → from mat. After ~1 month, big improvement.
4. **Alan Gordon's *The Way Out* (audiobook)** — book on chronic pain, but she "replaced 'pain' with 'symptoms'" and the framework mapped 1:1. **Game-changer for understanding the mind-body route is not "it's all in your head."**
5. **Curable app** (~$15) — Alan Gordon is a scientific advisor. She went all-in: full subscription, hours/day for weeks, exercises on repeat. **Within 2 weeks: chalk and cheese.** Within 2–3 months: "I don't have long covid anymore."

## Anchor framing: how Julie understood the mind-body route

> "The brain has a very crucial role in any type of illness, not just long covid. It doesn't mean it's all in your head in the way I'd understood that to mean." — Julie Black, ep #109

Operationally:

- The brain can produce real, measurable, physical symptoms — palpitations, breathlessness, insomnia, blood-vessel sensations — when it perceives danger
- The Curable approach: **stop sending the brain danger signals**. Catch and re-frame catastrophising thoughts (*"is that the helpful thought right now?"*), do somatic exercises that signal safety, work through trauma backlog, write to surface unconscious belief patterns
- This is **upstream of acceptance/mindfulness**: it's a deliberate retraining of the predictive-processing layer that decides whether a symptom *escalates* or *down-regulates*

## The "I don't have long covid anymore but I'm not recovered yet" zone

Julie articulates the same in-between zone Jackie #94 named: **the period where you stop *being sick* but are not yet living your full pre-illness life**. Her resolution: addressing the *anxiety about getting ill again* via continued Curable work + processing the bog-rescue trauma the app surfaced. This phase took another ~2–3 months.

> "I stopped being able to say 'I have long covid' but the constant thinking about it, the constant waiting for it to come back — that took separate work. The app's tools for trauma + anxiety are what cleared it." — Julie Black, ep #109

## Closing advice (anchor)

Three pieces, worth surfacing directly:

1. **"Submit to it now" ≠ "accept it forever."** The conflation is what blocks acceptance for most patients. Submitting to the current state lets the body rest; it does *not* mean the current state is permanent.
2. **Learn yoga nidra + meditation right from the beginning** — otherwise you're not really resting. Lying in bed catastrophising is not rest.
3. **Believe it's not forever.** "Whatever you're feeling today is not how you're going to feel tomorrow."

## Eric-relevance

- **Curable app + *The Way Out* book is the highest-evidence pain-reprocessing-therapy entry point** the wiki has surfaced for LC. Eric should know both exist and what they are. Cheap (~$15 + an audiobook); does not require a clinician.
- **The validation-via-negative-tests framing** is exactly what Eric needs as a scaffold: "I have ruled out structural pathology" is not "I am healthy" — it is "the mind-body route is now coherent for me to commit to."
- **Suzy Bolt's program** continues to accumulate evidence on the wiki (3 more wiki recoveries credit it). For UK-based patients in dysautonomia / breathing-pattern presentations, it is the lowest-cost most-LC-specific program option available.
- **Pre-illness lead-up pattern recognition**: chronic over-extension + acute trauma + acute viral hit is the wiki's most-replicated origin story. Eric's pattern fits.
- **The yoga-nidra-as-actual-rest framing** is portable: lying in bed worrying is not rest. Eric likely already knows this from his arc, but the formal articulation is useful.
