# #66 — Lauren Stiles — President & co-founder of Dysautonomia International

Lauren Stiles is the most authoritative US-based **patient-advocate-researcher** on dysautonomia / POTS. She co-founded [[dysautonomia-international]] (~2012), has supported the world's largest POTS research registry, and holds a Research Assistant Professor of Neurology appointment at Stony Brook University despite no MD or PhD (her background is law + marine biology). She is also a 12-year POTS patient — POTS triggered by a snowboarding concussion + likely viral co-trigger in 2010 and ultimately attributed to **Sjögren's syndrome**, treated with **IVIG (intravenous immunoglobulin)** which she calls the game-changer.

She got Covid in Feb 2020 and went on to develop a year-long worsening of her pre-existing POTS — i.e. she is a "LC plus" patient with a pre-existing diagnosis that flared.

## The dysautonomia umbrella (clarified)

She is emphatic that **"dysautonomia" is an umbrella, not a diagnosis**. If a clinician says "you have dysautonomia," the patient should ask "which one?" Subtypes she names:

- **POTS** (postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome) — most common
- **Orthostatic intolerance** (OI) — POTS without the tachycardia diagnostic threshold (probably same disease, different criteria)
- **Orthostatic hypotension** (OH) — more severe than POTS/OI, BP drop on standing, autonomic-failure pattern
- **Hyperhidrosis** — pathological excessive sweating
- **GI dysmotility** — gastroparesis, oesophageal dysmotility
- **Bladder dysfunction**
- **Dry eyes / dry mouth** (Sjögren's-overlap)

She frames mainstream pots as **not fatal** (no reported deaths from POTS itself) — important reassurance for newly-diagnosed patients in panic.

## The autonomic-treatment toolkit (most comprehensive single source in this wiki)

Self-management:
- **Salt 8–10 g/day + 3 L hydrating fluids/day** — must do both. Avoids straight 1g salt tablets (cause nausea); recommends Vitassium or whole-salt-on-food.
- **Compression** — abdominal compression most evidence-supported (most blood pools in the lower abdomen on standing, not the calves). Full leg + abdominal best. Granny-panties / Spanx as a cheap proxy.
- **Exercise** — important for red-blood-cell volume rebuild, but caveated for LC: pace yourself, expect setbacks, forgive yourself when you overdo it.
- **Sleep hygiene** — even one missed night dysregulates autonomic function in healthy people, more in POTS.
- **Diet** — research shows carbohydrates worsen POTS (more blood pooling in gut). Mediterranean / anti-inflammatory diet, omega-3s, smaller meals throughout day (graze rather than big-pasta-dinner).

Medications:
- **Beta blockers** — slow HR, can cause fatigue + brain fog
- **[[ivabradine]] / Corlanor** — slows HR without dropping BP, less fatigue/brain-fog, harder to get insurance coverage
- **[[midodrine]]** — vasoconstrictor, push blood up to brain, normalise BP, reduce brain fog
- **[[mestinon]]** (pyridostigmine) — boosts parasympathetic; **anti-inflammatory effect noted via vagal mechanism**. ~20% can't tolerate (diarrhoea), but if GI motility is too slow it's a feature not a bug. *"I'm hoping we're going to see some Mestinon clinical trials in long Covid."*
- **Antihistamines** (H1, OTC) — non-RCT but huge anecdotal patient signal in MCAS-overlap

## Trials that Dysautonomia International is funding

She announces (a wiki first-hear): a **long Covid POTS IVIG immunotherapy clinical trial** is being funded by Dysautonomia International. Other funded research: **LDN trial**, **ECP** (enhanced external counterpulsation — leg compression therapy), and autoantibody/immune-marker discovery in LC.

## Notable claims

- **67% of long Covid patients have moderate to severe dysautonomia** (Stanford collaboration, published).
- **50% of pre-COVID POTS was post-viral** (long-influenza, long-EBV, long-mycoplasma — we just didn't call it that). LC is therefore not a new disease class — it's a long-overdue reclassification.
- *"Build a bigger tent"* — argues LC advocacy must align with ME/CFS, POTS, MCAS, EDS, dysautonomia advocacy. Future pandemics are inevitable; healthcare systems need to be prepared for waves of post-infectious illness.
- **"There's no treatment for LC"** is a clinician knowledge gap, not reality. *"If you have long Covid POTS, that IS POTS. We have tools."*
