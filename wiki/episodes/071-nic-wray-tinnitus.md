# #71 — Nic Wray on Tinnitus

Communications manager at **Tinnitus UK**. The first Long Covid Podcast episode dedicated to tinnitus.

## What tinnitus is

A noise (ringing, buzzing, whining, music, or mixture) generated by the auditory system in the brain — not the ears — without an external source. **About 1 in 7 UK adults** experience it. About **1 in 3 people overall** have experienced tinnitus at some point. It can fluctuate (spikes and troughs) and severely impact mood, sleep, and concentration.

## Long Covid signal

- Anglia Ruskin international study (Aleks Brueckner) — early-pandemic survey: **~40% of UK respondents** reported tinnitus impacted by living through the pandemic; ~33% in the US (different lockdown intensities).
- Hospitalised covid patients: **~14%** reported new hearing problems after discharge.
- Recent Irish LC cohort study: **~38% of long-covid patients reported tinnitus**.

## Triggers (general + covid-specific)

Hearing loss, loud-noise exposure, stress and anxiety, ear infections, ear wax, neck/jaw problems — and now covid / long covid. **Spikes are usually triggered by change** — new infection, hearing change, new worry, environmental change (e.g. quiet WFH after busy office).

## What helps (tinnitus management)

> "There's no cure — but there's a lot that can be done. Most cases improve or even resolve through habituation as the brain stops listening to the sound."

- **Hearing aids** when there's hearing loss (treats the loss + the tinnitus together)
- **Quiet background sound** *below* the tinnitus volume (counterintuitive — louder masking can make the brain compete and ramp up). Natural sounds, music, fan, radio, dedicated tinnitus apps.
- **Stress reduction**: breathing exercises, meditation apps (try free first), relaxation
- **CBT** — most rigorously tested and effective treatment for tinnitus
- **Support groups + peer connection** — Tinnitus UK helpline, web chat, online support
- **Counselling** — counsellor doesn't need to know about tinnitus; CBT is about reframing the relationship to the sound

## Red flags (see GP)

- Tinnitus in **one ear only**
- **Pulsatile** (beats with heartbeat)
- Accompanied by **dizziness**
- **Sudden hearing loss** in one or both ears

## What does *not* help

- **Drugs** — there is no medication for tinnitus
- **Supplements** — explicitly warns against them
- **Avoiding all sound** — quiet environments make tinnitus stand out (the "birthday cake candle" analogy: a candle is invisible in daylight but vivid in a dark room)

## The "birthday cake candle" framing

Tinnitus is more intrusive in quiet spaces. Counterintuitively, gentle background sound *reduces* awareness because the brain stops focusing on the internal noise.

## Notable claims

> "There is no cure but there's a lot we can do."
> "Records of tinnitus go back to Assyrian kings — it's not new, not caused by 5G or microwaves."
> "About 1 in 7 UK adults — there's somebody with tinnitus in every supermarket queue."
