# #012 — Kelly Mitchell, the Breathing PT

**Guest**: Kelly Mitchell — specialist respiratory physiotherapist. NHS-trained 2007, specialised in respiratory 2014 (CF, bronchiectasis, COPD), left the trust in 2020 to specialise privately in **breathing pattern disorder** — including a large long-covid caseload.

Foundational episode for the **dysfunctional-breathing** lens that recurs across the podcast. Read alongside [[017-wibbs-coulson]] (yoga/movement angle), [[007-suzy-bolt]] (Suzy points patients to this kind of work), and [[008-rachel-whitfield]] (Rachel discovered her own faulty breathing through a remote breathing physio).

## The core thesis

> "Your body resets to a new, lower CO₂ baseline. You're walking around in a chronic alkalosis — vasoconstriction, electrolyte disturbance, reduced oxygen delivery to tissues. Symptoms follow." — Kelly, ep #12 (paraphrased)

Mechanism (as Kelly explains it):
- **Hyperventilation** → over-exhalation → low CO₂ (low end-tidal CO₂)
- Body becomes more alkaline → kidneys excrete bicarbonate to compensate
- Depleted bicarbonate impairs lactate clearance (relevant to PEM/heavy-legs)
- Low CO₂ → vasoconstriction (cold extremities, reduced cerebral perfusion → brain fog)
- Bohr effect inversion — haemoglobin holds onto O₂ instead of releasing at tissues. SatO₂ on the pulse-ox reads 99% but cells are oxygen-starved.
- Electrolyte disturbance (Mg, Ca, K) → muscle tremor, fasciculation, cramping

She cites a recently-released paper finding **74% of long-covid patients had low end-tidal CO₂** — indicative of chronic over-breathing.

## Why long covid drives breathing-pattern disorder

Three converging triggers:
1. The illness itself raises respiratory rate (it's a respiratory illness, often with fever).
2. Anxiety / fear of suffocation during the acute phase reinforces upper-chest mouth-breathing.
3. The "fragile homeostasis" persists *after* infection clears — the breathing pattern becomes self-sustaining.

Demographics over-represented for breathing pattern disorder are also over-represented for long covid:
- Women (progesterone is a respiratory stimulant; monthly variation)
- Asthmatics (40–50% have a breathing-pattern disorder)
- Voice professionals (teachers, call-centre workers, singers, priests)
- Athletes

Kelly's clinical impression is that **many long-covid patients had subtle dysfunctional breathing pre-covid** — covid exacerbated and made symptomatic what was previously latent.

## How to spot it

Symptom cluster — note how non-respiratory symptoms dominate:

- Air hunger, "can't get a satisfying breath"
- Chest pain, chest discomfort
- Pins and needles (fingers, toes, perioral)
- Dizziness, brain fog, fatigue
- **Post-exertional malaise**
- GI disturbance, anxiety, hypervigilance
- Cold hands/feet
- Muscle tremors, twitches

Key vital sign: **respiratory rate 8–12/min is healthy**; medical textbooks say 12–20 is normal but Kelly considers 17–18 already over-breathing if symptoms align. GPs typically miss this.

## What to do — Kelly's first-line advice

1. **Nose breathe.** The nose warms, humidifies, sterilises (nitric oxide), and produces nitric oxide which opens airways and has antiviral properties. Mouth breathing bypasses all of that. Most patients say "I can't breathe through my nose" — usually nose is patent, just feels tight because of resistance. Practice in a relaxed lying-down position; build slowly.
2. **Slow the rate.** Aim for 8–12/min. Many patients fight this with air hunger because their body has reset to wanting more breaths. Often needs guided rehab.
3. **Diaphragmatic / abdominal breathing.** At rest, no upper-chest movement should be visible. Self-test: hand on chest, hand on tummy, mirror; only the tummy hand should move.

Time frame: 6–8 weeks to retrain a breathing pattern minimum. Common saying in the field: "for every year you've been breathing poorly, expect a month to undo it."

## Pacing nuance

Kelly is **explicitly cautious about HR-pacing** for the dysautonomic subgroup — "we do not want to be throwing people up into exceptionally high heart rates on minimal exertion and holding them there." For her cohort with breathing-pattern disorder driving the symptoms, addressing the breathing comes *first*; pacing alone won't help if the underlying physiology is unresolved.

References [[008-rachel-whitfield]] specifically: Rachel's recovery worked through breathing + thought/belief work rather than rigid pacing. Different long-covid presentations need different approaches.

## Other tips for nervous-system regulation

- Adequate rest, hydration, eating well, supplementation as needed
- Cold-water immersion (vagal nerve stimulation) — recommended in passing
- Get outside, sunlight, walking (within tolerance)
- "Active rest" ≠ Netflix or scrolling. Genuine downshift: bath with no fan-noise, breath focus, 10 min/day minimum.
- Squid Game is *not* relaxation. Stress-coded media reactivates the sympathetic system.
