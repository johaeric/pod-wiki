# Episode 121 — Pamela Rose — Difficult Conversations in Chronic Illness

Pamela Rose was diagnosed with ME/CFS in 2010. Recovery took roughly 7 years. Now works full-time as a fatigue coach (says she may have been the first to use the title). Has worked with >1,000 clients across ME/CFS, fibromyalgia, post-viral fatigue, long Covid.

**Coaching model:** short engagements designed to give people the toolkit and then step back, not retain. Mix of pacing/energy management, emotional coping, mindset, nervous-system calming, problem-solving. No one-size-fits-all.

**Episode focus — communication challenges patients face:**
- *Loved ones who don't understand* — sometimes accept they won't and define support boundaries instead.
- *"You look fine"* — have a rehearsed reply ("wish the inside matched the outside").
- *Saying no* — fatigue happens to "nice people" who are bad at refusing.
- *People constantly asking how you are* — ask them to stop and instead trust you to flag good or bad days.
- *Helpful suggestions you don't want* — redirect: "I'm working on things at the moment, but if you hear about X please send it."
- *Bumping into people on a walk* — three categories: those who get it (be direct), those who half-get it (rehearsed deflection + topic change), strangers (don't tell unless practical).

**Practical strategies:**
1. Pick 2–3 trusted "inner circle" friends; let everyone else stay surface.
2. Create predictability — schedule regular check-ins instead of unscheduled drop-ins.
3. Rehearse hard sentences out loud beforehand.
4. People won't join the dots — you have to do it for them.
5. Don't beat yourself up over conversations that went badly. "Post-conversational concern" is universal.

> "Fatigue happens to nice people."
> — Pamela Rose, ep #121
