Amy Davies — embodiment coach and somatic practitioner. Recovered from chronic pain and fatigue she developed in 2018. Coaches LC, ME/CFS, and chronic pain clients.

## Internalised pressure — why it matters in LC

- Nervous system as threat-detector — pressure keeps the alarm bell ringing
- LC is itself stressful, on top of pre-existing pressure → the alarm bell can't shut off
- Slow, gradual signals of safety are what calm it; abrupt change reads as threat too

## External vs internalised pressure

- **External**: financial, relationship, illness symptoms, society — often unavoidable
- **Internalised**: self-imposed deadlines, perfectionism, self-criticism, achiever pattern, helper pattern, masking. *Often* unconscious until named.

## Common internalised-pressure patterns

- Pushing to recover by an arbitrary deadline
- Reading every piece of research / trying every supplement / doing every program
- "If I just try hard enough..." (the achiever cap that worked in school/work)
- Masking — appearing okay to others
- Self-critical inner drill sergeant when progress stalls
- Shame-spiral about being perfectionist about recovery

## Why the inner drill sergeant exists

- It's a protective part — it served you in school, work, life
- "Why wouldn't it step up to take charge of recovery?"
- The compassionate move is to *recognise the protective intent*, not shame the part

## What helps

- **Awareness with compassion** — notice the pattern without adding self-blame
- **The first arrow vs the second arrow** — the pain that's already happened (first) vs how you respond to it (second). The second is where internalised pressure adds suffering.
- **Reduce comparison** — the brain's gap-detection makes comparison painful; cultivated comparison is marketing.
- **Do less, more consistently** — even sub-therapeutic doses build the habit
- **Sit with not-knowing** — control-seeking is the opposite of what regulates the nervous system. The balm is often *support* (someone says "I don't know either, this is hard, I'll be here"), not *more strategy*.
- **Freeze response** — when overwhelmed, the body freezes. Tiniest possible action (toe wiggle, three breaths, notice the blanket is soft) is the right level — not a bigger to-do list.
- Watch for the language of urgency — when *everything* feels like it has to happen *right now*, you're in a stress state. Slow down.
