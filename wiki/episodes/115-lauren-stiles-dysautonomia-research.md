# #115 — Lauren Stiles return: a deep-dive into LC + dysautonomia research

Second appearance from Lauren Stiles ([[lauren-stiles]]; first at [[066-lauren-stiles]]). The wiki's most comprehensive **single-source LC dysautonomia research-pipeline** episode to date. Where #66 was the autonomic-treatment toolkit, **#115 is the trials and mechanism map**.

## Anchor: 70%+ of LC patients have clinically significant autonomic dysfunction

Stiles cites multiple lines of evidence converging on this. Mechanism is *upstream*, not just symptomatic: ANS regulates immune function, coagulation cascade, GI motility — so autonomic dysfunction connects to **microclots, viral persistence, immune dysregulation, and inflammatory mechanisms** simultaneously, rather than being a parallel mechanism.

## Anchor: ganglia damage as structural mechanism

New research (shared on Dysautonomia International social media the day before recording): **animal-model SARS-CoV-2 infections damage the autonomic ganglia structurally before respiratory symptoms appear.** Confirmed in two species. The sympathetic ganglia (chain of pearls along the spine) is rarely sampled at autopsy — Stiles successfully advocated for ganglia tissue collection in **NIH RECOVER autopsy**. Pathologists had to learn how to extract the ganglia.

ME/CFS autopsy literature already shows **ganglionitis** (decade-old reports). Bridges LC and ME/CFS as a shared structural lesion candidate.

## Anchor: the patient-knowledge → research-priority pipeline

Pre-Covid example Stiles uses to make the point:

- 2012: pots patients in support groups widely reported low-carb diets helping symptoms
- Stiles asked POTS experts; "shrugged shoulders, no data"
- Dysautonomia International put it on website **with explicit "no research, anecdote only" caveat** — got yelled at by clinicians
- Years later: Vanderbilt RCT — pots patients have abnormal glucose responses; orthostatic tachycardia worsens dramatically after carb intake; **clinician guidance now recommends low-carb POTS trial**

Generalisation: **the well-organised LC patient community is a primary research-priority signal** — clinician-researchers should be tuning to it, not dismissing it. Stiles names a few current researchers who do (Putrino, Iwasaki, Proal).

## Anchor: vagus nerve stimulation mechanism (Kevin Tracy lineage)

Stiles fan-girls **Kevin Tracy** (Feinstein Institutes) — the researcher who showed that vagal stimulation reduces inflammatory markers. Downstream studies have put **drug-resistant Crohn's** and **drug-resistant rheumatoid arthritis** into remission via VNS. Mechanism: vagal anti-inflammatory pathway (acetylcholine → α7nAChR → suppressed cytokine release).

Why this matters for LC: if much of LC is inflammation-driven, **VNS is the safest mechanism-targeted lever**. Stiles practices it herself: **VNS device before bed** as a sleep + parasympathetic intervention; reports she falls asleep faster.

## Anchor: cure as remission, not eradication

Stiles articulates a frame the wiki should hold:

> "Very few diseases have a permanent cure. Of 12,000 known diseases, almost none have it. The realistic goal is treatments that put people in remission or stable disease where they can work and raise their children. Patients still want the cure — but you'll also take the 10% improvement, then the 20%, until you can enjoy your life again."

Pairs with [[107-raelan-agle-recovery-stories]] hope-as-input — Stiles is naming the *outcomes architecture*; Raelan is naming the *psychological architecture*.

## Eric-relevance

- **VNS / vagus-nerve TENS** ([[vagus-nerve-tens]]) gets a substantial new evidence row — Stiles uses it personally for sleep, and 3 funded VNS-in-POTS trials are reporting results. **Eric should know VNS exists as a tool and what the mechanism is** (Kevin Tracy lineage).
- **Ivabradine** ([[ivabradine]]) is now in **two NIH-funded LC-POTS trials** — strongest signal yet that Eric's current med is the right architecture.
- **NIH IVIG trial** has strict autoimmune-marker eligibility — Eric likely doesn't qualify but should know the autoimmune-LC-pots subtype is being formally characterised. If his profile shifts (autoantibodies appear), this becomes Eric-relevant.
- **VYVGART (efgartigimod)** for LC POTS — phase-3 expansion. Already FDA-approved for myasthenia gravis. **Worth flagging on the long-watch list.** Mechanism: FcRn inhibition reduces pathogenic IgG; tests the autoimmune hypothesis.
- **Low-carb POTS data** is now research-backed — Vanderbilt RCT. Eric can experimentally trial low-carb meals and track stand-test response without a clinician.
- **Ganglia damage as structural mechanism** explains why some LC pots is permanent vs. some resolves — open question, not actionable yet.
- **Harvard Peter Novak IVIG trial** explicitly **doesn't require POTS criteria** — first trial that recognises the *broader* LC dysautonomia pool that doesn't meet 30bpm POTS threshold. Eric should track who else takes this approach.
