# #79 — Michael Towers: Chronic Fatigue Superhero

Comic-book creator + advocate. ~20 years of post-viral on/off illness; severe-bedridden phase of ~1 year (couldn't feed himself, walk, talk, sit up).

## What the comic is

Single-panel jokes about the chronic-illness experience: tests, treatments, foggy thinking, well-meaning friends, gallows humour. **Drawn from a bedridden patient's perspective for a chronic-illness audience.** Healthy people get half the jokes; people who've lived it get all of them.

## How it was made — the meta-story

Created on **the first day he was completely bedridden** — first time he couldn't sit up.

> "I don't really know where the idea came from. It just popped into my head that it would be funny to have a comic character who's a superhero who wants to help — but by the time he gets there, he's tired from travelling."

Five-year build:
- He drew (pencil); his wife coloured.
- **8-month gap** mid-project where he couldn't lift his hand at all.
- Multiple 3-6-month gaps where drawing was impossible.
- Rebuilt the early panels digitally on a tablet to assemble into a printable book.
- Eventually: 52 panels → a book → a sequel underway.

> "I just did my five minutes when I could. At the end of however long that took — five years — I had something."

## The principle: "if you can't do it the way you used to, do it 5 minutes at a time"

Towers names a pattern that shows up repeatedly in this wiki (Suzy Bolt #7/#77's 5% stretch; Jackie Baxter on radical rest):

- The pressure to "do it properly" is largely **self-imposed**.
- "I can't do a 4-hour walk so I won't go for a walk" → wrong default.
- 5 minutes, repeatedly, accumulates.
- Compatible with PEM-honouring pacing if the 5 minutes is genuinely under the threshold.

## "Chronic fatigue superhero is not me — it is all of us"

The character exists because the cohort is **giving people who are very ill themselves but who reach out to others anyway**. Towers names this as the defining trait of the chronic-illness community — every advocate / coach / writer in this wiki fits the pattern.

## Why dark humour works inside the cohort but lands wrong outside

Healthy friends often respond with sympathy or discomfort to the comics. Members of the chronic-illness community laugh out loud. The same panel is **gallows humour** to one audience and **cosmic horror** to another. This pattern explains why outsider attempts to make jokes about LC tend to fail — license is in lived experience, not in good intentions.

## Two favourite panels

1. **The alien probing one** — "I've been abducted by aliens, they put me on a table and prodded me!" Reply: "Same thing happened to me today, but it was at my gastroenterologist." (Towers's own favourite for the laugh.)
2. **The wheelchair-and-butterfly one** — superhero in a chair pushed by his girlfriend, chasing a butterfly. (His sentimental favourite.)

## On collaboration as a virtue

Pushes back on the cult of independence: "We talk about how important independence is. But sometimes the right answer is to lean on someone — partner, friend, community — and let them pick up the parts you can't do." Wife coloured in; community now contributes ideas; book wouldn't exist without both.

## On the gain side of LC

> "I would not have understood this condition before. I would have been sympathetic but I wouldn't have understood. Now I know thousands of people with thousands of life experiences. It's actually quite a blessing — not the condition, but the range of people you connect with through it."

## Eric-relevant intake

- The **"5 minutes at a time" principle** is directly transferable to Eric's own creative/work output during energy-limited weeks. Same shape as Bolt's 5%-stretch and Jackie's radical-rest framework — three independent confirmations.
- The **severe-bedridden cohort** gets its first wiki representation here: the cohort whose experience is *invisible even to the rest of the LC community*. Worth holding in mind when assessing severity scales.
- The "license to joke" frame is useful — Eric can use this to manage his own dark humour around LC without worrying that it minimises severity.
