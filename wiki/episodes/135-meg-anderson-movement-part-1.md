# Episode 135 — Dr Meg Anderson — Movement Part 1: Getting Ready

US doctor of physical therapy (DPT, ~10 yrs experience) who pivoted entirely into virtual LC rehab after 2020 hospital surge. Co-runs the [[energy-expansion-project]] with [[lily-spechler]]. This is **Part 1 of 2** on movement / exercise in LC — Part 1 is *when you are ready*, Part 2 is *how to actually do it*.

## The core thesis

**You can't safely add movement until upstream stability is in place.** Most "exercise didn't work for me" stories are people trying to exercise before their physiology is ready. Once the foundation is built, gentle movement is essential — deconditioning is itself a risk.

## Why move in LC at all

- POTS / autonomic dysfunction has muscle-pump and nervous-system components. **Muscles physically pump fluid back to the heart**; if they're weak, orthostatic challenges get worse. Re-training muscle and the autonomic reflex is part of the recovery substrate.
- The end-game is **autonomic resilience**, not just symptom management. Compression / hydration / salt get you through the day; the muscle-pump and nervous-system retraining is what restores the underlying mechanism.

## The RV CAMPING acronym (preconditioning checklist)

Anderson's structured framework for what must be in place before adding intentional movement:

- **R** — Respiratory / breathing dysfunction corrected. Resting respiratory rate close to ~6 breaths/min (normal range 6–20); diaphragm control adequate.
- **V** — Vagus nerve reset (parasympathetic dominance achievable on demand).
- **C** — Consistency in a daily routine.
- **A** — Activity modification (true sub-threshold pacing).
- **M** — Monitoring (HR, symptoms, recovery).
- **P** — Pacing & Positioning (incl. horizontal-first work).
- **I** — Intake (metabolic needs met — calories, protein, macros, micros). Without this, exercise causes deeper energy deficit.
- **N** — Normalising (some level of mass-cell / MCAS stability — exercise is inflammatory, requires a body that can clear inflammation).
- **G** — Grounding & Gratitude (nervous-system anchor practices).

## What absolute Anderson rules look like

- **Won't guide anyone into movement until breathing dysfunction is corrected.** A high resting respiratory rate guarantees a higher exercise rate; you reinforce the dysfunction.
- **Won't guide anyone with active mass-cell dysregulation into structured exercise.** Exercise is inflammatory — short-term up, long-term down — but only if the body can flush the short-term inflammation. MCAS needs to be partially under control first.
- **Calorie/protein deficit kills any exercise plan.** Add movement before metabolic needs are met → deeper deficit, predictable backslide.

## Why "exercise" gets confusion

- "Exercise" in the general culture means high-intensity, sweating, going to failure. None of that applies in LC.
- In ICU rehab, "exercise" can be rolling over in bed with three nurses lifting you. Anderson's PT-trained instinct: **the threshold is much lower than people in the home / community context expect.** Years on a sofa = standardly-unfit-by-the-clock-of-deconditioning, even if you don't *feel* like an ICU patient.

## Mechanism on inflammation

Exercise is a planned stress → short-term inflammation → long-term *reduction* in systemic inflammation. The catch: in MCAS-overlap LC, the short-term peak is way too big and the body can't flush it. Stabilise mast cells first.

## Anxiety + exercise

Anderson takes exposure-style positioning: start with the most ridiculously small dose ("1 minute of X"), get the win under the belt, repeat. Goal is not to push to next level — it's to give the nervous system **proof of safety** through repeated benign repetitions. She supplements with **reset-breathwork** for patients who can't tolerate any traditional exercise — a long-form breath protocol that simulates the sympathetic/parasympathetic cycle exercise would otherwise produce, at far lower physical cost.
