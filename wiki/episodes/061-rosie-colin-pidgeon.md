# #61 — Rosie & Colin Pidgeon — Long Covid in young people, and Rosie's artwork

Rosie Pidgeon got LC at age 16 (start of school year, ~2021). Symptoms: dizziness, fainting on getting up, **constant headache for over a year**, exhaustion, muscle pains. Father Colin describes the family-side of paediatric LC: working from a café near her school so he could pick her up within 30 min when she crashed; Rosie tried to attend school but mostly only managed an hour every few weeks (an art class).

Diagnosis route: never tested positive (test access timing); GP diagnosed clinically after a battery of normal blood tests. Colin echoes the "all normal is the worst kind of normal" frame Eric will recognise — *"part of the LC experience, all tests are normal"*.

Recovery trajectory at recording: substantial improvement from 6 months earlier, attributed (with explicit causal hedging) to a **bundle**: better pacing, **low-dose naltrexone (LDN) started in April**, and **acupuncture** started at the same time. Improvement also coincides with simply more time. Colin: *"in some ways we don't care what's helping you know we just take it and Bank it."* — outcome bucket: `helped_partial` for both LDN and acupuncture, with explicit attribution-uncertainty noted.

Self-management strategies Rosie has developed:
- **Ice packs** for headache/migraine
- Warm baths with bath salts for muscle pain
- Hot water bottles
- Routine of knowing what triggers worse fatigue/headache (i.e. pacing learned by trial)

## Notable claims and themes

- **Validation by clinician** as a turning point: Rosie's consultant said *"don't let anyone tell you this isn't real"* and *"I don't really know what to do here but I am not going to do nothing"*. This single sentence is logged as a recurrent recovery-relevant moment across the wiki ([[healthcare-gaslighting]] counterpoint).
- **Disbelief of paediatric LC**: explicit pushback against the "kids don't get long covid" line. Rosie's exhibition is for [[long-covid-kids]] charity.
- **Art as outlet** — Rosie is a 17yo artist; her exhibition "I am still here" (December 2022) included a canvas-series with one paint dot for every UK child with LC. Captures expressive value of creative practice during illness — see [[065-long-covid-creativity-2022]].
- **Rosie achieved a B grade at A-level art** despite barely attending school — counter-data to the "young people will be fine" narrative.
