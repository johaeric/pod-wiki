# #180 — Jules Rogers — The Art of Saying No

Jules Rogers (already documented in [[138-jules-rogers-recovery]]) returns ~1 year on. She now runs a **6-week "Start Your Chronic Fatigue Recovery"** course; planning the boundaries module made her realise how load-bearing boundaries are in recovery and how absent they were from her own life pre-illness. This episode is her boundaries primer.

## A taxonomy

Boundaries protect energy and ensure your needs get met. Types:
- **Physical** — proximity, hugging, body-language contact.
- **Emotional** — capacity to absorb others' emotional needs in a given moment.
- **Material** — money, possessions, the borrowed-car-with-no-petrol pattern.
- **Mental** — disagreements, opinion-holding, "you have yours, I have mine."
- **Time** — agreeing to commitments only if they don't deplete you.

## The internal work, not the language

The hardest part isn't *saying* the boundary — it's sitting with the discomfort and guilt that follow, and not folding when others push back. Each held boundary builds a new neural pathway; familiarity makes it easier; people adjust to the "new you" once you say it a few times.

Pattern Jules names:
- **All-in commitment** — her recovery turned when she committed to "is this going to help me heal?" as the deciding question. Said no to most things for that period; missed out; healed; now does the things she'd said no to.
- **No is a complete sentence** — the over-explaining trap creates a door for renegotiation. American phrase she clings to now: "That doesn't work for me." No justification owed.
- **Holding yourself back when feeling better** is the hardest boundary. The strivers' nervous system races to gallop again the moment energy returns. Reins in hand.
- **Children + illness** — parenting guilt compounds illness guilt. Self-compassion becomes alien but essential. Oxygen-mask analogy — you can't help others if you don't put yours on first.

## Where to start

> "Look at the activities that deplete your energy the most — start a boundary there. That's where you'll get the biggest return." — Jules Rogers, ep #180
