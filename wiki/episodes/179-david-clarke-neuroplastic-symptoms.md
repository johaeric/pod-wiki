# #179 — Dr David Clarke — Decoding Your Body's Secret Language

US gastroenterologist + internal-medicine consultant, 40+ years' practice. Now president of **ATNS — Association for the Treatment of Neuroplastic Symptoms** (symptomatic.me). Has personally diagnosed and treated 7,000+ patients with brain-to-body / neuroplastic conditions. Career was redirected by a single patient who recovered fully in 3 months of weekly counselling.

## The framing

Western medicine assumes: real symptoms ↔ organ disease or structural damage. Clarke's lifelong rebuttal: real symptoms can come from **brain-generated nerve circuits that are physically changed but capable of change** ("neuroplastic"). Pain perception lives in the brain — examples: the construction worker whose nail passed cleanly between his toes but produced agonising pain (BMJ case); soldiers who don't feel their wounds until safe.

Estimates: **20% of the adult population** carries some form of neuroplastic condition. **40% of GP visits**. If routine diagnostic tests are normal, the conditional probability of a neuroplastic condition is >90%.

## Adverse Childhood Experiences (ACEs)

Definition Clarke uses: anything that happened to you as a child that, if it happened to a child you care about, would make you sad or angry — or that made you think less of yourself. Doesn't have to be sexual abuse or violence. A high-achieving athlete who never learned to play counts. The only-praised-when-better-than-siblings child counts.

ACEs surface as four adult patterns:
1. **Stressful personality traits** — low self-esteem, perfectionism, self-criticism, putting others first.
2. **Choice of high-need partners** — repeating the caretaker role.
3. **No regular time for joy / play with no purpose**.
4. **Triggers** — people, situations, events that re-activate the original adversity.
5. **Unrecognised emotions** — usually anger at someone the patient also loves; "dormant volcanoes with magma underneath."

## What treatment looks like

- **Imagine your own child going through what you went through** — the canonical exercise. Brings the magnitude of the burden into honest view.
- **Reframe self as heroic, not weak** — Olympic-weightlifter analogy: if you've been carrying that much, of course your body broke down; now you can put the weight down and see how strong you actually are.
- **Letters to people who hurt you** — not to mail, just to put the emotion into words. "The more emotion goes into words, the less it has to go into your body."
- **Set firm boundaries with current triggers** (often a parent still in the patient's life).
- For LC specifically: virus is *real* — but if a person is already heavily stressed when the virus arrives, the brain may use the symptoms as the only way it knows to enforce needed rest. Clarke isn't denying biology; he is naming a parallel layer.

> "We're trying to flip somebody's self-image from weak and second-rate to strong and heroic — which is only being more accurate." — Dr David Clarke, ep #179
