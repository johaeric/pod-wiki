# #62 — Prof Nisreen Alwan — public-health researcher and long-hauler

Nisreen Alwan is Professor of Public Health at the University of Southampton — and one of the **earliest and most cited academic voices** on Long Covid. She got Covid in March 2020 (first wave, no test, working from home through lockdown) and noticed within the first two weeks the **fluctuating pattern** that distinguished it from flu. By summer 2020 she was writing public articles about it.

She is associated with [[alyssa-perego]]'s coining of the term *"Long Covid"* — they co-authored 2020 pieces arguing for retaining the patient-coined term over more clinical alternatives like "post-Covid syndrome".

## What she's done in research

- **2020 survey** — n=2,500+ LC patients, mostly UK. Findings:
  - Characterised the **fluctuating, episodic** symptom pattern as distinctive.
  - Identified **stress as a symptom trigger** (often under-discussed because of stigma — guests fear being labelled "it's all stress" so don't volunteer it).
  - **Adequate rest in the first two weeks** correlated with better long-term outcome. Patients with the more severe / not-improving cluster were more likely to report inadequate rest in the first two weeks. *(Logged on the [[radical-rest]] page as observational evidence — not RCT, but the largest signal in this wiki for early rest as a protective factor.)*
- **Stigma scale** — co-developed with [[maria-pantelic]]. Captures three dimensions: external discrimination, internalised stigma (judging yourself for having it), and anticipated stigma (avoiding GP because you fear being disbelieved).
- **Inequalities arm of [[stimulate-icp]]** — Alwan leads the inequalities side of the trial. Argues the apparent under-representation of ethnic minorities in LC diagnoses is a **labelling and identification problem**, not a true incidence difference (given those groups had higher infection and severe-COVID rates).

## Notable claims

- **Lived experience is a strength, not a bias** in research. Argues the standard "you're biased because you have the condition" framing is wrong: *"you're somehow 100% objective if you've not experienced a certain condition like long Covid — that is wrong."* PhD students have written to her saying they hide their lived experience of LC for fear of being dismissed as biased; she says this needs to flip.
- **Stress triggers symptoms** is real and biological (well-established for other illness relapses), and the fact that the LC community under-discusses it is a stigma problem, not a non-finding.
- **Public-health failure of learning**: clean air, masks-as-tools, post-viral preparedness, health-equity work — none of it has been institutionalised post-pandemic. Compares mask resistance to historical seatbelt resistance.
- **Air quality vs water quality** — clean water is uncontroversial; air quality somehow is. *"It's really interesting isn't it."*

## Eric-relevance

Alwan's **"adequate rest in the first two weeks"** finding is the strongest population-level signal in this wiki for the radical-rest hypothesis (alongside Suzy Bolt #7's recovery story and clinical opinion from #34, #51, #55). Eric did not radically rest in his first two weeks — this is hindsight, not action.
