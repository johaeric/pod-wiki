# #45 — Keith Littlewood — Hormones, the thyroid & more

A heterodox episode worth tagging carefully. Keith Littlewood — UK PT-turned-rehab-turned-endocrinology researcher, currently a PhD candidate at the University of Reading studying pollutants and thyroid physiology — articulates a **bioenergetic / Ray-Peat-adjacent framework** for Long Covid: that "Long Covid" is not a useful diagnosis but a name for a heterogeneous bag of pre-existing endocrine, nutritional, and environmental deficits that the SARS-CoV-2 infection unmasks.

His specific frame is in **tension with both ercipedia mainstream and several other guests on this podcast** — particularly the keto / low-carb framing of [[moira-newiss]] (#21, #44). The wiki tracks both as live hypotheses; clinical decisions belong with Eric's specialists.

## The Littlewood frame

> "I don't think the idea of long covid as a diagnosis is appropriate. If you broke your leg and it took longer to heal, you wouldn't say you had long leg break. The deficit is individual." — Littlewood, ep #45

Three layers in his model:

1. **Pre-existing deficits / pollutant load.** Inherited endocrine disruption, environmental estrogenic compounds, vitamin A/D deficiency, progesterone deficiency, sub-clinical hypothyroidism, PUFA accumulation in cell membranes. People accumulate these silently across years — convergent with Newiss's "iceberg" model in #44 but with different specifics.
2. **Tipping infection.** SARS-CoV-2 unmasks the deficit. The variability in LC trajectories reflects the variability in pre-existing endocrine state.
3. **Recovery is restoration of the missing factor.** Identify the specific deficit (thyroid, progesterone, vitamin A, etc.) and restore it. Mainstream LC research is missing this because the clinical biochemistry tests are under-sensitive.

## Thyroid — under-diagnosed in LC

Littlewood's central clinical claim: **thyroid dysfunction is widely missed in LC populations** because:

- TSH is unreliable in stressed states. Adrenaline and cortisol suppress pituitary TSH output, so a stressed patient looks "fine" on TSH but is functionally hypothyroid.
- Reference ranges are calibrated against population averages that include many sub-clinical-hypothyroid people — "normal" is not "optimal."
- T4 → T3 conversion happens in liver, kidney, and other peripheral tissues; receptor-level pollution can block conversion or block downstream binding.

**Practical lever he recommends:** body temperature + pulse on waking, then again 45–60 min after a meal. Stable mammals run ~37°C; sustained sub-36°C readings + low resting heart rate (or compensatory high one) is suggestive of thyroid + adrenal dysfunction. Classic symptoms: cold hands/feet, hair loss, constipation, infertility, brain fog, fatigue, seasonal mood, infertility, low cholesterol (in sustained low-thyroid states).

He cites a paper (author not named in episode) showing patients with **lower body temperatures who couldn't thermoregulate** had the worst severe-COVID outcomes — implying low metabolic baseline → poor viral defence.

Cross-ref ercipedia for thyroid-LC peer-reviewed evidence.

## Progesterone — central to his framework

Specific claims:
- **Women with PCOS, PMS, endometriosis, fibroids, heavy bleeding** are likely progesterone-deficient.
- **Older men also have low progesterone** — and this is part of the male-LC-mortality story.
- Progesterone has **antiviral properties**, restores aerobic metabolism, used in traumatic brain injury research, present in nasal epithelial receptors, supports macrophage / phagocytosis function.
- Excess endogenous + environmental **estrogen** suppresses the thyroid; women therefore at higher autoimmune-thyroid risk.

If true, this is a **major underexamined LC mechanism**. The wiki holds it as a hypothesis pending more specific patient-outcome data.

## Bioenergetic / anti-PUFA / pro-saturated-fat / pro-glucose stance

Heterodox from mainstream nutrition:

- **Saturated fat is more protective** than unsaturated when used as fuel — fewer reactive byproducts ("toxic aldehydes") in oxidative metabolism.
- **PUFAs in cell membranes** (cardiolipin specifically) are damaged by oxidative stress; anti-cardiolipin antibodies in COVID may reflect membrane damage from excess unsaturated fat, not autoimmune attack.
- **Carbohydrate / glucose is not the enemy.** "When you go to A&E they don't put a steak or keto into your arm — they put sugar + saline." Frames acute stress recovery as glucose-dependent.
- **Critical of low-carb / keto** for the chronically ill, because chronic glucose deprivation drives adrenaline/cortisol to compensate, embedding stress.

This is in **direct tension with Newiss's keto recommendation in #44** and Sally Riggs's recovery story in #23. The wiki notes both positions; neither has Long-Covid RCT support.

## Methylene blue (closing recommendation)

Littlewood specifically endorses **methylene blue** (synthetic dye, historic uses: malaria, methemoglobinemia, urinary infection) for restoring physiology in LC patients **and vaccine-injured patients**. Claims it's been used to clear TB lung scarring by an Indian respiratory physiologist (no name given), and references SARS-CoV-2 inhibition papers.

Methylene blue acts as a **mitochondrial electron-transport-chain support agent** at low doses; at higher doses it's an MAO inhibitor (with all the SSRI / serotonin-syndrome risks that implies). **Cannot be combined with SSRIs / SNRIs.** Source mentioned: R&D Labs (off-label / unregulated).

This is the kind of intervention that needs careful clinician oversight; the podcast does not name a specific patient who used it with documented outcome.

## Bag breathing — CO₂ retention

Littlewood uses bag breathing extensively in his practice. Frame: most people are mildly hyperventilating → low CO₂ → poor oxygen dissociation (Bohr effect) → functional hypoxia despite adequate inhaled O₂. Breathing into a paper bag for short intervals raises CO₂ and **simulates a small altitude effect.**

Reported anecdotal benefits: improved blood-glucose response, improved sleep, energy, reduced menopausal hot flushes. Convergent with [[buteyko-breathing]] (#29 [[lorna-nicholson]]) but framed as a hormonal/metabolic lever rather than just respiratory retraining.

## Vitamin A from organ meats

Specifically calls out vegetarian / vegan diets as a possible LC vulnerability source via vitamin A deficiency (retinol from liver / muscle meat). Tags this with sun-exposure-vitamin-A interaction (skin retinol depletion in the sun).

Cross-ref ercipedia on vitamin A and viral immunity for evidence weight.

## The polypharmacy + diagnostic-test critique

> "Sometimes I get clients who come to me already taking 15 different supplements. Let's stop that and weedle it back." — Littlewood, ep #45

Convergent with [[joachim-gerlach]] #41 [[polypharmacy-warning]]. He also recommends **books** explicitly:

- Ivan Illich, *Medical Nemesis* (1976) — critique of medicalisation.
- Joseph Dumit, *Drugs for Life* — critique of disease-by-diagnosis pharma model.

## Markers of health (his summary check)

Littlewood's six personal-health markers worth tracking:

- **Energy**
- **Digestion**
- **Sleep**
- **Libido**
- **Absence of pain**
- **Mood**

If any are off, work backward to the nutritional / hormonal / environmental cause rather than treating the symptom.

## Eric-relevant signal

- **Thyroid testing.** Eric's last full thyroid panel? If TSH-only, repeat with full panel (free T4, free T3, reverse T3, TPO antibodies) given the Littlewood hypothesis. Body temperature + pulse on waking + post-meal is a free easy week-long self-experiment.
- **Methylene blue.** Eric is on antihistamines (zyrtec/pepcid) — these are not SSRIs and don't pose serotonin-syndrome risk. But methylene blue with mestinon, ivabradine, fludrocortisone needs a specialist sign-off — at minimum a pharmacist interaction check. **Do not start unilaterally.**
- **Bag breathing.** Free, low-risk experiment. Convergent with Lorna Nicholson's Buteyko #29 framing. May be worth 1-2 weeks observation if Eric's resting heart rate, sleep quality, or post-meal blood-glucose are at all variable.
- **The Newiss / Littlewood split on keto vs glucose.** Eric should not adopt either as dogma. Both have first-principles arguments; neither has LC-specific RCT evidence. Default to: real food + protein + adequate carbs + skip ultra-processed.
