# RA #235 — Raelan's 3-component recovery framework (host-solo synthesis)

**Format**: Host-solo synthesis. After ~100 recovery interviews, Raelan articulates the three-component recovery framework she's distilled. Tightest articulation of her synthesis on the corpus — pairs with [[107-raelan-agle-recovery-stories]] (LCP #107) which covers similar ground at more length.

## The three components

### 1. Nervous-system work — *virtually everyone needs it*

The autonomic nervous system / fight-flight-freeze framing. *"When we're not in the right state, the body isn't in a state to heal."* The body is *not* broken — it's responding correctly to a nervous system that has decided everything is dangerous. Tools mentioned: meditation, therapy, processing past trauma, daily strategies for safety/calm/happiness, leaving toxic environments / careers / relationships, positive affirmations, self-compassion (esp. for type-A overachievers).

**Critically**: many recoveries on the corpus did this without naming it as "nervous-system work" — they just *did* the right things. The pattern is what matters, not the vocabulary.

### 2. Brain retraining — *most people need it*

Treating the chronic-illness pattern as a *learned phobia map*: the body has built solidified neural pathways pairing "leaving the house = danger," "exercise = danger," "this food = danger," "social interaction = danger." Recovery requires un-learning these pairings, slowly, with deliberate exposure plus reframing.

This explains the "person heard the brain-retraining frame and 50% better in a day" stories that sound implausible — they're not implausible because the pathways are *learned* and once a patient internalises that they're learned, they can be unlearned. (Caveat: most patients take weeks or months, not days.)

**Critically**: many recovered patients did *gradual exposure therapy* without naming it that — same mechanism, different vocabulary. Sopfit's 30-second walks (Donna Shaw RA #228), Matt Butler's CBT behavioural experiments (RA #170), Kyle's welcoming-discomfort meditation (RA #232) are all the same family.

### 3. Underlying infections / gut / mould / metals / nutrition — *some people need it*

Real problems for the patients who have them. Worth investigating. **But** Raelan's strong claim: these are not the *primary drivers* for most LC / ME-CFS recoveries on her channel. Many recoveries happened *without* addressing these. Her position: necessary for some, not for most.

This is the most contested of the three on the LCP corpus — clinicians like Amy Proal (LCP #103), Tania Dempsey (LCP #38, #88), Asad Khan (LCP #37), Robert Groysman (LCP #76, #91, #92, #187) would weight #3 much more heavily. Hold both positions on the wiki; the patient population probably has subgroups for which each is right.

## Notable framing claims

- **Most recovered patients (including Raelan herself) "stumbled upon" recovery without understanding what they were doing.** Older recovery stories are useful but the *narrative* the patient gives may not be the actual mechanism.
- **The wiki's value is the meta-pattern, not any single story.** Raelan explicitly: "we put all these stories out there, and the patterns evolve."
- **Phobia-treatment analogy** as the strongest concrete frame for what brain retraining actually is. Worth holding for any future Eric conversation about why brain retraining isn't "thinking yourself well."
