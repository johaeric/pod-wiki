# RA #170 — Matt Butler's long covid (and ME/CFS) full recovery story

**Guest**: Matt Butler — 31, London UK. Full recovery from a probably-LC / probably-post-viral-CFS arc. Fits more into the ME/CFS frame than the typical LC-clinic frame; explicit on this in the interview.

## Pre-illness pattern (predisposing)

- 140-mile cycle the week before infection.
- High-pressure work; explicitly enjoyed *"riding waves of stress."*
- Multiple commitments: piano, orchestras, music, work, hobbies. *"Spread myself too thin."*
- Identity load was a load-bearing factor in the recovery.

## Long-covid course

- **Acute Covid**: mild, 1 jab at the time. Continued working through isolation.
- **~Weeks 3–4 post-acute**: spent a stressful emotional day + intense workout → sudden crash at his work desk. *"I just felt very unsafe."*
- **Months 1–3 post-crash**: severe boom-bust; bedbound much of the time. Heart-rate 140–150 bpm standing (POTS-like, undiagnosed). Insomnia, depression, severe PEM. Moved in with parents.
- **NHS long covid clinic**: scans clean, no treatments offered.

## What helped (his ranked recovery components)

1. **Pamela Rose fatigue-coach pacing course** ([[pamela-rose]], LCP #121) — first inflection. Daily/weekly schedule of activity + rest + joy. *"Having a plan was really motivating."*
2. **Yoga + meditation + breathing — 2 hrs/day, 2 months straight** — daily practice integrated into Pamela's schedule. *"The edge just went off slightly"* within a week. See [[meditation]], [[breathwork]], [[yoga]].
3. **CBT with a psychologist** — behavioural experiments, gradual exposure, challenging catastrophising thoughts. Tightest description on the corpus of CBT used as a *recovery-stage* tool, distinct from CBT-as-symptom-management. See [[cbt]].
4. **[[lightning-process]] — intro audio (~4-hr course) only.** Did not do the full LP workshop. Took the "I am safe" self-talk technique from it. Useful in-the-moment lever during walks where his nervous system was screaming danger.
5. **[[dynamic-neural-retraining-system|DNRS]] — full program.** Visualisations, talking-to-the-brain, exposure techniques, "stand on bits of paper around the floor" rounds. Hard work — *"every time I increased activity, halfway into the walk I felt the crash coming."*
6. **Lightweight training** as graduated re-entry. Couldn't do cardio (HR too erratic) but weight training built confidence.
7. **Identity work** — explicit recognition that the high-stress always-on identity needed to change. *"Change requires change."*

## What did not specifically help

- **Specialist symptom-by-symptom fixes** (vitamins, individual remedies for individual symptoms) — eventually stopped them; the brain-retraining work resolved them collectively.

## Notable framing claims

- **Belief is a precondition for recovery.** *"For half the period I was ill, deep down I didn't believe I'd recover. Only when I started believing did progress start."* Matches [[raelan-agle]]'s "hope as a recovery input" framing.
- **Forums can hurt.** *"There are places online that reinforce your beliefs you won't get better."* Same wisdom Kristyna (RA #147) and Erik Hajj (RA #150) name.
- **CBT-as-recovery-tool** — using CBT to do graded behavioural experiments around feared activities, distinct from CBT as a coping-with-illness tool. Worth attention as a low-cost first-step alternative for patients who can't afford a full DNRS / Gupta / ANS Rewire commitment.
- **"I am safe" language self-talk** — the simplest single technique he names, taken from the LP intro. Usable in-the-moment during walks, before crashes; *"the first time I tried it I walked 15 minutes."*
- **Identity is load-bearing.** Echoes Joanna Rayl LCP #56, Natalie Gold LCP #189, Amy Davies LCP #212 ("internalised pressure").
