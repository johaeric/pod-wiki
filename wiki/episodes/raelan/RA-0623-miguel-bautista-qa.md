# RA #623 — Miguel Bautista Q&A on ME/CFS + LC recovery

**Guest**: Miguel Bautista — coach, recovered patient. Identified in earlier ingests (Erik Hajj RA #150 credited him for the pendulum metaphor + pain-receptor visualisation). Now disambiguated in his own episode. Founder of **CFS Recovery** program. Author of *Thriver Not Survivor*. Has his own popular YouTube channel.

## Miguel's recovery story (background)

- Athlete pre-illness; football + wrestling captain. Got sick at 18 after over-pushing as a personal trainer (skipping sleep, energy drinks, supplements).
- 4 cycles of get-better-then-crash over ~3.5 years.
- Final crash: 8 months bedridden + 1+ month in hospital (ICU first).
- Hospital doctor cracked the code: *"What you have is a hypersensitive nervous system. You drove a Formula 1 car through downtown — your nervous system isn't built for that load."*
- Recovery in last 6+ years; full recovery; now runs CFS Recovery program; ran a portion of the Honolulu Marathon recently.

## Miguel's recovery framework (Q&A synthesis)

This is the cleanest single-episode articulation of his framework on the corpus.

1. **CFS as a hypersensitive nervous system** — every sensory input (light, sound, food, pain, even thoughts) gets amplified. The body interprets all stimulation as danger.
2. **Stress-threshold model** — everyone has a threshold; pre-illness pattern of pushing past it stacks up; eventually a final straw (covid, surgery, stressor) tips into chronic flare. Not a one-event illness — a slow-burn buildup.
3. **The Golden Rule of recovery**: *respond well to symptoms.* This is the highest-leverage thing you can do. Aim for **emotional neutrality** about symptoms, not forced positivity. Forced positivity = your brain knows you're lying = sympathetic spike. Neutrality calms the loop.
4. **Symptoms are not random** — your nervous system targets what you value (athlete → palpitations + body aches; intellectual → brain fog) to keep you in fight-or-flight.
5. **"Don't Google CFS"** — once you see worst-case images, you can't unsee them. Replace consumption with recovery stories.
6. **Don't measure progress too granularly** — second-by-second walking-distance tracking causes more stress than information. Adjustment periods fluctuate.
7. **Brain retraining = neuroplasticity, bidirectional** — if you went from normal → CFS, you can go back. Same machinery.
8. **"Slowing down" doesn't mean slamming the brakes** — when in a flare, drop activity to ~1/3, not zero. Slamming brakes (going from active → bedbound for a week) requires more energy to restart.

## Notable framing claims

- **"You don't need a program to recover — programs offer information and a system, but the patient does the work."** Anchor articulation of the program-as-scaffold framing.
- **Visualization-as-mental-escape** — at his worst, he constructed an imaginary world (standing on a beach in Hawaii, watching sunsets, eating with friends) to survive bedridden boredom. Not just survival — also brain-retraining (brain doesn't strongly distinguish vivid imagination from reality).
- **Find joy not stress** — meditation works for some; for him, video editing produces the same flow / parasympathetic state. Find your version.
- **MCAS / food sensitivity is fluctuation-correlated** — the same food may be tolerated outside a flare and cause anaphylactoid reactions inside one. Useful diagnostic — *if your food intolerance fluctuates, it's likely nervous-system-driven, not fixed.*
