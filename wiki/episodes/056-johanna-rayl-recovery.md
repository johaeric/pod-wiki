# #56 — Johanna Rayl — Long Covid Recovery

[[johanna-rayl]] is a US economics PhD student (climate / environmental policy) who got LC in April 2020 and has now (mid-2022) reached full or near-full recovery. The episode is **a clean recovery story** focused on what worked.

## Pre-illness context

- High chronic stress for ~2 years pre-COVID
- "Picture of health" externally — runner / cyclist training for triathlon
- "Pushing hard in all areas of life" pattern
- Personality cluster typical for recovered CFS/LC stories

## Acute illness

April 2020. Moderate severity (didn't hospitalise but in bed 18 hours/day for ~1 week with breathlessness even sitting up). Did not get a positive test (early-2020 cohort).

## Failed paths (year 1)

- Cardiologist, allergist, immunologist work-ups — all normal
- Acupuncture + herbal medicine — minor benefit, not transformative
- **Physical therapy — counterproductive**, made things worse, stopped
- Started PhD program in fall — gave up rest progress

## Turning point (year 1.5)

Searched **"full CFS recovery"**. Found:

- **CFS Unraveled** (Dan Neuffer's interview-curation site)
- Pattern: many people *do* fully recover — but the path is autonomic-nervous-system retraining
- → enrolled in **ANS Rewire** program

> "Light bulb moment" — autonomic dysregulation is the *root* of LC/CFS, not a side-effect. Symptoms are nervous-system output.

## What worked (Jan 2022 onward)

1. **Medical leave from PhD** — January 2022. Paid leave + insurance retained. Recognises this as a privileged option not available to most LC patients. *"The most important decision I made besides finding the program."*
2. **ANS Rewire** ([[dan-neuffer]]) — full daily commitment. Removing triggers + brain training + reframing.
3. **Curable app** (~$15) — chronic-pain neuroscience education + in-the-moment reframing exercises. Targets the **fear → symptom amplification** loop.
4. **Radical rest + giving back energy on good days** — explicitly resisting the "I feel better, do all the things" trap.
5. **Movement, not exercise** — "if it doesn't feel good, it's probably not helping." Some days that meant stretching arms overhead while supine. Decoupled from the achievement-frame.

Within ~2 months on leave, "things really began to change." She is now (mid-2022) reporting a full or near-full recovery — back to swimming, walking 3× to Lake Michigan a day, etc.

## Eight pieces of advice (to be linked from her writing)

1. **Don't set recovery deadlines** — pressure signals the nervous system to stay distressed
2. **Notice emotions in push-crash cycle** — grief often hides behind anger/self-blame
3. **Understand the fears around your illness** (curable-app territory)
4. **Reframe "exercise" as "movement"** — if it doesn't feel good, skip it
5. **Sympathetic-state environments trigger flares** — pay attention to sensory load
6. **Rest is the message your body is asking for** — surrender phase
7. **You can't practice symptom-response on a good day** — bad days are training opportunities
8. **The mind-body distinction is wrong** — emotional and physical health are one system
