# #83 — Ryan Prior on Patient-Led Research, Expert Patients & Drug Repurposing

US journalist + author + documentary producer with **ME/CFS** since long before LC. Author of **The Long Haul** (Hachette, 2022). Director of **Forgotten Plague** (ME/CFS documentary). Now a columnist (*Patient Revolution*, Psychology Today) and think-tank fellow working on the intersection of patient power, citizen science, and disability rights.

## His arc

- Has had ME/CFS for over a decade. Was at CNN covering the 2016 election, science, public health.
- **March 2020**: knew the post-viral wave was coming. "A profound sense of dread."
- Initially blocked from publishing on it because no peer-reviewed paper had appeared. **Ed Yong's June 2020 Atlantic piece** ("long-haulers", citing the [[patient-led-research-collaborative|PLRC]]) "scooped" him.
- Eventually published 6-7 LC stories at CNN through 2020. "Hair on fire."
- Wrote The Long Haul (2022) — wanted to deliver a "happy ending" with a cure, ended up writing about consciousness-shift and a 10-year time horizon instead.

## The "expert patient" thesis (his core contribution)

The reframing the wiki should adopt:

- **The patient with lived experience is not just an anecdote.** Especially when the patient is also a journalist / data scientist / public-policy researcher / clinician.
- Examples: Hannah Davis (data scientist), Fiona Lowenstein (journalist), Lisa McCorkell (public policy), David Saperstein (neurologist with LC), Liza DiLeo Thomas (#81). All "experts who became patients" — and now experts whose authority comes from both layers.
- Aristotle's **phronesis** (practical wisdom): wisdom isn't useful unless applied. Patients applying their experience daily to navigate the illness *are* practising — therefore they *are* the experts on the lived condition.
- **Citizen science is mainstream again**. Until ~100 years ago, science was less specialised; the post-Covid wave plus social media restores something closer to that older mode.

> "The wise person is a person who does the thing the way a wise person does it. Patients with chronic illness are doing this 24/7."

## Drug repurposing as the highest-leverage strategy

He keeps returning to **David Fajgenbaum** (Castleman disease, *Chasing My Cure*). Fajgenbaum's argument:

- "Santa Claus syndrome" — patients (and the public) believe some external smart person is going to come down the chimney and deliver the cure.
- For most rare or under-funded conditions, there is no Santa Claus. **You have to become the Santa Claus.**
- Many of the drugs that will treat LC may **already be FDA-approved** and sitting on pharmacy shelves — for some other condition. The work is repurposing, not novel discovery.
- Fajgenbaum runs a Clinton-Global-Initiative drug-repurposing programme broader than Castleman.

For LC specifically Prior thinks the repurposing-as-strategy framing is right.

## The patient-led research case study

- **PLRC** ([[patient-led-research-collaborative]]): formed in [[body-politic]] Slack's "Data Nerds" channel. First LC symptom survey, May 2020. Now an independent funded org working with WHO, CDC.
- **Amy Marcus, We the Scientists** (book about Castleman / NPC): same family-driven, social-media-organised, force-the-establishment process applied across multiple rare diseases.
- This wiki already tracks the same pattern from [[062-nisreen-alwan|#62]], [[067-decodeme|#67]], [[069-fiona-lowenstein|#69]] — Prior is the first guest to consolidate the framing across multiple diseases and into a normative argument about how science should work.

## On the language: "long covid" + "long-hauler"

- "Long covid" approval rating "90+%". Almost no patient debate. **Includes the word "covid"** — the journalistic principle of *don't bury the lede* applies; the term should name the cause.
- ME/CFS by contrast was decades of internecine warfare between camps over names. Frustrating; productive of nothing.
- "Long-hauler" — gritty, poetic, folksy, "sounds like it came out of a country song." It sticks where ME/CFS doesn't.

This is a useful editorial point for the wiki: when describing the cohort, lean on "long-hauler" / "long covid" rather than synonyms.

## On the "10-year happy ending"

Asked at the close of the episode about progress, his honest answer:

- LC is unlikely to be "cured" in 1-3 years.
- Historical analogs (HIV/AIDS, Alzheimer's, breast cancer, PTSD) all show the same arc: knockdown-drag-out wars between patients and the medical establishment, ultimately resolved by **collective consciousness shift** more than by a single drug.
- His own success criterion as a writer is *"in 10 years, has consciousness shifted?"* not *"has my book cured anyone?"*

## Quotes

> "Necessity is the mother of invention. Patients want it the most, so patients will move things faster — about 10× faster than formal research."

> "There is no Santa Claus. You have to become the Santa Claus." (paraphrasing Fajgenbaum)

> "If you don't use the term 'long covid' you're burying the lede."

> "If healthcare isn't patient-centred, what is it? Insurance-centred? Pharma-centred?"

## Eric-relevant intake

- The **expert-patient framing** legitimises Eric's own role in this wiki: he is patient + technologist working systematically to extract structure from unstructured testimony. That's exactly the pattern Prior describes.
- **Drug repurposing** is the most actionable single takeaway from this episode for personal-care purposes — it shifts the question from "is there a cure for LC?" to *"which already-approved drug, currently used for X, has plausible mechanism for Y in my own case?"* The wiki already tracks several (LDN, mestinon, ivabradine, metformin) but the framing makes them feel less like "off-label experiments" and more like "legitimate first-line searches."
- The **10-year horizon** is a useful reframe against impatience — both for advocacy expectations and personal-recovery expectations.
- **"The Long Haul"** belongs on the wiki's reading list alongside Lucy Gahan's *Reclaiming Life and the Things That Matter* (#78), Caroline Pover's book (#84), Fajgenbaum's *Chasing My Cure*, Marcus's *We the Scientists*.
