# #032 — Esther — Recovery Story (TMS / Mind-Body)

**Guest**: **Esther** (no surname given) — Netherlands, near Rotterdam. Almost 50 at recording. Working mum, two kids. Previously fit and active. Now runs **longcovidcured.com**, a curated blog of recovery stories and resources organised around the TMS / Mind-Body lineage.

The wiki's **fourth full recovery story**, and the first via an explicit **TMS / Mind-Body Syndrome** route. Recovery has a precise date: **2 June 2021**, the day she listened to a Nicole Sachs podcast and felt symptoms drop ~80% within hours.

## Illness arc

- **March 2020** — first wave; one week of fatigue, then better.
- **March → December 2020** — relapse-remit pattern: weeks ill, weeks well, sometimes back to work. Symptoms accumulated: shortness of breath, headaches, paresthesia, exhaustion. Tested negative early (no test available March/April). Dismissed at hospital despite being unable to walk 5 m without breathlessness.
- **December 2020 → June 2021** — persistent. OT and physio not helpful: OT had her grading activity (150 → 20 → 11 → 6 points/day; 10-minute walk = 3 days bedbound). Physio gave up: "your symptoms are too severe to build on." Severe sensory hypersensitivity (light, sound, touch). Wheelchair through the forest behind her house — emotional low point.
- **2 June 2021** — listened to **Nicole Sachs** podcast with another LC recoverer talking about [[tms-mind-body-syndrome|TMS]]. Within hours: ~80% symptom drop. The remaining 20% faded over the following months.

## Why this counts as `key_to_recovery` for TMS / mind-body

The transition is unusually clean: a single listening triggered the inflection. She immediately moved from passive sickness to active practice — collecting from **Howard Schubiner** (*Unlearn Your Pain*) and Sachs's **journal-speak** technique. Subsequent days: symptom flares (e.g. neck/shoulder seizure) responded to recognising "the brain trying to protect me" rather than treating as physical injury.

Pattern she now recognises retrospectively: **30 years of migraines, recurring fatigue, knee/back pain** — same nervous-system pattern, covid was the trigger that brought it to a head.

## Quotes

> "Once you acknowledge that you're worried, angry, scared — and let those emotions be felt — your brain can relax. Then it tells the body everything is okay." — Esther

> "What I learned: the crucial thing was not doing too much. The crucial thing was being **afraid** of doing too much. That alone was enough to set off the symptoms." — Esther

## What she emphasises (for any reader looking to try this)

1. **Rule out structural damage first.** Get a clinician to confirm there's nothing wrong with heart/lungs/brain on imaging — TMS practice is for *after* that gate.
2. **Journal speak** — Nicole Sachs's freewriting method to surface repressed emotion. Daily, private, throwaway.
3. **Schubiner's work** — neural-pathway / neuroplastic framing of chronic symptoms.
4. **Self-compassion** — Kristin Neff's meditations. Esther flags herself as someone with high self-criticism; without softening that, the practice doesn't land.
5. **Move anyway** — small acts (e.g. picking up the kids on an e-bike) as evidence to the brain that "this isn't dangerous."

## How to read this for Eric

- **POTS-with-tilt** is structural autonomic dysfunction with a clear physiology. The TMS frame can co-exist with that (autonomic state is influenced by emotion / fear loops) but it is not a substitute for ivabradine / mestinon / florinef in someone with confirmed POTS.
- The most transferable piece is the **fear-of-symptoms-as-amplifier** observation — recurs across this podcast (Riggs #23, Nicholson #29, Boon Lim #40) regardless of the practitioner's vocabulary.
- Cross-check before recommending: this is one recovery via this route. The wiki now has 3 routes for 4 recoveries (Lightning Process / neuroplasticity / multi-component pharma+keto+SSP / TMS).
