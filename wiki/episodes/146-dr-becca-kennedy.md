# #146 — Dr Becca Kennedy: chronic pain, fatigue, neuroplastic symptoms

Family medicine physician (US, 20+ years). Founder of [[resilience-healthcare]]. **Former lead of the long-COVID clinic at Kaiser Permanente** — assessed hundreds of LC patients. Trained in Howard Schubiner's neuroplastic-symptoms model after years of seeing chronic-symptom patients the standard medical model couldn't help.

The wiki's clearest **clinician-side** articulation of the mind-body / neuroplastic-symptoms / EAET frame; complements [[esther-rotterdam]] (#32, patient-side TMS), [[curable-app]] (Alan Gordon PRT route), [[sam-munslow]] (#34, coaching framing).

## Core claims

- **30–50% of primary-care visits in standard practice** present "medically unexplained symptoms" — chronic pain, migraine, IBS, dizziness, tinnitus, pelvic pain, etc — that fall under the **neuroplastic / mind-body symptoms** umbrella. Not pockets, gaping holes.
- **The body is not damaged; the nervous system is hypervigilant** — functioning as designed but interpreting too much as danger.
- **Real physiological changes** — cytokines, mitochondria, ATP, cortisol, serotonin, inflammatory cells — all produced by chronic fight-flight-freeze signalling. The body changes in measurable ways. **Different from "all in your head."**
- **Capital-T and little-t trauma both feed the danger interpretation.** Personality traits (perfectionism, people-pleasing, high self-expectation, control, emotional repression — especially of anger) develop as protective mechanisms and amplify the danger interpretation downstream.
- **The pandemic was a worldwide trauma.** Even patients with stable home lives experienced massive nervous-system stress (invisible threat, isolation, no end in sight, can't be around other humans = not-safe, political/economic chaos). Fertile soil for nervous-system hypervigilance.
- **Empowerment, not blame.** Patients are not at fault. Doctors should figure this out — it's their job. But once you understand the model, you have agency you didn't have before.

## What she does clinically

- **First**: rule-out evaluation. Standard tests; thorough but not exotic; primary-care doctors can do this competently. "It is important to get an evaluation by your regular doctor." (Caveat: warns about non-validated tests showing false positives.)
- **Then**: educate the model — body not damaged; danger signals over-firing.
- **Tools she layers in**:
  - **Stress-management basics** — meditation, breathwork, exercise where tolerated. Sometimes enough on its own.
  - **Expressive writing** (Pennebaker lineage; different from journaling — focused emotional disclosure).
  - **Emotional Awareness and Expression Therapy (EAET)** — Schubiner + Mark Lumley. Explicitly teaches getting stored repressed emotions up and out.
  - **Connecting current dysregulation to lifetime trauma context** — so the brain understands why it's interpreting danger and can be re-trained to respond differently.

## Clinician's perspective on diagnostic gaslighting

> "When doctors don't understand the extent to which brain signalling can cause real physiologic changes, they end up gaslighting patients. It's our job to figure this out."

Self-critical of her profession. Argues the *model* is the problem: medicine handles "structural damage" well (broken bones, infections, tumours) and "fails when the generator is brain signalling. The damage frame is wrong; **changes** is the correct word — and changes are reversible."

## Success-story framing

- A 75-year-old patient, 3.5 years of debilitating LC ("couldn't lift a coffee cup"). After learning the model + expressive writing + meditation + breathwork: now "remodelling her house and playing with her grandkids."
- Another patient walked in with a wheeled walker; later back riding her horse.
- Kennedy herself developed chronic pain + fatigue *while doing this work* — has all the personality traits — and worked through it using the same approach.

## Eric-relevant takeaways

- **The neuroplastic-symptoms framing** is now anchored on the wiki from a *clinician* perspective. Eric has been moving along the multilateral / somato-neurological track ([[dan-neuffer]] #102, ANS Rewire) which is compatible — neuroplastic symptoms is the upstream-of-secondary-dysfunction model, ANS Rewire is the practical retraining program.
- **EAET / expressive writing** are tools Eric could try as a contained experiment without changing his medication or pacing protocol. Schubiner + Lumley's published EAET protocol is the cleanest source.
- **30–50% of primary-care visits** is a stat worth keeping for context — explains why so many patients on the podcast share the experience of being told "nothing's wrong" before finding the right model.
- **Pandemic-as-worldwide-trauma** lens — Kennedy is the wiki's first guest to name the *systemic-trauma* component of LC. Eric has lived this; the framing is useful.
