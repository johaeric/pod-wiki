# #102 — Dan Neuffer (founder of ANS Rewire) on autonomic-rewire as a multilateral approach

Dan's first proper guest appearance after years of being **named-but-not-interviewed** by other guests ([[gez-medinger]] #46, [[jacob-teitelbaum]] #55, [[johanna-rayl]] #56). The episode is part biography (his own ~6.5–7 year ME/CFS arc), part hypothesis-defence (dysautonomia as the root mechanism for the whole post-viral cluster), part method-statement (multilateral / "somato-neurological" approach), and part terminology argument that the wiki should capture even though it cuts against community usage.

## Dan's hypothesis (anchor)

One illness, one pathogenesis. ME/CFS, fibromyalgia, POTS, multiple chemical sensitivity, post-viral fatigue syndrome — and a meaningful subset of "long covid" — are all **manifestations of dysautonomia**. The autonomic nervous system regulates everything (heart, gut, hormones, immune system, skin, thermoregulation), so when it dysregulates, *every* organ system shows symptoms. Symptom heterogeneity between patients is *expected* under this model — not a refutation of it.

> "I came to my conclusions a little irritated. If anybody walks into a doctor and says 'my gut is off, I sweat at night, I get exhausted, I get pain, sounds upset me…' — surely either this person has dysautonomia or they're crazy. No one person can have so many different illnesses. But the second framing keeps getting adopted instead." — Dan Neuffer, ep #102

## Anchor terminology critique (controversial; logged as Dan's position)

Dan **rejects "long covid" as a term** on these grounds:

- It's **non-scientific** — implies "you still have COVID" without evidence the virus is still active in most patients.
- It **lumps unlike conditions** — organ damage from the acute infection (his example: a friend who nearly had a lung amputated) is being grouped with post-viral fatigue. Different pathology, different prognosis.
- It **mystifies the illness for the patient** — adding fear-priming on top of an already terrifying experience.
- He prefers **post-viral fatigue syndrome (PVFS)** for the dysautonomia-driven subset, with the explicit recognition that some LC presentations have additional pathogenesis layered on top.

> "You wouldn't see a physician in the ED saying 'we've got three surgeries — they all have long-car-accident.' One has a ruptured spleen, another a collapsed lung, another organ damage. *That's a diagnosis.* You don't just say long-car-accident."

This is a **deliberate counter-position to the bulk of the wiki**, where "long covid" is used freely. It deserves logging as Dan's framing without the wiki adopting it. Pairs with [[ryan-prior]]'s contrasting argument (#83) that "long covid" worked *because* it was a non-clinical patient-coined term that didn't bury the lede the way "myalgic encephalomyelitis" did.

## The multilateral approach (anchor concept)

Dan rejects both purist camps:

- **Purist brain-training camp** treats physical interventions as *contraindications* to brain-training — engaging with biological symptoms is framed as confirming the alarm and undermining the retraining. Dan disagrees.
- **Purist biomedical camp** chases symptoms downstream forever — biohacking, supplements, "fix the imbalance" framing — without addressing the dysautonomia upstream.

His method is to do both, in the right relationship: **address dysautonomia upstream + treat secondary biological dysfunctions in parallel + maintain context that retraining is the load-bearing piece.**

> "Most people are doing either pure-biological or pure-brain-training. I personally believe the two should work side by side."

## Coined term: somato-neurological approach

Dan claims to have coined this. The structure he proposes:

- **Mind ↔ nervous system ↔ body** (the standard mind-body axis)
- **Body ↔ nervous system ↔ body** (somatic input drives ANS state irrespective of cognition — temperature, smells, light, sound)
- **Pure neurological-body link** — sensory-trigger flares that happen without cognitive awareness (barometric pressure changes a classic example)

> "Some people are like a weather forecasting system — they get a flare-up and it's a beautiful day; they don't know the weather's changing. That's a purely neurological response."

Dan flags **multi-sensory triggering** (touch, hot, cold, smells, sound, lights) as one of the **key tell-tale signs** that someone has dysautonomia-rooted post-viral illness vs something else. Useful diagnostic heuristic to add to the wiki.

## Why the program isn't the program

> "You haven't done the *Dan Neuffer* ANS Rewire program. You've done the *Jackie Baxter* rewire program."

Two operational implications worth surfacing:

- The **education** is what the program sells; the application is necessarily individualised. This is why guest-by-guest recovery stories on ANS Rewire look different.
- Dan **discourages buying the program before watching the four free intro lessons** — explicit. If the dysautonomia frame doesn't resonate, the program won't work because engagement requires belief that the model fits your illness. ("Some come back six months later and say 'now I'm ready.' That's fine.")

## On pain psychology and chronic pain

Worth flagging: Dan **partially disagrees with the "all chronic pain is in the brain" framing** he attributes to pain-research scientists. His example — severe SI-joint dysfunction, real biomechanical problem, real pain, which the brain *also* learns to over-signal. Both are true. The pure-brain framing risks under-treating fixable physical drivers; the pure-physical framing risks chasing scans forever when the pain has neuroplastic legs. Practical take: **address both, in context.**

## Useful anti-fear closing message

> "If life were about having solutions we wouldn't have many problems. The solutions are often not complicated; it's our ability to engage with them. So mindset is more important than finding the correct strategy. A mediocre strategy you can engage with beats a miracle cure you can't."

## Eric-relevance

- The **dysautonomia-as-root-cause hypothesis** maps onto Eric's confirmed POTS + PEM cleanly. Dan's frame would call Eric's whole symptom cluster downstream of dysautonomia — including any MCAS overlap.
- The **multilateral position** is the wiki's clearest articulation of how to combine the autonomic-rewire / brain-retraining work with Eric's existing pharmacological stack (ivabradine, mestinon, florinef, antihistamines) — no contraindication, just integration.
- The **multi-sensory trigger heuristic** (sounds/smells/light/cold/heat all flaring symptoms) is a quick self-check Eric can run to confirm dysautonomia-style triggering vs other patterns.
- Dan's **"watch the four free lessons first"** is the pragmatic entry test — cheap to evaluate before paying.
- The **terminology critique** is worth Eric being aware of, even if he doesn't adopt it — when a clinician dismisses "long covid" they may be coming from this framing rather than denying his illness exists.
