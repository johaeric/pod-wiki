Solo essay riffing on James Nestor's *Breath* quote "you are the pilot, not the passenger." A meta-recovery piece about reclaiming agency when an illness has taken control of your life.

## Four strategies for reclaiming pilot mode

1. **Micro-agency** — start with one tiny supportive thing per day; habit-track it; don't over-commit; bring an accountability partner.
2. **Sensory grounding** — 5-4-3-2-1 (name 5 things you see, 4 you feel, 3 you hear, 2 you smell, 1 you taste). Surround yourself with comfort cues (candle, music, soft fabrics, photos). Senses are how the nervous system reads "am I safe."
3. **Rewrite your inner script** — language matters. "I'm pausing" not "I'm stuck." "I'm healing" not "I'm broken." "Recovery journey" framing because journeys go somewhere.
4. **Boundaries as navigation** — "saying no is a flight correction; saying yes to rest is landing gear." Try the New Yorker line "that doesn't work for me" — no apology, no excuse. Pairs with #180 (Jules Rogers on boundary-setting).

## On setbacks and co-pilots

- Setbacks are normal turbulence. Damage limitation, not failure. Ask: "What is one thing I can choose right now?"
- Audit your co-pilots: is your partner/friend/family member helping you navigate or snoozing on the job? Sometimes they need training, not replacement.

## Notes for Eric

A stand-alone Jackie essay — useful as a framework for *reclaiming agency* when the illness has eroded it.
