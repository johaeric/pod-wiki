# #022 — Eddie Duncan & the LOCO-RISE study

**Guest**: **Professor Eddie Duncan** — Professor of Applied Health Research, University of Stirling (Chief Scientist Office NMAHP unit); occupational therapist by background. Co-PI on **LOCO-RISE** with Prof Kay Cooper (RGU, physio) studying long-covid community rehabilitation across four Scottish health boards.

Major update to [[loco-rise]] trial page. This episode is the design walkthrough.

## LOCO-RISE design

- **Mixed-methods evaluation** of *what is already being delivered* (not a new intervention trial)
- 4 health boards as case studies: NHS Grampion, NHS Tayside (CARES clinic — Scotland's only dedicated LC clinic at the time), NHS Lothian, NHS Ayrshire & Arran
- **400 patients total** (100 per board), outcome measures at baseline, end of rehab, 3-month follow-up
- Standardised long-covid measures: fatigue, respiratory, daily functioning, mental health, cognition
- + qualitative interviews with patients **and** clinicians (capturing emerging clinical learning)
- Inbuilt cross-board learning sessions to disseminate findings *as the study runs*
- Lived-experience PPI panel: **Fiona Smyth**, **Allison Dewar**, **[[jane-ormerod]]** — meet monthly with research team

Funded April 2021; data collection started early 2022 (just begun at recording). First learning event summer 2023, conclusion ~2024.

Wider team: Dr Jackie Morris (Dundee, physio), Dr Jenny Preston (Stirling/Ayrshire, OT), Dr Paul Swinton (RGU, statistics), Prof James Chalmers (Dundee, respiratory consultant), Dr Lindsay Alexander (RGU, physio).

## Eddie's emerging observations

- ~880,000 people in Scotland believed to have LC. Patient flow into community rehab in surveyed boards is **dramatically below what numbers suggest** — likely a referral-pathway / GP-awareness gap.
- Tayside (with publicised CARES clinic) has long waiting list. Other boards: low referral volume despite identical eligibility — communication about Pathways is the bottleneck.
- Regional models compared:
  - **England**: £33M for *new dedicated* long-covid clinics (mix of hub-and-spoke, virtual)
  - **Wales / Scotland**: invest in *existing* services (respiratory physio, OT) to bolt LC capability onto them. Scotland announced £10M (highest per-capita of UK nations).
  - No data yet on which model is better — LOCO-RISE plus England's national clinics study will start to answer.

## On HBOT (hyperbaric oxygen)

Eddie cautions: HBOT is **not harm-free** (eg ear/sinus/lung issues; some pre-existing conditions make it risky), evidence in chronic conditions including LC mostly fails to show clinical benefit despite anecdotal reports, and patients spending limited funds on untested interventions worsens economic burden. He flags an **ongoing Karolinska (Finland) RCT on HBOT for LC** — that's where evaluation should happen, not in unregulated private practice.

## Precautionary principle

Eddie cites Trish Greenhalgh (Oxford) on the precautionary principle as a basis for delivering low-risk, high-theoretical-benefit interventions (eg respiratory physiotherapy adapted from existing populations) for LC *now*, while research catches up. Untested-and-potentially-risky interventions belong in trials.

## Quotes

> "Patient flow doesn't match prevalence — there's almost certainly a referral-pathway problem, not a 'patients don't need rehab' problem." — Eddie

> "The risk of harm of a face mask was very low; the potential benefit was high. That's the same logic for low-risk LC rehab now." — Eddie, paraphrasing Greenhalgh
