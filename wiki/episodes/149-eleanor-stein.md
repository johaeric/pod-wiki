# #149 — Dr Eleanor Stein: resilience, recovery, self-management

Canadian medical doctor and psychiatrist; 25+ years specialising in ME/FM/MCS, latterly LC. Got ME herself in **1989** as a second-year psychiatry resident — recovery arc spans the entire history of the modern ME field. Self-published a *Self-Management Manual* (2012); now retired from clinical practice, focuses on education and webinars (eleanorsteinmd.ca).

The wiki's deepest **clinician-with-lived-experience** voice and the strongest articulation of **self-management as biology, not psychology**.

## Distinctive frame: self-management is biology

- "I took it as my mission to learn the biology behind self-management — it's just as real as any other type of biology."
- Neuroplasticity is ~100-year-old science (Donald Hebb, 1930s). What you think about repeatedly hard-wires in the brain via measurable functional MRI changes.
- **Brain → body → brain is a reciprocal pathway.** Symptoms are real and physiological, *and* mind-state shifts measurably modulate them. "It's not a psychological vs biomedical question — that question isn't actually relevant."
- The wiki's first articulation of *why* mind-body work is biology, in language Eric (or any sceptical patient) can read straight.

## Self-management defined

> "Everything I do every day that respects my body's needs and limitations as much as I'm aware of them."

Two parallel levels:

1. **Observe + believe + give the body what it needs** — sleep schedule, food, rest, social connection. The hard part is the *believing*; she observed her body for years without trusting it.
2. **Work on the mind to allow the body to receive what it needs** — accepting reality, resisting the urge to push through, giving up the cultural-script of "be productive."

## "Imminently dangerous? Familiar?" — when to monitor symptoms vs shift focus

Borrowed from parenting expert Barbara Coloroso. Two-question filter for each symptom flare:

- **Imminently dangerous?** If yes → ER. If new symptom → check it out.
- **Familiar?** If yes (you've had it 100 times, you know it waxes and wanes, you maybe even know what triggered it) → it's safe and likely *helpful* to **shift the focus of attention** to something mood-elevating.

Rationale: the brain can't generate positive mood and active danger signalling at the same time. **Sit outside in the sun while the symptoms are running** → brain notices the dissonance ("if you were really in danger you'd be in hospital, not enjoying sunshine") → over many repetitions, dials down the danger signal.

## On wearables — nuanced position

- Logged the limits of wearables clearly: **sleep-stage data is often wrong** for patients with disrupted sleep (her own polysomnography results disagreed wildly with her Oura ring on REM/SWS). Total sleep time + HRV are more reliable.
- **Heart-rate pacing in real time is a strong use** — wore a Polar chest strap for 2–3 years, capped at HR < 120, "incredibly helpful" for figuring out what tiny activities were spiking her unexpectedly.
- **If you're the obsessive-data type** → don't get a tracker. "Good self-management is to not get one." Logged for the wiki: wearables are not universally helpful.
- The talk on wearable trackers is on her free video page.

## Top tips (the ones she's currently yelling from rooftops)

1. **Sunlight, eyes open, no sunglasses, multiple times a day.** Quantum-biology-informed: different wavelengths through the day power mitochondria; modern glass blocks several important bands. "If I had to give one tip — get outside in actual sunlight."
2. **Live like a cave person re sun-cycle.** Wake at dawn (40 min before sunrise) or close to it; bed within 1–2 hours of sunset. Massive personal shift for her — used to sleep until 11 am/12 pm; now alarm at 6:15.
3. **Eat real food** (one ingredient, no chemicals, not processed). No single dietary prescription — paleo, keto, vegan, carnivore — find your own pattern.
4. **Mindset that recovery is possible** — placebo science is the same as belief science. Even imagining what 100% would feel like primes the neural pathways. "It will not hurt."

## On rest

- "Old school" — rest = horizontal, eyes closed, ears closed. **Not** Netflix, not podcasts. Cognitive load while resting prevents nervous-system rest.
- Pre-emptively before a bigger activity, or post-activity to recover.
- Mantra she uses now: "Being." Lying awake at 3 am, instead of stress about not sleeping → "what an amazing opportunity to *be* without expectation." Fell back asleep using this last night.

## On exercise prescription

- **Doesn't give it.** Patients who feel better naturally start doing more. Patients severely ill intuitively limit themselves. "If I tell you on the outside how many minutes to be upright, I'm wrong 100% of the time."
- Teaches **early-warning-sign awareness** instead. Each patient has unique pre-crash signals (cold, sweating, eyes itchy, head heavy, can't talk clearly). Write yours on the fridge. When they appear → stop, rest.
- Implicit alignment with **Workwell guidelines** (cf. [[workwell-foundation]], Anderson #135/#136, Davenport #141), but at the level of patient self-knowledge rather than HR thresholds.

## Quantum biology

- Newer interest. Sunlight is the obvious patient-accessible Quantum-biology-relevant intervention.
- Cites a recent talk by Dr Eric Gordon (cell-danger response researcher) on making cells/mitochondria feel safe. Worth tracking on the wiki — Gordon is a relevant clinician for the bioenergetic/CDR thread.
- Light boxes / blue glasses for housebound patients (Joffe #143 also recommends).

## On gas-lighting / chemical sensitivity (controversial)

- Acknowledges this part is contentious. Says it carefully.
- Two things in parallel: (1) reduce exposures so the brain isn't constantly thrown into panic; (2) **also** let the brain know that very low levels of chemicals are probably not dangerous. **Both layers** — the biomedical reduction *and* the neuroplastic re-training of the danger response.
- Operatively similar to the [[mind-body]] story but applied to MCS specifically.

## Memorable formulations

- "Self-management is biology. It's just as real as any other type."
- "Damage is the wrong word; *changes* are reversible."
- "Resting is horizontal, eyes closed, ears closed. Cognitive load is not rest."
- "If I tell you on the outside how to pace, I'm wrong 100% of the time."

## Eric-relevant takeaways

- The **self-management-as-biology** framing is the cleanest case for why Eric's mind-body work (breath, rest, gratitude, etc.) and his medication stack are not in opposition — both are physiology.
- **Sunlight in the morning, eyes open, no sunglasses** is a free, contained experiment Eric can run.
- **Imminently dangerous? Familiar?** filter is a useful daily-practice tool — applies to Eric's POTS spikes, GI flares, and any familiar symptom that doesn't need new evaluation.
- **Self-management as horizontal rest, eyes/ears closed** — operative phrase is "no Netflix while resting." Eric should consider whether his "rest" is actually cognitively load-bearing.
- **Eric Gordon / cell-danger-response** is a name to track for future ercipedia / podcast crosscheck.
