# #67 — DecodeME — Prof Chris Ponting and Andy Devereux-Cooke

DecodeME is the **largest GWAS (genome-wide association study) of ME/CFS in the world** — funded ~£3.2M, recruiting 25,000 people in the UK. The episode is a deep dive on the study design, why it matters, and how lived-experience is woven into every level of decision-making.

## The study

- **Online questionnaire phase** — open to UK residents 16+ with a clinical diagnosis of ME/CFS or its named variants. Paper version available for severe patients who can't tolerate screens. The questionnaire alone has captured *"150,000 years of lived experience."*
- **DNA collection phase** — saliva sample posted home, returned by post. *"Spit for science."* From the questionnaire respondents, 25,000 will be selected for DNA donation: **20,000 with pre-COVID ME/CFS** and **5,000 with post-COVID ME/CFS** (the additional arm added when the pandemic arrived).
- **Selection criteria** — Canadian Consensus or IOM criteria. The team deliberately won't publish the exact criteria sequence to avoid biasing recruitment (peer reviewers required this). Patients who aren't selected for DNA aren't being told their diagnosis is invalid — it's a sample-selection issue, not a judgement call.
- **Output** — comparison of allele frequency in ME/CFS vs general population. Hits will indicate which gene families (mitochondrial, immune, neurological) matter most. Each hit is a launching pad for follow-on research, including drug repurposing — Ponting cites the acute-COVID GWAS as precedent (a hit in that study identified an existing drug that reduced ICU mortality).

## Why this matters for Long Covid

Two reasons:
1. The **post-COVID ME/CFS arm of 5,000 people** lets the study compare genetic predispositions in pre-COVID ME/CFS vs post-COVID ME/CFS. If the patterns overlap, "long Covid is ME/CFS triggered by SARS-CoV-2" gets genetic support. If they diverge, that's also informative.
2. The questionnaire data will be fed back to the UK Department of Health and Social Care to inform the **forthcoming UK Government ME/CFS strategy** — direct policy influence.

**Plug for the wiki**: anyone with an ME/CFS diagnosis post-Covid is currently under-represented; they need the questionnaire signups specifically. URL: `decodeme.org.uk`.

## Why the rules matter (community history context)

Andy Devereux-Cooke gives the political backstory: the prior NICE guideline (2007) recommended **CBT** and **graded exercise** for ME/CFS — both based on the model that ME/CFS is "deconditioning + faulty illness beliefs." NICE redid the guideline in 2021 and **withdrew both recommendations** as not evidence-supported. The community had been saying that for two decades. DecodeME is built knowing this history — every patient-rep voice in the project is a deliberate counterweight.

CBT-as-illness-belief-reframe: same — **harmed** (community position, NICE-2021 endorsement). Both already on file from earlier episodes; this episode adds another row of corroboration.

## On microclots

When asked about biomarkers, Ponting cites **[[douglas-kell|Doug Kell]]'s microclot work** as the most replicated mechanistic finding to date (replicated independently). Should become part of the diagnostic toolkit, in his view, "as soon as possible." Cross-ref the Pretorius/Kell episode [[033-pretorius-kell-microclots]].

## Notable framings

- *"We know nothing"* (Ponting) — DecodeME is built on the assumption that we have no biomarker, no diagnostic test, no drugs. What we do have is **lived experience**. Hence the GWAS-from-scratch approach.
- *"Naive is good"* (Ponting) — assuming you don't already know the answer is what enables surprise findings. Experiments where you can't be surprised aren't worth the funding.
- **Patient-rep is structural, not decorative** — *"every group that meets has someone from the community."* PPI is woven into the protocol at every level.
