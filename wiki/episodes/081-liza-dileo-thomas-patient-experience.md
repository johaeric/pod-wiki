# #81 — Liza DiLeo Thomas on Empathy, Communication & "Normal Tests"

US emergency physician (~20 years, New Orleans), long-hauler herself, also Medical Director for Patient & Provider Advocacy and a Certified Patient Experience Professional. The most operationally useful single episode in the wiki on **how clinicians actually talk to LC patients** — and the antipatterns that make patients feel gaslit.

## Her arc

- Took over patient-grievance review years ago at her ED — read the complaints, learned what bad clinical communication actually looks like.
- Certified as Patient Experience Professional ~2018-19. Now teaches empathy + communication courses to new providers.
- Got sick with covid → long covid. Has not returned to ED work since.
- Now applying the lens *as a patient* to her teaching practice, and revising how she communicates with patients on the basis of her own experience.

## What "patient experience" research actually shows

- Better-communicating doctors → **better clinical outcomes**, not just better satisfaction scores. (Patients more likely to take meds correctly; more likely to seek follow-up care.)
- Empathy can be **measured and taught**. There's an established empathy-score instrument used on medical students.
- **Empathy declines in third-year medical students** when they go clinical, never fully recovers in residency. Likely drivers: long hours, isolation, the realisation that you can't cure everyone. Burnout exacerbates the decline; clinical experience over a career partially restores it.
- Having a chronic illness yourself (or in your immediate family) → measurably more empathic clinician.

## Her revised definition of empathy

> "I used to define it as putting yourself in someone else's shoes. But you can't, unless you've actually been in those shoes. So now: empathy is the ability to imagine what someone might be thinking and *believe* how they're feeling."

The **"believe"** is the load-bearing word. Without it, empathy collapses into sympathy or condescension. With it, the patient is witnessed.

(Direct overlap with [[078-lucy-gahan-psychology]]'s witnessing concept, from a different vocabulary.)

## The "must be / sounds like" technique

What she teaches in the empathy course:

- **"That must be awful."**
- **"Sounds like that's painful."**
- **"That must be so frightening."**

These are *empathic* phrases. They take more effort than the reflexive *sympathetic* phrase ("I'm so sorry") and pause the encounter just long enough to actually witness the patient. She uses the presence/absence of these phrases as a private tell for how empathic the people around her are being post-illness.

## The "great news, everything's normal" antipattern

Her single most important behavioural change since getting sick:

- Pre-illness: workup negative → walk into room and announce "great news, everything's normal."
- Patients looked deflated; her ED colleagues couldn't understand why.
- Post-illness: she gets it. **The patient does NOT want a heart attack.** They want to be told *yes you're sick, yes there's something wrong, no it's not great news that you're in the ED, your tests are normal but we just haven't figured this out yet.*
- A normal-test panel is not a diagnosis of "you're fine." It's "we ruled out the things our tests can rule out."

This generalises directly to LC: every LC patient has a stack of normal tests being used as license to disbelieve them. The fix is the language clinicians use when delivering negative results.

## "I don't know but I believe you"

Quotes a doctor on Twitter (handle since lost):

> "It took me 25 years of practice to figure out the best thing to tell my patients is: I don't know what's wrong with you, but I believe you, and I will work to figure this out."

She closed her recent ED-physicians lecture on LC with a version of this. Frames it as the most useful single sentence ED physicians can deploy with LC patients.

## The medical-jargon lesson — "course of antibiotics"

Specific example she uses in teaching: a patient asked her *"What do you mean by course? Is there a class I need to take?"* — after years of using "course of antibiotics" without realising it was jargon. The list of equivalent terms is long: fracture vs broken, lab values, "we're managing this," etc.

## The cardiologist + the discharge papers (incident she shares)

A friend with possible LC walked into a cardiology clinic appointment; the cardiologist entered the room with the discharge papers already in his hand because the chest-pain workup was negative. She prompted the friend to ask about POTS specifically — friend asked, cardiologist agreed it was likely, referred to autonomic specialist. Without the friend's prompt, she would have been discharged.

The lesson is double-edged: clinicians need education on dysautonomia presentation; *and* patients need to be coached to advocate even within an appointment that's already framed as ending.

## Education = the lever

- Tulane Medical School symposium on LC (April 2023, end of month) — first in New Orleans; Thomas on a panel.
- Body Politic Slack used as a research source: she put a call out for ED-presentation experiences before the lecture; got 21 responses, dysautonomia dominated.
- Argues education needs to start with **medical students and residents**, not mid-career physicians, because empathy decline starts in clinical years.

## Eric-relevant intake

- The **"normal tests don't mean fine"** antipattern is exactly the kind Eric is likely to face if he's referred to a non-LC-aware specialist. Useful framing to push back: *I'm not asking you to find a heart attack — I'm asking you to acknowledge that what we ruled out is not the same as what's wrong.*
- The **"must be / sounds like"** vocabulary is portable into Eric's own conversations as a *patient* with friends and family who are also chronically ill — small but real witnessing tool.
- "Empathy declines in clinical years" is useful context when Eric encounters a younger physician being dismissive — it's not malice, it's training. Doesn't excuse it, but reframes how to push back constructively.
- **Body Politic** as a source channel for ED-presentation data is a useful pointer in itself — the wiki should treat their slack as a structured signal source.
