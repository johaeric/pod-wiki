Kelly George — former professor of media and communication, US (Pennsylvania), mother of two young kids. Got COVID after 2022 mask mandates lifted in her area; never returned to work. ~3-year recovery. Identifies as having had mild ME/CFS pre-COVID that the virus tipped into severe.

## Acute and chronic phase

- Acute: flu-l like onset; got progressively worse over weeks 1–3 instead of better
- Severe brain fog, muscle weakness ("couldn't lift arm to brush teeth"), heart palpitations, debilitating fatigue
- Largely bed/house-bound on and off for the first ~2 years
- Crashed hard before learning what crashes were

## What helped — Kelly's recovery framework

- **Acupuncture** early on — *not* curative, but the practitioner's deep listening and validation regulated her nervous system. Pattern: *being heard is itself nervous-system medicine*.
- **Connection** with old friends/acquaintances who already had ME/CFS — being understood without explanation
- **CFS Health program** (Toby Morrison) — joined after a year of using free YouTube material first. Found the multi-coach model especially valuable (movement coach, etc.). Now mentors within the program.
- **Pacing / activity management** — the single biggest factor. Recognising crashes as a thing.
- **Meditation** — already a yoga instructor pre-illness, so had an entry point. Did it intuitively before learning it was nervous-system regulation.
- **Pet snuggles** as nervous-system regulation
- **Communicating with husband about *his* experience** of the illness — turning the illness into an "us vs the illness" frame

## On recovery as a moving target

> "I don't think you can know you're recovered. I could get another illness tomorrow that puts me back in bed. I can do everything I want to do without monitoring my energy — that's recovered enough for me."

Now works as a career and wellness coach, specialising in LC/CFS recovery, moms, and higher-ed clients.

## Notes for Eric

A clean **CFS Health (Toby Morrison)** recovery testimony, similar in shape to a Bateman-Horne / pacing-grounded recovery. Two notable points: (1) the *acupuncture-as-listening-medicine* framing — the value was the practitioner taking her seriously, not the needles; (2) the explicit *husband loss* framing — partners lose the activities they shared, so naming the loss bidirectionally bridges isolation. Worth holding next to #205 (Nadine McKie on co-regulation).
