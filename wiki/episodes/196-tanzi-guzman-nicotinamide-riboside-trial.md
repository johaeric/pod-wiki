**Dr Edmarie Guzman-Velez** (now assistant professor of neurology at Rutgers Robert Wood Johnson, Center for Healthy Aging Research; previously Mass General/Harvard) led a clinical trial of **nicotinamide riboside (NR / Niagen)** for long-COVID cognitive symptoms. Co-investigator **Dr Rudy Tanzi** (Harvard Medical School, Mass General; director of Genetics & Aging Research Unit; director of McCance Center for Brain Health) was involved in design and interpretation. **Conflict of interest disclosed**: Tanzi is a long-time consultant to Niagen Biosciences and holds equity; he was *not* involved in trial execution.

## Trial design

- Population: LC ≥ 2 months post-acute, brain fog + ≥ 2 other symptoms, none hospitalised
- Dose: **2,000 mg/day NR** (twice the bottle-recommended dose)
- Cross-over / intra-individual: 2-week placebo run-in for everyone, then either NR for 20 weeks *or* placebo 10 weeks → NR 10 weeks
- Outcome measures every 5–10 weeks: NAD+ levels, neuropsych tests (Trail Making B, etc.), validated questionnaires (sleep, fatigue, depression, anxiety)
- 18 completed full 20 weeks; pooled-analysis n ≈ 43 (10 weeks NR vs baseline)
- Funding: McCance Center + Niagen Biosciences

## Results

- **NAD+ levels rose ~3-fold within 5 weeks**, sustained through 20 weeks
- Direct placebo-vs-NR comparison: **no significant differences** (under-powered)
- Pooled "10 weeks of NR vs own baseline" analysis (n=43):
  - Significant improvement in **fatigue, sleep quality, depression**
  - **Trail Making Test B improved by 8.4 seconds** (executive function) — Tanzi calls this comparable to or better than Alzheimer-trial signals
- Did *not* show across-the-board cognitive improvement; executive function specifically

## Mechanism reasoning

- NAD+ is the energy co-factor at the mitochondrial respiratory chain
- COVID activates PARP enzymes → NAD+ consumed defending the genome → depletion
- Hospitalised acute COVID patients have demonstrably depleted NAD+; LC depletion is *assumed* (not directly shown in this trial)
- NR is the most direct NAD+ precursor that survives the gut and gets into cells (NMN does not — must be reverted to NR first; oral NAD+ is destroyed in gut)

## Caveats

- 2,000 mg/day is **double the bottle-recommended dose** of TrueNiagen — the trial team's IRB approved it based on prior safety data. Don't extrapolate without medical supervision.
- B3 / niacin / nicotinamide are *not* the same as NR for raising NAD+ effectively
- Underpowered for definitive answer — confirmatory larger trials needed

## Notes for Eric

This is the **first peer-reviewed RCT-level evidence** for any oral supplement on this wiki for LC. Effect size is modest but real. Compared with #166 (oxaloacetate trial) it is similar in shape: small, mitochondrial-fuel-based, modest cognitive signal. If considering a trial, **2,000 mg/day** is what was used; **1,000 mg/day** is the bottle-recommended dose and may be the safer default for self-experimentation under medical guidance.
