# #164 — Clayton Powers: Listening + Validating in Healthcare

US physical therapist (Utah) specialising in mecfs / POTS / LC. Affiliated with [[bateman-horne-center]] and University of Utah LC clinic. Originally trained in orthopaedic / vestibular / concussion PT; pre-covid pivot driven by a single ME/CFS patient who told him "no, that's not going to work for me" when he proposed graded exercise.

## Position

- **Listening + validating is treatment.** Not a soft-skill add-on — it's the load-bearing first step. Many Powers patients have been gaslit through the medical system; "just being there with that person" produces measurable improvement before any intervention is added.
- **Telehealth + at-home care should be standard, not exception** for PEM patients. PEM-triggering travel-to-the-clinic is actively iatrogenic. Notes that Medicare/Medicaid US reimbursement for PT telehealth has been *cut back* post-pandemic — explicit advocacy concern.
- **Screen for PEM first** in any LC/mecfs patient before any movement plan. Then screen for MCAS, CCI, vascular compressions, MS, etc. as contributors.
- **Education + relationship-as-treatment**: he's documented one case where a patient on the brink of divorce (husband thought she was lazy) reconciled after a joint Powers PEM-explanation call.

## Energy / pacing toolkit

- **Heart-rate pacing devices** (Apple Watch, Garmin, **[[visible]]** armband, **Lumia Health** new device tracking blood flow to head) — useful as starter scaffolding; can be discontinued once patient internalises symptoms.
- **Recharge-faster strategies** beyond just lying down:
  - **Cold packs on neck + face** (vagal stim + cooling)
  - **Pneumatic compression devices** (inflatable leg cuffs to push blood back to head)
  - **IV saline infusions** (pulls patient out of crash faster — see [[iv-saline]])
  - **Supplemental O2 short bursts** for crash recovery
- **Disabled-parking sign-off** — in Utah, PTs can now sign these. Patient embarrassment vs. energy-savings tradeoff.
- **What matters most to this person?** Rock-climber patient: optimised so partner did all chores → patient could still climb 1-2 routes with HR monitor + breathing breaks. Teenage patient: chose to allow family help so he could "have some joy with his energy."

## Eric-relevant takeaways

- **Pneumatic compression for PEM recovery** is not on Eric's radar — worth adding to the menu of crash-recovery options alongside cold packs (already used).
- **IV saline** is worth knowing about as an option *for crash recovery specifically* (not a daily strategy) — re-anchors [[iv-saline]] beyond Sanjay Gupta's regular-infusion frame.
- **Cold packs on neck/face** is a contained zero-cost tool Eric can experiment with on bad days — Powers and Reema (#60) both anchor it.
- The "energy money / shower-cost ($100 standing vs $40 sitting vs $20 sponge-bath)" framing is operationally useful — Eric already paces; this gives him an explicit dollar-amount metaphor for *which version* of a task to choose.
