# #76 — Dr Robert Groysman on Stellate Ganglion Block

The wiki's anchor episode for **Stellate Ganglion Block (SGB)** as a long-covid intervention. Groysman has performed ~1,800 SGBs in his career — originally for PTSD, pivoting to LC parosmia/anosmia from January 2021 (before "long covid" was named).

## What the stellate ganglion is

A "switchboard" of the autonomic nervous system in the lower neck. Two consolidated plexuses (inferior cervical + superior thoracic). Most important relay between the **amygdala** (memory of fear) and the **chest organs** (heart, lungs, head sweating, etc.). When dysregulated → sympathetic overdrive → most LC symptoms.

> "The parasympathetic should be in the driver's seat 99% of the time, sympathetic riding shotgun. In LC, it's reversed."

## What an SGB is

Local anaesthetic injection (no steroid) into the sympathetic track around C6-C7 — Groysman uses a **modified C4 + C6 protocol**, drawing on Eugene Lipov's work showing two-level blocks superior in PTSD trials. Done under **ultrasound guidance** (he is firmly against fluoroscopy-only — fluoroscopy can't see vessels/nerves/thyroid/oesophagus, all in a 2.5-inch danger cube around the target).

**Block confirmed by Horner's syndrome** on the treated side: droopy eyelid, small pupil, red eye, stuffy nostril, sometimes flushing/horseness. **At least three of five signs** = real SGB. If only neck numbness with no Horner's = local infiltration, not an SGB.

## Why effects outlast the anaesthetic

Local anaesthetic wears off in hours but SGB benefits last weeks-to-years. Mechanism unclear — "flips a switch" in autonomic regulation. Only one other treatment in medicine works like this: **ketamine**.

## What Groysman treats with SGB

| Symptom | Reported response |
|---|---|
| **Parosmia / anosmia / phantom smells** | Often immediate within 30s of block — "alcohol smells normal" |
| **Brain fog** | Typically improves; transient post-block exhaustion common |
| **Fatigue / chronic fatigue** | Often improves; transient post-block tiredness |
| **Anxiety / insomnia / depression / PTSD** | Strong responder population (his original specialism) |
| **Tinnitus** (LC-associated) | Small N — one patient cleared after one block |

Treats people up to **27.5 months post-covid**. Success defined as "tolerating foods/drinks they couldn't before" — not necessarily restoration of normal taste.

## Protocols for non-responders

- Standard: right side first (80% of population is right-dominant).
- Repeat blocks in non-responders.
- **3-day or 5-day protocol**: daily SGBs.
- **Pulse radiofrequency**: needle goes to same place but with electrical pulse stunning the sympathetic track for 1 week to 3 months — used when system "snaps back" too quickly.
- Some patients are left-dominant (need left side). Some non-dominant — need both sides (never simultaneously: bilateral block of recurrent laryngeal nerve = airway emergency).

## Cautions

- **Stable POTS**: Groysman is cautious. Likens to "an acrobat on six chairs balanced on one ladder leg" — stable but fragile. SGB can destabilise. He does **not refuse** people with POTS, but warns. Has done many with normal responses.
- LC POTS (covid-acquired) ≠ pre-existing POTS for this purpose. He's mainly cautious about pre-existing.
- Ultrasound-guided required; advanced block ("not a YouTube-video procedure"); never bilateral simultaneously.

## Risks

- Intra-arterial injection → seizure (vertebral or carotid) — rare with ultrasound
- Hematoma (rare, more in anticoagulated patients)
- Infection / bleeding / nerve injury (any invasive procedure)
- Wrong-target intercalibrachial plexus block → temporarily numb arm but no SGB benefit
- Recurrent laryngeal nerve block → temporary hoarseness (expected)
- Post-block fatigue (1 day to several days, especially if pre-existing brain fog)

## "Spontaneous recovery may be compensation, not cure"

Groysman's important hypothesis: many people who appear to recover spontaneously at 18-24 months and then **relapse fully** with another infection / surgery / illness may not have *healed* — they've compensated, with weak parasympathetic tone and impaired healing. SGB tries to actually flip the autonomic switch back, allowing real repair.

> "Healing happens under parasympathetic control. If parasympathetic tone is low, you don't heal — you compensate. And compensation breaks at the next insult."

## Notable claims

> "If you don't develop Horner's syndrome, you didn't have a sympathetic block. It's that simple."
>
> "Long covid is real. I don't care what your doctor says. There's at least one doctor who believes you."
>
> "I think we're probably the earliest treaters of long covid — we knew what it was as long-haulers before 'long covid' was a phrase."
