# #141 — Todd Davenport: a deep dive into Post-Exertional Malaise

DPT and exercise scientist at University of the Pacific (Stockton, CA); 25 years a physiotherapist, 15 years researching ME/CFS, scientific advisor to [[workwell-foundation]]. His group ran the original **two-day cardiopulmonary exercise test (2-day CPET)** studies that demonstrated objectively reduced ventilatory anaerobic threshold (VAT) on day 2 in PEM patients. Anchor episode for the wiki's [[post-exertional-malaise]] page.

## What PEM actually is

- The **International Consensus Criteria (ICC, 2011)** name it **postexertional neuroimmune exhaustion** — Davenport prefers this because it captures both the exhaustion and the suspected mechanism (neurological + immune).
- Multi-system, multi-symptom: fatigue + cognitive dysfunction + sleep impairment + widespread pain + numbness/tingling + headaches + breathlessness/palpitations + GI / GU disturbances.
- Triggers: physical exertion, **cognitive exertion** (a short iPad video can be enough), and **environmental** exposures (smells, chemicals, light, sound).
- Lag time **24–72 hours**, sometimes shorter; recovery is prolonged; the post-PEM functional ceiling is *lower* than baseline. Variation is the only constant.

## The plug-in hybrid car analogy (credit: Dr Chris Snell)

- Aerobic metabolism = the gas motor (long-term, steady, prolonged work, charges the battery).
- Creatine-phosphate / glycolytic = the battery (short-term burst).
- In PEM the gas motor is broken. You over-rely on the battery, can't charge it, get stuck at the side of the road = a crash.
- Davenport notes "I want to acknowledge Dr Chris Snell who introduced us to this analogy."

## The 2-day CPET — what makes it the gold standard

- Day 1 max CPET on cycle ergometer with mask measuring O₂/CO₂ exchange → identifies **ventilatory anaerobic threshold (VAT)**, the point where aerobic metabolism breaks down.
- Day 2 CPET 24h later, **after PEM has been provoked**.
- Healthy/deconditioned controls *replicate or improve* on day 2 (motor learning).
- ME/PEM patients *cannot replicate* — VAT drops, work output at submaximal exertion falls. **Unfakeable** — gas-exchange physiology cannot be voluntarily manipulated.
- Operational consequence: the patient's functional metabolic ceiling has dropped; activities possible day 1 may be impossible day 2.
- Limitation: highly specialised, few centres, deliberately provokes a crash. Davenport flags Mark Faghy (#120, Derby) has developed a **two-day spread protocol** to reduce harm.

## The energy envelope (credit: Dr Leonard Jason)

- Identify daily resources, **stay below the threshold**, ideally bank some.
- Pacing = balancing activity with rest; *not* doing nothing — "resting takes a lot of work."
- Health-coaching model rather than exercise prescription.
- Functional pacing language: warning signs of overdoing → stop early → preserve baseline.

## "Beware the clinician who has you hop on the treadmill"

- Acute responses to exertion in PEM are abnormal; graded exercise has not been favoured in clinical practice guidelines for ME/CFS.
- A 2024 study showed **adherence to pacing predicts better outcomes in LC**.
- What you do with your energy is the *patient's* decision, not the clinician's.

## Crash care plan basics

- **Lying supine, feet up** — increases cardiac return.
- **Compression** — lower-extremity + abdominal — supports blood return.
- **Hydration + salt + electrolytes** — addresses documented low plasma volume in ME.
- **Pre-prepared list** of warning signs + what to try.
- Davenport recommends the **[[bateman-horne-center]] crash care plan** as the canonical filled-in document.

## Memorable formulations

- "This is not 'cup of coffee and a lie down' fatigue."
- "The only constant with PEM is variation — between people, between days, between phases of recovery."
- "Pacing isn't doing nothing. It's balancing activity with rest, and that takes planning, prioritising, and discipline."
- "Try and figure out what your gas motor can do, and stay within it."
