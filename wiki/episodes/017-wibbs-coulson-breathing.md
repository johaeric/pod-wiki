# #017 — Wibbs Coulson — Breathing for Long Covid

**Guest**: Wibbs Coulson — Cardiac/A&E nurse (~13 yr) turned yoga and breathwork teacher (~7 yr). Hosts the *Move Breathe Live* podcast. Runs a long-covid-specific online breath-and-movement class — Jackie attends and provides personal testimony as the host. The episode is foundational for the [[long-covid-breathing]]-adjacent thread but is **separate from Jackie's own course** — Wibbs's class is independent.

This is the episode the host had foreshadowed in #02 ("six weeks into Wibbs's class") and Suzy referenced in #07. He is one of the recurring practitioners Eric should know about.

## The thesis: physiology first, psychology second

Wibbs's framing of LC and chronic stress:
- All chronic conditions share a substrate of **inefficient cellular fuelling** — bad breathing → low CO₂ tolerance → anaerobic skew → fatigue, brain fog, anxiety.
- "Physiology drives psychology" (he names the *Physiology First* mental health charity for the same idea). Bad days *feel like* bad thoughts — but the bad thoughts are downstream of energy mismanagement.
- Tools: nose-breathing, slow-deep-low breathing, CO₂ tolerance training, rib biomechanics, vagal-tone work, gentle movement (yin yoga), down-regulation (yoga nidra).

Same physiological case as [[012-kelly-mitchell]] (low CO₂ → vasoconstriction, Bohr effect, alkalosis) — Wibbs and Kelly converge from different professional roots.

## Mount Sinai pattern (named in the episode)

Wibbs cites work from **Mount Sinai Hospital** (NYC) finding LC patients commonly **shallow mouth-breathe into the upper chest** — sympathetic-ganglia-rich territory — instead of using the diaphragm into the lower ribs (vagally-rich). Goal of his classes: shift breathing **down and slow**, recruiting the diaphragm and the vagus.

## Tools introduced in his class

- **Nose-breathing** — full case made (warming, humidification, NO production with antimicrobial properties; he refers to *George Catlin, Shut Your Mouth and Save Your Life* (1860s, racism-noted); references Patrick McKeown).
- **BOLT score** (Body Oxygen Level Test) — proxy for CO₂ tolerance.
  - Sit 3–4 normal breaths, exhale, pinch nose, time first urge to breathe (not maximum hold).
  - Healthy/functional ≥ 25 sec; many LC patients start ~6 sec.
  - Jackie's BOLT went from ~6 sec to ~24 sec over a few weeks of his class.
- **CO₂ tolerance test** — full inhale, slow even nose-exhale, time until you can no longer keep it slow/quiet. Aim 45+ sec.
- **Rib-band breathing** — strap around lower ribs, breathe out *into* the band, build 3-D rib expansion, intercostal/oblique recruitment.
- **Breath-hold + head-shake** trick to clear nasal congestion (acute trick to enable nose breathing immediately).

## Aerobic vs anaerobic framing

Big-muscle-car (anaerobic, inefficient) vs Tesla (aerobic, efficient). LC patients are running anaerobically with high RR; the work is to retrain to aerobic at lower RR. Quotes a paper finding **larger lung capacity correlates with longer healthier life** — hence rib-mobility work.

## Yin yoga + Yoga Nidra (Thursday class)

- **Yin yoga** = static postures held 2–5 min. Low cost, high vagal benefit.
- **Yoga nidra** = guided deep-rest meditation; "non-sleep deep rest" (NSDR) per Andrew Huberman. He cites the claim that **1 hour of yoga nidra ≈ 4 hours of sleep** for nervous-system recovery.
- Pattern: practice Y-Nidra before bed for sleep onset/maintenance.

## On classes vs apps

Wibbs is explicit about **agency**: in any yoga class, if you're spent, take child's pose. If the teacher makes you feel bad, never go back. Patient autonomy is part of recovery.

## On expectations

> "Every day is going to be different. When it's better, maybe celebrate it; when it's worse, don't commiserate. Just be — today is worse, but yesterday was better than they've ever had before. Who knows what tomorrow will bring." — Wibbs, ep #17
