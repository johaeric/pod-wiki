# #86 — Harry Leeming on the Visible App

Ex-Formula 1 engineer + tech founder who got covid in September 2020 (29yo, "fit and healthy", climbing mountains pre-illness). Hit dysautonomia + brain fog + fatigue; couldn't stand at worst points. After tracking his own crash via fitness wearable that "was designed for fitness, not illness", he built **Visible** — a wearables-driven app to measure and manage long-covid-family conditions.

## Visible — what it is

Two products in one app:

### 1. Free app (smartphone-camera based)

- Morning check-in via finger-on-camera 60s reading → heart rate + HRV.
- Self-tracked: symptoms, **physical / cognitive / emotional exertion**, menstrual cycle, sleep.
- Morning **stability score** (capacity-for-activity vs your own baseline) using last 3 months of data.
- Trends tab — pattern-spotting (cognitive-exertion vs symptoms; menstrual cycle vs symptoms).
- Available in App Store.

### 2. Visible Plus (wearable-integrated, beta)

- Continuous HRV + heart-rate streaming via Polar wearable (chosen specifically because it has **no screen** — reduces hypervigilance).
- Heart-rate-limit alerts in real time.
- 200 beta testers at recording; long waitlist.

## "Digital biomarkers" — what Leeming means

A pattern in objectively measurable wearable data that exists in sick people but not in healthy people. Examples:

- **Time spent upright** correlates with symptom severity in dysautonomia.
- **Automatic capture of orthostatic HR-jump** throughout the day — equivalent to a continuous, passive NASA-Lean / tilt-table test.

The strategic argument:

- **Once we can measure, we can move research / treatment forward.** Conditions without biomarkers historically get attributed to "psychosomatic" — same error as Ms before MRI, asthma before steroids.
- The endpoint: making "invisible" illnesses visible to family, clinicians, researchers, and policymakers.

## On the wearable hypervigilance problem

Notable design decision: **the Polar wearable used by Visible Plus has no screen** — to prevent the constant heart-rate-watching anxiety that can entrench the very sympathetic-overdrive pattern they're trying to dampen. (Direct contrast to Apple Watch / Garmin etc.)

Pairs with [[082-jay-wiles-hrv]]'s "data is motivation only" framing — same caution from a different angle.

## On building with lived experience

- Half of Visible's team have LC or ME themselves.
- Fully remote, asynchronous-by-default. No 9am stand-ups. Built that way from day one because "the company has to be flexible enough to function for the team."
- Beta-testing community feedback is read and responded to within 24 hours.
- Bi-weekly engineering blog posts about progress.

## Research opt-in (the broader contribution)

- Visible has "one of the largest data sets on long covid and ME" — 25k users, 20M rows of data at recording, growing rapidly via word of mouth.
- App has an opt-in research-data-sharing flow. Default off.
- First study in ethics review: **Imperial College London (Dr Viktor Mehta)** — menstrual cycle ↔ symptom severity. The community knows this signal anecdotally; nobody's published it. Visible's data set could be the first.
- More studies pending announcement.

## On the "self-management" word

Acknowledges the term is contested in the LC community — sounds like blaming the patient. Frames Visible's approach as **giving patients back the data and tools** that already exist for diabetes, asthma, etc. Not asking them to manage alone — supplying the infrastructure.

## On post-exertional symptom exacerbation (PEM/PESE)

Visible's free-app approach:
- Stability score gives morning advice.
- Trends tab lets users see *yesterday vs today* patterns rather than minute-to-minute.
- Visible Plus adds in-the-moment HR limit alerts.
- The app deliberately **does NOT add hour-by-hour tracking demands** — feedback loops at that frequency aren't actionable, and excessive tracking itself has cost.

## Quotes

> "We were using fitness trackers in the context of illness, not fitness — completely the wrong way."

> "It's a classic error of medicine. The less we understand a condition, the more we attribute it to people being psychosomatic. We did this with MS before MRI, with asthma before steroids."

> "Empathy is the one bit of wisdom I've acquired in the last couple of years."

## Eric-relevant intake

- **Visible app is directly Eric-applicable.** Free tier is no-cost; the "stability score + trends + menstrual-correlation" feature set is exactly the framing a POTS/PEM patient should be using to inform pacing decisions.
- **Polar-no-screen** design is a useful counter-frame to Eric's existing Apple Watch / Garmin habits — if hypervigilance is a problem, switch wrist hardware, don't just try to ignore the screen.
- **Imperial menstrual-cycle study** is the first wiki signal of an actual peer-reviewed-track investigation of the menstrual-symptom link — pairs with [[082-jay-wiles-hrv]] (PMS HRV-suppression physiology) and [[080-anjali-agarwal-eds-physio]] (POTS/MCAS flares with cycle).
- **Lived-experience-by-default company building** is a useful template Eric could point to in workplace conversations about flexible work.
