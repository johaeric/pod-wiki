Part 2 of Dan Neuffer's conversation on belief. Continued from #197.

## Practical belief-building tools

- **Symptom + activity journal** — repeat baseline at intake, then monthly without looking at last entry. Records become anchors when setbacks induce selective amnesia about progress.
- **Identity framing** — "I'm becoming someone who supports my body's healing." Identity-shifts shortcut behaviour change.
- **Manage your physiological state first** — calm the nervous system, *then* think. Breathing exercises before any reflection.
- **Build many small bits of light, not one big bulb** — recovery is dozens of small gains compounding.

## Protecting your beliefs

- **Curate environment** — stay away from people, narratives, and channels that drain hope. Be specifically suspicious of "science-based fear narratives" — there's a lot of pseudo-science out there that *sounds* rigorous.
- **Note the bipolar-media trap** — social media amplifies extremes, hides the middle. The truth is rarely at either pole.
- **Self-compassion when you slip** — missing a day is fine, just don't let it become a new pattern.

## Setbacks reframed

- Expect them. Don't fall into a noibo spiral when they hit.
- *Setbacks are when recovery actions are most productive*: a setback means the dysfunction is amplified, so the same intervention dose produces a bigger plasticity gain (5% rather than 1%, conceptually).
- "Failure is feedback, not failure" — Edison's 1,000 ways to not make a light bulb.

## On consistency over perfection

- Even half a breath practice is more valuable than skipping because you can't do "the full thing"
- Therapeutic dose vs habit dose are different — even sub-therapeutic doses build the *habit* of doing it, which compounds
- "Seek to make your recovery actions feel positive, not disciplined" — that is the secret sauce

## On long-term resilience

- Beth French (ultra-marathon swimmer, ME/CFS recovered) inspired Dan's belief that recovered patients can become *more* resilient than the average person, not just back to baseline.

## Notes for Eric

The most actionable nugget here is the **monthly symptom-+-activity journal repeated without referencing the last entry**, then comparing — bypasses the selective amnesia that setbacks induce. Identity framing ("I'm becoming someone who supports my body's healing") is a fast way to skip three layers of psychological work. The setback-as-opportunity reframe is mathematically defensible and worth holding when the next bad week arrives.
