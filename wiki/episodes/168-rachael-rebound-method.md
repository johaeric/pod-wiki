# #168 — Rachael: The Rebound Method (Return)

Return appearance ~one year after [[127-rachael-recovery-story]]. UK ex-competitive tennis player, PT, AI product manager. Now training for the Brighton Marathon ("my fudge to covid"); business [[rebound-athletic]] has grown from a 90-day reconditioning program to a wider membership ~30 strong, and she has codified what she does as the **Baseline Formula**.

## Where Rachael is now

- Physically: training for marathon; can absorb mild flare without it derailing her week.
- Cognitively: another step-up since New Year 2026 (post-Christmas viral run hit hard but recovered).
- Manifestation/meditation practice continued; signing up for the marathon was confidence-by-doing — every successful run reinforced trust in her body.
- "Recovery doesn't always mean symptom-free" — when she gave her first recovery interview a year ago, she still had occasional flares but trusted the trajectory.

## The Baseline Formula (codified protocol)

**Mindset prerequisite (taught first, before the formula itself):**

- **Consistency over progression.** Counter-intuitive for ex-athletes. On good days, do *the same* not *more*. Consistency *is* progression — at the pace the body can absorb without crashing. Without this shift, the rest fails.

**Three components of the formula:**

### 1. Movement modality — muscle activation / isolation exercises

- Single-muscle-group **isolation exercises** (the type used in injury rehab and warm-ups), not compound movements.
- Why: lowest energy demand → most efficient route to repeatable strength training given mitochondrial dysfunction.
- Targets stabilising muscles (hip flexors, scapular muscles, core) — useful for de-conditioning recovery + injury prevention later.
- Low-impact; many are mat-based or horizontal — accessible for POTS / orthostatic-intolerance patients.
- Slow, controlled, mindful — fosters Mind-Body re-connection where higher-intensity training would just trigger fear/PEM associations.
- Example contrast: a *squat* is compound (multi-muscle, high energy); a *clam* (lying-on-side glute-medius isolation) is isolation (single-muscle, low energy).

### 2. Dosage / volume

- **Handbrake question**: *Can I perform this amount of movement day after day without worsening symptoms?* (You wouldn't actually repeat daily — rest matters — but the answer being yes is the test.)
- **Do less than you think you should / can / want to.**
- **Effort gauge**: should always feel *easy*. Sweating / huffing / straining = too much. As capacity rebuilds, the amount that feels easy increases — that's the progress signal.

### 3. Predictable progressions

- **Time**: stay at a baseline for as long as it takes to build *confidence in tolerance* — could be 2 weeks, 4 weeks, 6 weeks. Loose definition deliberate. No predetermined weekly progression schedule (key contrast with graded exercise therapy + classic Progressive Overload).
- **Micro-progressions**: tiny incremental changes — small volume bump, one exercise added — and re-ask the handbrake question.
- **Flexibility / permission to scale back**: not "going backwards." Recovery isn't linear; movement isn't linear. Some weeks = mobility + stretches only.

## On graded exercise therapy

- Rachael is explicit: GET is **not appropriate for most stages of LC / ME/CFS recovery**, particularly while exercise intolerance is active. Possibly useful much later, after capacity is rebuilt.
- Cites the recent re-review of GET evidence: **Cochrane's 2020 review** (which had concluded GET still suitable for ME/CFS) was challenged on **flawed control groups, ignored harm signals, fatigue as unreliable outcome metric**. Subsequent re-analyses found **GET harmful for >50% of ME/CFS participants**. **NICE guidelines now recommend against GET for ME/CFS.**
- Mechanism story for why GET fails: **mitochondrial dysfunction in LC/ME/CFS** is now research-confirmed and linked to exercise intolerance. GET applies graded loading to a substrate that can't recover from it.
- Rachael's own GET experience: bedbound, taken to an in-clinic studio class that included walking lunges, skipping, jogging across a room — "I knew exactly what was going to happen after this. And it did."

## Membership pattern

- ~30 active members; mix of LC + ME/CFS / CFS-presenting.
- **Bedbound / very-low-capacity members**: Rachael advises *not* to invest yet — focus on breathwork, yoga, gentle bedside mobility. Membership best for those past the lowest capacity floor.
- Adapts to individual goals (foil-surfing, paddle tennis, climbing, basic ADLs) — same underlying formula, different end-points.
- Member examples she names: *Jason* (CFS-presenting business-owner who pushed through pre-illness; learned to stop pushing → got back to foil-surfing → rolled into the 90-day Rebound Method); *Paula* (4-year LC, persistent heavy-leg pain, struggled with over-doing-it on good days; maintained consistency, danced at Ibiza after 4 years).

## Why this episode matters

- **Wiki-new anchor for the Baseline Formula** as a named, codified reconditioning protocol distinct from graded exercise therapy. The wiki has had general "movement, not exercise" framing (Mooney #170, Davenport #141, Brown #47/#51) but no named alternative protocol with concrete components — until now. Pairs the *philosophy* with operational detail: what movement, how much, when to progress.
- **Cochrane re-review history** is wiki-new — flag this as a concrete answer to "but Cochrane said GET works for ME/CFS." That review was challenged on methodology; harm-signal evidence accumulated; NICE has since pivoted.
- **Mitochondrial-dysfunction → exercise-intolerance** mechanism story is now linked to a concrete prescription protocol — fills out [[mitochondrial-dysfunction]] from the rehab side.
- **Isolation exercises (muscle activation)** as the *correct movement modality* for LC reconditioning is wiki-new specificity. Pairs with [[walking]] and [[pilates]] as the practical menu.
- "Consistency over progression" framing is operationally distinct from "do less" pacing advice — it's about *what to do on good days*, the most common point of self-sabotage in LC recovery.

## Eric-relevant takeaways

- The **Baseline Formula** is the cleanest concrete framework Eric has yet seen for *how* to reintroduce strength work without the GET trap. Specifically:
  - Start with isolation exercises (clams, glute bridges, scapular work) — most can be done lying-flat / horizontally, friendly to POTS.
  - Find a volume Eric could *repeat daily if he had to*, and do less than that.
  - Stay at baseline 2-6 weeks before any micro-progression.
- Eric's perfectionist / push-through pattern is exactly what the *consistency-over-progression* mindset shift targets. On a good day, doing the same not more is the protocol.
- Worth carrying in mind even if Eric doesn't engage Rebound Athletic directly — the *principles* are the take-home.
- *Caveat*: Rebound Athletic membership is the load-bearing operational delivery of this; the principles can be applied independently.
