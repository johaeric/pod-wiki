# #040 — Dr Boon Lim — Autonomic Dysfunction, the Heart & Long Covid

**Guest**: **Dr Boon Lim** — Cardiologist + electrophysiologist, Imperial College London / Hammersmith Hospital. Clinical lead of the **Imperial Syncope Diagnostic Unit** (one of London's largest tilt-table services). Author of stop-fainting.com and *Keeping Your Heart Healthy*. First author of the much-cited **"long covid autonomic dysfunction — rationale for treatment and management"** (Jan 2021), one of the earliest LC autonomic papers.

The wiki's **anchor episode for [[autonomic-dysfunction]]** and one of the most teaching-rich episodes in the podcast. Pair with #29 Nicholson (POTS+breathing, patient/practitioner) and #5 Amy Small (POTS, GP/patient).

## The autonomic frame for LC

Lim's headline claim, drawn from tilt-testing 60–80 LC patients:
- **~25%** meet full POTS criteria (HR rise ≥30 sustained, no BP drop)
- **~25%** meet sub-threshold POTS
- **~Most of the rest** show oscillatory BP/HR patterns indicating adrenaline surging
- **Net: 60–70%** of LC patients have a meaningful autonomic dysfunction component
- **One distinguishing question**: do symptoms get worse standing up and better lying down? If yes → autonomic. If no (e.g. chest pain agnostic to posture) → consider myocarditis/pericarditis.

## The mechanism, plain English

When you stand up, gravity pools blood into the legs → empty heart → carotid baroreceptors not stretched → panic signal to brain → adrenaline surge → tachycardia + vasoconstriction → in healthy people, baroreceptors re-stretch and the surge dies down within ~10s. **In LC autonomic dysfunction, that loop overshoots and runs continuously while upright.** Lying flat resolves it; upright sustains it; deconditioning makes it worse, which makes more lying flat, etc.

Add: **chronic stress / anxiety / high expectations amplify the same loop** — emotional state feeds the baroreceptor-driven sympathetic surge.

## The "bottom-up" toolkit (his prescription order)

1. **Cold flask of water with electrolytes by the bed**. Drink 500 mL while still lying flat in the morning, wait 3–5 min for redistribution, then sit up. Front-load fluid through the day (~2 L by lunchtime, 3rd L by 5 pm, taper after — to avoid nocturnal urination).
2. **Salt** — sea salt or Himalayan, generous. Caveat: monitor BP; only go heavy if BP runs <100/70 or low-normal. Target sub-135/85.
3. **Compression** — waist-high compression garment for daytime use only.
4. **Isometric exercises** — squeeze glutes/calves/quads before standing; toe-lifts at the desk; "active fidgeting" at workstations. Use a stool under the desk to keep legs moving.
5. **Cool finish to the shower / bath** (Wim-Hof-lite). Vasoconstricts; reduces post-bath dizziness. Wild swimming exemplifies the pattern: horizontal + cold + compression (water pressure / wetsuit).
6. **Head-of-bed elevation 10–15°** chronically — paradoxically expands plasma volume over weeks via aldosterone upregulation (acts like endogenous fludrocortisone).
7. **Lighter, lower-carb, more frequent meals** — large stodgy meals + alcohol + warm room is the worst combination (vasodilation in gut → systemic crash).

## The "top-down" toolkit

Ranked importance: **forgiveness > gratitude > compassion** for downregulating the sympathetic ramp. Cited by Lim as the most important emotional state for breakthrough.
- **Diaphragmatic breathing** — slow nose-in / nose-out, hand on belly + chest. He demos a 2-minute exercise with Jackie on-air; she reports immediate relaxation. He calls it "the best drug, in everyone's reach right now."
- **Conscious dual control of breath** — only the lung is under both autonomic and voluntary control. Exploiting that is the leverage point.
- **Coherent breathing while taking your own pulse** to feel HR shift in real time.
- **Mindfulness, gratitude practice, forgiveness** — reframing the historical trauma. Lim's own personal anecdote: caught covid two months earlier in a stressed, sleep-deprived state; convalesced alone in France; later reframes the experience as a teaching moment.
- "Locus of control inside yourself, not in the white-coat tower."

## The drug toolkit (when needed)

- **Midodrine** — vasoconstrictor; boosts BP for the chronically hypotensive
- **Fludrocortisone** — mineralocorticoid; volume expansion. **Caveat: weak steroid; <1 year preferred.**
- **Propranolol** — beta-blocker for palpitation-dominant; not for hypotensive patients
- **Ivabradine** — sinus-node inhibitor for HR reduction without sympathetic blockade (Eric is on this)

## Reading "everything is normal" from a cardiologist

- Specialty silos hide the autonomic picture. Subspecialist tests are normal because they look at the wrong thing.
- **Validation = treatment.** Tilt-table data showing the BP/HR oscillation is itself therapeutic — reorients the patient out of self-doubt.
- Lim explicitly endorses: forgive the doctor who told you everything was fine; they had 10–15 minutes and operate within their silo.

## On myocarditis vs. autonomic dysfunction

- True myocarditis/pericarditis: chest pain is **postural-agnostic**.
- Autonomic chest pain: comes on standing, resolves lying flat with feet elevated and water.
- Pre-covid Lim observed myocarditis was almost always preceded by a viral illness in a run-down/stressed state. Chronic stress increases susceptibility to viral illness *and* the immune attack on the heart. Same template applies for LC.

## Programs Lim names with appreciation

- **Suzy Bolt's** 360 Mind Body Soul programme (#7) — patients of his return from it noticeably better
- **English National Opera Breathe** — published evidence of benefit
- Pattern: leader-led communities with passionate education converge on the same autonomic-regulation principles even when they don't use that vocabulary.
