Katie Brennan — American economist working in NHS population health management, lived in UK ~20 years. Got COVID March 2020 (early UK wave). Reinfected after moving back to US, brain fog tipped severe — couldn't string sentences for board presentations. Met Andrea Heinberg, a German Olympic biathlon competitor turned personal trainer, at a 6 am outdoor exercise class. The two now run **Thrive90**, a recovery program built on cognitive-motor coordination drills sprinkled into fitness.

## Recovery arc

- ~1.5 years of pushing through (denial / "if I just try harder")
- Reinfection severely worsened brain fog; took time off and dove into research
- Hypothesis: nervous system stuck in fight-or-flight is the central problem
- Started with: vagus-nerve work, eye exercises, breathwork, gentle stretching, nervous-system-focused massage from a friend, deliberate pacing using symptoms as signals (not enemies)
- Joined Andrea's class — coordinated body-brain micro-drills (ankle tilts, etc.) gave **immediate, measurable** mobility/symptom changes
- Worked with Andrea on a recovery-pathway program → Thrive90

## Why the Olympic-athlete framework translates

- Pacing is embedded in elite athlete culture (no late nights in season; nutrition; recovery > training-volume)
- Margins matter — 1% gain at Olympic level vs 0.5% gain in LC — but the *concept* is the same
- "Train less than you think" applies in both directions: athletes recover from training, LC patients recover from baseline
- Visualisation and breath-work are mainstream sports-psychology tools

## What changed for Katie

- 70-80% of immune system is gut-linked → focused on anti-inflammatory diet
- Reduced toxic load at home (cleaning products, soaps, detergents)
- Sense of humour, ability to plan, brain-clarity returned
- "Self-correction stack effect" — once nervous, immune, and metabolic systems started shifting, gains compounded fast

## Notes for Eric

The most novel contribution here is **cognitive-motor coordination drills** (ankle tilts, mobility drills with cognitive overlay) as a low-energy nervous-system tool — different from pure breathwork or pure pacing.
