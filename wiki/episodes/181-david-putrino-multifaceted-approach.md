# #181 — Dr David Putrino — The Multifaceted Approach

Professor of Rehab + Director of Rehabilitation Innovation at Mount Sinai. Runs six centres including their **Center for Infection-Associated Chronic Illness** (LC, ME-CFS, fibromyalgia, hEDS, chronic Lyme, vector-borne illness, dysautonomia). Implementation scientist by background — focus on getting interventions to patients quickly.

## Rehabilitation, redefined

Renamed his department from *Rehabilitation Medicine* to *Rehabilitation and Human Performance*. The same conceptual stack runs the spectrum from elite athletes (mitochondrial efficiency, oxidative-stress clearance, capillarisation) to people newly disabled by LC. **Increasing functional capacity ≠ curing.** Sometimes a wheelchair multiplies someone's life by orders of magnitude — that's rehab.

Multidisciplinary by design: physios, neuropsychologists doing **cognitive remediation therapy**, nutritionists, social workers matching patients to neighbours by zip code. He treats LC, ME-CFS, fibro, hEDS, chronic Lyme under one roof on purpose.

## Athletes ↔ LC parallel

LC may be partly an illness of **accelerated cellular aging**. Things that work for elite athletes turn out to plausibly help LC patients in subsets:
- **Oxaloacetate, CoQ10, NAD+** — Krebs-cycle support, mitochondrial efficiency.
- **HBOT** — capillarisation and oxygen bioavailability.
- **Metformin, low-dose rapamycin** — longevity-science overlap; reported help in subsets. Frame: "make cells more efficient at generating energy; boost immune function."

Cites **Rob Wüst** for muscle physiology / energy generation in this space.

## Symptoms have multiple drivers — treat the driver

> "What's more important is what's *driving* the PEM. What's the cause of the fatigue? Then there will be a treatment." — David Putrino, ep #181

Fatigue might be:
- **Disrupted slow-wave sleep** — cites **David Joffe**'s work; phenotype = vivid dreams, can sleep indefinitely, wakes unrefreshed. Sometimes responds to **modafinil** or similar.
- **Autonomic dysfunction (sympathetic overdrive)** — racing HR, then crash to parasympathetic shutdown after exertion ("tired but wired"). Breathwork central.
- **POTS subtypes:** crucial distinction — **hypertensive supine + drop on standing** vs **hyperadrenergic** (BP rises non-stop standing). Wrong drug for wrong subtype is harmful and confounds clinical trials.

## Putrino's ladder

1. **Stabilise symptoms** first — pacing (incl. apps **Visible**, **Guava** for data-driven patients; not for anyone who'd become fixated on logging). Help patients understand *why* a symptom is coming.
2. **Reduce symptoms** — vagus-nerve stim, ramped-up breathwork, antioxidants, nutritionist work (a lot of patients arrive **malnourished** — afraid of every food, surviving on three "safe" ones; needs adequate protein/calories rebuilt).
3. **Precision interventions** — once stable, drill into drivers: persistent pathogens (incl. SARS-CoV-2 in tissue, EBV reactivation, Babesia, Bartonella), autoimmunity, hormonal dysfunction.

## Paxlovid LC trial: failed, partially

Co-led with Yale (Iwasaki, Krumholz). Failed primary endpoint. Subgroup analysis underway — small percentage benefited. **Paxlovid does not penetrate tissue**, so any persistent SARS-CoV-2 hiding in gut, muscle, breast, lymphoid won't be reached. "We need drugs that penetrate the immune-privileged sites where chronic pathogens hide."

## Hormones / sex skew

Paper under review (Iwasaki + Putrino): testosterone appears **protective**. The lower testosterone (in women and men), and the lower estradiol in men (men make estrogens by testosterone conversion), the worse the symptoms. Decades of LC/ME-CFS/fibro literature explained the female skew with "anxiety/depression" — never asked the obvious sixth-grade-health-class question.

> "When you ask a drug company to embrace complexity, they ask how much it'll cost. Decades of medications were tested in male animals to avoid 'menstrual-cycle noise' — the noise is the point." — David Putrino, ep #181
