# #019 — Cass MacDonald — Life Adjustments for Chronic Conditions

**Guest**: Cass MacDonald — 46, non-binary, lives in Edinburgh. Office-based nurse (surveillance/audit/research role at the local Health Board). Pre-LC: **Ehlers-Danlos hypermobility type, chronic pain syndrome, asthma, autism, major depressive disorder**. Caught covid April 2020; severe long covid since. Partial return-to-work and second sick-leave at recording.

The single most **operationally useful** episode for someone newly trying to *organise their life* around long covid. No miracle interventions — instead, a thorough taxonomy of pacing, aids, OT, benefits, return-to-work, communication. Cass's pre-existing chronic-illness experience makes them a high-quality guide.

This pairs with [[013-aj-finding-my-sparkle]] (cross-condition wisdom) — both speakers came in with chronic-illness toolkits that LC could plug into.

## Recovery status

No. Currently signed off again with LC. Multiple systems remain affected: pain (now treated with **gabapentin**, weight-gain side effect), asthma (permanent step-up of inhaler post-covid — the bufomix-equivalent stays), antihistamines (now permanent year-round, not just hayfever season), GI symptoms.

## Symptom picture

- Atypical acute (April 2020): no cough, brief mild fever, terrible headache, exhaustion, **smell loss**.
- Severe dyspnea on stairs / 10 min walk; needed stronger inhaler, GP-advised hospital admission, declined.
- After return to work May 2020: 1 day in office → home. Pain flare June → put on gabapentin. Booked temporary 25-hr/week contract — financially worse, but the only sustainable option. Then crashed end of August 2020, signed off until February 2021.
- Brain fog, dizziness, **hand tremors** preventing embroidery, GI issues.
- Existing EDS pain massively amplified — both nerve-related and muscle pain. New permanent-grade chronic pain.

## Practical toolkit (the part Eric should re-read)

### Personal care — "the three weapons"
1. **Stool in the shower** — washing hair standing was a day-ender.
2. **Adjustable hair-dryer holder** — holding-and-moving a hairdryer is heavier than it sounds.
3. **Electric toothbrush** — fewer "spoons" despite being heavier.

Plus: shoes without laces, long shoehorn, easy-on clothing (fewer buttons), drying-rack ironing-skip.

### Household
- **Cleaner once a fortnight** — sheets, hoover, the heavy stuff. Pre-PIP, Cass couldn't afford this; PIP made it possible (see Benefits below).
- **Online grocery delivery** — heavy shop done remote; pop-out for milk only on good days.
- **Bread machine** for the days when "weighing flour separately is too many steps but pressing one button isn't."
- Ready meals + tinned soups baseline. Baked potatoes max-fresh-cooking.
- Sewing machine doubles as a fitness-for-work test — how long can you sit upright?

### Medication accounting
> "Each time you take medication, takes energy. Eating takes energy. Digestion takes energy. You have to factor that into your day." — Cass, ep #19

Eric's read-across: same logic as physical pacing, but applied to invisible loads.

## Pacing, framed honestly

Cass admits to being a **boom-and-bust pacer** — *"if I miraculously have the energy, I use it, knowing I might pay tomorrow."* Honesty is the lesson: pacing is hard even with prior chronic-illness experience.

> "Pacing is a skill for life. Even if I get back to my pre-LC level — I'd still want to actively think about it, so I never overdo it again."

## Return to work — the procedural detail

Cass walks through the UK NHS-employee process:
- **Phased return** twice — first via her boss + union rep alone (no Occupational Health appointment available); second time **with Occupational Health** support, longer phase (8 weeks), explicit blend of WFH and office.
- **Union (RCN/Unison)** — repeatedly named as the single most useful resource. Cass's loud advice: **join a union, even if you never need it**. They cannot help with issues that pre-date your joining, but they can with everything else.
- **Reasonable adjustments** under UK law:
  - Noise-cancelling headphones (autism)
  - Pink screen overlay (dyslexia)
  - Physio-assessed proper chair
  - Long-term permission to use a **rollator** (Rizo Mark 2 — folds, fits) at work — for fatigue + balance + sit-anywhere
  - Permission for medical naps if escalated (would not get a single bed — limit of "reasonable")

## UK Benefits — what Cass says actually works

- **Personal Independence Payment (PIP)** — got it first time, full standard rate, no mobility component. Critical: "It's not about your *condition*, it's about how you cope with daily-living activities — washing, cooking, mobility — *consistently and safely*."
- **Access to Work** — government grant, separate from PIP. Cass uses it for taxis to/from work (the bus-and-walk would be too much). Can also fund equipment + counselling. Self-employed people can apply.
- **Universal Credit** + Statutory Sick Pay as relevant.
- Resources: **benefitsandwork.co.uk** for application guides; Citizens Advice; ACAS for employment law; advocacy groups locally.
- **Take someone to the assessment.** Same advice as Iain in #11.

## Communication

Echoing the ranting/honest-conversation thread across the wiki:

- **Up-front conversation with friends**: here's what I can do, here's what I can't, here's what I might need (parking, walking distance, somewhere to sit).
- "I might have to cancel" — name it explicitly; offer alternatives proactively (close-to-home venues, smaller groups, virtual attendance).
- Cass's friends call them in over Whatsapp / Teams to office Christmas parties.
- **Don't say "get well soon" to someone with a chronic condition.** Better: "I hope this passes" / "I hope you're back to your usual operating parameters soon."
- The mindset shift: from *sick* (passive, hands-off) to *managing a chronic condition* (ownership) — important framing for both patient and family.

## On accepting the chronic frame

> "It might be like this for a very long time. Sometimes you can only have this conversation with your friends once you yourself have accepted that. And sometimes you'll grieve for who you were. That is okay. You will figure out ways to cope. Yes, maybe running was your thing. Maybe now it's seated hot yoga via the internet. You will adjust." — Cass, ep #19
