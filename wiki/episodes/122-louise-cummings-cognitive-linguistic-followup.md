# Episode 122 — Prof Louise Cummings — Follow-up Study on Cognitive & Linguistic Difficulties

Cummings (clinical linguist, Hong Kong PolyU) returns ~2 years after ep #18. Two new findings:

**1. Six-month follow-up of original 69-patient cohort.** 41 LC adults + 26 healthy controls (15 of original 26 had since had Covid and had to be replaced). Most language-task improvements were spontaneous *except* immediate and delayed verbal recall, which remained significantly impaired. Confrontation naming scores looked normal but with marked **cognitive struggle and inefficiency** to reach the target word — the score hides the effort. Cognitive issues outlasted physical symptoms in many cases.

**2. Employment outcomes (avg 21 months post-infection).** Of 41 patients:
- 37 employed pre-Covid → only **1** had returned to full pre-Covid role (an 18-y-o student, who later dropped to part-time)
- 11/36 in some reduced employment
- 25 not working; 7 ill-health retirement, 12 on social security

**What helped people return to work:**
1. *Extended phased returns* — over months, not weeks
2. *Role change* — fewer comms demands, less patient-facing
3. *Adjustments* — work-from-home, reduced hours, mobility scooter, computer adaptations
4. *Strong occupational health advocate*

**What hindered:** Inflexibility, insensitive HR meetings, infeasible redeployments, threats of dismissal as people tried to recover.

Quote from a 59 y/o participant: *"I returned to work or else was heading for dismissal due to ill health. I returned and worked to stated job description but was told I could have no reasonable adjustments or phased return... I was ill every day I was in work."*

**Current work:** International survey of 4,432 adults across 62 countries / 21 languages — looking for **subtypes of brain fog** and the statistical link between brain-fog subtype and employment outcome.

> "The cognitive demands of their role tended to basically prevent them getting back in."
> — Cummings, ep #122
