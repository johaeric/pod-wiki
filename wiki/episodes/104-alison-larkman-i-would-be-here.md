# #104 — Alison Larkman: "I Would Be Here If I Could" — an art project on the invisibility of severe ME / LC

Bristol-based artist Alison Larkman, **ME ~11 years** (initial trigger glandular fever in her 20s; mild for ~20 years; severe-onset 11 years before recording), introduces her **Arts Council England-funded participatory art project** — *I Would Be Here If I Could*. Not a recovery / mechanism / intervention episode. The wiki includes it because:

- It captures the **shared invisibility experience** between LC and ME from a participant who lives the severe end of it
- It's a **practical advocacy vehicle** — anyone with LC / ME / other illness that prevents physical presence can participate by leaving an audio message
- It articulates the *humanness* of "things you only realise mattered when they were taken away" — which the wiki has from recovery stories ([[094-jackies-recovery-story]], [[098-charlotte-hammond-recovery]]) but not yet from the perspective of someone *still* in it

## The project

**Premise:** people with chronic illness that prevents physical presence record an **audio message** about a place they would go if they could (a shopping centre, a beach, the right-hand side of a particular statue). A bespoke **giant mirror box** then tours the UK May–September the following year, **stopping at the places chosen by participants**, playing each message at its destination.

Operationally:

- Online submission via project website (recorded on participants' phones); guides + FAQs for accessibility; deadlines extended for severe-end participants who need more time or carer assistance.
- All messages preserved on an **online interactive map** — even places the box can't reach.
- Visitors at each location can step *inside* the mirror box, hear the message via headset, and **send postcards back** to the absent person who chose the place (addresses anonymised through the project).
- **Live-streaming** of the box's location so the absent person can see the weather, hear the surroundings, direct the box "a little to the left."
- Two-way mirror = box partly disappears into its surroundings (visual analogue of the absent person it represents).
- Postcode-based notification: people can sign up to be alerted when the box is within 25 miles of them.
- **Disruptive by design** — box appears unannounced at mountaintops, city centres, art venues. Tour route not pre-published. Press coverage expected.

## Why this matters on the wiki

The wiki has plenty of *recovery* voices and plenty of *clinician* voices. It has fewer voices articulating **what severe chronic illness actually feels like to live in** — and how invisibility compounds the illness itself. Larkman's framing is the clearest such articulation in this batch:

- **2 million people self-reporting LC in the UK; 500k with ME; 65k with severe ME (bedbound).** Larkman's grant-writing research; numbers worth preserving on the wiki for the scale-of-invisibility argument.
- **"Our absence makes us invisible"** — the project's working thesis. People at a place don't notice who isn't there; the box makes the absence visible.
- **Difficult-conversations dynamic**: the box short-circuits the social avoidance of chronic illness. People walking past a giant mirror box at a mountaintop have to wonder why it's there — and they will hear a voice that explains why.

## The "if you could be anywhere…" inventory (worth surfacing)

What people request — slices of life rather than dramatic destinations:

- Shopping centres (which Larkman herself never even liked when well — "you don't realise how amazing it is to wander through somewhere you haven't even thought about until you can't")
- Train stations
- Specific positions relative to landmarks ("right of the statue, not the left")
- Mountaintops
- Beaches

The mundane is what gets missed. Reframe for any LC patient still grieving the loss of "normal life" — the bar for *what counted* turns out to have been very low and very specific.

## Creative-output-as-coping mechanism

Larkman is on the same axis as many recovery-story guests: **creativity-as-energy-conserving, mood-supporting outlet** that doesn't pretend to cure anything.

> "Drawing a picture is not going to make your symptoms go away. You're not going to wake up one morning and say 'because I wrote a poem yesterday everything is fine.' But if it can take you out of it just for a few moments…" — Jackie Baxter, ep #104

Larkman herself: "I went from helping run companies and charging about, to coming into contact with a much more quieter sensitive side of myself."

Sound engineers who initially planned to clean up audio messages were told **not to edit out background noise / coughs / repetitions** — "isn't it great you can hear them, it sounds like it should." The humanness is the point. Useful counter-cue against [[self-compassion]]'s perfectionism trap (a pattern Jackie names in this episode about herself).

## Eric-relevance

Not directly clinically relevant. Worth following because:

- Larkman's articulation of the **"things you didn't notice mattered until they were taken away"** is one of the clearest in the wiki — Eric was in this state for years.
- The **online interactive map** of the project will become a kind of distributed memorial / archive of the LC and severe-ME experience — worth knowing as a reference point.
- Cross-condition solidarity (LC ↔ severe ME) is operationalised practically here, not just rhetorically.
