# #172 — Jo Thomas — Overcoming the Curse of Recovery Perfection

UK fatigue coach, nurse, and NLP-trained practitioner. Got POTS / dysautonomia in 2017 after a tick bite; recovered in layered fashion (medical management → mindset / pacing → personal-driver work). Says she is "probably fully recovered" — at least 1–2 years symptom-free at recording, but doesn't dwell on the binary.

## Recovery arc

1. **Medical management** for POTS (rate-control medication) — improved palpitations, dizziness, breathlessness. Did not touch the chronic fatigue.
2. **Mindset / pacing / nervous-system regulation** — the chronic fatigue layer. She frames this as "lifestyle work, mindset shifts, NSR practices."
3. **Pandemic-era resurgence** while working ICU + specialist department. Pushed through low-grade chronic stress, hit burnout, symptoms returned. Did NLP coach training during temporary leave; this gave her tools to address deep-seated drivers (perfectionism, overachieving, people-pleasing).
4. **Maintenance phase** — occasional mild symptoms now read as a signal that something is "out of sync" (stress, brewing virus). Not feared.

## Recovery perfection

The episode's central topic. Patients improve, then become **terrified of slipping back** and rigidly enforce every recovery practice — paradoxically holding them in fragility. Jo's reframe:

- **Test the waters** when stable: try a small activity step-up. Your responsibility is to attempt it. The body's response is not your responsibility — symptoms aren't your "fault."
- **Indifference, not perfection**, when symptoms appear (citing Dan Buglio's 5 Fs).
- The mindset must feel **believable** — coaching forces an "I'll fully recover" antidote prematurely fails. Find the next-step mindset (e.g., "I can improve") that doesn't trigger body resistance.

> "Recovery probably won't look linear. Responding appropriately to what life throws at you — that *is* a good recovery." — Jo Thomas, ep #172
