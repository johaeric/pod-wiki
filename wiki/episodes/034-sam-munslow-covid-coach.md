# #034 — Sam Munslow — Author, *The Covid Coach*

**Guest**: **Sam Munslow** — leadership and mental-health coach, founder of Blue Monkey Coaching. Author of ***The Covid Coach*** — a diary of her own LC illness assembled retrospectively from her social-media posts plus the gaps filled in by family. She got covid 5 April 2020 (very early UK wave). Family of four (husband + two boys); recovered fully by recording.

The wiki's **fifth full recovery story** and the first that frames the recovery as primarily *psychological skill* — rebuilding the self-talk that the illness collapsed. No single intervention to credit; the "what worked" was a posture toward the illness.

## Illness arc

- **5 April 2020** — onset; mild-cold symptoms then acute breathlessness over the night. GP didn't test ("we want to keep you out of the system"). Husband recurrently checked her breathing through the night for months.
- **Months in bed** — couldn't read, couldn't keep a real diary; her social-media posts and her family later filled in the timeline. Insomnia, joint pain, skin so hyperaesthetic the shower felt like hailstones.
- **Mid-2020 → 2021** — slow recovery. End of the book (planned ending: "I'm well again") was bumped by a relapse — kept in deliberately, to avoid presenting recovery as a clean line.
- **By recording (April 2022)** — fully well. Side note: covid permanently altered her chemical tolerance — can no longer use hair dye or makeup, now grey-haired and unable to wear cosmetics. (Filed alongside Asad Khan #37 / Tina Peers #38 as a likely **MCAS-overlap signature**.)

## "Mental self-harming" — the central frame

Coaching language for the inner-critic loop that ramped during illness. She frames it deliberately as "harming" because the *severity* matches. Pattern she names:

- "Bad days" relabelled as "rest days" — language change as treatment.
- Predicting the future ("I'll never run again") = a guaranteed loss; living in the now removed it.
- Guilt-as-bad-mother — turning Lego-rejection moments into evidence-of-failure.
- Resentment of supportive partner = misdirected; depersonalising it ("you resent the situation, not me") opened communication.

> "Acceptance brings you back to *how am I now, where am I now*. Fighting it takes energy you don't have." — Munslow

## The coach who couldn't coach herself

A through-line: she had the toolkit pre-illness but lost it for months — "humanness kicked in and all my coaching skills went out the window." Recovery involved literally re-reading her old writing and reapplying it to herself. Hence having a coach now ("every coach needs a coach").

## Practical takeaways she names

1. **Write down what you say to yourself** even if just a few lines. Re-read the next day. Awareness alone often defangs it.
2. **Communicate with the people supporting you** — name the resentment as situation-resentment, not person-resentment. Give them permission to hate the situation.
3. **Look at it from their perspective.** Several months in, she only realised her husband's nightly breath-checking was traumatic *for him*; that recognition reshaped how she received care.
4. **Permission to swap the shower for a bath with epsom salts** — picked up from a peer in a Facebook group. Small reframes from the community land harder than self-instruction.
5. **Joining a support group despite the fear** of "moan-fest" — recovery stories leave the group when they recover, so the visible signal is biased toward illness; balance that consciously.

## Quotes

> "We forget what we used to be like, very quickly — especially with something where you're focusing all your energy on relaxing your breathing so it doesn't hurt." — Munslow

> "It's a really crap situation for everyone. Recognising it isn't personal — it's the situation — depersonalises it and makes you able to speak openly." — Munslow

> "Covid was a gift, in a way. To anyone still going through it: cursing me right now, I get that. Coming out the other side, my perspective on what matters changed completely." — Munslow
