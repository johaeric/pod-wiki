# #92 — Q&A with Dr Groysman, Part 2

Continuation of [[091-groysman-qa-pt1]]. Wider Q&A — covers microclots scepticism, headache workup, diet/supplements, blood-test panels for LC, MCAS-adjacent topics, parosmia mechanism, and the Healthcare-side question of dealing with dismissive doctors.

## Microclots — Groysman's sceptical stance

Adds a notable counterweight to the wiki's [[033-pretorius-kell-microclots]] anchor. Groysman's position:

> "Anybody can pretty much do this in any doctor's office if you have a microscope and this fluorescent dye and a centrifuge. Before we hang our hat on this test, I think it needs to be validated. Does it really represent clot formation inside the body or not? I don't have an answer to that."

His clinical concerns:
- **Validation gap** — does the platelet-poor-plasma + fluorescent-dye microscopy represent in-vivo clotting?
- **Clinical relevance unproven** — even if microclots exist, are they impeding blood flow?
- **Anti-coagulants are not benign** — risk of catastrophic bleed (aneurysm, AV fistula, GI bleed, undiagnosed lesion). Has seen patients on **three anticoagulants simultaneously**.
- Distinguishes: **major pharma anticoagulants** (apixaban, rivaroxaban, etc.) carry higher bleed risk than **nattokinase / serrapeptase**, but the wider question of "what exactly are we treating, and is it clinically relevant?" stands.

This is the wiki's strongest sceptical voice on the microclot model — worth reading alongside [[033-pretorius-kell-microclots]] (the anchor pro-microclot episode), [[037-dr-asad-khan]] (anchor triple-therapy clinician), [[072-banerjee-stimulate-icp]] (rivaroxaban arm in formal trial). Logged as a hypothesis-of-record dispute, not a settled question.

## Headache (paediatric example, 11-year-old)

- Distinguish **headache vs scalp pain vs muscle pain** around head.
- Take prior history: pre-LC migraines exacerbated by LC are common.
- Standard workup: history + exam + imaging if indicated.
- **Medication side-effect** screening (some meds cause headache).

## Best for low heart rate

Unusual presentation in LC (high HR is more typical). Workup:
- Investigate baseline (athletes often run <60 bpm normally).
- Check medications (beta-blockers, calcium-channel blockers).
- **Caution with VNS** if baseline HR already low — VNS lowers HR further.

Jackie adds the meta-point: **"you need to know your previous baseline"** — most people never measured it pre-LC, complicating "is this abnormal" assessment.

## Diet + supplements

- Anti-inflammatory food, low-histamine if MCAS picture.
- **Test before supplementing**:
  - Iron — frequently low (poor absorption + increased utilisation)
  - **Vitamin D** — frequently low
  - **B12 and B6** — frequently low; occasionally B12 high; B1/B2 occasionally low
- **Coordinate with a clinician** — not benign at high doses (vit D toxicity possible; iron iatrogenic effects).

## Blood-test panels

Groysman's pragmatic stance (a useful "what to ask for" template):
- **CBC + differential**
- **Comprehensive metabolic profile**
- **CRP** (and other inflammatory markers if indicated)
- **Interleukins / cytokines** — *available* but unclear clinical utility; expensive; insurance unlikely to cover.
- **Clotting factors** indirectly or specific protein assays.
- Med-school 101 reminder: *"Don't order a test unless it's going to alter your treatment course."*

## Severe neurological symptoms (myoclonus, speech difficulty)

No definitive treatment yet. Some clinicians use **nerve-stabilising agents** (gabapentin / pregabalin / Cymbalta) — Groysman notes some patients view them as "toxins". Reports they're commonly prescribed; no strong personal recommendation.

## Gut healing

LC causes gut dysfunction in **just about every patient**, even without overt GI symptoms. Two layers:

1. **Microbiome correction** — probiotic, room-temperature stable. **Histamine-aware strain selection** if MCAS-picture (avoid histamine-producing strains; choose stabilising ones). Cross-ref [[099-viola-sampson-microbiome]] for the deeper microbiome anchor.
2. **Gut-barrier repair** — analogue of blood-brain barrier. Peptides named: **BPC-157**, **KPV**.

Most patients can recover gut function with probiotics + time; malabsorption corrects as gut repairs.

## Adrenals + LC

Adrenals downstream of sympathetic overdrive — adrenal "dumps" reflect SNS activation, not adrenal pathology. Cortisol spike → blood-sugar rise (fight-or-flight). The autonomic story unifies the picture.

## Stiff lower-leg skin + sneeze-induced sensation

Likely **MCAS-related connective tissue change** or generic LC connective-tissue changes — *not* a nerve issue (stiffness is a muscle/tendon/ligament symptom, not neural).

## Spike protein + SGB interaction

Speaks again to scepticism on the spike-protein hypothesis (#91 also). If spike *does* exist + drives chronic inflammation, treat both — but unrelated mechanisms in principle. SGB recurrence after benefit could indicate ongoing inflammation source.

## Why delayed-onset LC?

**Chronic inflammation takes months to develop.** Acute inflammation → cytokine activation → over months → chronic state. Explains the common pattern: "I felt OK for 2-3 months after acute COVID, then it hit."

## Why some people, not others?

No definitive answer. Risk factors named:
- **Female > male**
- **Older > younger**
- **Autoimmune comorbidity** (RA, lupus, Crohn's, ulcerative colitis)
- **Prior viral history** — Lyme, EBV (caveat: nearly everyone has had EBV at some point — not a useful filter)
- **Genetic predisposition** — speculation only.

## Brain fog mechanisms (consolidated list)

Five named potential drivers:
1. Chronic brain inflammation
2. Vascular / blood-flow changes from sympathetic overdrive
3. Sleep disruption / poor deep sleep
4. Medication side-effect
5. Gut-dysbiosis / pathogen-toxin via gut-brain axis

## Parosmia / dysgeusia mechanism

Groysman's "**brown-out reset**" framing:
- Acute COVID directly damages **olfactory epithelium and support cells** + olfactory nerves.
- Body attempts repair — but the reboot misfires → parosmia/dysgeusia rather than full restoration.
- **SGB acts as external reboot** — patients sometimes report **30-second** post-block normalisation. Speed of effect rules out structural repair → consistent with **functional dysregulation** (regional blood flow, autonomic input) rather than structural damage.

## Final question — dealing with dismissive doctors

Groysman's plea to clinicians: **"open yourself up to learning new things... long covid is not in people's heads."** The historic chronic-fatigue-syndrome and fibromyalgia pattern — sent-to-psychiatrist-because-tests-normal — is being repeated with LC.

To patients: if your doctor is dismissive after attempted dialogue → **find another**. There are practitioners who are interested and helpful; finding them is a search problem.
