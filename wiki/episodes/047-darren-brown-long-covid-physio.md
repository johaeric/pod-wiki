# #47 — Darren Brown — Long Covid Physio

Advocacy / professional-organisation episode. Darren Brown is a clinical-academic physiotherapist at the NHS in London (specialist clinical and research background in HIV disability and rehabilitation, plus cancer care). He has long covid himself and is the **founding chair of Long Covid Physio** — the international association of physiotherapists with LC and their allies, founded in **November 2020** on Twitter.

Brown's framing borrows from HIV activism (Peter Staley's "connect to create change") and is unusually clear on the **physio profession's collective harm-and-then-pivot** moment with LC: physiotherapists were the people whose default response to fatigue was "exercise more," and they were the people who got worse trying it on themselves.

## What Long Covid Physio is

International, patient-led association. Founding principle: **peer support** for physiotherapists / Allied Health Professionals living with LC. Membership across at least the UK, Ireland, Canada, US, Netherlands, Belgium, Finland, Cameroon, Nigeria, New Zealand, India, with active recruitment in Spanish/Portuguese-speaking and Southeast Asian regions.

Three streams of work:
1. **Peer support** in safe spaces (members only — not for "outsiders to gawp and learn from").
2. **Education** — website resources; the World Physiotherapy briefing paper; the physio.cam video series; the Forum.
3. **Policy** — collaboration with NHS England and Improvement, World Physiotherapy, partner LC orgs.

Cross-collaboration with [[long-covid-support]] (placeholder), Long Covid Kids, Long Covid SOS, Long Covid Nurses & Midwives UK ([[long-covid-nurses-and-midwives-uk]]).

## Landmark output: the World Physiotherapy Briefing Paper (June 2021)

**"Safe Rehabilitation Approaches for People Living with Long Covid"** — published as the 9th World Physiotherapy COVID briefing paper. Translated into **11 languages**. Used in the formation of rehabilitation policies and guidelines globally. World Physiotherapy Day 2021 used "long COVID" as its theme and built educational one-pagers (translated into **60+ languages**) on top of the briefing paper.

Core position: **safe rehabilitation = stability + maintenance + symptom-titrated activity**, not progressive overload. Pacing is rehabilitation. Education is rehabilitation. Heart-rate monitoring is rehabilitation. Rehabilitation is broader than exercise.

> "Just because you can doesn't mean you should." — Brown, ep #47

This is a clean reframing of the GET-doctrine that hurt the ME/CFS community for decades and was being repeated against LC patients in 2020–2021. The paper is now the bedrock of what World Physiotherapy considers safe rehab in this disease.

## Video series (with physio.cam / Lara)

8-part animated explainer series, 4 released by recording date:
1. What is long covid?
2. Why is it called long covid?
3. Common symptoms (specifically the disbelieved ones — overlap with ME/CFS classification)
4. The episodic nature of long covid disability
5. (forthcoming) Impact on day-to-day life — co-produced with Somerset NHS Foundation Trust peer-support groups

144,000+ views across platforms by recording date. Co-produced — peer-review, multiple iterations with PWLC consultants.

## Long Covid International Forum (Sept 9–10, 2022)

The flagship event. 80 speakers, 17 countries, 50%+ speakers with LC, 50%+ women, 33%+ Black/Asian/minority-ethnic. Five formats:

- Welcome & introduction
- Science sessions (≤ 30 min, invited speaker + community-led panel translation)
- Discussion panels (no slides; 50%+ speakers with LC)
- Workshops (live-illustrated by physio.cam, breakout rooms — everyone gets a voice)
- Summary panels (delegates reflecting on highlights)

Disability-inclusive design: closed captions, on-demand recording, scheduled rest breaks, **no back-to-back sessions**, scholarships for PWLC / disabled / chronic-illness attendees (no fee). Healthcare professional fee $50; speakers paid stipends.

Sponsors: Kaiser Permanente, Visible app, University of Toronto Temerty Faculty of Medicine, Realize Canada. Academic partner: Rocky Mountain University of Health Professions.
