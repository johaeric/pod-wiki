# Episode 120 — Dr Mark Faghy — Research Update & First UK Long-Covid Antiviral Trial

Faghy returns to update on the Derby team's work since 2022. Three threads:

1. **Cohort observation (~100 patients, 4 months, weekly contact)** — confirms LC's day-to-day episodic nature; patient state can swing 180° in 48 hours, which standard 6/12-month cohort designs miss. Snapshot assessments are hostage to whichever day the patient is tested.
2. **Mechanistic CPET work (Rebecca Rowe, Callum Thomas)** — two-day cardiopulmonary exercise tests in >100 patients show impairment along the entire chain (respiratory → cardiovascular → muscular). High anaerobic contribution; reduced oxygen utilisation. Standard "normal range" reference values miss the small-but-meaningful deviations LC patients show. Co-author on a paper arguing *there is no normal*.
3. **First UK antiviral trial (Gilead-funded, £1.25m, starting Easter 2024)** — Phase 4 tolerability study: 5 consecutive days of IV antiviral infusion (60min day 1, 30min days 2–5). Targets viral persistence (Faghy credits Amy Proal's work, ep #103). Dose regime designed by working backwards from patient capacity. 1,200 expressions of interest in a weekend. If tolerable, will move straight to RCT. Severe / bedbound patients (e.g. PPI rep Lindsay) cannot access this protocol — a separate, less burdensome regime is being designed.

Recurring theme: lived-experience data has reshaped the team's research agenda. Their PPI group meets monthly + WhatsApp. Patient diaries surface what CRF data sheets cannot. Open-access publication is policy.

Faghy is explicit: viral persistence is **one of seven mechanisms** underpinning LC. The antiviral won't cure everyone, but it's the first targeted-mechanism candidate they can put in patients in the UK.

> "If we understand we can affect, rather than 'oh let's try this and hope something sticks.'"
> — Mark Faghy, ep #120
