# #119 — Daniel Gomez & Ewa Beldzik: fMRI imaging study for LC fatigue mechanism

Two postdocs sharing a US neuroimaging study designed to **map the neural and physiological substrates of LC mental fatigue**. Daniel Gomez (engineer; postdoc at Massachusetts General Hospital, MR physics-and-instrumentation) + Ewa Beldzik (cognitive neuroscientist at MIT, **Laura Lewis lab** — sleep and arousal). Recruiting in the Boston area.

## What the study does

Functional MRI (fMRI) — making **videos of the brain** during three tasks:

1. **Breath-hold task** (15s) — controls for vascular / physiological response without neuronal activation. Lets the team separate "what's the blood doing on its own" from "what's the neural signal."
2. **Simple visual task** — strong sensory-system response; tests whether basic visual-system signal differs in LC patients vs healthy controls.
3. **Color-word matching cognitive task** — Stroop-style cognitive control task; tests prefrontal cortex involvement; behavioral output measurable (response time, errors). The fatigue-detection lever — patients hypothesised to *degrade over the 12-min task* in a way controls don't.

## Anchor: hypothesis-driven design

The mechanism Gomez is testing:

- **Acute COVID** in ICU autopsies showed **lesions linked to lack of oxygenation** — hypoxic damage
- **LC** shows **no anatomical change in 98% of cases on standard clinical MRI**
- *But* if the **lack-of-oxygenation pathway is partially active without reaching tissue-injury threshold**, **functional MRI** sensitive to blood oxygenation should detect *functional* changes the *anatomical* MRI misses
- **Microclots / endothelitis** literature ([[033-pretorius-kell-microclots]]) compatible with this — if blood flow regulation is impaired, fMRI of vasculature reactivity (via breath-hold task) will show it

The wiki holds this as **first detailed mechanism-targeted neuroimaging study aimed at the LC fatigue subpopulation**. Distinct from cognitive-symptom population studies that look for anatomical pathology.

## Anchor: why fMRI is the right tool here

> "Functional images are much more sensitive to any change that's happening. This is a double-edged sword — it's sensitive to everything which is great, but also bad because it makes it hard to distinguish things." — Daniel Gomez

The advantage is also the constraint: you can detect very subtle change, but you have to control for *every* physiological variable (breathing rate, CO2 expired, motion, hematocrit). Hence the breath-hold control, the respiratory-belt monitoring, the pulse oximeter, the expired-CO2 mask. **40-50 GB of data per session; terabytes total.**

## Recruitment + timeline

- Currently scanned: ~12-13 healthy controls + 10-11 patients (target 30 each)
- Pace: 1-2 scans/week (other groups also use the equipment)
- Boston area only; transport offered
- Enrollment criteria: MRI-compatible (no implanted metal); LC with severe mental fatigue; no severe lung issues (would confound respiration); not on certain medications
- Data collection complete: ~July-Aug 2024 expected
- Final results: mid-to-late 2025

## Anchor: blood tests should *exclude* not *identify*

> "Tests should be only used to exclude conditions, not to identify them. If someone of course has complaints, then check that it's not everything we know about — and once that's done, the conversation should *start*, not end. Trying to identify *where* to look — that's where the interesting questions are." — Daniel Gomez

The wiki has a parallel framing in [[081-liza-dileo-thomas-patient-experience]] ("great news, everything's normal" antipattern) — but Gomez articulates the *research-design* version of it. **This is the methodological inverse of the standard clinic where normal Bloods = "you're fine."**

## Anchor: the chronic-fatigue research lineage

Beldzik notes that some of the tasks (e.g., Stroop / color-word matching) have been used in pre-Covid chronic-fatigue research. **What's new is combining them at this resolution + with the blood-flow-control protocol + in the LC subpopulation specifically.**

Implication: **the LC fatigue → fMRI question is a research-priority lever for ME/CFS research too.** Whatever they find applies upstream.
