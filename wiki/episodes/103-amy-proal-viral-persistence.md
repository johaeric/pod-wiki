# #103 — Amy Proal: viral persistence in long covid (anchor episode)

The wiki's strongest single articulation of **viral persistence as the leading mechanistic hypothesis** for a meaningful subset of LC, from the microbiologist running the international research effort coordinating it. Amy Proal is **President + CSO of [[polybio]]** (PolyBio Research Foundation) and runs the **Long Covid Research Consortium** — a collaborative network of teams at Harvard Medical School, Stanford, Karolinska, UCSF, Penn, Mt Sinai, and others. PolyBio existed *before* COVID — they were already studying ME/CFS, post-Ebola, post-Lyme, and other infection-associated chronic illness. They knew from day one that a subset of COVID patients would develop persistent symptoms.

## The viral-reservoir hypothesis (anchor)

The core claim: **SARS-CoV-2 is not always cleared after acute infection**. In a meaningful subset of LC patients, residual virus persists in *tissue* (gut, lung, lymph nodes, olfactory bulb, spinal cord, possibly brain) — *not* in blood, because pathogens migrate to tissue precisely to evade circulating immune cells. Standard blood PCR tests therefore miss it; the wiki should not interpret a negative blood test as evidence of clearance.

Mechanism candidates by which a tissue reservoir drives chronic symptoms:
- **Spike protein leakage** into blood (found up to 16 months post-infection in published work) — implies *replication* still happening in the reservoir, not just inert genetic remnants
- **Microclot seeding** (per Pretorius/Kell #33) — spike protein has been shown to seed amyloid clot formation
- **Gut barrier degradation** ("leaky gut") → microbial translocation into blood → secondary inflammation
- **Vagal-nerve / brainstem dysregulation** — viral inflammation around the vagus nerve produces flu-like, autonomic, nausea, pain symptoms because that brainstem region happens to control all of them

## RNA virus persistence — the under-studied frontier

Herpes viruses (DNA — EBV, HHV-6, HHV-7, CMV) are well-documented persistent pathogens. **RNA viruses (SARS-CoV-2, Ebola, Zika, measles) have historically been assumed to clear after acute infection** — but the assumption rests on absence of evidence rather than evidence of absence. Diane Griffin (Johns Hopkins) has shown measles persists in mouse brain post-acute illness, even *deliberately mutating* to evade immune detection. Post-Ebola syndrome — virus retained in eye tissue, semen, breast milk — was Proal's initial cue to look for SARS-CoV-2 persistence in LC.

> "The reason you haven't heard RNA viruses persist isn't because they don't — it's because no research team really studied it that way." — Amy Proal, ep #103

## Specific findings the Consortium has published or is publishing

- **Tongue tissue (loss of taste)**: virus found in tongue tissue of taste-loss patients up to 163 days
- **Olfactory bulb (smell loss)**: virus persistence in nasal/olfactory tissue
- **Gut tissue**: SARS-CoV-2 spike protein found in intestinal biopsies up to **676 days** post initial infection (longest examined; persistence may extend further). This is via gut biopsy collected through colonoscopy — a real procedure done on real LC patients.
- **UCSF PET imaging** (Henrich/Peluso group): radiolabeled antibody binding to activated T cells produces a **whole-body map of immune activation** in LC patients. Findings: T-cell activation in **gut wall, spinal cord, lymph nodes**. Same patients had viral RNA + spike protein in their gut biopsies.
- **Spike protein in blood up to 12–16 months** post-infection. Argues active replication, since the body would otherwise clear free protein within months.

## The "mild acute → severe chronic" pattern

A subset of LC patients had **mild or asymptomatic** acute COVID, then progressively worsened. Hard to explain on a "residual inflammatory damage" model — much more compatible with **ongoing pathogen activity** that escalates over time.

The **relapsing-remitting** symptom pattern is similarly consistent: pathogens have variable activity (replicating phases vs immune-controlled phases), so symptoms wax and wane.

## Why some patients clear and others don't

Proal's three candidate factors:

1. **Immune state at time of infection** — antibody neutralisation level, T-cell readiness; can be impacted by *prior* infection load (e.g. EBV reactivation predisposing the immune system before COVID even arrived).
2. **Existing pathogen burden** — the Karolinska twin study (CMV) showed twins with one herpes virus had measurably different immune-system parameters. Stack enough hits — EBV + bad colds + mold exposure — and you can be too immune-modulated to clear SARS-CoV-2 cleanly.
3. **Human genetic variation** — clotting genes, immune-system genes. Already-elevated clotting predisposition + spike-protein-seeded microclots = bigger problem than for someone with neutral genetics.

## LC vs ME/CFS — Proal's framing

> "Long covid is not ME/CFS. They share common pathways and overlapping symptoms — but the upstream pathogen matters."

- ME/CFS is heterogeneous (multiple upstream infections — herpes viruses, enteroviruses, Lyme — converging on a common downstream symptom set via the vagus nerve / brainstem).
- LC is *one* upstream pathogen (SARS-CoV-2) that **must** be retained as a name for trial design — antiviral RCTs need a specifically SARS-CoV-2-onset cohort to be interpretable. **Direct counter-position to [[dan-neuffer]] (#102)**, who would prefer "post-viral fatigue syndrome" subsumes LC. Wiki holds both positions.
- Both presentations show common downstream **vagal/brainstem inflammation** producing flu-like, autonomic, pain, nausea symptoms — same final common pathway, different upstream drivers.

## Methodology — why tissue biopsy + imaging matters

The Consortium's deliberate choice: **tissue, not just blood**.

- **Gut biopsy** (colonoscopy)
- **Lung biopsy** (bronchoscopy)
- **Lymph-node biopsy**
- **PET-imaging** with antibody-tagged radiolabels — UCSF method ported from prior HIV-reservoir-imaging work, now applied to SARS-CoV-2 (next: EBV, then Lyme spirochete)

This is technically harder than blood-draw studies, takes longer to gain ethical approval, and depends on patients willing to undergo invasive procedures. Proal explicitly thanks them. **Wiki signal**: when interpreting "viral-persistence sceptic" papers based purely on blood PCR (e.g. Groysman in [[091-groysman-qa-pt1]] / [[092-groysman-qa-pt2]]), note that those papers are looking in the wrong place by Proal's framing.

## Treatment implications — antivirals + combinations

- **Paxlovid trials** — already running ([[paxlovid-lc-trial]] at Yale, Stanford, etc.)
- **Truvada (tenofovir + emtricitabine)** — HIV antiviral with documented activity against EBV and hepatitis. PolyBio + Mt Sinai planning a Truvada trial. Generic, available — does not need pharma buy-in.
- **Combination therapy** — Proal flags that with persistent pathogens, single-antiviral courses are often insufficient (per HIV experience). May need 2–3 drugs, possibly with an immunomodulator that boosts the immune response *toward* the reservoir simultaneously. **Critical design principle for future LC RCTs.**
- **Tissue-specific drug screening** — Penn researcher (likely Sara Cherry's group) testing candidate antivirals in *intestinal* vs *lung* vs *endothelial* tissue models — different reservoirs may respond to different drugs.
- **Mt Sinai clinic** with **David Putrino** — building a treatment clinic to translate Consortium findings into care, with the explicit goal of being a *replicable* model.

## Anti-psychosomatic argument (worth preserving)

> "When someone says LC must be psychosomatic — that is so categorically ridiculous. If you understand the *number* of ways viruses like SARS-CoV-2 drive chronic disease — they hijack mitochondria, hijack human gene expression, modulate the immune system, damage blood vessels — to jump to 'it's the person's thinking' is hilarious. Those people just don't understand viruses." — Amy Proal, ep #103

## Recent Consortium review paper

A multi-author review article (Proal et al., authors = the Consortium teams) was published the week of recording. Walks through:
- Documented evidence of SARS-CoV-2 tissue persistence (with a chart of studies)
- Mechanisms by which a reservoir would drive chronic symptoms
- Open research questions designed to be the questions Phase 2 of the Consortium answers
- **Explicit call for accelerated antiviral RCTs**, including combination therapies — directed at NIH

## Eric-relevance

- **Confirms that "LC = psychological" is a dead position** — not worth Eric engaging with clinicians who hold it.
- **Spike protein in blood 12–16 months** is testable at academic-research level (not commercially available yet) — worth Eric tracking for when it becomes available.
- **Paxlovid / Truvada / combination antiviral trials** are the candidate route for any persistence-driven component of Eric's case — but currently US-only and small N. Not actionable for Eric yet, but worth tracking the Yale + Mt Sinai readouts.
- **Asymptomatic-acute-then-severe-chronic pattern** is consistent with Eric's profile; persistence is one of the candidate mechanisms. Compatible with — not in conflict with — the dysautonomia framing ([[ans-rewire]], polyvagal). The frames operate at different levels: viral persistence = cause; dysautonomia = downstream consequence of the immune/vagal disruption the virus drives.
- The frame also explains why Eric's microclot/clotting markers (if any) and his MCAS-overlap symptoms could share a single upstream — spike protein in tissue.
