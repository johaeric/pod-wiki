Nora Rodden — Cornell biology then NYC + Harvard MBA. Three sequential health crises across her 20s and early 30s: chronic back pain (after being hit by a car at Cornell), GI symptoms, then severe insomnia at HBS. Now CEO and co-founder of **Nirvana**, a neuroplastic-recovery app distilling the methodology that helped her.

## Three layered conditions, one underlying mechanism

- **Back pain** post-car-accident — chiro, PT, acupuncture, steroid injections, **nerve ablation** (failed) — clue that the source wasn't structural
- **GI symptoms** — many years; tried diets, antibiotics, GI specialists
- **Insomnia** at Harvard — pre-bed mini-panic-attacks; the worst of the three subjectively
- All three resolved through neuroplastic / mind-body / TMS-style approach

## The clinical trial — Psychophysiologic Symptom Relief Therapy (PSRT)

- Pilot study at MGH-affiliated team; back-pain protocol
- ~3 months of group video sessions with: pain education, mind-body framework, somatic tracking, breathwork, expressive writing prompts
- **Result**: 66% of participants pain-free at end vs ~10% in placebo arm; substantially better than the mindfulness-only arm
- Showed mind-body / neuroplastic recovery is *different from* mindfulness

## Insomnia recovery — outcome independence

- Found a TMS Wiki post on insomnia from a community member
- Key shift: **outcome independence**. Rather than chase sleep, *stop caring*. Convince the brain that zero hours of sleep is OK.
- Tools: safety mantras / affirmations ("even if I sleep zero hours, I will be okay"); reviewing analytical data ("how many times have I performed well on little sleep — actually quite often")
- Resolved within months of consistent practice

## GI recovery — *The F It Diet*

- Read *The F It Diet* — author's solution to chronic dieting was to eat whatever she wanted to calm hunger
- Applied analytical brain to the inconsistent FODMAP triggers (sometimes onion = bloated, sometimes not). The inconsistency was the data — it wasn't actually about onions.
- Took the longest to fully heal, partly because of disordered-eating history and societal weight pressure

## On being analytical vs. somatic

- Says she is *bad* at "feel into your body" prompts despite being a yoga teacher
- Found **expressive writing — writing faster than she could think** — was *her* way to bypass the analytical brain and access feeling
- Different routes work for different people

## Awareness chain

- Hindsight: chronic acne, lifelong nail-biting (cuticle-shredding) → the body had been signalling stress her whole life
- Recovery isn't a single light-bulb moment — it's gradual, then occasional re-tests when symptoms reappear

## Notes for Eric

The most valuable single concept here is **outcome independence**: *stop caring about the metric*, and the metric self-corrects. Useful for sleep specifically, also generalisable to symptom-tracking obsession (sleep, HR, glucose, etc.). The PSRT trial is real and stronger than most LC-adjacent mind-body trial evidence — worth holding next to #196 (NR trial) as evidence that *some* mind-body approaches have RCT support. Nora's "analytical brain bypass via fast handwriting" is the most concrete *how* on this wiki for a left-brain-dominant person who can't access feeling.

## Key quote

> — Nora Rodden, ep #211
