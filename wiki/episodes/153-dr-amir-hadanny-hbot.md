# #153 — Dr Amir Hadanny — Hyperbaric Solutions to Long Covid Challenges

Israeli physician (neurosurgery training + hyperbaric-medicine specialty since 2008) + bioinformatics PhD. Practises in Tel Aviv, Florida, and Dubai. **Anchor RCT-evidence HBOT episode for LC** — pairs with [[096-dr-masha-makeeva-hbot]] (clinician-and-patient endorsement). Hadanny's team has now run hyperbaric RCTs in fibromyalgia, ME/CFS, PTSD, traumatic brain injury, stroke — and a sham-controlled LC RCT with **published positive results + 18-month sustained-improvement follow-up**.

## Why this episode matters

- **First sham-controlled LC HBOT RCT discussed in detail on the wiki.** Methodology stress-tested for blinding; symptoms questionnaires + objective MRI/fMRI/SPECT imaging + cognitive-battery confirmed convergent improvements.
- **18-month follow-up showing sustained gains** — counter to the "feels good then fades" critique of HBOT.
- **The "covid horns" imaging signature**: orbital-frontal lobes + temporal poles + hippocampi dysfunction on SPECT, mapped to viral entry through the cribriform plate and olfactory tract. Matches Perrin's #160 mechanism story from a different angle.
- **Eight-vs-twelve-week dose distinction**: clinic experience says LC patients with **>6 months illness duration need 60 sessions, not the 40-session RCT protocol**. Trial = 40 sessions × 5 days/week × 2 hours/day = 8 weeks. Established LC = 12 weeks.

## The protocol

- **Two-hour daily session** in a multi-place metal chamber, ~2.0 ATA (typical hyperbaric pressure).
- **Oxygen fluctuations**: cycles of 100% O₂ via mask + air breaks throughout the session — not continuous oxygen. Hadanny: this is the **mechanistic key**. Interval-training analogy: tricks mitochondria into sensing relative drops, triggering hypoxia-induced gene-expression cascades → stem-cell mobilisation, angiogenesis, mitochondrial biogenesis.
- **40 sessions for trial; 60 for established LC.** No skipping days; consecutive 5-days-per-week.
- **Patient selection**: SPECT scan first. If the typical orbital-frontal / temporal-pole metabolic-deficit pattern is present, treat. If not, decline — they can't help.

## The four healing-a-wound components (Hadanny framework)

1. **Energy** — extra oxygen via Henry's-law plasma dissolution.
2. **Trigger** — oxygen fluctuations as the mitochondrial signal.
3. **Stem cells** — mobilised by the trigger.
4. **Angiogenesis** — new blood vessels (~2 months minimum to grow; the reason 8–12 weeks is the floor).

The 8–12 week duration is biological, not arbitrary — fetal blood-vessel growth takes the same kind of timeline.

## The Aviv RCT

- **N≈90 LC patients, sham-controlled.** Patients didn't know which arm they were in (sham = chamber pressure-up enough to require ear equalisation + air through mask; ~50/50 patient guesses post-trial = good blinding).
- Outcomes:
  - **Subjective**: significant improvement in symptoms, quality-of-life, energy/fatigue, sleep, pain.
  - **Objective MRI/fMRI**: improved perfusion (new blood vessels), improved micro-structure (DTI), improved network connectivity in treated group vs sham. Subjective and objective improvements correlated.
  - **Cognitive battery**: clinically + statistically significant improvements across attention, executive function, processing speed, memory.
  - **18-month follow-up**: gains sustained.
- Three publications out of the trial: baseline-vs-8-weeks subjective, baseline-vs-8-weeks imaging, 18-month follow-up.
- Hadanny is now compiling **hundreds of routine-clinic LC patients** retrospectively for a larger cohort paper (60-session real-world dose).

## Hadanny's framing of LC vs other neuroinflammation

- LC is neuroanatomically distinctive — viral entry through the cribriform plate maps to **frontal lobes** (cognitive / brain-fog) + **temporal poles + hippocampi** (memory). "We're not really sure" about the underlying cellular mechanism (inflammation? mitochondrial? histamine? thrombotic? possibly intertwined) but the metabolic-imaging signature is consistent enough to guide selection.
- "Networks not regions" — the brain works in connected networks since fMRI; LC dysfunction propagates from the orbital-frontal seed across networks, explaining why people with LC have such varied symptomatology beyond pure cognitive dysfunction.

## Eric-relevant takeaways

- **HBOT remains a strong candidate** for LC in general but Eric is dominantly autonomic/POTS, not cognitive-injured-frontal-lobe. The Aviv selection criteria would imaging-first; if no SPECT signature, no benefit predicted.
- **8-week minimum / 12-week recommended commitment** — practical and financial barrier (UK private clinics; MS Charity UK route from #96 still cheapest).
- The **oxygen-fluctuation protocol distinguishes Hadanny's HBOT from generic mild-HBOT** — soft-shell 1.3 ATA chambers without the on/off oxygen cycling are **probably weaker**.
