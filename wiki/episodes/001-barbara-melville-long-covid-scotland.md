# #001 — Barbara Melville on founding Long Covid Scotland

**Guest**: Barbara Melville — linguist; first-wave long-hauler (March 2020); founder of [[long-covid-scotland]], a Scottish patient advocacy organisation.

**Format**: Interview, advocacy/policy focus. Almost no concrete intervention discussion — this is a campaigning episode.

## Recovery status

Still ill at time of recording (≈17 months in). Reports orthostatic intolerance ("a year not being able to walk because of that"), persistent breathlessness, autonomic dysfunction, mild brain fog. Notably **does not have PEM** in the classic sense — "I don't get the same kind of pattern of post-exertional malaise that some people describe."

## Diagnoses / clinical findings

- Provisional **POTS** (postural orthostatic tachycardia syndrome)
- **Heart damage** found on a research MRI scan (paid for privately) — invisible on standard bloods/chest X-ray/ECG
- "Terry's nails" (white nails with a red band, a possible marker of liver/heart pathology) — dismissed by her GP as "just pale"

## Key claims and themes

- **Healthcare gaslighting**: 111 told her her collapse was anxiety; paramedics rolled their eyes when she couldn't stand. A "lovely doctor named Olly" early on identified autonomic nervous system involvement well before that became a public conversation.
- **Anxiety-of-being-called-anxious**: many patients underreport mental-health needs because they've been pre-emptively dismissed as anxious.
- **The case for one-stop specialist clinics**: respiratory + cardiology + neurology + rheumatology + allergy in one place; lower infection risk, better mental-health effect, central coordination instead of "DIY" referrals.
- **Inequality in care**: she could go private (MRI, cardiology). Patients without the means are stuck.
- **Cross-condition solidarity**: ME/CFS, POTS, MCAS, Lyme, lupus, fibromyalgia communities have walked this road; Long Covid Scotland actively builds these relationships.
- **A health-economics argument for clinics**: repeat A&E attendances for chest pain/breathlessness probably cost more than a clinic model would. References the Scottish "heart failure GP" model as a possible cheaper alternative to a glossy specialist building.
