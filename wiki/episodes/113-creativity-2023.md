# #113 — Long Covid Creativity 2023

Annual creativity compilation episode — Jackie hosts patient creators sharing how art became their recovery substrate. **Third in the creativity-compilation series** (#14 in 2021, #65 in 2022, #113 in 2023). Same wiki principle as previous compilations: the *practice* matters more than the medium; the wiki tracks *what types of creativity show up across recoveries*.

## Anchor framing across contributors

Recurring patterns:

- **Creativity displaces the symptom focus** — most explicit from Miriam ("when I crochet, nothing else matters") and Wendy ("takes me away from the covid head fuzziness")
- **Career pivot** — Naila and Juliana both built new self-employed creative livelihoods compatible with LC unpredictability
- **Community is the second-order benefit** — Naila names the Instagram nail community; Jess names the restart-creative cohort; Anne names the readings as connection
- **Online > in-person formats** — Jess explicitly tried in-person and it failed for both her (as teacher) and clients (as participants); pivoted to online which works
- **Output gives a reason to continue** — exhibition, book, social media post, performance; the externalisation matters

## Anchor: Louise on emotional repression and chronic illness

Louise references **Gabor Maté's** chronic-illness-↔-emotional-repression framing — that the nervous-system fragility of LC/CFS leaves emotions felt at unbearable intensity, and creativity is the release valve. The wiki holds this as **patient-side adjacent to [[mind-body]]** but doesn't endorse Maté's stronger claims.

## Anchor: Skye Yu's framing of why she wrote *Still Moving*

> "I never wanted to give words to this experience — never wanted to imbue life into it via language. But all that not-sharing revealed that if I didn't write this one day I would start screaming and not stop." — Skye Yu

Generalises: *not naming* the LC experience has its own cost; many LC patients try silence as protection, find it doesn't protect. The wiki's parallel is [[078-lucy-gahan-psychology]] on naming and witnessing.

## Eric-relevance

- The compilation pattern is **non-prescriptive** — Eric isn't being told to paint or write. The signal is: *find the thing that displaces symptoms and creates output you can come back to.* The wiki has now logged this across three years.
- **[[restart-creative]]** (Jess Taylor's CIC) is the wiki's first dedicated **online LC-specific art programme** with funded delivery — UK-only but worth filing.
- **Skye Yu's *Still Moving*** memoir is now on the wiki's books-by-LC-patients list.
- The **Maté repression-and-chronic-illness frame** appears frequently across recovery stories; Eric has heard variants of it from [[007-suzy-bolt]] and [[048-saskia-mulder]] in different forms.
