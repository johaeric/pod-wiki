# #016 — ReDIRECT Study

**Guests**: Dr **David Blane** (GP and clinical research fellow, University of Glasgow, co-lead) and Prof **Emily Combet** (human nutrition, University of Glasgow, co-lead). Plus the wider team — counterweight (delivery partner), Long Covid Scotland PPI input, statistician Prof Macleod.

A research-design episode: ReDIRECT = **Re**mote **D**iet **I**ntervention to **Re**duce long-Co**v**id symp**T**oms. **Re**purposed from DiRECT (the type-2-diabetes remission trial that showed total-diet-replacement → 10–15 kg weight loss → diabetes remission at 1 and 2 years).

## Trial design

- **N = 200** people with LC ≥3 months and **BMI > 27** (overweight/obesity).
- **UK-wide remote intervention** — recruitment via social media, Long Covid Scotland networks, GP practices in Glasgow. No site visits.
- **1:1 randomisation** to (a) immediate intervention or (b) 6-month wait-list control then crossed over.
- **Intervention**: 12 weeks total diet replacement (TDR) — soups + shakes ~800 kcal/day → average 10+ kg loss → followed by structured food reintroduction with dietitian support. Counterweight delivers via app + 1:1 dietitian.
- **Outcomes**: standard (weight, quality of life, productivity) **plus a personalised symptom outcome** — each participant nominates their most-troublesome LC symptom (fatigue / dyspnea / pain / anxiety-depression / other) and that's tracked individually. Combet calls this "mosaic outcome" — relatively novel in trial design and well-suited to LC's heterogeneity.
- **Process evaluation** (Blane leading) — qualitative interviews with participants on what helped/hurt and why.
- **Health economic** (Emma McIntosh leading).
- **Timeline**: open recruitment November 2021; primary endpoint at 6 months. Earliest results late 2022.

## Underlying hypothesis

> "There's a lot of commonality in the symptoms of long covid and those associated with overweight/obesity. We're hypothesising the **anti-inflammatory effect of weight loss** might help some LC patients." — Combet, ep #16

Caveats Combet and Blane both stress:
- LC almost certainly has multiple underlying mechanisms; this trial tests one hypothesis for a subgroup.
- LC patients have varied weight trajectories — some lose weight (fatigue, GI), some gain (deconditioning).
- Result could be null; design is set up to detect that fairly.
- Won't answer *why* if positive — would seed follow-on mechanism studies.

## PPI lessons named in the episode

- LC Scotland surveyed members upfront on whether a remote weight-management trial would even be welcome — strong yes.
- PPI lead Jane (Long Covid Scotland co-applicant) tested questionnaires for cognitive load — a 20-page form is a non-starter for brain-fogged patients. Tweaks made.
- Recruitment uptake expected higher via social media / LC networks than via primary care (where LC and BMI coding is patchy).

## Why it matters here

This is the **first methodologically detailed trial-design episode** that includes a personalised symptom outcome. Worth flagging to anyone designing or critiquing LC trials. Also: **weight-management as an LC intervention** is newly-on-the-podcast — first explicit claim to track.
