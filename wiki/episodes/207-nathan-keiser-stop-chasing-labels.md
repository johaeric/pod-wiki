Dr Nathan Keiser — Doctor of Chiropractic Neurology, US-based, 17 years in practice. Background in concussion / TBI rehab; pivoted into LC/CFS/POTS as similar-mechanism conditions emerged. Sees people for 2-week intensive clinical visits. Treats autonomic and neurological dysfunction via **targeted neuroplastic rehab** rather than syndrome-based approaches.

## Mechanism over syndrome

- Long-COVID, POTS, dysautonomia, ME/CFS are mostly *syndromic* labels — collections of symptoms, not unified mechanisms
- His approach: identify the *specific* mechanism failing in each individual, then target the right exercise/intervention dose at that bottleneck
- Symptom → mechanism → fix the mechanism → that bottleneck closes → next bottleneck reveals itself

## Cerebral hypoperfusion as a core mechanism

- Uses **Doppler ultrasound** to measure blood flow in the middle cerebral arteries (carotid → into brain)
- Tilt-test reveals dramatic cerebral perfusion drops on standing in many LC/POTS patients
- **Endothelial dysfunction** = breakdown in the signalling between blood vessel and the area it feeds. Inflammatory swelling narrows the lumen and reduces dynamic regulation
- **Structural compression** matters: he sees patients whose neck rotation reduces an artery's blood flow by 40%. COVID was the catalyst that tipped a previously-tolerated structural issue into symptomatic territory
- *That's a fixable problem* — but only if you look for it

## Targeted neuroplastic rehab

- The brain is not one organ — it's networks. Rehab targets specific bottleneck pathways (e.g. cerebellum-frontal connectivity).
- Just as you wouldn't rehab a sprained ankle by doing Olympic weightlifting, you shouldn't rehab a vestibular-frontal pathway by doing whole-body activity. *Direct the energy at what's broken.*
- Vestibular rehab: not "do not throw up" tolerance training, but tiny head-turn drills with eyes-closed return, watching for pupil expansion as the failure marker. Stop the moment it fails. Recover overnight.
- "Push to the edge of capacity, recover, push again" — same principle as elite-athlete training, just at a much smaller scale.

## Cautions

- **Vagus-nerve stimulators**: "we've had a ton of people wreck themselves" using VNS when the vagal pathway isn't actually the bottleneck. Wrong tool for them; generates maladaptive plasticity
- **Breathwork** done at typical "deep, slow" advice often produces hypocapnia (CO2 too low) → tingling, anxiety, worse symptoms. Many LC patients are already hypocapnic *not* because of how they breathe but because of broader chemoreceptor dysfunction
- *Tools are not magic* — they're tests that give feedback. If a tool isn't working, that's information

## When something starts working *and then stops*

- That's usually *good news* — you've moved past that bottleneck
- Find the *new* bottleneck and target that

## On long-haulers reading too much

- Information firehose (rapamycin, methylene blue, etc.) is overwhelming
- Test things against *who you are* — not against general claims
- Working with an experienced clinician shaves time off the trial-and-error process

## Notes for Eric

Important counter-weight to #203 (Carissa, who advocates VNS). Both can be true: VNS is high-leverage for some, harmful for others. Two takeaways for Eric: (1) **structural compression of cerebral blood flow with neck rotation** is rarely tested but commonly missed — worth raising with Eric's clinicians if any positional component to his POTS feels mechanical; (2) **breathwork-induced hypocapnia** is a thing — if Eric's breathwork ever produces tingling/anxiety/worse, that's a sign to tighten the breath window rather than push slower-deeper. Keiser's mechanism-first framing is closest in spirit to Groysman (#187) and Galland (#191).
