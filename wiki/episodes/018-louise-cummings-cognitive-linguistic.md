# #018 — Louise Cummings — Cognitive & Linguistic Difficulties in Long Covid

**Guest**: Prof **Louise Cummings** — Professor of Clinical Linguistics, Hong Kong Polytechnic University; UK-trained speech-and-language therapist. Background in adults with neurodegenerative disorders (Parkinson's, Alzheimer's). Pivoted in early 2020 to a long-covid cohort after stranding in the UK during the first wave.

A research-design episode focused specifically on **brain fog as a measurable phenomenon**. Loadbearing because it's the first episode that takes "I can't think properly" out of the patient-anecdote box and shows it on standardised tests. Eric does not have the cognitive presentation; this is still useful for understanding *why* others' "brain fog" claims are not vague.

## Why standard cognitive tests miss LC brain fog

Cummings's central methodological point:
- Speech-and-language clinicians' default battery (developed for **aphasia** — stroke / TBI breakdown of grammar/syntax/morphology) does not catch what's wrong in LC.
- LC brain fog manifests at the **highest** language levels — **pragmatics and discourse** — which integrate cognition with language. Memory, attention, word retrieval *under realistic narrative load*.
- She cites participants frustrated that neurologists ran cognitive screens that came back clean — they knew they weren't fine, but the wrong test had been used.

## Study design

- **142 participants**, six groups:
  1. LC + brain fog
  2. Had covid, no cognitive symptoms
  3. Healthy never-covid controls
  4 + 5. Two ESL groups (English second language)
  6. ME/CFS comparison group
- **Discourse production tasks** — describe a picture, tell a story (sequence-of-pictures or full Cinderella narrative back).
- Six-month longitudinal follow-up in progress at recording, with matched (not identical) materials to avoid priming.
- Recruited via Facebook + word-of-mouth in LC support communities.

## Findings (early, pending full statistical analysis)

Three large deficits in the LC group, **not** in healthy controls:

1. **Verbal recall** — immediate and delayed recall of a 100-word story.
2. **Verbal fluency** — letter fluency (words beginning with F, A, etc.) AND category fluency (animals, vegetables).
3. **Discourse informativeness** — when telling a story, the "informativeness score" is markedly reduced. They can speak; they cannot organise the high-level structure (motivations, event order, what to mention, what to skip).

The ME/CFS comparison group **matched healthy controls** on all but letter fluency — surprising, and tentatively suggests that fatigue alone may not be the main driver of LC cognitive deficits. (Caveat: small ME group; needs replication.)

## What patients can do — "cognitive pacing"

Cummings's chief recommendation, first articulated this strongly on the podcast:

> "I think we need to do **cognitive pacing** as well as physical pacing. People think they're resting because they're watching TV — they're not. The brain has to process all that information. Close down the stimulation." — Louise Cummings, ep #18

This becomes a major theme/intervention concept across later episodes — flag forward.

## Notable quotes

> "Even if they felt physically sufficiently recovered to return, it was the cognitive linguistic issues that were preventing return to work."

> "You can spend a lot of time at clinic getting more 'normal' tests. There comes a point where you don't need another test telling you you're fine."
