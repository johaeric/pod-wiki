# #82 — Jay Wiles on HRV as an Autonomic-Dysfunction Guide

Clinical health psychologist specialised in psychophysiology and biometrics, board-certified in biofeedback and HRV biofeedback. Co-founder of **Hanu Health** (continuous HRV wearable + coaching). Did NOT develop long covid himself, but has worked with many LC patients via the clinic.

## What HRV is — the directional-guide framing

- HRV = beat-to-beat variability in heart rate across the respiratory cycle (respiratory sinus arrhythmia — natural, not pathological).
- Inhale → vagal brake off → HR ↑ (more O₂ available, deliver it).
- Exhale → vagal brake on → HR ↓ (conserve cardiac energy).
- The amount of swing = HRV. **Stress → HRV ↓. Relaxation / good adaptation → HRV ↑.**

## The "relative not normative" rule

The single most important framing he keeps repeating, because the entire wearable industry violates it:

- **HRV is not like cholesterol.** There is no normative range. Comparing your HRV to anyone else's is meaningless.
- HRV is only meaningful **relative to your own baseline** and **as a trend over time**.
- A 100ms reading is not "low" — it depends entirely on what your usual range is and which direction it's moving.
- 90% of people emailing him want to "raise their HRV." Wrong question. **Right question**: is my HRV trending in a direction that suggests adaptation or over-taxation?

## What HRV does for LC

- LC patients commonly stuck in sympathetic overdrive → HRV chronically suppressed.
- His own covid (original strain, late 2020): HRV crashed from baseline ~110ms to **14ms**; took a full **3 months** to return to normal even though acute symptoms cleared in days.
- In LC clinic patients: **15–30 minute** HRV recovery time after a walk (versus 2-5 minutes in healthy adults). Indicates the nervous system can't keep up with energy mobilisation demands.
- Useful as a downstream-of-sympathetic-overdrive signal — but **not as a self-fulfilling prophecy**. Many patients use a low reading as an excuse not to do anything; Wiles cautions against that.

## How to actually use it

- Pair the **objective** data with **subjective** experience — neither alone is enough.
- A persistent downward trend over days/weeks → red flag for over-taxation; lean into rest and breathwork.
- Don't check it more often than your nervous system can usefully act on. Some people benefit from continuous; others should look weekly.
- The endpoint is **becoming your own biofeedback machine** — wearables expedite the development of interoception, then become unnecessary.

## What he recommends to actually move HRV (in LC-applicable order)

1. **Breathwork / HRV biofeedback** — paced breathing produces a real-time, visible HRV elevation. He is emphatic that the *breathing* (not the data) is the active ingredient: the data is for motivation, accountability, and dialing in the practice.
2. **Sleep** — foundational; quality > quantity; deep + REM cycles do the autonomic repair.
3. **Yoga Nidra / non-sleep deep rest meditation** — names this explicitly as effective.
4. **Nutrition** — anti-inflammatory; remove processed foods.
5. **General movement** — walking, mobility, stretching, myofascial release.
6. **Reduce alcohol** — significantly suppresses ANS function and HRV; old "glass of wine has anti-inflammatory benefit" claim has been "nullified."
7. **HIIT / cardio** — for healthy people; mostly out-of-reach for LC patients early on.

## On menstrual cycle / PMS HRV drop

Direct mechanism walk-through (extends the wiki's [[hormonal-dysfunction]] theme):

- During ovulation the body must mobilise significant energy for either disposal or fertilisation.
- → ↑ respiratory rate, ↑ heart rate, ↓ HRV.
- → cortisol release; immunological suppression (higher illness susceptibility during menses).
- After the process completes, all three metrics stabilise back.
- Mitigation: emotional-regulation tools (biofeedback, breathwork) to prevent the hormonal swings driving downstream emotional dysregulation that further suppresses HRV.

## "Emotional health is the redheaded stepchild"

His four-pillar model: exercise + sleep + nutrition + **stress mitigation / emotional health**. Argues the fourth pillar is treated as soft and discretionary; for LC patients he believes it deserves equal weight with the other three — and that **HRV biofeedback is the practical handle**.

## The "data is magical only as motivation" point

> "Spoiler alert — your HRV is going to increase whether you're looking at the data or not. The breathwork is the active ingredient. The data is for keeping you motivated and dialling in the practice."

(This is an important counter-claim against wearable hype.)

## Quotes

> "From 110 milliseconds to 14 milliseconds. Body temperature up 5°. It hit me pretty hard. I was lucky — symptoms gone in days, but HRV took 3 months."

> "We have to rule in both subjective experience and objective data. I don't like one or the other — it's both."

> "If you disregard one of the four pillars, the rest of the house can fall."

## Eric-relevant intake

- Eric likely already has HRV data via wearable — if so, the **"don't compare to other people; only your own trend matters"** rule is the most useful single takeaway and prevents wasted anxiety on absolute numbers.
- The **15-30min HRV recovery time after a walk** is a concrete pacing signal Eric could use — if recovery is slower than that, treat as evidence the walk was over-threshold.
- Wiles confirms what other wiki anchors imply: HRV biofeedback is the **same active ingredient as diaphragmatic breathwork** ([[040-boon-lim-autonomic]], [[029-lorna-nicholson-pots-breathing]]) — just with a real-time motivational layer. The data is a coaching tool, not a treatment in itself.
- The **PMS HRV-suppression mechanism** is useful context for relatives / partners with LC, not directly Eric-relevant.
- **Alcohol → HRV suppression** is a relevant nudge if Eric is using occasional wine; the cost is paid in nervous-system recovery slack.
