# Episode 137 — Lorrie Rivers — EFT (Meridian) Tapping

Lorrie's second podcast appearance (first was #70). She is a 20+-year ME/CFS recovery coach (severe ME/CFS, then LC, then lifelong fibromyalgia — all recovered). This episode focuses entirely on **EFT tapping** as a self-administered nervous-system regulation tool.

## What EFT Meridian Tapping is

- An energetic / acupressure technique developed by Roger Callahan (1960s phobia research) and simplified into the modern EFT format by Gary Craig.
- Self-applied tapping on **specific acupressure / meridian points** (side of hand / "karate chop point", inner eyebrow, outer eye, under eye, under nose, under mouth/chin, collar bone, under arm, top of head) while *speaking* the negative emotion or symptom out loud.
- Closely paired with verbal acceptance phrases ("even though I have X, I deeply and completely love and accept myself…") — the technique pairs *acknowledgement* with *physical regulation*.

## Mechanism (Lorrie's framing)

- Stimulating the meridian points while focusing on the negative content tells the **amygdala** that the trigger is safe — repeated exposure de-fuses the amygdala response over sessions.
- MRI evidence: amygdala lights up before tapping; quiets after 1–2 sessions.
- **Crucial distinction from brain retraining:** brain retraining tries to convince the mind that everything is fine when it isn't, which keeps the nervous system in fight-flight. EFT *acknowledges what is felt first* and lets the nervous system experience that acknowledgement as safe — the shift comes naturally without forcing it.

## Why it matters in LC

- Self-administered. No practitioner required after learning.
- Scalable to **physical symptoms, anxiety, food cravings, PTSD, trauma, success goals** — same protocol, different content.
- Can be done lying down. Can be done by *imagining the tapping* if physical tapping causes PEM (Lorrie reports paraplegic clients getting full benefit by imagined tapping).
- Cumulative effect — over time, ocean-and-waves metaphor: the stressors still arrive, but the underlying expansiveness becomes steady, and emotional shifts move through more quickly.

## Live demonstration

Most of the second half is a live tapping session with Jackie on her recent (uncomfortable) post-recovery concert experience. Jackie's ball-in-stomach went from 8/9 → 5/6 in one round. Standard practice would continue rounds until the rating reaches 0–1.

## Cautions

- People with severe PTSD should work with a trained EFT practitioner — tapping can unlock material that becomes overwhelming if surfaced alone.
- Possible somatic responses: yawning, burping (one client burped incessantly), drowsiness afterwards, occasionally feeling more emotionally stirred up before settling. The stir-up is a sign that suppressed material is releasing — not a sign to stop.
- Don't drive while tapping (until experienced, then it can be done one-handed).
