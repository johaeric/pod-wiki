# #150 — From Self-Blame to Self-Discovery (host-solo follow-up)

Short host-solo (~16 min) responding to a listener fan-mail question after [[147-dan-neuffer-resilience-pt1]]:

> "I heard you mention that a big piece of recovery for you was identifying what you were doing before becoming unwell that contributed, while also not blaming yourself. I can really relate as I was also very stressed, perfectionistic, overworking and over-exercising prior to having COVID. My question is: can you speak to how you did this? How did you work through the self-blame and come to an embodied understanding that it wasn't your fault?"

Anchor host-solo for the **identifying-pre-illness-patterns-without-blame** thread.

## Jackie's framing

- "I'm not saying I deserved long COVID. I'm not blaming myself for getting sick. **However** — understanding that some of the patterns I had before getting unwell may have contributed to becoming unwell, not making good recovery, and prolonging that recovery."
- The Jenny Adams (#145) **boiling-frog** analogy — slow stress accumulation goes unnoticed until you're cooked.
- COVID was the **match to the gasoline** — body shut down because everything else had filled the bucket.
- "If I'd known being like this would put my health at risk, I'd have made changes. **I didn't know.** I did the best I could as a 29-year-old."
- Maya Angelou: "Do the best you can until you know better. Then when you know better, do better."

## How she actually worked through it

1. **Identify the patterns** — perfectionism, people-pleasing, drive to be doing-things-right-now, push-through-everything.
2. **Reduce stress where possible** — job, home life, activities. "Some things we have control over, some we don't. Everything we improve in our lives will help."
3. **Recognise the protective origin** — "many of these traits go back to childhood. Often it's our body trying to *protect* us — emphasis on trying."
4. **Therapy** — "I found working with a therapist was very helpful — she was able to guide me, question me, dig into things I maybe didn't really want to dig into but really needed to."
5. **Self-awareness as the first step** — notice when you're slipping back into the patterns; gently challenge them.
6. **What would I say to a friend?** — apply the same kindness/compassion to yourself.
7. **Distinguish blame from responsibility** — "make blame with compassion. There's no use in blame."

## The two-arrows analogy (Buddhist)

- **First arrow**: the insult to the body. Already happened. Can't be undone.
- **Second arrow**: your reaction to it. *That* you have power over.
- For Jackie: take some responsibility, accept it, use it as information to do better from now on.

## What "doing better" looks like for Jackie now

- Respect for her body that came from finding out what happened when she didn't.
- Appreciation for health.
- Gratitude for the smallest things.
- Embracing what's important in life (health, joy, connection, nature).
- Letting go of things that aren't.
- "New me is better than old me. I'm not perfect because nothing is. But I am more authentically me than I ever have been."

## Two quotes she leans on

> "The secret to getting unstuck is radically accepting where you're at now with grace. Let go of expecting perfectionism from yourself. You won't always be your most productive, make the right decisions, or get it right. A little self-compassion will take you a long way." (Source unattributed; Jackie says she found it recently.)

> "Maybe the journey isn't so much about becoming anything. Maybe it's about *un*becoming everything that isn't you, so you can be who you were meant to be in the first place." (Paulo Coelho, Brazilian writer; Jackie credits Suzy Bolt's recent post.)
