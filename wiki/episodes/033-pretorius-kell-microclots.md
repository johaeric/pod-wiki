# #033 — Resia Pretorius & Douglas Kell — Microclots

**Guests**:
- **Prof Resia Pretorius** — Stellenbosch University, South Africa. Decade-long programme on clotting pathologies, platelets, and microclot formation across diabetes, Parkinson's, Alzheimer's, RA — into acute covid and long covid.
- **Prof Douglas Kell** — University of Liverpool. Systems biologist / biochemist. Pretorius's 10-year collaborator; came to her after she missed citing his iron-metabolism review.

The mechanistic flagship episode for the **microclot hypothesis**. Establishes the core finding: in long covid blood, fluorescent staining (thioflavin T, Amytracker) reveals **amyloid-form fibrin microclots resistant to fibrinolysis**, alongside **hyperactivated platelets** and **endothelitis** — feeding back as a positive-feedback loop.

## The microclot finding

- **2020** — Dr Jaco Laubscher (SA, diabetes collaborator) flags clotting in acute covid. Pretorius lab confirms via fluorescence microscopy.
- **Early 2021** — long covid samples arrive. Persistent microclots; their proteomic content can only be released after **double trypsin digestion** — diagnostic of the amyloid form.
- Trapped inside: inflammatory mediators (von Willebrand factor, plasminogen, antiplasmin, fibrin γ chain). Standard plasma assays read normal because **the analytes are sequestered in the clots**.
- Spike protein and bacterial LPS both trigger amyloid-form fibrin in vitro — a tiny fraction of "seed" molecules can flip the whole pool.

## Why a "vascular disease, not respiratory" framing

- Capillaries (~24 µm minimum diameter) get blocked or restricted by these microclots even though the clots are porous and squeezable.
- Result: **systemic cellular hypoxia** in muscle, brain, gut — explaining the symptom heterogeneity without needing a different mechanism per system.
- Endothelial inflammation (endothelitis) → platelet hyperactivation → microclot formation → endothelial inflammation. Break any link, the cycle quiets.

## Treatment principles (from clinical collaborators, **not** the researchers themselves prescribing)

- **Triple therapy** (Laubscher, Pretorius, Kell paper): an antiplatelet + a second antiplatelet + an anticoagulant. Common combination: aspirin + clopidogrel + a DOAC (dabigatran or apixaban). **Stops new microclots forming; the body slowly degrades existing ones.**
- **HELP-apheresis** (Beate Jaeger, Mülheim Germany; centres in US, Cyprus): mechanically removes circulating microclots. Best paired with triple therapy.
- **Statins** — anti-inflammatory effect on endothelium (cholesterol angle is largely irrelevant per Kell).
- **Antihistamines** — particularly with the MCAS overlap (Dani et al. UK).
- **Nutraceuticals** as background measures (Kell): polyphenols, iron chelators (green tea), clot-lysers like **nattokinase**, lumbrokinase, serrapeptase. None likely to harm; some plausible mechanistic case.

> "We need diagnostics and treatment in parallel — anti-coagulation has bleeding risk and you need to identify the right patients." — Pretorius

## Why isn't this a UK trial yet?

- Microclot results need to be **replicated in the UK** before NHS uptake; Sheffield has reproduced Pretorius's microscopy work.
- Pathology labs lack the right microscopes; Pretorius/Kell are building a press-button instrument to validate the lab-grade method.
- Clinicians are siloed (haematology vs cardiology vs respiratory vs GP). There is no obvious specialty owner for "long covid".
- Triple-therapy components are already approved drugs for AF/stroke — repurposing trial would be cheap and fast.
