Solo essay on **rest** — what it actually is, why "lying still" isn't enough, and how to make rest restorative when the nervous system is stuck in survival.

## Why rest is hard with LC/ME-CFS

- Body in protection / survival mode — letting go feels unsafe
- Hypervigilance + threat-scanning makes still-but-unrested ("tired-but-wired") common
- Guilt, shame, boredom, frustration, "wasting time" feelings can hijack rest
- Lying down doesn't equal restorative rest

## Banking energy for healing

A reframe Jackie picked up from a recovery-story guest: think of rest like saving money. Most of us don't spend 100% of what we earn — we save some. Rest banks energy that the body uses for repair. Even small daily savings compound.

## Seven types of rest

1. **Physical** — stillness, lying down, micro-rests
2. **Mental / cognitive** — quieting the inner chatter; yoga nidra is good for this
3. **Sensory** — light, sound, screens, movement, temperature; reducing input
4. **Emotional** — permission to feel without performing; let the holding-it-together drop
5. **Social** — stepping back from interactions that require pretence; choosing who you spend time with
6. **Creative** — fill the well; doesn't have to be good
7. **Nervous system** — safety cues, co-regulation, breath, presence

Often we try to fix exhaustion with physical rest alone when it's another type that's missing.

## Practical downshifting cues

- Soft, longer exhale (don't force)
- Visual anchor — let eyes land on something neutral; name it
- Contact points — feel the weight of body supported
- Sound cues — soft, predictable (rain, white noise, familiar music)
- Micro-rests of 30–60 seconds wrinkled through the day
- **Permission slips** (literal post-it: "permission to rest")
- Co-regulation: pet, partner, warm blanket, soothing voice

## Signs rest is working

- Saliva returns to mouth, jaw softens, slower blink, sense of being more present, micro-sigh
- Doesn't always feel blissful — sometimes just feels like *less effort*

## Notes for Eric

The seven-types-of-rest framework is a useful diagnostic when "I'm resting and it's not helping" comes up — it's almost always one of the non-physical types that's missing. The **literal permission slip** is a small, slightly silly tool that works. Pair with #197/#198 (Dan Neuffer on consistency) — small consistent rest beats one heroic nap.
