# #156 — Nadyne McKie — Empowering Caregivers and Children Through Compassion and Play

Return appearance after [[155-nadyne-mckie-kindness]]. Focus: **paediatric LC + the caregiving spouse / parent.** Nadyne brings personal + clinical experience: her son had post-COVID LC two years pre-recording and is now well; she works with multiple paediatric LC patients and their parents in private practice; her book has a dedicated chapter for caregivers and children.

## Why this episode matters

- **Anchor paediatric-LC + caregiver episode on the wiki.** Pairs with [[061-rosie-colin-pidgeon]] (paediatric LC, father carer) and [[011-iain-partner-with-chronic-illness]] (partner-as-carer), but is the first episode to build a generalised model of *caregiver depletion as a recovery-rate-limiter*.
- **Energy-expenditure typology for kids** is wiki-new: learning + socialising + screen-time are the dominant energy drains for paediatric LC patients, not movement. *Maintaining* social contact while truly resting requires careful gating.
- **School-as-flu-pond**: cultural-norm critique of pushing kids into school with germs; longer-term implication for chronic illness patterns.
- **"Just for now"** language for kids matches the language used for adults (#155) — same operative move.
- **Believing-the-child** framing: Nadyne argues the consequence of not believing children's symptoms is they *learn to ignore their own body signals* — sets up the perfectionism / push-through pattern that recurs across recovery stories on this wiki.

## Practical patterns named

- **"You can't pour from an empty cup"** — caregiver self-care is not optional; depleted carers transmit dysregulation to the child via NS-to-NS coupling.
- **Phased school return** + **PE letter** + **psychoeducation for the school** as standard advocacy moves.
- **Micro-breaks for kids** during cognitive load — short breath/eye-rest practices framed as play, not therapy.
- **Believing-the-child as a long-term skill teaching exercise**: even if the kid can't articulate the symptom, the act of being heard prevents the body-signal-suppression pattern that ME/CFS / LC adult patients describe in their childhood histories.
- **Asking for help / accepting help**: support is a recovery enabler. Therapy-rate access varies — Nadyne notes her own acceptance of the "even self-care can be exhausting" reality and advocates for support as *non-negotiable* in caregiving for chronic illness.

## Her son's recovery — what helped

- Long period off school; phased return.
- Energy-expenditure awareness — sport day skipped (deliberately) when not appropriate, even when others questioned it.
- "Just for now" framing that allowed both parent and child to set boundaries without catastrophising the future.
- Awareness now (post-recovery) of "Whispers" of fatigue under stress — same pattern Nadyne herself manages.

## Quotes

> "If we don't listen and get curious when our children tell us things that they're feeling, we might in effect be encouraging the ignoring of body signals. And we know the consequence of that — those are the people who get to a stage where they cannot keep going anymore."
> — Nadyne McKie, ep #156

> "Wouldn't it be lovely if all schools taught children how to empower themselves to help their bodies be calm in stressful situations? I wish I'd learned that. I haven't used Pythagoras's theorem since."
> — Nadyne McKie, ep #156
