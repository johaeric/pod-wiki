# #143 — David Joffe: sleep challenges in LC

Australian respiratory and sleep specialist. PhD on circadian rhythm and cognitive impairment in OSA; long-standing interests in reflux, ventilation, dopaminergic pathways, PTSD. Started reading about LC after seeing his first post-COVID patient April 2020. Co-author with Razer, Doug Hill, David Putrino on a forthcoming LC-sleep paper.

Anchor sleep episode for the wiki — first comprehensive clinical sleep frame for LC.

## Why sleep is load-bearing

- **Slow-wave sleep (SWS)** = restorative — drops BP/HR, flushes the system, parasympathetic.
- **REM sleep** = "rebooting the PC" — memory consolidation, executive function, new learning.
- **Sleep deprivation under stress** drops lymphocytes → immune dysregulation, weight gain, metabolic derangement *independent* of OSA.
- All metabolism is **circadian-clock-tied** (alcohol dehydrogenase, HMG-CoA reductase, cortisol) — desynchrony has system-wide consequences.

## What's specifically broken in LC sleep

- **Dopaminergic injury** — Joffe argues the LC field has under-weighted dopamine vs serotonin. He sees clinically:
  - More **restless legs** (40%+ increase in women with LC).
  - More **PTSD, major depression, narcolepsy**.
  - More **REM sleep without atonia / REM behaviour disorder** — patients (often young) acting out dreams. Historically this is a 60–65 yr old men's condition with **80% Parkinson's progression at 14 years**. Clinical worry — "what does this mean for these young people?"
- **Serotonin–melatonin axis** — low serotonin (now demonstrated in LC) means low melatonin → desynchronised circadian clock.
- **Vagal injury → autonomic disregulation** in sleep. SWS *should* be parasympathetic-dominant; if vagus is clobbered (gut motility, esophageal emptying, MCAS-like reactions), the body can't drop into the parasympathetic state SWS needs.
- **Carotid body / CO₂ chemoreceptor disturbance** — happy hypoxics, plus disrupted sleep-onset breathing cycles for some patients.

## What Joffe uses clinically

### 1. Slow-release melatonin (low dose, evening)

- **2 mg slow-release at 7 pm** — peaks at 9 pm, models normal melatonin onset, **resets circadian clock**.
- Joffe gives this to virtually all his LC patients.
- Higher doses are not better. TGA-approved (Australia), pharmaceutical-grade, prescription only.
- Adjacent benefits: reduces violence in REM behaviour disorder; **endothelial protection** at low dose (statins-like) — relevant given LC endotheliitis.

### 2. Sleep hygiene basics

- Room **23–24°C**, dark, quiet.
- Drop core temperature to initiate sleep — warm milk works because cooling-after, not the tryptophan.
- **Two duvets** for partnered patients (different thermal couples; reduces partner-arousal disruption).
- No phones/screens in bed; eye shades; ear plugs.
- **Heat warning**: rooms staying above 32–34°C at night → 25% less SWS — climate change is now a sleep-medicine problem.
- Day-time **second sleep window ~1 pm** — 20–30 min nap (don't go past 30, or you'll sink into SWS and wake with sleep inertia). Studies on pilots and ATC validate the gain.

### 3. Light hygiene

- **Sunlight, eyes-open, no sunglasses** in the morning to entrain the SCN.
- For housebound LC patients: light boxes ("blue glasses with the lights on top" — high-flux at close range).
- LC vitamin D dosing matters because: housebound + virus upregulates osteoclasts.

### 4. Gut as sleep co-factor

- "Biggest reservoir of [neurotransmitters] is the gut." Correcting dysbiosis matters for sleep too.
- Joffe puts patients on a specific probiotic — "best-studied probiotic, Mexican four-way blinded quadruple crossover study, substantial increase in viral shedding in faeces" (he doesn't name it on-air; ask in clinic context).
- For LC constipation / gastroparesis: **prucalopride** (5-HT4 agonist, designated for chronic constipation) — also a histamine blocker in his framing.

### 5. Sleep studies are diagnostic gold

- Home sleep studies are basically "apnea/no apnea" — robot-scored, miss the LC-relevant findings.
- Lab polygraphic study (full electrodes + video) is what he wants for any LC patient with brain fog or sleep disturbance.
- Reduction of SWS or REM is **a fabulous biomarker of misery** — irrefutable, can't fake EEG.

## Specific framings worth keeping

- **"Pent-like, but the lithium battery"** — PEM analogy: mitochondria not running on thermonuclear power, running on lithium; range-limited. Sleep is what charges the battery; if you don't charge fully, no distance.
- **Cognitive overdoing → physical crash** and vice versa — Davenport's framing in the same batch (#141), independently arrived at.
- **Hypopnea/disrupted CO₂ regulation in LC** — "not everything that is breathlessness is lung disease." Cords, neuromuscular, vagal, cardiac, microclots all in the differential.

## Eric-relevant takeaways

- **Slow-release melatonin (2 mg, 7 pm)** is now logged as a clinician-recommended LC sleep intervention worth raising with his clinician. Cheap, well-studied, low risk. (Eric does not have major LC-attributable insomnia; below-threshold, but worth knowing.)
- **REM-behaviour-disorder warning signal** — if Eric ever hears about himself acting out dreams, get a sleep study; Joffe's data say this is showing up in young LC patients with potentially serious downstream concern.
- **Daytime nap ≤ 20 min around 1 pm** — Joffe says use the second sleep window; don't ignore it. Aligns with Eric's existing pacing.
- **ORS / sodium-glucose** discussion from Ussher #139 + Joffe's "vagal trash → autonomic crash overnight" frame fit together: hydration support overnight is part of the autonomic-stability story.
- **Probiotic** mentioned but not named on-air. If Eric wants, look for the recent Mexican-trial-validated probiotic with shedding endpoint as the hook.
