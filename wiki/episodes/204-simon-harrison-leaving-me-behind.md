Simon Harrison — UK; pre-illness an extremely physically active 34-year-old (mountain biking, road cycling, snowboarding, surfing). 2013 onset triggered by what he describes as a **seizure-like event** (not infection-triggered). Wrote a memoir, *Leaving ME Behind*. Recovered ~2.5 years before this 2026 interview, so over a decade-long arc total.

## Acute phase

- "Brain on fire" seizure-like episode opened the illness
- Sensory cranked up: light, sound, vibration, chemical sensitivity
- Severe fatigue, brain fog, dropping into bed, ribs and limbs heavy

## Self-treatment phase (denial / "burnout")

Did *not* seek a diagnosis for ~2.5 years. Self-described as "fiercely independent." Tried, in parallel:
- Yoga, tai chi
- Reiki, acupuncture
- Breathwork
- Mindfulness body scans (the most enduring tool)
- Diet and lifestyle changes
- Restoration-project mental model: treated his body like a classic car

## After diagnosis

- GP at year 2.5 confirmed ME/CFS; understood expert in fatigue-related illness
- Recommended **graded exercise therapy** (note: now contentious; pre-NICE 2021 update) and **recovery in a brighter location**
- Worst phase came right after diagnosis — ~1 year mostly in bed, ~2 years sofa/bed mixture
- Eventually agreed to GP's "go somewhere sunny" advice → year in Portugal via house-sitting

## What helped most

- **Mindfulness body scans** — entry point to reconnecting with body, became foundational
- **Breathwork** — tested the "edge of his window of tolerance"
- **Activity reframe**: question exhaustion — am I afraid, or actually depleted? Borrowed from elite-athlete mindset
- **Year in Portugal** — sunlight, smaller world, geographical reset; freed him from the home-routine pressure
- **Many small tools applied consistently** — no single inflection
- **Recalibrating tiredness** — relearning what normal tiredness feels like vs PEM tiredness

## Final phase

- Built up jogging from 3-4 seconds at a time
- Recovery is a series of plateaus and breakthroughs
- "Recovery is non-linear, but it goes somewhere"

## Notes for Eric

Worth holding for the **GET caveat** — Simon found GET *useful* (rare on this wiki), but his case (a) wasn't infection-triggered, (b) wasn't classic POTS/PEM-dominant — sensory hypersensitivity was the dominant symptom. Don't generalise his GET experience to typical post-COVID PEM cases. The most useful single tool he names is **mindfulness body scans** as the foundation that everything else built on. The "go somewhere sunny" intervention is impossibly individual — but the principle (geographic/routine reset to break out of the home-rumination loop) is reasonable.

## Key quote

> "Setbacks aren't failures. They're rungs on the same ladder. As long as you can recover by tomorrow, you're moving up — even if it doesn't feel that way today."
> — Simon Harrison, ep #204
