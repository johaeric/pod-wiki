# #030 — Dr Gavin Francis — GP & Author

**Guest**: **Dr Gavin Francis** — GP in Edinburgh + locum in Orkney. Author of multiple medical/travel books — most recently ***Recovery* (2022)** and ***Intensive Care*** (2021, pandemic GP diary). His *Recovery* is positioned as deliberately concise, LC-friendly reading.

A philosophical-clinical episode. Builds the [[convalescence]] theme. No interventions to evidence-log; the whole episode is **framing**.

## Convalescence — the lost concept

Latin *con-valescere*: "to grow strong with". Pre-1950s medicine had a whole infrastructure: convalescent hospitals, sanatoria, the Florence Nightingale ward design (well-scrubbed, well-starched, view of something green and growing). Antibiotics + steroids + the prescription-as-magic-bullet culture pushed it out:

> "We got the idea that all you need to recover is the right prescription. It's just not true." — Francis

> "Doctors are far more like gardeners than mechanics. Nature does most of the work; you can't reach in and fix something the way you fix a machine." — Francis

## Why we're rubbish at it

- Cultural / economic pressure to "push through"; sickness coded as wasted time
- Belief that the body is mechanical and prescription-fixable
- Loss of the convalescent infrastructure that *enforced* rest

Pushing through can work short-term; it can also produce a worse breakdown later. He explicitly references his LC patients as exemplars.

## Naming the illness

Two camps among his patients:
- Those who **don't** want a diagnosis unless treatment follows from it
- Those who **do** want a name — because once an illness has a name, it has an existence separate from themselves; less frightening; can be researched; can be a connection point with others.

Both are legitimate. Good clinicians read which kind of patient is in front of them in the first few minutes.

## Acceptance — a useful distinction

Jackie pushes back on "acceptance" as commonly misused. Francis offers:
> "Acceptance of *this moment* — not acceptance of being this way *forever*. The body is dynamic; molecules are constantly being replaced. A static view of the body is an illusion, and there's always a source of hope in that." — Francis

## Recovery as snakes-and-ladders (his book metaphor)

Climbing a board with random rolls of the dice toward Health — but not actually random, because every up-and-down adds **wisdom + experience + strategies + trigger awareness**. Health isn't a game.

## General advice he'd offer

- Learn the new language of your body and its limits — slow process for those used to a body that just does what's asked.
- Charge the **environment** with as much space, light, cleanliness, view-of-something-growing as you can (the Nightingale axis).
- **Don't compare your trajectory to others'.** Different pressures, different starting points, no useful information in the comparison.
- **Sick notes / benefits / time off** when possible — but be aware extended time off can isolate and harm mental health.
- Find **clinicians you trust**. If you really can't, ask about switching.
- **Beliefs and expectations are powerful inputs** to the recovery process — choose information sources carefully.
- We understand the world through stories. Some don't have fairy-tale endings. We do have the ability to write a small part of our own story — explore that.

## Quotes

> "If a doctor expects to cure every patient, they'll be disappointed. If a patient expects the same of the doctor, they'll be disappointed too." — Francis

> "Recovery is a balance. Even with progressive conditions, you can move toward more autonomy, more dignity, more in accord with your wishes." — Francis
