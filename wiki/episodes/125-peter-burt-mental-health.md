# Episode 125 — Peter Burt — Mental Health, Vulnerability, Asking For Help

Peter Burt is a contracting-company director, husband, father of two teenage sons. Got Covid early in the pandemic, hit hard physically; spent 2+ years cycling boom-and-bust trying to keep working. Reached out to Jackie after listening to one episode in a hospital car park because it sounded like his own internal voice.

**The pattern he describes — and Jackie strongly recognises it from her own:**
- High-multitasking work + endurance hobbies (long road-bike rides) used as stress-coping mechanism. "Lazy people don't get long Covid" theme: looking back, was heading somewhere bad even before Covid.
- Identity tied to being the action dad / main breadwinner / hardest worker in the room. Came from a "men don't cry" upbringing. Several broken family units in childhood; learned not to be seen.
- Pushed through illness for ~2 years, hit "biggest crash" in summer 2023 after a brief spring upswing. That crash forced reckoning.

**What helped:**
1. *Breathwork* — found Patrick McKeown / James Nestor via Jackie's podcast. Started with a BOLT score of 2. First time in 2 years he could actively change his own state ("got me down to one beer a day"). Big turning point.
2. *Therapist (Amy)* — finally agreed to talk to someone after years of his wife and best-friend boss suggesting it. Non-judgemental space let him say things he couldn't say to his wife (he didn't want to pour his bucket into hers). Two important realisations:
   - "You're denying people the opportunity to do for you what you love doing for them." (When she pointed this out he broke down.)
   - He was *grieving* the man he used to be — and eventually realised he didn't want that man back.

**Beer-count analogy** for symptom load (developed with his wife): baseline = 1–2 beers; phone calls / walks / conversation each add a beer; over-do it and you crash with a hangover that can last weeks. Lay-people understand "drunk" in a way they cannot understand "fatigue."

**Core message:** men can talk, men do cry, the "pull your socks up" generation drilled the wrong lesson. Even without therapy, find a non-judgemental ear, ask one question, then just listen. He hopes the call goes out.

> "I'd come out of there blurting some horrendous stuff out... why does that hit me so hard? Why does that one silly little question resonate so deep?"
> — Peter Burt, ep #125
