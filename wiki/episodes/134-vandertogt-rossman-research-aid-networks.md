# Episode 134 — Vicky van der Togt & Jeremy Rossman — Research-Aid Networks

A double interview with the founder ([[vicky-van-der-togt]], LC patient since March 2020) and president ([[jeremy-rossman]], virologist) of [[research-aid-networks]] — a non-profit acting as a *bridge between researchers and community advocacy groups*, and co-authors of a 2024 hypothesis paper proposing that **acid-base disregulation (acidosis)** is a central inter-mediator across LC symptoms.

## Research-Aid Networks (RAN)

- Founded by Vicky early in the pandemic (Netherlands base) after realising long-term covid symptoms in her own 2020 illness weren't being acknowledged by Dutch government messaging.
- Mission: facilitate **collaboration** between research scientists and patient-advocacy / community groups internationally — translating science across language and accessibility barriers, supporting advocates personally, and reducing duplicated work across countries.
- Held weekly meetings during the worst pandemic phase to disseminate the latest research into community languages; relaunched in 2024.
- Built explicitly around the principle that *information must reach communities through trusted local voices*; RAN supports the local groups, not as the originator.

## The acidosis hypothesis paper (2024)

Lead authors: Rossman + van der Togt.

**Core claim**: covid-driven changes in tissue oxygenation (microvascular dysfunction, lung damage, microclotting, endothelial inflammation) push muscles into **anaerobic respiration**, which produces excess lactate / acid. Sustained whole-body acid-base disregulation explains a broad swath of LC symptoms in a way that *also* aligns with ME/CFS data on elevated lactate and exertional intolerance.

This is *not* claimed as the only mechanism — clotting, inflammation, viral persistence are also active — but as a **central linker** between the upstream pathology and the symptom profile.

**Why it matters**: acid-base disturbance is **measurable** and **treatable** with both well-established medical and exercise-science tools. If the hypothesis is supported, intervention points open up immediately.

## Testing plan

- **Step 1**: measure baseline lactate / acid-base in LC vs control populations (resting + with mild activity / exertion). Some published evidence already suggests it (mecfs lactate work; LC anaerobic-threshold work).
- **Step 2**: continuous biosensor monitoring (pH / lactate). Glucose-monitor-style adhesive patches for lactate are in development by various groups; collaboration with one such company is in progress.
- **Step 3**: if disregulation confirmed, intervention trials.

## How they collaborate

- Vicky as *facilitator-translator* — sends research papers to Jeremy with "explain like I'm five"; he re-explains; if she can understand, the lay summary is ready for public release.
- Strong commitment to **lay summaries + audio versions** of papers as core practice.
- Their own paper had a layperson-readable summary published alongside.

## Eric-relevance

- Wiki's **first explicit acid-base / acidosis frame** for LC — links the breathing/CO₂ story (#12, #17, #29, #132) and the bioenergetic / mitochondrial story (#21, #44, #45, #128) under one inter-mediator. Watch for the Step 1 lactate measurement paper.
- Lactate-monitor patches (when commercial) would be a useful pacing tool: visible feedback for **anaerobic-threshold limits** during activity. Echoes the pulse-oximeter / [[bolt-score]] / HRV instruments already on the wiki.
