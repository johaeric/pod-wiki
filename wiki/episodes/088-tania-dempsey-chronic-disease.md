# #88 — Tania Dempsey on Vulnerability + Trigger + Comorbidity Clusters

US internist who became a complex-chronic-disease specialist after years of frustration with the "pill for a symptom" model. Now centres her practice on **MCAS as a unifying diagnosis** for a large fraction of multi-system chronic illness. Published on post-vaccination MCAS-pattern conditions (HPV vaccine paper). One of the wiki's most operationally useful clinician-side episodes for thinking about LC alongside ME/CFS, Lyme, mould-illness, fibromyalgia, etc.

## The vulnerability + trigger model (her core thesis)

> "There's a vulnerability — genetic, epigenetic, environmental — and there's a trigger that brings it to the surface."

The **iceberg metaphor** (from Dr Claudia Miller's chemical-intolerance work that she uses in presentations):

- Below the waterline: accumulated genetic susceptibility + environmental exposures + traumas + subclinical mast-cell or immune dysfunction. The patient is mostly asymptomatic.
- Above the waterline: a triggering event — virus (covid, EBV, HSV), vaccine, infection (Lyme, Bartonella), surgical implant (mesh, breast), antibiotic, mould exposure, chemical exposure — that *brings the iceberg to the surface*.
- The result: a multi-system constellation that looks like several diseases but reflects one underlying upstream dysregulation.

This frame **explains the trifecta clustering** the wiki has tracked across [[038-dr-tina-peers-mcas]], [[068-dr-david-saperstein]], [[080-anjali-agarwal-eds-physio]]: the trifecta wasn't dormant in those patients, it was sub-clinical → trigger → surface.

## Mass cells everywhere = symptoms everywhere

Mast cells live at every body-environment interface:

| Location | Long-covid manifestation |
|---|---|
| Brain / neuro | Brain fog, depression, anxiety, OCD, neuropathies, autonomic dysfunction → POTS |
| Skin | Rash, hives, weird growths, lipomas |
| Respiratory | Chronic sinus, bronchospasm, asthma-like |
| GI | Slow motility, IBS, SIBO, colitis, food intolerance, bloating |

The pattern of "why does one person get GI and another get neuro?" — Dempsey's answer: mast cells in different patients have **mutational** abnormalities concentrated in different tissue locations, possibly seeded by where viral particles / spike protein deposit during the acute phase, and possibly reflecting pre-existing sub-clinical patterns in those tissues.

(This is an open hypothesis she explicitly says could change in a year — flagged as such in the wiki.)

## The comorbid clusters (her standard slide)

She has a slide with arrows between these — all converging on **infection** in the centre:

- MCAS
- Autonomic dysfunction / dysautonomia / POTS
- Connective-tissue / hypermobility (often worsened by trigger event)
- Brain inflammation ("autoimmune encephalopathy"; in kids, PANS/PANDAS)
- GI: SIBO, motility issues
- Small fiber neuropathy
- **Endocrine / metabolic** — she emphasises this is probably **bigger** than initially thought; the "biggest root cause" candidate she's revising upward.

## Triggers she explicitly tracks (beyond covid)

- Vaccines — explicit pro-vaccine framing; not anti-vax. But notes some patients react to specific excipients in specific formulations.
- **Foreign bodies**: hernia mesh, surgical clips, breast implants, joint implants. "Not totally inert."
- Infections — Lyme + Bartonella + tick-borne (her practice is in Lyme-endemic NY).
- Mould / mycotoxins.
- Antibiotics — sometimes the antibiotic + the patient's underlying state combine to flip them.

## The treatment dance

> "It's like a dance. Sometimes you're addressing the root cause. Sometimes you're managing symptoms. You have to do both."

Order of operations she leans on:

1. **Foundational**: nutritional status. Patients are often "wasting away" — limited diets, malabsorption, mitochondria starved. Mast cells have receptors for vitamin D; correcting deficiency can dampen activation.
   - Re vitamin D allergy reports: "You can't be allergic to a vitamin you can't live without — almost always it's the excipient or formulation."
2. **Calm the gut** if needed so the patient can absorb food.
3. **Mast-cell stabilisation** — H1 + H2 antihistamine cocktail (one at a time, 2-4 weeks each, swap formulations if reactive to excipients), quercetin (low dose self-trial OK; med interactions caveat), then move to ketotifen / cromolyn / omalizumab for non-responders.
4. **Limbic retraining** — DNRS (Hopper) and similar. Explicitly *not* telling patients "it's in your head" — the immune-nervous-system loop is short-circuiting and limbic retraining can interrupt it.
5. **Address triggers** — mould remediation, treat persistent infections, address the foreign body, etc.

## On medical trauma

She names this directly: many of her patients have iatrogenic medical trauma layered on top of the underlying illness — gaslighting, accumulated specialist shrugs, etc. The trauma itself drives nervous-system reactivity which feeds the mast-cell loop. **You have to address the trauma to make the rest of the protocol work.**

(Pairs with [[078-lucy-gahan-psychology]]'s witnessing framing and [[081-liza-dileo-thomas-patient-experience]]'s "I believe you" lever — three different angles on the same need.)

## What patients can do without a clinician

She has noticeably softened on this since pre-pandemic — too many patients, not enough specialists. Her revised guidance:

- **Limbic retraining** — fully self-doable online (DNRS, Gupta, etc.)
- **Self-guided meditation, vagal-nerve exercises** — also self-doable
- **Symptom-trigger tracking** — journal, notice patterns, pay attention to menstrual cycle ↔ symptoms
- **Vitamin D + low-dose vitamin C + low-dose quercetin** — generally safe to self-trial (caveats apply)
- **OTC antihistamines** — can self-trial one at a time, two weeks each, swap if reactive to a formulation
- "Always better with a practitioner — but I can't pretend everyone has access."

## The randomized-trial problem she names explicitly

> "How do you do a randomized control trial when every patient's mast cells are mutated differently?"

Argues this is part of why standard evidence pipelines have failed these patients: the heterogeneity makes RCT designs unsuited. Doesn't mean abandon evidence — means reach for trial designs that fit (n-of-1 protocols, basket/umbrella designs, real-world-evidence platforms).

## Quotes

> "Father of the problem — well, my version is: there's a vulnerability and there's a trigger. Get the trigger out, calm the vulnerability, the cascade settles."

> "We don't know everything. We'll never know everything. But we keep questioning."

> "Patients shouldn't have to be advocates beyond advocates for themselves. But that's the situation we're in. So learn your body better than anyone else."

## Eric-relevant intake

- The **vulnerability + trigger** model gives Eric a useful frame for thinking about *why* him: not malice or random chance, but a sub-clinical underlying state that covid surfaced. Doesn't change treatment but reframes self-blame.
- The **endocrine/metabolic emphasis** as her revised "biggest root cause" candidate is a wiki signal worth tracking — pairs with [[045-keith-littlewood-hormones-thyroid]]'s thyroid-and-progesterone framing.
- The **excipient-reactivity** insight is operationally useful: if Eric tries a drug or supplement and "reacts", swap formulation/brand before concluding the molecule itself doesn't suit him.
- **Limbic retraining** as a self-doable add-on is a low-cost trial Eric could undertake — corroborates similar recommendations in [[023-sally-riggs-psychologist-long-hauler]] and the brain-retraining program family.
- The **medical-trauma framing** is one Eric should hold lightly but seriously — accumulated shrug-encounters add genuine biological cost.
