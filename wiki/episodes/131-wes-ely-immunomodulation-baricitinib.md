# Episode 131 — Dr Wes Ely — Immunomodulation & the RVLC trial

Wes Ely is an internist/pulmonologist/critical-care physician at Vanderbilt; PI of the **RVLC (Reverse Long Covid)** trial — a 550-patient placebo-controlled, double-blind RCT of **baricitinib** (a JAK1/2 inhibitor) for established LC. Funded as a multi-year NIH grant; he discloses no financial ties to Lilly (the drug's maker).

## Backstory: ICU delirium → PICS → "wait, this isn't PICS"

- Ely has spent ~25 years studying acquired brain dysfunction during critical illness — **delirium / CAM-ICU** instrument, post-intensive-care syndrome (**PICS**: dementia, PTSD/depression, neuromuscular weakness).
- When LC patients started turning up at his Vanderbilt support groups asking to join, he assumed they had PICS. Then he met long-haulers who had **never been hospitalised**. Realised PICS was the wrong frame; published *Don't Silence Long Covid* in STAT.
- Frame shift: long covid sits inside the broader **iAChC (infection-associated chronic conditions)** family — long lyme, post-SARS, ME/CFS, fibromyalgia, MS — and any treatment they find may generalise.

## Why baricitinib

- His group ran **COV-BARRIER**, the first RCT of baricitinib in acute covid; produced the largest survival advantage of any drug in covid (added on top of dexamethasone in 80% of arm).
- The drug-repurposing pick came from a Lancet paper (April 2020) by Justin Stebbing and colleagues using the BenevolentAI engine to screen hundreds of drugs — baricitinib was the top hit. AI-mediated repurposing → COV-BARRIER → now the LC follow-up.
- Mechanism of action in LC: dampen interferon, immunoglobulin, TNF, interleukin signalling; turn off micro-glial / astrocytic activation in brain; "turn the light switch off" on the persistently activated immune state.
- Drug already FDA-approved for RA, lupus, alopecia areata, and acute covid → high safety baseline.

## RVLC trial design

- **N = 550**, randomised 1:1 placebo vs baricitinib 4 mg PO daily.
- **Sites**: California, Nashville TN, Atlanta GA, Minnesota. North-American-first by logistical necessity (in-person testing).
- **Eligibility**: covid-confirmed (proof required; 50-patient slot for first-wave pre-test patients), LC ≥6 months (not the WHO 3-month definition — explicitly enriched).
- **Treatment 6 months on, 6 months off**, with comprehensive testing at 0 / mid / 6m / 12m.
- Cognitive battery at intake to confirm objective brain involvement.
- Exclusions: profound liver disease, active TB, active major infection.
- Will track EBV reactivation and viral reactivations specifically (known JAK-inhibitor concern at long durations).
- Patients reimbursed for time/inconvenience.
- Public website launching April 2024.

## Co-investigators (the network)

Carolyn Bramante (Minnesota; led the metformin RCT), Priscilla Chu, Michael Peluso, Steve Deeks, Matt Durstenfeld (UCSF), Vince Marconi, Nadine Rouphael, Christina Gavegnano (Emory; baricitinib in HIV), Akiko Iwasaki (Yale), Marian Levy (Penn). **Patient-investigators**: Hannah Davis, Jaime Seltzer, Lisa McCorkell.

## "Either answer matters"

Ely's stance: a negative result is **not failure** — if dampening this set of immune pathways doesn't relieve LC, that's important mechanistic information about where to look next. Standard study-design discipline; he insists on it.

## Eric-relevant

- The CMA-tagged dosing (4 mg/day × 6 months) is a much longer exposure than acute-covid trials and the relevant safety question. If RVLC is positive, off-label use will follow fast.
- This is the strongest immunomodulation arm in the LC trial pipeline, and an explicitly *parallel track* to the antiviral-suppression hypothesis ([[paxlovid-lc-trial]], [[gilead-antiviral-lc-uk]]). Both could be needed eventually.
- Monitoring **EBV reactivation** during JAK inhibition matches the chronic-EBV-reactivation pattern in ME/CFS and the [[viral-persistence]] story.
