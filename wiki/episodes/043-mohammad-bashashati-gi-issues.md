# #43 — Dr Mohammad Bashashati — GI issues in Covid & Long Covid

The wiki's **anchor episode for GI / gut-brain-interaction symptoms in Long Covid**. Bashashati is a senior GI fellow at Texas Tech University Health Sciences Center in El Paso, internist, motility specialist, and one of the few clinicians researching post-COVID gastrointestinal disorders. The framing he introduces — **post-COVID Disorders of Gut-Brain Interaction (DGBI)** — is the canonical clinical taxonomy for this symptom cluster.

## Acute COVID GI prevalence

GI symptoms in acute COVID affect **up to ~1/3 of patients**. Their published cohort (n≈1,000):

- **45% diarrhea** (acute phase)
- **35% nausea ± vomiting**
- **30% abdominal pain**

Higher than other published estimates. Numbers reflect the original/early-2021 variants — no clean data for Omicron yet.

Other documented GI complications: GI bleeding, pancreatitis, mesenteric/pancolitis, body-of-Auerbach (myenteric plexus) damage suggestive of direct viral injury, GI tract necrosis from clot-driven hypoperfusion in shock states.

## Post-COVID DGBI definition

**DGBI** (formerly "functional GI disorders") = chronic GI disorders without anatomical abnormality, attributed to malfunction of the GI tract and impaired gut-brain crosstalk in the context of microbiota / immune / antigen imbalance.

**Post-COVID DGBI** specifically:

- Chronic GI symptoms meeting DGBI criteria for ≥ 3 months
- Symptom onset at ≥ 6 months post-infection
- Prior COVID infection
- Pre-existing DGBI **excludes** post-COVID DGBI labelling (though pre-existing cases can worsen post-COVID — that's still classified as the existing DGBI, not post-COVID DGBI)

Important detail from Bashashati's cohort: **40% of post-COVID DGBI patients did not have GI symptoms during the acute phase.** Onset post-recovery, no acute-phase warning signal.

## Prevalence post-COVID

- **India/Bangladesh study** (n=288): 9% develop DGBI at 6 months. 5.3% IBS-like, 2.1% functional dyspepsia, 1.8% overlap.
- **Bashashati's case series** (n=164, ≥ 6 months post-COVID with chronic GI symptoms): **66% met DGBI criteria.** Most common: functional dyspepsia, then IBS-like.
- **US respiratory-illness clinic** (n=200): 79 developed IBS or functional dyspepsia or both (58 dyspepsia, 2 IBS, 19 overlap).

Italian publication forthcoming.

## Mechanism

- **ACE2 expressed in gut epithelium** → direct viral infection of gut cells.
- → low-grade chronic inflammation, secretory changes, microbiota shifts.
- → enteric nervous system (ENS, ~200–600 million neurons) altered signalling.
- → cross-talk to CNS via vagus, dorsal-root ganglia → centralised symptoms (visceral hypersensitivity, motility changes).
- Stress / anxiety / depression around having COVID amplify the loop.

Bashashati frames this as **post-infectious DGBI** — a known phenomenon. The reference precedent is **Walkerton, Ontario, May 2000** (E. coli + Campylobacter jejuni contaminated drinking water). 36% of those with clinically suspected gastroenteritis developed post-infectious IBS at 2 years. The Walkerton cohort eventually showed **favourable spontaneous remission** in most cases — the optimistic end-state extrapolation for post-COVID DGBI.

## Diagnostic workflow (clinical, not research)

DGBI is a diagnosis of exclusion:

1. History + physical
2. Bloods, stool studies (infection, parasites, calprotectin/inflammation)
3. Endoscopy / colonoscopy with random biopsies if indicated
4. SIBO breath test
5. Thyroid function
6. Imaging if needed
7. **Then** Rome IV criteria classification

Research uses the Rome IV questionnaire only — clinical practice runs the full workup first.

## Treatment toolkit

Symptom-led, not curative. Patients often have multiple overlapping DGBIs and need layered therapy.

- **IBS-D**: anti-diarrhoeals, antispasmodics, neuromodulators (low-dose tricyclics or SSRIs/SNRIs centrally acting), short antibiotic course if SIBO
- **Reflux/heartburn**: PPIs, dietary trigger avoidance (acid, caffeine reduction)
- **Constipation**: fibre, hydration, exercise
- **Faecal incontinence**: fibre, anti-diarrhoeals, pelvic floor physio + biofeedback
- **Rumination syndrome**: diaphragmatic breathing, mindfulness, virtual-reality biofeedback, neuromodulators
- **Bloating**: rule out *H. pylori*, coeliac, SIBO; **low-FODMAP diet** (elimination → slow reintroduction → maintenance), low-salt
- PRN/as-needed dosing patterns are common because severity fluctuates day-to-day

## Quoted positions

> "Whatever mechanism proposed in these disorders is microscopical at molecular and cellular levels — that's why nothing shows on endoscopy or imaging." — Bashashati, ep #43

> "From now on, if a doctor diagnoses DGBI, they should simply ask: were you infected with COVID-19, and did these symptoms start after?" — Bashashati, ep #43

His call-to-action: introduce post-COVID DGBI to the wider medical community; data is being lost because clinicians aren't asking about COVID history.

## Eric-relevant signal

- Eric does not currently report dominant GI symptoms (his stack is autonomic + MCAS-leaning). But the **MCAS / mast-cell-in-gut overlap** is real — if any GI flares appear, the post-COVID DGBI lens is the right one to bring to a GI consult.
- Walkerton's favourable long-term prognosis is a useful narrative anchor: post-infectious GI disorders **do tend to remit** even when chronic.
