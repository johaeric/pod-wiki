# #175 — Jackie dives into Cold Water Immersion

Host-solo episode. Jackie answers the second-most-asked question of the show (after "how did you recover?"): cold water — why it worked for her, why it doesn't work for everyone, and how to know if/when to try it. **Not medical advice.** Cites Susanna Søberg's research (Danish, see her *Winter Swimming*) and Mark Harper's *Chill* (LCP ep #87).

## Why cold water (the physiology)

Two simultaneous responses:
- **Cold shock response** — sympathetic activation (gasp, faster breath, increased HR + BP, vasoconstriction, brown adipose thermogenesis).
- **Diving response** — parasympathetic activation from hydrostatic pressure on baroreceptors (lowers HR/BP, raises vagal tone).

This **autonomic conflict** is the point. Once the cold shock passes (90–120 seconds), the body settles. Documented benefits: increased dopamine, endorphins, noradrenaline (activates brown fat → mitochondrial density), stabilises cortisol/serotonin, reduced inflammation, improved insulin sensitivity, mental-health uplift, takes you out of the overthinking left brain ("step to the right" — Jill Bolte Taylor).

## Why it can backfire in LC / ME-CFS

> "Don't stress up if you don't know how to stress down." — Jackie Baxter, ep #175

The two mistakes she sees others make:
1. **Starting too soon** — cold is a stressor. If your system is already permanently stuck in danger / can't downregulate, you'll get the activation without the relaxation. Straight off the cliff into a crash.
2. **Staying in too long** — max benefits hit ~90s–2 min. Beyond that, body just gets colder, then needs energy to rewarm — energy you can't afford.

Jackie's own use was tactical: short immersions during her own plateau, gradually building. Found it gave her outdoor-adventure feeling when she couldn't hike, and physical compression as a gentler reintroduction to movement. Dan Neuffer told her later it functioned like "hardcore brain training."

## Self-test before trying

- Can you control your breath when stressed? If not, **don't get in cold water.**
- Try cold-water-on-face first (sink, flannel, end of shower).
- Do you have working stress-down strategies you practise routinely?
- Are there contraindications (cardiac arrhythmias, epilepsy, Raynaud's, certain medications)? Always consult a doctor.

## Safety + routine notes

Highlights of her routine: never alone, footwear on, less time than you think, exit before shivering (the after-drop), take care with entry/exit, don't put your head under, don't get straight into a hot shower (blood rush), warm naturally with layers + warm drink + sugary snack.
