# #116 — Dr Claire Taylor: GP, MCAS-and-POTS expert clinician

UK GP with an unusual cross-specialty path (rheumatology in Shetland → cardiology + tilt-test supervision while pregnant → ME charity → private LC practice). **Now runs a UK-wide virtual + in-person LC + ME/CFS + MCAS + POTS clinic** as a private GP service. Expert advisor in long covid for the **World Health Network**.

## Why this episode matters

Taylor is the wiki's **first practising GP-as-LC-specialist** voice. Adjacent to but distinct from:
- [[038-dr-tina-peers-mcas]] — independent UK MCAS specialist
- [[057-sanjay-gupta-pots-specialist]] — UK cardiologist for POTS
- [[037-dr-asad-khan]] — clinician-patient

Taylor's lane is the **integrative GP role** — taking a full history, doing the simple stand test in clinic, and writing the individualised stack (POTS meds + MCAS meds + lifestyle + functional Bloods). Critically — she **does this at GP level, not specialist level**, arguing this is what every GP could in principle do if trained.

## Anchor: nearly everyone she sees has dysautonomia

> "The latest study said 79% of LC patients have POTS. I actually think nearly everyone has a dysautonomia — whether it reaches threshold for POTS or not. The 30 bpm cutoff was based on about 30 people."

Taylor's clinical pattern:

- 10-minute stand test detects POTS within minutes
- Many patients miss POTS criteria but have inappropriate sinus tachycardia (e.g., heart rate 180 bpm walking up 3 stairs or brushing teeth) — this isn't deconditioning, it's **autonomic dysfunction without a label**
- Diagnosis is the inflection — many patients are pragmatic once told "this is what it is, here's what we can do"

## Anchor: standard treatment menu

Taylor's first-line package (always individualised):

**Lifestyle:**
- Salt 6-10 g/day (with caveats for some patients) — she has graphed a patient's heart-rate-on-standing dropping ~10-15 bpm with each salt step-up
- Electrolytes (often nicer than salt-on-food)
- Compression — abdominal/upper-thigh, not just knee-high
- Fluids 3 L/day (per Stiles)

**Medications often added:**
- [[ivabradine]] (preferred for many) or [[beta-blockers]]
- [[midodrine]] for low-BP-on-standing
- [[mestinon]]
- [[fludrocortisone]] for volume retention
- For MCAS: H1 + H2 antihistamines at higher-than-OTC doses; [[ketotifen]]; [[sodium-cromoglicate]] for gut MCAS

## Anchor: vagus nerve thickening + diaphragm flattening (recent study)

Taylor cites a recent imaging study finding **1 in 5 LC patients have thickened vagus nerves** (probably inflammation) and **flattened diaphragms** (probably impaired innervation reducing lung volume). Her interpretation:

- The vagus-nerve thickening explains autonomic symptoms beyond standing (e.g., palpitations after eating)
- Diaphragm flattening connects to "air hunger" symptoms with normal lung function tests — and is why **respiratory physiotherapy and breathing rehabilitation help** (Eric note: this is the clinical rationale for [[long-covid-breathing]], [[vikki-jones]] / Buteyko approaches)

## Anchor: MCAS workup divergence

Taylor describes the **two global MCAS consensuses** as different:

- **Strict (consensus 1):** requires elevated tryptase, prostaglandin, methyl-histamine — pernicious cold-chain urine collection; many false negatives
- **Pragmatic (consensus 2):** if you have MCAS symptoms + ruled out alternatives + responds to MCAS treatment, that's enough for diagnosis

Taylor uses the pragmatic version. Important for LC patients: most positive-MCAS-response is "treat empirically and see."

## Anchor: blood-test framing

Taylor's Bloods strategy goes beyond standard CRP / FBC. Markers she runs (often via private labs because NHS doesn't):

- IL-6, IL-10
- TNF-α, TNF-γ
- CCL5, VEGF
- Inflammation, viral, autoimmune, clotting markers

Her observation: standard Bloods come back normal in LC patients who are clearly inflamed. The inflammation is in cytokines and chemokines that aren't routinely tested for. She pushes to bring this into mainstream medicine — both for diagnosis and for treatment guidance.

## Anchor: the trauma of being told to stay home in 2020

> "In 2020 people at the illest time of their lives were told to stay home. The trauma from that is unaddressed. People wrote letters to their families when they didn't know if they'd wake up. That would not have happened pre-pandemic. It is part of the unprepared response — and it left a lot of unresolved trauma."

Pairs with [[094-jackies-recovery-story]] — Jackie names the same thing about her own onset. Taylor names it as a *systemic clinical concern*, not just an individual experience.

## Anchor: education problem, not knowledge problem

> "We have thousands of papers on LC. You can't expect every doctor to read every one. That's why we need a specialty for this — like cardiology has cardiologists. The science is largely there; the bottleneck is education + access."

Taylor's framing: GPs aren't lazy or hostile, they're untrained on POTS / MCAS / ME / dysautonomia because **none of these were taught in standard UK medical curricula**. Including her own — she had to learn rheumatology ad-hoc, cardiology ad-hoc, MCAS during pandemic.
