# #49 — Wild Swimming with Dr Adrian Baker & Leanne

Practical episode on **cold-water / wild swimming** for chronic-illness symptom management. Two guests:

- **Dr Adrian Baker** — UK GP based in the north of Scotland (Inverness area), 20+ years; lifelong cold-water swimmer; **caught COVID March 2020 and is now fully recovered** after ~18 months of gradual return.
- **Leanne** — full-time night-shift hospital worker, three children, North Scotland. Fibromyalgia diagnosed 2017; rheumatoid arthritis diagnosed 6 weeks before recording. Started cold-water swimming on Adrian's recommendation; has built a community group of ~107 swimmers.

This episode is the wiki's clearest exposition of the **cold-water exposure** intervention as a practical chronic-illness lever. Mechanism, dose-response, safety, and community-building are all covered in usable detail.

## Adrian's recovery story (brief)

Caught COVID early March 2020 (one of the first three GPs at his practice to get it). Off work 10 days, full-blown symptoms, "not quite hospitalised but close." Returned to work but couldn't last past lunchtime — bone-deep fatigue. Could not exercise for many months. **Costochondritis** (severe breastbone / chest pain at the rib-sternum junction) was the last symptom to clear, taking ~18 months. He emphasises that he sees this same pattern in many of his LC patients — knowing what it is reduces the terror of "is this a heart attack?"

Now back to normal. His framing of recovery: gradual, pacing, rest, "be good to yourself," and (eventually) cold-water swimming.

## Leanne's case — fibromyalgia + RA management

- Pre-2017: full-time mum, full-time hospital night shifts.
- 2017: fibromyalgia diagnosis. Standard pharmaceuticals (gabapentin / amitriptyline class — implied) didn't agree with her shift work.
- Adrian (her GP at the time) suggested sea swimming. Initial reaction: "I had a big argument with him." First time in: 5 minutes, hated it.
- ~6 months later: tried again. The pain subsided in the water. "It just gave me my life back."
- Has swum daily since (year-round, in a swimsuit not wetsuit, by choice). Did a charity year-long every-day-in-the-sea challenge for Mikey's Line.
- 6 weeks before recording: rheumatoid arthritis dx. Specialist: "your joints don't coincide with RA — keep swimming." She is **off paracetamol** and reports vitamin C as her main supplement.
- Built a community group of ~107 swimmers across local NHS staff and beyond.

She is **`key_to_recovery` for fibromyalgia (and apparently RA management)** — not LC. But the mechanism-convergence with LC inflammation makes this the clearest "this can transform a chronic-inflammatory illness" data point yet on the wiki.

## Why it works (mechanism summary)

From Adrian's GP framing:

1. **Endorphins.** Body's own opiates; pain relief lasts the day, not just the swim.
2. **Diving reflex.** Face submerged → pulse slows, breathing slows, parasympathetic shift. Prehistoric mammalian reflex.
3. **Cold-induced microcirculation.** Repeated cold extremity exposure stimulates *new* microcapillary formation. Helps arthritic joints (osteoarthritis and inflammatory).
4. **Anti-inflammatory.** Cold + water immersion downregulates inflammation systemically.
5. **Cold-shock adaptation.** The hyperventilation response disappears within **5–7 consecutive daily immersions**. After that, breathing in cold water is normal.
6. **Mental-health + social.** Endorphin lift + small-achievement loop + community / tribe.

> "It's free. It's freezing. And it's free." — Leanne, ep #49

## Practical heuristics worth carrying

- **Time-in-water rule**: ~1 minute per degree Celsius. (Mid-winter Scotland, ~5°C → ~5 minutes.)
- **Slow long exhale**, head out for early sessions. The breath is the training.
- **Do NOT take a hot shower immediately afterwards.** Skin vasoconstriction is protecting the core; sudden vasodilation drops BP and can faint you. Found out the hard way is a real injury risk.
- **Get out of wet kit within 5–10 minutes** — the second cold wave is bigger than the first.
- **Slow rewarming**: dry layers, thick wool hat, warm-but-not-hot drink, mat under feet, hot water bottle.
- **Shivering is normal** and can last 1–4 hours. Not a danger sign.
- **Tow floats** for visibility + emergency flotation. (And as Adrian demonstrated, for transporting crabs.)
- **Read the water before getting in.** Tides and currents change fast. Adrian's own near-miss with a local east-west rip current is the cautionary tale.
- **Company > solo.** Especially in first weeks, cold weather, or unfamiliar water.
- **Wet suit vs costume = preference.** Wet suit lets you stay longer; costume cools faster.
- **You don't need to swim.** Knee-deep paddle counts. Submerging to chest counts. That's enough for the cold-shock and endorphin hit.

## Eric-relevant signal

- **Cold-water exposure is a free, low-friction lever** with mechanism-plausible benefits across MCAS, microcirculation, autonomic regulation, mood, and social isolation. Risks are manageable.
- **Eric's autonomic profile + temperature dysregulation** suggests entering very gradually — knee-deep paddle in summer, build over weeks. The Adrian "5–7 consecutive sessions" guidance is a useful structuring principle.
- **Avoid hot shower afterwards** is the most consequential safety reminder — easy to get wrong otherwise.
- **Costochondritis** as a long-tail LC symptom (~18 months to clear, in Adrian's case) is a useful piece of prognostic background. If Eric ever develops chest pain that doesn't fit cardiac patterns, costochondritis is on the differential.
