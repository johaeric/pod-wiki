# #020 — Amy Anker's recovery & Positively Covid

**Guest**: **Amy Anker** — stained-glass artist, ~23 years in business, lives in the Rocky Mountains, Montana. Pre-LC: very active (hiking, fishing 2–3×/week, gym 3×/week — husband is a personal trainer). Caught covid early November 2020. **Fully recovered** at recording (~14 months later). Founded **[[positively-covid]]** as a free resource of meditations + recovery stories.

> ⚠️ **Naming clarification.** This is **not Dr Amy Small** (ep #5, Edinburgh GP with POTS). The previous indexer noted ep #20 as Amy Small's follow-up, but the actual ep is a different Amy. Cross-ref on dr-amy-small.md should be removed/corrected.

This is the **second full-recovery story** in the wiki (after [[recovery-stories/rachel-whitfield]]) and the first that names **neuroplasticity / nervous-system-retraining + positivity practice** as the credited mechanism.

## Recovery status

**Yes — fully recovered.** Linear-ish, not overnight: best stretch felt at ~6 months; a wildfire-smoke valley filled with smoke around month 11 caused a major relapse; full recovery clicked in the months after the smoke cleared, when she identified and broke a "looping pattern" in her nervous system.

## Symptom picture (acute + LC)

Strongly **neurological** dominant:
- Loss of smell + taste
- **Numb palms** ("felt like wearing gloves")
- **Scalp burning pain** — "the area a yamaka would cover"; first thought to be shingles. Couldn't brush hair, couldn't put head on pillow for weeks.
- **Hyperacusis** — "the slightest sound was a gunshot"
- Hand tremors (months)
- **Insomnia** — wide awake 3+ hours every night, terrified
- Tachycardia
- **Hell-surges** — 2–3-hour dark depressive episodes she classifies as "psychosis-adjacent"; came on out of the blue, lifted as suddenly. Started ~2 months in.

She names her neurologist's diagnosis: **dysautonomia**.

## Theoretical framing she adopted

She was already reading **Norman Doidge — *The Brain That Changes Itself*** at infection — neuroplasticity. This shaped her belief from day one that recovery was possible.

Discovery much later that she had drifted into a **looping pattern** — like phantom-limb pain. The acute injury (the virus) had healed, but her nervous system was firing the pain/symptom loop on minimal triggers ("I'd pull a weed in the garden — back to bed for hours"). Once she could see the loop, she could interrupt it.

Mechanism cluster (her own words):
- Mind-body connection
- Habit-stacking ("we are all a bundle of habits — change them, build new ways of being")
- Anticipating pain → producing pain
- Positivity, hope, optimism as fundamental healing inputs (not sole fixes)

## Recovery routine (key_to_recovery — credited mechanism)

A four-pillar daily practice she called the seeds-she-planted:

1. **Positive mindset** — gratitude lists, deliberate framing, no negative-content soak.
2. **Real rest** — not Netflix. No phone-scrolling. Active down-shifting.
3. **Nourish the body** — healthy food, hydration.
4. **Move every day** — walking specifically. Even 5 min around the block. "Bilateral movement matters. Neuronal growth happens when you walk."

Plus: **interrupt the looping pattern** — the realisation that her thinking was contributing to the nervous-system reaction; deliberate "I'm okay, we're going to get through this" reassurance to her own NS.

## What didn't help

- **Long social-media exposure to LC support groups.** Started off helpful (camaraderie, validation), then noticed seeing 2-year-mark posts wrecked her day. Started rationing — once a day, smile or laugh = stop, log off. Names this as a deliberate choice — left the groups by the end.

## Why she built Positively Covid

Couldn't find recovery stories — *the people who recovered had moved on with their lives, leaving the support groups dominated by the still-suffering*. She vowed to stay public after recovery. Site: meditations she wrote during illness ("born of necessity"), recovery stories from others, daily-routine guidance.

> "It's possible. If you don't think it's possible, it won't happen. I knew it. I held onto that so tightly. That's what won." — Amy, ep #20

## Notable quotes

> "I refuse to operate at the pace society imposes."

> "Compassion arises when kindness meets suffering — that's why I won't leave the support groups."

## How to read this for Eric

Amy's recovery is genuine and fully described — but read with clean caveats:
- N=1 mind-body recovery in someone with **strongly neurological** LC dominance and no documented organ damage. Eric's POTS+PEM is autonomically-driven; some of Amy's mechanisms (NS-regulation, walking, positivity practice) plausibly map; others (stop scrolling, daily walks, gratitude) are universal-low-cost-low-risk additions.
- She herself flags that **organ damage / structural pathology** is a different group and her approach won't substitute for that.
- Cross-ref ercipedia for evidence on neuroplasticity / mind-body interventions in chronic illness.
