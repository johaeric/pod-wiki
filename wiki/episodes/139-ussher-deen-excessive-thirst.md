# Episode 139 — Patrick Ussher & Peter Deen — Excessive Thirst in ME/CFS & LC

A double interview between a 6-year ME/CFS patient who almost died from acute hyponatremia (Patrick Ussher) and a Dutch professor of water-homeostasis biology (Peter Deen). Their collaboration started after Patrick wrote a Health Rising blog summarising his research; Peter wrote in offering scientific partnership.

## The clinical picture (Ussher)

- **Excessive thirst** is reported by ~40% of LC patients (cited large LC survey) as one of their worst symptoms; in ME/CFS it sits on a spectrum from mild to extreme.
- At his worst (mid-2020 → Jan 2021), Ussher was drinking **6–8 L water on baseline days, 10–20 L during a crash, including 10 L overnight**. Muscles twitching/spasming. Sense of "blood not reaching skin or brain". Whole body screaming with thirst.
- January 2021: hospitalised with **blood sodium of 116** (normal 135–145) — life-threatening dilutional hyponatremia.
- Diagnosed with **primary polydipsia / psychogenic polydipsia** — the persistent medical assumption that excessive water drinking in patients without obvious organic cause is *psychiatric*. Treated as such; overheard staff joking about it.

## The hypothesis (Ussher + Deen)

Patrick spent a year reading the entire ~300-paper literature on primary/psychogenic polydipsia from the 1930s onwards and concluded:

1. Primary polydipsia has been called a "mystery" since the 1950s; consensus understanding hasn't advanced meaningfully since 1959.
2. The brain's **hypovolemic thirst centre** wasn't even discovered until 1968 — predating that, the entire psychogenic frame was built without knowing the body had a separate thirst pathway for low blood volume.
3. ME/CFS patients have **measurably low blood volume** (Visser/Van Campen: up to 1.5 L deficit in some patients, ~20% of total blood volume).
4. Ussher reads early case studies of "compulsive water drinking" and recognises *ME/CFS-like* patients (breathless on exertion, post-exertional weakness, ache-everywhere, "hysterical weakness of the legs") — patients being drawn from the same population, misdiagnosed as psychiatric.

**Working hypothesis**: at least a subset of "primary polydipsia" patients are actually **thirsty for blood, not water** — driven by the hypovolemic thirst centre in response to a chronically low blood volume, with a **suppressed renin-angiotensin-aldosterone (RAAS) axis** preventing the normal salt-craving signal that would drive them to fix the blood volume properly.

This is paradoxical physiology: in a healthy person, low blood volume → angiotensin II → salt craving + thirst → drink salty fluids → expand blood volume. In ME/CFS / LC the RAAS is suppressed, salt craving is absent, only the thirst signal fires — patient drinks plain water → makes the dilution *worse* → hyponatremia.

## The treatment (Ussher's self-discovery)

After hospital discharge with no working diagnosis, Ussher worked out the mechanism himself and switched to **oral rehydration solution (ORS)**.

- Glucose + salt in ORS triggers the **sodium-glucose co-transporter** in the stomach, which actively pulls salty solution into the bloodstream — much more efficiently than salt-on-food.
- Meadow study (cited): drinking 1 L of ORS over 30 minutes in POTS patients matched 1 L IV saline for blood-volume expansion.
- Current daily protocol: **1.5 L of ORS daily**. By the end of a litre over a couple of hours, blood feels like it's reaching skin/muscles/brain; in a crash, ORS has near-Biblical effect — can move from couch-stapled to walking around within 30–60 minutes.
- Important rule: ORS works much better when *most* of fluid intake comes from ORS. Drinking lots of plain water alongside dilutes the effect.
- For UK readers: **dioralyte**. US: pedialyte / normalyte / Vitalyte.

## Wiki position

- This episode formalises **excessive thirst** + **hypovolemic thirst** as a distinct symptom-pattern with an actionable intervention (ORS).
- Confirms the pattern Eric and others have noticed: hydration alone doesn't work; salt + water alone is suboptimal; ORS-style sodium-glucose-water co-transport is *significantly* better than salt-on-food.
- Ussher's hospital experience is the wiki's strongest example of [[healthcare-gaslighting]] — being misdiagnosed as psychiatric when the underlying physiology was clear-cut.
