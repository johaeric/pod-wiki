# #97 — Harlan Krumholz: Yale LISTEN study & Paxlovid LC trial

US cardiologist; Director of Center for Outcomes Research and Evaluation (CORE) at Yale. Co-leads two LC studies with **Akiko Iwasaki** (Yale immunologist):
- **Yale LISTEN study** — longitudinal observational study of LC.
- **Pax LC trial** — Paxlovid trial for LC (15-day extended-dose; placebo-controlled, deep immune phenotyping).

⚠️ **Naming clash**: this is Yale's LISTEN — different from the UK NIHR LISTEN trial in [[058-fiona-jones-listen-trial]] (Bridges-self-management). See [[yale-listen-study]] (Yale, observational) and [[listen-trial]] (Fiona Jones, UK RCT).

## On the broken healthcare system (foundational frame)

Krumholz's overarching critique: the US (and UK) healthcare system is **transactional, hierarchical, reactive** — set up for short interactions, rapid pattern recognition, triage. LC broke this because:
- The condition doesn't fit a pattern.
- It needs **time + investment** to characterise; clinicians don't have hours per consultation.
- Acute COVID had already stressed a fragile system.
- Result: clinicians felt inadequate; patients felt dismissed; nothing worked.

His project: **digital, decentralised, democratised research** — a deliberate attempt to design *out* the structural barriers that exclude or burden patients.

## Yale LISTEN study (the observational arm)

**[[yale-listen-study]]** — open to anyone in the world via app-based participation:
- Surveys + (in US subset) blood + saliva collection.
- N ≈ 2,200 enrolled at recording.
- Combines symptom Atlas with deep immune phenotyping by Iwasaki's lab.

The study's research-design innovations:
- **Town halls** — investigators meet with participants to share data + discuss.
- **All home-based** — no travel required.
- **Always returns results** — the study commits to share findings back.
- **Digital data agency** via [[hugo-health]] — the participant grants permission for their data, retains the right to revoke.
- Frames participants as **partners**, not "subjects." Krumholz: *"I'm embarrassed to say studies I ran, I didn't tell people what the result was. It's horrible to think about now."*

## Pax LC trial — the interventional arm

**[[paxlovid-lc-trial]]** — placebo-controlled trial of **15-day extended-dose Paxlovid** for LC:
- N = 100; deep immune phenotyping in all participants (Iwasaki lab).
- **Tests viral-persistence hypothesis** — if persistent virus drives LC for some, then 15-day Paxlovid should kick it out.
- **Why 15 days**: acute Paxlovid is 5 days; rebound is common after 5; 15 days targets full clearance.
- **Why Paxlovid (and not other antivirals)**: oral, FDA-approved, partner pharma interest; Krumholz acknowledges this is partly funding-driven.
- **Comparator placebo includes ritonavir** (the potentiator-and-flavour-causer) — preserves blinding around metallic taste.
- **24-week follow-up after randomisation**; cracking the blind at ~28 days.
- **41 states** via partnership with Trusted Medical (Kevin Carr) — physicians licensed in each state to enable national decentralised enrolment.

The trial-design philosophy is critical to flag:
- Anyone can do the screening online.
- Eligible enrollees use their devices for surveys; receive medication and have blood drawn at home.
- **No clinician acts as gatekeeper** — participants step forward and qualify based on records.

## Hugo Health — data agency at scale

**[[hugo-health]]** is Krumholz's startup whose mission is to give participants agency over their own data:
- Patients link their accounts (medical records, wearables, pharmacies, insurance).
- Data flows to participant's cloud-based account, refreshed.
- Permission to share into a study is **revokable**.
- Long-term vision: data assets that grow over time, usable for research and care — but always patient-controlled.

This is the wiki's first explicit articulation of **digital-data-agency-as-research-infrastructure**. Pairs with the [[054-elizabeth-dreicer-consuli]] equity / infrastructure framing, and with [[095-laina-gauthier-mamaril-zombies]]'s cripistemology + format-as-political-act framing.

## On Akiko Iwasaki — the partnership

Krumholz's co-PI is one of the world's leading immunologists. Their division of labour:
- **Iwasaki**: deep immune phenotyping (thousands of measurements per participant).
- **Krumholz**: clinical research / participant-centric design / data infrastructure.
- **Joint output**: Atlas of Long Covid — symptom-phenotype map overlaid on immune-phenotype map → diagnostics + therapeutic targets.

The Atlas idea is the most ambitious mechanism-to-clinical-presentation-mapping project named on the wiki to date.

## On vaccine injury

Important and easy-to-miss disclosure: Krumholz is **also studying people reporting vaccine injury**. He explicitly believes vaccines were a "modern miracle" that saved millions of lives, *and* that some people had real harms from them — same kind of long-tail post-immunological response. Pairs with [[037-dr-asad-khan]] [[038-dr-tina-peers-mcas]] [[084-caroline-pover-vaccine-injuries]].

## Timeline

- Pax LC trial: enrolment expected to finish late November 2023; results early 2024 (~February).
- LISTEN study: ongoing, no end date — accumulating data.
- Atlas of Long Covid: emerging output, expanding over multiple years.

## Cultural shift Krumholz is articulating

Three principles he keeps returning to — worth writing down because they're operationally portable:
1. **Treat participants as partners, not subjects.**
2. **Always return results — the study is worthy of trust only if it does.**
3. **Make participation easy** — digital, decentralised, home-based; respect cognitive load (information sheets short and accessible — same point [[viki-male]] makes).
