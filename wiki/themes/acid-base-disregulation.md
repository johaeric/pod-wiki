# Acid-base disregulation (acidosis hypothesis)

A unifying-mechanism frame proposed in the 2024 Rossman / van der Togt hypothesis paper (#134): persistent **acid-base disregulation** — driven by reduced oxygen delivery to tissues — sits between upstream covid pathology and the symptom profile patients experience.

## The chain

1. **Upstream pathology** (any combination of):
   - Microvascular dysfunction reducing oxygen delivery (cf. #33 Pretorius/Kell, [[microclots]]).
   - Lung damage / breathing pattern dysfunction reducing oxygen *intake* (cf. #12 Kelly Mitchell, #132 Courtney/Golan).
   - Endothelial inflammation (cf. [[endothelial-dysfunction]]).
   - Microclotting reducing capillary perfusion.
2. **Tissue hypoxia** → muscle cells switch to **anaerobic respiration**.
3. **Excess lactate / acid** is produced. Across the whole body, sustained over time, this becomes systemic acid stress.
4. **Symptoms**: fatigue, exercise intolerance, brain fog, muscle pain, breathlessness, autonomic disregulation, all of which can be explained at least partly by acid-base disturbance affecting clotting, inflammation, muscle function, brain function, and more.

## Why "central inter-mediator", not "the cause"

Rossman is explicit: this is not a single-cause theory. Microclots, viral persistence, autoimmunity, mast-cell activation, mitochondrial dysfunction are all real and active. Acidosis is **the convergence point** where many upstream pathologies produce the same downstream symptom pattern.

This frames LC as **multiple causal paths → similar physiological consequence → heterogeneous but overlapping symptom presentations**. It explains why patients respond to very different interventions: each intervention may relieve a different upstream contributor that meets at the same acidosis bottleneck.

## How it connects existing wiki frames

- **Breathing therapy** (#12, #17, #29, #132): low ETCO₂ is a marker of acid-base disturbance; gentle CO₂ recovery work is a direct correction.
- **Mitochondrial dysfunction** (#21, #44, AXA1125 #35/#52): reduced ATP and CO₂ production sit alongside excess lactate as part of the same picture.
- **POTS / autonomic dysfunction** (#40, #66): low blood volume contributes to reduced oxygen delivery to tissues.
- **Bioenergetic framework** (#45 Littlewood): pro-glucose / anti-PUFA pathways argue similar territory from the metabolic angle.
- **Cold-water exposure** (#49, #94, #127): cold exposure + breath training elevate stress tolerance and may improve oxygen handling — speculative tie-in.

## How it would be tested

1. Measure baseline lactate / acid-base in LC vs control populations (resting + with mild activity / exertion). Existing literature suggests early support.
2. Continuous biosensor monitoring (pH / lactate via glucose-monitor-style adhesive patches — in development).
3. If disregulation confirmed, intervention trials (existing exercise-science and medical literature on managing acidosis).

## Wiki position

This is the wiki's **first explicit acid-base / acidosis frame**. Logged as a hypothesis-to-test, not as established mechanism. Watch for the Step 1 paper from the Rossman/van der Togt collaboration.
