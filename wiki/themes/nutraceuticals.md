# Nutraceuticals

The use of plant-derived bioactive molecules (flavonoids, polyphenols, alkaloids, etc.) as therapeutic interventions, occupying a regulatory tier between dietary supplements and phytopharmaceuticals.

## The framing the wiki adopts

From [[joachim-gerlach]] (ep #41), the relevant tiering is:

```
dietary supplement → nutraceutical → functional food → food for special medical purpose
                                  → phytopharmaceutical → pharmaceutical
```

A nutraceutical is essentially a dietary supplement with a stronger pharmacological intent and (sometimes) clinical-trial evidence. The regulatory status varies wildly by country.

## Recurring claims to track across episodes

- **Body recognises evolutionary-familiar molecules.** Plant flavonoids, etc. have been around for billions of years and the body has co-evolved transport / metabolism mechanisms for them.
- **Site-of-need accumulation.** Curcumin example: low serum levels, high tissue levels at sites of inflammation (per Gerlach #41).
- **Context-dependent action.** Same molecule can trigger apoptosis in irreparable cells and support repair in salvageable ones (Gerlach's claim re baicalein).

These are claims the wiki should *track* — they are not yet confirmed by peer-reviewed evidence. Cross-ref ercipedia when patients/clinicians actually try specific molecules.

## Caveat the wiki holds firm on

The shorthand "natural therefore safe" is wrong. From Gerlach #41:

> "Something natural doesn't mean at all that it cannot have severe side effects."

Specific risks named:
- Vitamin D3 / zinc without serum-level monitoring → overshoot
- Probiotics in MCAS patients → hypersensitivity reactions
- Supplement-drug interactions via CYP450 metabolism shifts → unintended pharmaceutical overdose

## Long-haulers and supplement-stacking

Gerlach repeatedly warns that long-haulers presenting to him often have chaotic stacks of 10–20 supplements with redundant mechanisms (multiple anti-inflammatories doing the same job; multiple antivirals; etc.). He advises **categorical targeting**: one strong agent per category (anti-inflammatory / anti-allergic / anti-pathogenic / mitochondrial / neurological).

This is consistent with the wiki's broader [[polypharmacy-warning]] theme.
