# Episodic Disability

A framework, originating in HIV research ([[kelly-o-brien]], University of Toronto), that defines disability as **fluctuating, unpredictable, and dimensional** — not a static binary. Particularly load-bearing for LC, ME/CFS, and other relapsing-remitting conditions.

## Core definition

Disability spans these dimensions, any or all of which can wax and wane day-to-day:

1. Physical impairment of body or body function
2. Cognitive impairment
3. Mental / emotional health impairment
4. Difficulties with day-to-day activities (functioning)
5. Challenges to social participation / community life
6. **Uncertainty or worry about future health** — uniquely added by the episodic framework

Triggers can be internal (symptom flares, PEM) or external (stigma, shame, inaccessible environments).

## Why it matters for LC

- The fluctuating / waxing-waning / relapsing-remitting nature of LC is **the most universally reported feature** across podcast guests.
- Existing benefits / Social Security assessments use a **single-snapshot** model ("can you walk 100m today?") which systematically under-counts episodic disability.
- The **Episodic Disability Questionnaire** measures presence, severity, AND episodic nature of disability across all dimensions — Brown reports it has been instrumental in his clinical letters supporting LC patient benefits claims.

## How disability theory got here

| Model | Era | Lens | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Biomedical | classical | impairment of body part | ignores environment; oppressive when used alone |
| Social | 1970s–90s | environment disables, not body | misses lived physical impairments |
| WHO ICF | 1990s+ | interaction of health, body, activity, participation, environment | static; widely used despite criticism |
| Episodic Disability | post-2000s (HIV) | adds fluctuation + uncertainty | newer; still being validated for LC |

## Active research

Canadian Institute of Health Research-funded study (Brown, O'Brien et al.) — 40 qualitative interviews UK/Ireland/Canada/US to test framework fit for LC and adapt the questionnaire. Two-phase: qualitative, then psychometric validation.

## Rehab implications

Episodic disability re-frames rehab as **education, equipment, adaptations, pacing, symptom monitoring** — not as physical exercise. This frame protects against GET-style harm while still offering meaningful clinical support. See [[darren-brown]] (#47, #51) and [[long-covid-physio]].

## Empirical confirmation in LC (#120 Faghy)

[[120-mark-faghy-research-update]]: the Derby team's 16-week cohort (~100 patients, weekly contact) is — by Faghy's knowledge — the only LC study to date that has captured the granular day-to-day fluctuation pattern. Patients can swing 180° within 48 hours. **Standard 6/12-month cohort designs miss this** — their conclusions are hostage to whichever single day each participant was assessed.

Strong implication for service design: services must be **fluid enough to accommodate** today-being-good-or-awful. Static one-shot assessments at fixed intervals can't drive the right intervention.
