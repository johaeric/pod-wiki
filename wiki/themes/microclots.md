# Microclots

The **fluorescence-microscopy-visible amyloid-form fibrin microclots** found in long covid plasma by Pretorius / Kell. The leading mechanism candidate that ties together the heterogeneous LC symptom picture into one story: **systemic cellular hypoxia** driven by capillary blockage.

## What they are (per #33)

- **Amyloid-form fibrin** — a misfolded crosslinked form of fibrin not seen in healthy controls.
- **Resistant to fibrinolysis** — the body's plasmin system slowly degrades them, but much slower than normal clots. Hence persistence.
- **Trap inflammatory mediators inside** — von Willebrand factor, plasminogen, antiplasmin. Standard plasma assays read normal because the analytes are sequestered.
- **Triggered by tiny seeds** — one molecule of LPS per 100 million fibrinogen, or by spike protein. Once a fraction "flips" amyloid, neighbours follow.

## Why "vascular disease, not respiratory"

- Microclots block / restrict capillaries (~24 µm).
- Endothelial inflammation feeds back: endothelitis → platelet hyperactivation → microclot formation → endothelitis.
- The result is **systemic cellular hypoxia** — explaining symptom heterogeneity (brain fog, PEM, neuropathy, gut dysmotility) without needing a different mechanism per system.

## Treatment implications

- **Triple therapy** anticoagulation stops new microclots forming; body slowly degrades existing ones.
- **HELP-apheresis** (Jaeger) physically removes circulating microclots.
- **Statins** anti-inflammatory; **antihistamines** for MCAS-overlap; **nutraceuticals** (nattokinase / lumbrokinase / serrapeptase) plausibly help.
- **Reinfection regenerates microclots** — Khan's pattern.

## Validation / clinical-relevance dispute (added from #92)

[[robert-groysman]] (#92) is the wiki's first clinically-prominent sceptic on the microclot model. His position:

> "Before we hang our hat on this test, I think it needs to be validated. Does it really represent clot formation inside the body or not? I don't have an answer to that. Have they been proven to be significant — impeding blood flow? It has not."

His clinical concerns:
- **Validation gap** — does fluorescence-microscopy on platelet-poor plasma represent in-vivo clotting?
- **Clinical relevance unproven** — even if microclots exist, is flow being impeded?
- **Anticoagulants are not benign** — has seen patients on three simultaneously; bleed risk from undiagnosed lesions can be catastrophic.

Worth holding alongside [[033-pretorius-kell-microclots]] (the original anchor) and [[037-dr-asad-khan]] (the clinical anchor). Three positions on one disputed mechanism is a feature, not a bug — log all of them.

## Diagnostic obstacle

- Pathology labs lack the right microscopes; Pretorius/Kell are building a press-button instrument.
- Sheffield (UK) has reproduced the imaging.
- UK clinical adoption gated on UK replication, despite SA + EU work being well-published.
