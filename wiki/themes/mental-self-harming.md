# Mental Self-Harming

Sam Munslow's coaching framing (#34) for the inner-critic loop that ramps during chronic illness. Names it deliberately as "harming" because the *severity* matches: the cognitive damage from sustained self-attack matches the damage from many overt self-harm patterns.

## Recognising it

- Predicting catastrophic futures ("I'll never run again")
- Re-running past failures (the unanswered Lego presentation)
- Guilt-as-proof-of-bad-mother / partner / employee / friend
- Resenting supportive people for the help they're providing
- Layering self-blame onto every limit ("I should be doing better than this")

## What helps (Munslow's coaching)

1. **Write down what you say to yourself.** Re-read the next day. Awareness alone often defangs.
2. **Reframe language.** "Rest days" instead of "bad days." Reframe resentment as situation-resentment, not person-resentment.
3. **Live in the now.** "What can I do today" not "what will I never do again."
4. **See it from their perspective.** The supporter's experience is also traumatic — recognising that often unblocks communication.

## Why this is in the wiki separately from acceptance / self-compassion

Munslow's specific contribution is naming the phenomenon as harming, not as "negative self-talk" — that lifts the perceived severity to match the lived severity, and makes the corrective feel proportionate.
