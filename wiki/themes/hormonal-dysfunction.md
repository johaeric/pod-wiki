# Hormonal dysfunction in Long Covid

The endocrine dimension of LC: thyroid, progesterone, estrogen, HPA-axis cortisol, the pituitary axis, and environmental endocrine-disrupting compounds. Mostly under-tested in routine LC clinics; commonly suspected in patients with cold intolerance, hair loss, menstrual irregularity, infertility, weight changes, sleep disruption, mood/affect changes, or libido changes that don't fit neatly into the autonomic / mast-cell / microclot frames.

## Thyroid

The thyroid is intimately linked to nearly every body function: metabolism, body temperature, cardiovascular tone, reproductive function, mental clarity, hair, skin, GI motility. From [[keith-littlewood]] (#45):

- **TSH is unreliable in stressed patients.** Adrenaline + cortisol suppress pituitary TSH; a stressed patient can look "fine" on a TSH-only panel and be functionally hypothyroid. Full panel: free T4, free T3, reverse T3, TPO antibodies, sometimes thyroglobulin antibodies.
- **Body temperature + pulse** on waking and 45–60 min post-meal is a useful crude clinical signal. Sustained < 36°C is a red flag.
- **Conversion can be blocked downstream** of pituitary signalling — by liver dysfunction, environmental pollutants binding receptors, nutrient deficiency (selenium, iodine, zinc), or chronic inflammation suppressing peripheral T4→T3 conversion.
- **Estrogen excess suppresses thyroid.** Female autoimmune-thyroid prevalence is ~10× male prevalence.
- **Hair loss, infertility, brain fog, cold extremities, constipation, low cholesterol-in-sustained-states, seasonal-mood-disorder, low pulse** all in the symptom cluster.

Cross-ref ercipedia thyroid pages for peer-reviewed LC-specific evidence.

## Progesterone

Litlewood's #45 framing positions progesterone as a **major under-examined LC variable**:

- **Antiviral activity.** Progesterone receptors in nasal epithelium; supports macrophage / phagocytosis activity; restores aerobic metabolism.
- **Deficient in PCOS, PMS, endometriosis, fibroids, heavy bleeding women** and in **older men.**
- **Reverses hypoxia** by restoring aerobic energy metabolism (Littlewood's mechanistic claim).
- Used in traumatic brain injury research.

The N=1-from-clinician evidence is suggestive but pre-RCT; tracked here as a working hypothesis.

## Estrogen excess

- Endogenous + environmental endocrine disruptors (cleaning products, plastics, agricultural runoff, oral contraceptives in women's history, alcohol-derived phytoestrogens).
- Accumulates because the **liver clears it via CYP-mediated and B-vitamin (especially thiamine) pathways**; B-vitamin deficiency or liver dysfunction → estrogen accumulation → thyroid suppression.
- Drives reproductive-tissue overgrowth (fibroids, endometriosis, prolactinomas).
- Cross-pathway with [[microclots]] and [[viral-persistence]] not yet mapped.

## HPA-axis

Mitochondria are themselves endocrine — they make stress hormones in the adrenal glands ([[moira-newiss]] #44). Chronic Battleship-mode mitochondria → chronic HPA-axis dysregulation → adrenal fatigue / cortisol-rhythm-flattening. Convergent with the [[autonomic-dysfunction]] picture.

## Eric-relevant signal

- Eric does not currently report classic thyroid or progesterone symptoms but the male-progesterone deficiency claim from Littlewood is a flag worth checking with his endocrinologist (if he has one) or GP.
- A full thyroid panel (not just TSH) is a low-cost diagnostic that may be worth requesting.
- Body temperature + pulse on waking can be tracked privately for a week to test the Littlewood hypothesis without involving any clinician.

## Menstrual cycle as a flare amplifier

- (ep #26) [[jackie-maybin]] — anchor menstrual-health episode.
- (ep #80) [[anjali-agarwal]] reports hypermobile women experiencing **POTS spike + joint instability spike + MCAS flare** during periods every cycle — multi-system synchronisation across the trifecta.
- (ep #82) [[jay-wiles]] articulates the **HRV-suppression-during-ovulation/PMS** physiology: ovulation is energetically expensive, drives ↑ respiratory rate, ↑ heart rate, ↓ HRV, ↓ immunological function, ↑ cortisol; intervene with biofeedback / breathwork rather than expecting "no symptom shift."
- (ep #93) [[viki-male]] — **anchor LC-menstruation immunology episode**. Survey (n~450) shows **70%** of LC menstruators notice a cycle-symptom pattern; **>3/4** of those say the late luteal + period phase is worse. Frames the second half of the cycle as a "mini-pregnancy" immunologically — analogous to MS / IBD cycle-coupling, suggesting an autoimmune dimension. Hormonal contraception and HRT are flagged as candidate symptom-management levers (pre-RCT). The **LC-perimenopause symptom overlap** is now a recognised clinical signal — sometimes "treat perimenopause first" substantially helps QoL.
- (ep #138) [[jules-rogers]] — **first explicit positive HRT outcome from a recovered patient** on the wiki. Late addition to her recovery toolkit; HRT shifted **brain fog specifically**. Hadn't planned ever to take HRT; her own research found supportive evidence in some perimenopausal LC patients. Confirms the perimenopause-LC overlap as a sub-pattern with its own treatment levers. See [[recovery-stories/jules-rogers-recovery]].
- (ep #144) [[dr-sarah]] — **second positive HRT row from a recovered patient.** Mid-40s GP/dermatologist; saw an LC + menopause hormone specialist; titrated HRT very slowly over a year, alongside her other six pillars. Couldn't isolate the contribution but logs as meaningful. Sub-pattern firming up — perimenopausal LC patients should consider HRT as a candidate lever in a multi-component recovery stack. See [[recovery-stories/dr-sarah-recovery]].

## Estrogen-as-inflammatory-driver framing (added from #173 Driscoll)

[[diana-driscoll]] (#173) reframes the female skew of POTS / dysautonomia / inflammatory LC partly through **estrogen surges being inflammatory to blood vessels**. In an inflammatory POTS patient with vascular fragility, the ovulation / luteal hormone shift compounds the vascular damage; in male patients without these surges, the recovery arc is on average shorter. Adds a mechanistic story to the female-skew observation rather than just acknowledging it.

She also flags the **hot-flash-vs-inflammatory-episode distinction** — both feel hot, but the inflammatory-POTS variant adds tachycardia + acute panic that take patients to the ER. Often misread as menopause.

## Testosterone-as-protective hypothesis (added from #181 Putrino + Iwasaki)

[[david-putrino]] (#181) flags a paper under review with [[akiko-iwasaki]]: **testosterone may be protective against severe LC.** The lower the testosterone in women *and* men with LC, the worse the symptoms. Estradiol in men also predictive (men make estrogens by testosterone conversion, so estradiol acts as a relative-testosterone marker). Re-reads the female-skew literature, which had largely settled on "women have higher rates of anxiety / depression" as the explanation — Putrino frames this as a sixth-grade-health-class question never asked.

⚠️ Both rows are mechanistic / population-level rather than therapeutic. Wiki position: **testosterone-replacement is not (yet) a recommended LC treatment**, but the marker may be diagnostically useful and worth checking.
