# Mitochondrial dysfunction & cell danger response

The mitochondrial side of long covid: the small organelles that produce cellular energy (ATP) are highly sensitive to their biochemical environment, and under chronic stress (any axis — psychological, physical, viral, chemical, nutritional) they can **slow or stop energy production and switch to a defensive "cell danger response" (CDR)** — Robert Naviaux's framework, used widely in ME/CFS research.

When too many cells are in CDR, the population-level effect is **fatigue, brain fog, post-exertional malaise**.

## How podcast guests describe it

> "Mitochondria are canaries in the coal mine. They sense biochemical stress and shut down energy production. That's when you can't get out of bed." — Moira Newiss, ep #21

CDR-relevant inputs:
- Chronic mental / emotional stress
- Physical stress (sedentary, or excessive exertion)
- Blood-sugar instability
- Sleep deprivation
- Chemical exposures
- Nutrient depletion
- Unresolved infection / immune activation (viral persistence, EBV reactivation)

## Practical levers

- **Reduce total stress load** across all axes — biggest single move
- **Metabolic flexibility** — train the body to burn fat (low-carb / keto sometimes; cf [[keto-diet]]) to take pressure off glucose-burn pathway and stabilise blood sugar
- **Mitochondrial co-factors** — CoQ10, selenium, zinc, magnesium, B-vitamins; food-first, supplements if depleted
- **Protect sleep** — repair happens at night
- **Pace** — push only within mitochondrial capacity (cf [[pacing]], [[post-exertional-malaise]])

## Powerhouse-vs-Battleship + iceberg model (added from #44)

[[moira-newiss]] returned in #44 to make the CDR model the centrepiece of an explicit teaching episode. Two framings worth carrying forward:

**Powerhouse vs Battleship.** Mitochondria are dual-mode. They sense total cellular stress (mental, emotional, physical, blood-sugar, sleep, environmental, viral, medication-related) and switch from energy-production (Powerhouse) to defence (Battleship). LC = stuck in Battleship. The healing cycle (per Naviaux) has to be completed; it often isn't.

**Iceberg / tipping point.** Pre-COVID stressors accumulate silently below the waterline (poor diet, sleep deprivation, blood-sugar instability, sedentary work, gut issues). The virus tips the iceberg over. Two people with the same exposure end up at different LC trajectories because their pre-existing stress loads differ.

**Mitochondria as endocrine signalers.** They make stress hormones in the adrenal glands — they're not just sensors, they're signal-emitters. The implication: any mitochondrial dysregulation has direct downstream consequences for the HPA axis.

## Accelerated-aging framing (added from #181 Putrino)

David Putrino (Mount Sinai) reframes part of LC as an **illness of accelerated cellular aging**. The mitochondrial-dysfunction story dovetails with longevity science: if mitochondria are aging fast, then interventions originally tested in elite-athlete and longevity contexts become candidate therapies for LC subsets — including:

- **Krebs-cycle / ETC support**: oxaloacetate, CoQ10, NAD+
- **mTOR pathway**: low-dose rapamycin
- **AMPK pathway**: metformin
- **Capillarisation + oxygen bioavailability**: HBOT (cf [[hbot]])

These have been used in athletic communities for decades to make mitochondria more resilient to over-exertion, more efficient, and better at clearing reactive oxygen species. Putrino's framing: the same conceptual stack that helps athletes squeeze the last 1% out of their performance may help LC patients in the mitochondrial-dysfunction subset rebuild functional capacity.

⚠️ Doesn't replace the CDR / total-stress-load framing — it's a *what to try* layer, not a *why it happens* layer. Reduce total stress load first; layer in mitochondrial-support tools second.

## SARS-CoV-2-specific damage (added from #187 Groysman, #191 Galland)

Two convergent mechanism stories from clinicians focused specifically on COVID-induced mitochondrial damage:

**Groysman (#187): the mitophagy-block model.** Many viruses hijack mitochondria to replicate. SARS-CoV-2 is unusual in that **it also damages the recycling mechanism (mitophagy)**, mostly via the spike protein. You can't make new mitochondria from scratch — they need to be rebuilt from existing ones — so damage + impaired recycling compounds. Bursting damaged mitochondria release mtDNA that the cGAS–STING pathway misreads as viral, triggering inflammatory cell death and collateral damage to neighbouring cells.

**Implication for treatment sequencing.** Aggressive mitophagy inducers (prolonged fasting, rapamycin, high-dose anti-aging stacks) push a broken system harder, causing more bursting and more inflammation. Groysman's two-step protocol: **Step 1** — daily antioxidants + matrix support to prep cells. **Step 2** — gentle, intermittent mitophagy + biogenesis support (~7 days/month). 3–12+ months. Slow and gentle wins. Crashing daily/weekly is itself active damage.

**Galland (#191): the relative-ischaemia model of PEM.** Capillary damage from COVID → reduced O₂ and perfusion to working tissue. Stress the tissue (exercise, cognition) → it operates with insufficient oxygen → mitochondrial Krebs cycle starts running *backwards* → further damage. Implication: blanket mitochondrial support can fail because the bottleneck is **oxygen delivery**, not enzymatic capacity. Need to address microcirculation/microclots first, *then* support mitochondria — and avoid oxidative therapies (HBOT, ozone) that add stress without supplying antioxidants.

**Galland on "fancy" mitochondrial supplements.** CoQ10 ubiquinone/ubiquinol *ratio* matters; too much ubiquinol disrupts the electron transport chain. NAD+ infusions up-regulate NAD breakdown (creates dependency). Don't blindly chase trendy mitochondrial products.
