# Polyvagal theory

Stephen Porges's three-state model of the autonomic nervous system, used on this podcast as the explanatory framework for why "just be positive / just accept it / just meditate" often *doesn't work* for LC patients.

## The three states

1. **Ventral vagal — rest-and-digest.** Connection, gratitude, acceptance, self-compassion all accessible here.
2. **Sympathetic — fight-or-flight.** Panic, urgency, crisis-focus. Acceptance and gratitude **not** accessible.
3. **Dorsal vagal — immobilization.** Heaviness, brain fog, shutdown, can't think, no higher functions accessible. **Most LC fatigue lives here.**

The three states form a ladder. Healthy NS function isn't permanent residence at the top — it's flexible movement up and down it. Trauma history compresses flexibility; chronic stress (including the chronic stress of LC itself) drops people to the bottom.

## Why this matters for LC

- It explains why generic "mindset" advice falls flat in immobilization — those tools require the rest-and-digest state to access
- It validates the patient's experience: not personal failure, just nervous-system state
- It points to interventions that **work upstream of cognition**: breath, body, vagal-tone work, listening interventions, somatic processing

> "If acceptance isn't working, your nervous system can't access it from where you are. Get back up the ladder first." — Sally Riggs, ep #23

## Caveats raised on the podcast

- Meditation, yoga, vagal-nerve YouTube tricks **don't work for everyone** — especially with trauma history. Pushing past tolerance can throw you back into immobilization.
- Practitioner-delivered work (SSP, SIRPA, somatic experiencing) typically required when self-help tools aren't moving the needle.
- **(#101)** Most "calm your nervous system" content online is built for people *already* in rest-and-digest. LC patients are predominantly in dorsal-vagal shutdown — *the opposite condition*. Generic advice is upstream-blocked.
- **(#101)** Verbal/cognitive safety affirmations ("I am safe") don't reach neuroception — only somatic cues (perception, body position, sensation) do. The somatic action matters; the words are irrelevant.
- **(#101) Track nervous-system state, not symptoms.** Counter-recommendation to standard LC clinic advice. Symptom-tracking can amplify hypervigilance; state-tracking gives the patient an actionable lever.

## Anchor strategies (LC-tuned)

From Sally Riggs's #101:
- **Orienting** — slow visual sweep of the environment (especially behind/under/above), no judgement; watch for the autonomic response (deep breath, yawn, soft stomach) that signals safety acceptance.
- **Self-hugging** — somatic substitute for co-regulation when no other person is present.
- **Weighted blanket / positioned towel-roll for back support** — micro-cues that scaffold the nervous system *while* doing other tasks (talking, working).
- **Warning-signal mapping** — every patient maps the body cue that signals fight-or-flight onset for *them* (Sally: chest pain → coat-hanger pain; Jackie: breath-rate acceleration). The cue is individual; the practice generalises.
