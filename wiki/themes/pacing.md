# Pacing

The energy-management discipline at the heart of Long COVID and ME/CFS care: stay below the threshold that triggers PEM, build a buffer, treat energy as a finite daily envelope. Often described as the *first* thing patients have to learn — and one of the hardest, because it inverts the cultural script of "push through illness".

Variants and adjacent practices:

- **Heart-rate pacing** — wearable-driven; cap activity at HR < (anaerobic threshold − margin)
- **Stop-rest-recover** — formal scheduled rest blocks (vs. recover-when-tired)
- **The energy-envelope model** — Bruce Campbell, ME/CFS framework
- **Activity-management** — physiotherapy-led pacing (e.g. Long COVID Physio collective)
- **[[cognitive-pacing]]** — the equivalent for mental-load activity (Cummings, ep #18). TV and scrolling drain like physical activity.
- **Aggressive pacing** — Cass MacDonald (#19): on bad days you skip whole categories of task, not just trim them.

## Why this is theme rather than intervention

Pacing isn't a treatment — it's a *posture* toward all activity, the ground that other interventions sit on. Wiki convention: pacing as a *theme*, individual pacing methods (heart-rate pacing, activity-rest cycling) as *interventions* with their own outcome logs.

## Episode coverage

- (ep #2) [[jackie-baxter]] learning pacing the hard way — three months of unstructured graded-exercise rebound into a yearlong plateau.
- (ep #5) [[dr-amy-small]] — argues all post-covid patients should be taught pacing at week 4 by default. Foundational to her own substantial recovery.
- (ep #6) [[nick-sculthorpe]] — HEARTPACE study tests HR-driven pacing; explicit acknowledgement that POTS subgroup needs step-count instead.
- (ep #7) [[suzy-bolt]] — pacing alongside acceptance + community as foundation for her own recovery.
- (ep #17) [[wibbs-coulson]] — pacing as nervous-system regulation; "down-shift" via yin yoga / yoga nidra.
- (ep #18) [[louise-cummings]] — names **cognitive pacing** as essential alongside physical pacing. TV ≠ rest.
- (ep #19) [[cass-macdonald]] — the most operationally detailed pacing testimony. Honest about being a "boom-and-bust pacer" despite years of pre-LC chronic-illness experience. Aids, OT-led adjustments, energy budget for medication and digestion.
- (ep #80) [[anjali-agarwal]] — "three Ps" framing (plan, pace, prioritise) within an EDS/MCAS/POTS treatment scaffold. Frames excess movement as histamine-release driver → connective-tissue cascade. "Find your bar" individual ceiling rather than generic targets.
- (ep #141) [[todd-davenport]] — anchor PEM episode; sources the **energy envelope** language to **Dr Leonard Jason**. "Pacing isn't doing nothing — it's balancing activity with rest, and that takes planning, prioritising, and discipline." Reframes pacing as a *health-coaching* model the patient owns, not an exercise prescription a clinician hands down.
- (ep #172) [[jo-thomas]] — frames pacing-up as **"testing the waters"**: your responsibility is to attempt the next step; the body's response is *not* your responsibility. Decouples symptom outcomes from self-blame and breaks the recovery-perfection trap of fearing every activity step.
- (ep #181) [[david-putrino]] — Mount Sinai's first-line stabilisation step. Recommends data-driven pacing apps **Visible** and **Guava** for patients who tolerate logging; warns against them for patients prone to fixation. Emphasises understanding *what is driving* the PEM rather than treating PEM directly.

## Common arguments on the podcast

- "The deconditioning model is wrong for LC patients with PEM. Push and you go backwards."
- "It's grief work as much as rehab — you mourn the activity-life you used to have."
- "Wearables (Garmin, Visible armband, Polar) make the abstract concrete."
- "Even when you fully recover, keep pacing — never go back to ignoring your energy budget." (Cass, #19)
