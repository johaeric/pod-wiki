# Autonomic Dysfunction

The most consolidated mechanism story in the wiki for symptoms that worsen on standing and improve on lying down — and for the broader LC pattern of sympathetic overdrive, breathing-pattern disorder, sensory overstimulation, and orthostatic intolerance.

## Boon Lim's anchor estimate (#40)

In the Imperial Syncope Diagnostic Unit's tilt-table cohort of 60–80 LC patients:
- **~25%** full POTS criteria (HR rise ≥30, no BP drop)
- **~25%** sub-threshold POTS
- **~Most of the rest** show oscillatory BP/HR patterns indicating adrenaline surge
- **Net: ~60–70%** of LC has a meaningful autonomic dysfunction component

## The discriminating question

**Do symptoms get worse standing up and better lying down?**
- Yes → autonomic dysfunction
- Postural-agnostic chest pain → consider myocarditis / pericarditis instead

## The mechanism, plain English

Standing → blood pools to legs → empty heart → carotid baroreceptors not stretched → panic signal → adrenaline surge → tachycardia + vasoconstriction. In healthy people: refill within seconds; baroreceptors re-stretch; surge dies down. In LC: the loop overshoots and continues running while upright. Lying flat resolves it. Deconditioning makes it worse.

**Stress / fear / high expectations amplify the same loop** — this is why "rest *and* nervous-system regulation" is the recurring prescription in this wiki.

## The Lim toolkit (#40)

Bottom-up: cold water + electrolytes → salt loading → compression → isometric exercises → cool-finish shower → head-of-bed elevation → light low-carb meals.

Top-down: diaphragmatic breath × forgiveness/gratitude/compassion → mindfulness → community.

Drugs (when needed): midodrine → fludrocortisone (≤1 yr) → propranolol or ivabradine for palpitations.

## Convergent vocabularies

Different practitioners use **polyvagal / fright-or-flight / nervous-system loop / SIRPA / TMS / mind-body / autonomic / dysautonomia** — convergent on the same physiology. Treat the vocabulary as cosmetic; the toolkit is consistent.
