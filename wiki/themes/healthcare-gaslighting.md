# Healthcare gaslighting

The pattern of being told one's symptoms are "anxiety", "stress", or "all in your head" — by triage lines, GPs, A&E staff — when the underlying physiology is real and identifiable. A near-universal theme on the podcast and a major source of trauma layered on top of the illness itself.

## What this covers

- Triage / 111 / NHS Direct dismissals
- "It's just anxiety" framing of dyspnoea, tachycardia, post-exertional crashes
- Gendered patterns (women dismissed more than men; men also dismissed but often differently)
- "Anxiety about being called anxious" — patients suppressing real mental-health needs to avoid further dismissal
- Tests-come-back-normal as license to disbelieve patients
- Specialist diagnoses (POTS, MCAS, dysautonomia) routinely missed in primary care

## Episode coverage

- (ep #1) [[barbara-melville]]: 111 told her her collapse was anxiety; paramedics rolled their eyes; her white "Terry's nails" were dismissed as "just pale" by her GP. A research-MRI later revealed actual heart damage.
- (ep #60) [[reema-ahmad]]: South-Asian patient describes sustained dismissal layered over racial bias. Anchor for the racial dimension.
- (ep #61) [[colin-pidgeon]] / [[rosie-pidgeon]]: clinician validation as an inflection point — *"don't let anyone tell you this isn't real"* (Rosie's eventual consultant). Counter-data: the GP-level dismissal pattern that preceded it.
- (ep #62) [[nisreen-alwan]]: stigma scale captures three dimensions (external, internalised, anticipated). Patients who fear being disbelieved don't seek care — anticipated stigma is structural.
- (ep #65) [[colin-pidgeon]]'s poem *"Stormy"* — verbatim of paediatric LC dismissal from the carer's lens.
- (ep #69) [[fiona-lowenstein]]: **racial gaslighting at the acute-care entry point** — Black women presenting with the same Covid symptoms in NYC March 2020 were turned away as "anxiety"; some did not survive. Class/race/relationship privilege gated who got admitted.
- (ep #81) [[liza-dileo-thomas]]: clinician-side anatomy of the dismissal pattern. Names the "great news, everything's normal" antipattern as iatrogenic for chronically-ill patients; argues empathy is measurable, teachable, and declining in third-year medical students. Counter-frame: *I don't know but I believe you* as the high-leverage clinician phrase for LC.
- (ep #84) [[caroline-pover]]: an even harsher dismissal pattern for vaccine-injured patients — "people cross the street", "step back", and the pattern of being told to call it long covid because the support is more available there. The dismissal compounds when the diagnosis itself is socially undesirable.
