# Polypharmacy warning

A recurring concern that long-haulers — desperate, under-served, and exposed to a flood of online recommendations — accumulate large stacks of supplements and off-label drugs that interact in unintended ways and rarely get reviewed by a clinician with the full picture.

## Mechanisms of harm

- **Mechanistic redundancy** — many supplements target the same pathway. Multiple anti-inflammatories, multiple antivirals, multiple mast-cell stabilisers — adding more does not multiply the effect.
- **CYP450-mediated drug-supplement interactions.** Some supplements suppress or induce metabolising enzymes, leading to either accumulation (overdose) or under-dosing of pharmaceuticals on a fixed regimen ([[joachim-gerlach]] #41).
- **MCAS triggers.** Some supplements (especially probiotics, herbal extracts with histamine-liberating properties) cause hypersensitivity reactions in MCAS-overlapping patients ([[tina-peers]] #38, Gerlach #41).
- **Vitamin / mineral overshoot.** Vitamin D3 and zinc without serum-level monitoring can overshoot and cause toxicity (Gerlach #41).
- **Decision fatigue + cost.** Stack management consumes cognitive resources Long-Covid patients can least afford.

## Heuristics to apply

From the episodes so far:

1. **Categorise before stacking.** Anti-inflammatory / anti-allergic / anti-pathogenic / mitochondrial / autonomic. One strong agent per category beats five overlapping ones.
2. **Get serum levels** for any vitamin/mineral before chronic supplementation.
3. **Tell every clinician about every supplement.** Gerlach #41: Vedicinals will refuse new patients on heavy pharmaceutical regimens unless they coordinate directly with the patient's prescribing doctor.
4. **Watch the histamine-load of supplements** in MCAS-suspect patients.

## Eric-relevant signal

Eric is on a tightly-curated pharmaceutical stack (ivabradine + mestinon + fludrocortisone + zyrtec + pepcid + bufomix prn + D-vitamin). Adding any new supplement should pass the "categorical addition, not redundant" test, and be reviewed against CYP450 interactions with the existing stack — particularly because mestinon, ivabradine, and fludrocortisone all have specific interaction profiles.
