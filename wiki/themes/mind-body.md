# Mind-Body

The cluster of approaches that frame LC symptoms as (at least partly) self-sustaining nervous-system patterns — and aim to interrupt them via cognitive, behavioural, somatic, and meditative practices. Distinct from "it's all in your head" (which is gaslighting) — the mind-body claim is that **physiological dysregulation** of the autonomic and limbic systems can be modulated by deliberate practice.

This theme is present across multiple recovery stories on the podcast and is also the most contested. Read with care.

## Recurring sub-claims

- **Sympathetic-dominant nervous system** is keeping the body in flight/fight (Suzy Bolt #7, Rachel Whitfield #8, Wibbs Coulson #17, Amy Anker #20)
- **Brain retraining** can shift this (Lightning Process #8, Gupta Program — placeholder, ANS Rewire — placeholder)
- **Looping pattern** — even after acute injury heals, the NS continues firing on minimal triggers (Amy Anker #20 — phantom-pain analogy)
- **Anticipating a crash produces the crash** ("Fear of relapse can create the relapse" — Rachel #8)
- **Mindful interruption** of those anticipations is the operative lever (Amy #20)
- **Real rest is not Netflix** — Squid Game and other suspense media reactivate sympathetic tone (Kelly #12, Wibbs #17)

## Caveats present in the episodes themselves

- Suzy Bolt explicitly cautions: get medical help where needed; mind-body is *one* layer
- Amy Anker explicitly cautions: this is not for patients with structural / organ damage where pathology is unresolved
- All recovered guests describe a *combination* of interventions — not pure mind-body monotherapy
- Risk of self-blame: "if I just thought correctly I would recover" can land badly with patients who have been doing this work and are still ill
