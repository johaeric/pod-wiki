# Employment & long covid

The legal, financial, and practical landscape around staying in work, leaving work, and the UK benefits system for people disabled by long covid. Built primarily from ep #27 (Jenny Ceolta-Smith, OT + LC patient).

## High-signal advice (UK-focused)

**Don't resign.** Wait to be dismissed. Resigning weakens any subsequent disability-discrimination claim via ACAS conciliation or employment tribunal.

**Document everything in writing.** Bounce back after meetings: "thanks for confirming today that…". Especially important when LC cognitive dysfunction makes recall unreliable.

**Time limits are tight.** ACAS gives you **3 months less 1 day** from the alleged discrimination event to raise a tribunal case.

**Check legal cover on house insurance.** Insurer will assess if a case has >51 % chance of success; can fund significant legal support.

## The UK system map

- **ACAS** — pre-tribunal conciliation, free helpline, mandatory step before tribunal
- **National Law Centres** (e.g. GM Law) — webinar on self-represented tribunal process
- **Equality Advisory Service** — free letter templates / helpline
- **Unions** — first port of call where applicable
- **Equality Act** — reasonable-adjustments framework (most common LC ask: work-from-home, often refused)

## Benefits

- **New Style ESA** — non-means-tested, **£74.70/week** (at recording), up to 1 year, while still officially employed and on sick pay. Easy claim, fit-note + medical questionnaire.
- **PIP** — non-means-tested; **nightmare process** but worth fighting; high success rate at appeal. Backdated to claim date. Unlocks council tax reductions, blue badge, vehicle tax exemption, travel discounts. **Compatible with working.**
- **Universal Credit** — for those eligible
- **Carer's Allowance** — partner-side
- **Permitted Work** — under ESA, up to 16 h/week / £150/week without losing benefit. Watch the rules carefully — over-£150 in any week can suspend benefit.

## Where to get help

- GP surgery well-being / welfare workers
- Social-prescribing link workers
- Long Covid clinics with benefits support
- Housing-association welfare-rights workers
- Citizens Advice
- Long Covid Support Employment Group (@longcovidwork)

## On the wider experience

Job loss compounded by chronic illness is a major loss with grief, financial stress, and identity rupture. Many guests find advocacy work and peer support networks fill the structure / purpose gap.

## NHS staff — occupational injury angle (added from #42)

From Dr [[alison-twycross]] / [[long-covid-nurses-and-midwives-uk]]:

- **Nurses get LC at higher rates than medics.** Hypothesised driver: prolonged bedside contact + viral load.
- **No data by profession.** Counting is poor; ~10,000 registered nurses in NHS England with LC is a working estimate.
- **Health and Safety at Work legislation argument.** Many NHS employers were not adhering to it during the pre-PPE phase; case law is missing because LC patients are too sick to bring claims.
- **Two-archetype employer model.** Balance-books-staff-as-bother vs people-as-asset. Mostly the former.
- **Cross-nation devolution gap.** Different deals in England / Scotland / Wales / Northern Ireland; within England, no consistency across the 240 NHS organisations because the NHS England guidance is *guidance*, not enforcement.

### Ill-health retirement (IHR) misuse

After NHS England removed the dismissal-after-12-months-LC line in Feb 2022, employers shifted to **inappropriate IHR** as a soft-dismissal route:

- IHR *is* a legitimate option **once reasonable adjustments + phased return have been tried and failed**. It is *not* a first-line response to long covid, but is being used as one.
- In some trusts, applying for IHR is treated as the staff member "admitting unfitness" and triggers the sickness-capability procedure.
- Almost no LC-only IHR cases are succeeding. People who do succeed have other co-existing illnesses recognised separately.
- Twycross's next campaign is to gather data on IHR misuse via a padlet.

### NHS-Wales half-pay reprieve

On 1 April 2022 NHS Wales staff off sick > 1 year with LC were due to drop to half-pay. The afternoon of 1 April: U-turn — full pay extended to end of June 2022. Plaid Cymru in the Senedd plus social-media outcry credited.

## Remote / hybrid working — the post-pandemic battleground (#105)

Lancaster University's Inclusive Remote and Hybrid Working Study (Holland PI, [[calum-carson]] senior research associate) is the wiki's current best-positioned research project on disabled workers' post-pandemic working experience. Captured concerns:

- **Substitution risk** — employers may use "you can work from home" to *replace* in-office reasonable adjustments rather than supplement them. Removes disabled workers' freedom to choose.
- **Mandated-day hybrid is not hybrid** — fixed Mondays/Fridays in the office defeats fluctuating-condition flexibility.
- **Promotion-pathway access** — predecessor study found disabled workers worried about losing visibility and Thursday-drinks-style social capital.
- **Manager-level flexibility outweighs policy** — Carson's prior PhD finding (on the living wage) generalises: day-to-day manager response matters more than the org-chart strategy.
- **Sectoral lens**: education + healthcare are LC-overrepresented AND least adaptable to remote working — structural double-burden.

Outputs (Dec 2024 launch): publicly accessible report + commissioned short film + employer best-practice guide.

## Empirical employment-outcome data (#122)

[[122-louise-cummings-cognitive-linguistic-followup]]: 41 LC patients followed for ~21 months post-infection. **37 employed pre-Covid → 1 had returned to full pre-Covid role** (and even she dropped back to part-time within a month of follow-up assessment). 11/36 in some reduced employment; 25 not working (7 ill-health retirement, 12 on social security).

**What helped people return to work:**
1. *Extended phased returns* (months, not weeks)
2. *Role change* — fewer comms demands, less patient-facing
3. *Adjustments* — work-from-home, reduced hours, mobility scooter, computer adaptations
4. *Strong occupational-health advocate*

**What hindered:** inflexibility, insensitive HR meetings, infeasible redeployments, dismissal threats. Insensitive HR meetings cause distress that survives even successful returns.

**Cognitive demand was the dominant blocker** — even when physical symptoms had settled, the cognitive demand of the previous role kept people out. Healthcare-staff sub-pattern: nurses and patient-facing clinicians needed role-changes (away from multitasking ward work) to return at all.
