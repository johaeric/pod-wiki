# Post-infectious IBS / DGBI

The class of irritable bowel syndrome / gut-brain interaction disorders that follow acute infectious gastroenteritis. Long-recognised in gastroenterology — best-studied via the **Walkerton, Ontario water-contamination outbreak (May 2000)** — and the framework into which **post-COVID DGBI** falls.

## Walkerton, May 2000 (the canonical reference)

E. coli O157:H7 + *Campylobacter jejuni* contaminated the public drinking water. 7 deaths, ~2,000 ill. Two-year follow-up: **36% post-infectious IBS** in those with clinically suspected gastroenteritis. Long-term follow-up showed **favourable spontaneous remission** in most cases — symptoms eventually resolved without specific intervention.

This is the optimistic prognostic precedent for post-COVID DGBI patients.

## Why it matters for Long Covid

Bashashati (#43) explicitly positions post-COVID DGBI as a member of this class. The framing carries two implications:

1. **The mechanism is plausible and not novel.** Acute infection → low-grade chronic inflammation + microbiota shift + enteric-nervous-system rewiring → chronic GI symptoms. We've seen this before; we know roughly how to treat it.
2. **The prognosis is cautiously good.** Even without curative treatment, post-infectious IBS tends to remit over time. Patients can be told there is a realistic path to spontaneous improvement, even if the timeline is years.

## Where it might diverge

- **Multi-system involvement.** Long Covid GI sits inside a broader autonomic / immune / vascular illness in many patients. Walkerton patients were otherwise healthy. The post-COVID cohort may have lower remission rates because the GI piece is one fragment of a system-wide condition.
- **ACE2 specificity.** Direct gut-cell infection via ACE2 may produce different cellular damage from Walkerton's bacterial mechanism.
