# Cognitive Pacing

The energy-management discipline applied to **mental load** rather than physical activity. The theory: cognitive work expends real metabolic energy; for LC patients with brain fog or fatigue, screen time, reading, conversation, and stimulating media are *not* rest — they're more activity.

> "We need to do cognitive pacing as well as physical pacing. People think they're resting because they're watching TV. They're not. The brain has to process all that information." — [[louise-cummings]], ep #18

## Why it matters in LC

- LC brain fog has measurable cognitive deficits (Cummings ep #18: verbal recall, verbal fluency, discourse informativeness all impaired).
- Patients who try to rest physically while consuming high-stimulation media remain in cognitive overload.
- Crashes can be triggered by *mental* over-extension (sustained Zoom calls, reading dense material, decision-making) just as they can by physical over-extension.

## Practical examples from the podcast

- **AJ (ep #13)**: "Anything to do with screens really can take a lot out of you and you might not realise that at the time." She rations phone use; sometimes turns off phone and puts in drawer.
- **Cass (ep #19)**: Even taking medication and digesting food count as energy expenditures. Sewing machine time as a fitness-for-work test (cognitively demanding).
- **Cummings (ep #18)**: Close down stimulation. Reading and TV both count as cognitive work. Best rest is genuine downshift — closed eyes, breath focus, low sensory input.
- **Amy (ep #20)**: Limited social media to once a day, single positive moment, log off.

## What counts as cognitive rest

- Closed eyes, no audio
- Breath-focus / yoga nidra
- Walking outdoors with no podcast/headphones
- Bath in dim light, fan off
- Sleep

## What does NOT count (despite feeling restful)

- Netflix, especially anything plot-driven (Squid Game named explicitly in #12)
- Phone scrolling, Instagram, news
- Reading dense material
- Music *with lyrics*, sometimes (mixed accounts)
