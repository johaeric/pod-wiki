# Perfectionism (as recovery-blocker)

A recurring trait in the wiki's recovery-story cohort and a recurring obstacle in the recovery process itself. Perfectionism is named in 6+ episodes and surfaces consistently in recovered-patient retrospectives as **upstream of the over-doing pattern that drives the initial illness onset and prolongs recovery**.

## Anchor episode

**[[152-jenny-adams-perfectionism-adhd]]** — return appearance from [[145-jenny-adams-recovery]]. Recorded *unedited* as a deliberate perfectionism exercise. First explicit guest-led deep-dive on perfectionism-as-illness-driver. Adams: **"Perfectionism is a trauma response."** (Gabor Maté lineage.) Live compassionate-inquiry exercise on Jackie traces the belief from teenage years: *if I do x, y, z perfectly, then I will be loved / accepted / safe.*

## Operative framings

- **All-or-nothing thinking** as the everyday face of perfectionism (Adams, Jackie). The folded-laundry pile that becomes a 4-week task because there's no "do a few items now" in the all-or-nothing frame.
- **"I'll be happy when" syndrome** — the productivity treadmill where the goalposts move with each achieved task. The feeling we're chasing is rarely behind the task itself.
- **"Done is better than perfect."** Adams's working motto; aspirational anchor for any reader in this pattern.
- **The 90-second emotion-wave** — when you let the discomfort sit, it usually moves through in ~90 seconds; suppressing it compounds and prolongs it. Adams operationalised this in #152 as a tool against the perfectionism-driven push-through impulse.
- **Self-compassion as the sequel** — perfectionism-aware patients gradually move from inner-critic-driving-doing to "what would I say to a friend?" framings (Jackie, Adams, Nadyne, Stein, host-solo episodes).

## Why it matters on the wiki

- A high proportion of recovery stories on the wiki ([[094-jackies-recovery-story]], [[127-rachael-recovery-story]], [[138-jules-rogers-recovery]], [[144-dr-sarah-recovery]], [[145-jenny-adams-recovery]], etc.) include some version of "I had to learn to stop pushing through" as an inflection point. Perfectionism is a frequent upstream driver of that pattern.
- The **trauma-response framing** anchors perfectionism not as a personality quirk but as a downstream of unmet early-life needs around safety + acceptance + love. This makes addressing it part of the [[trauma-and-illness]] work, not separate.
- For **ADHD-burnout patients** specifically, perfectionism + RSD + masking + cycle-blind productivity is the signature stack that often precedes ME/CFS / LC presentations (Adams clinical observation: "probably 80% of women presenting with chronic illness in my client base have undiagnosed ADHD").
- *Eric-relevant.* Eric's own retrospective work on this matters more than any one tool — perfectionism is an operational pattern, addressed by daily practice, not a one-off intervention.
