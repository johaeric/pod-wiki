# Viral persistence

The hypothesis that **SARS-CoV-2 (or its fragments / RNA) persists in tissue reservoirs** — gut, brain, lung, kidney, vascular endothelium — months or years after the initial infection, driving ongoing immune activation and the multi-organ pattern of long covid.

Strongest podcast evidence pointer (ep #28 Ziyad Al-Aly): an **NIH 44-person autopsy preprint** finding SARS-CoV-2 in brain tissue, lung tissue, kidney, liver months post-acute infection. Earlier studies from Wuhan also flagged viral material in stool / organ tissue (cited by Moira Newiss ep #21). **Anchor episode #103 ([[amy-proal]])** brings the **PolyBio Research Foundation + Long Covid Research Consortium** framing — viral persistence as the leading mechanistic hypothesis for a meaningful subset of LC, with documented tissue-biopsy and PET-imaging findings.

If true:
- Justifies trials of antiviral therapy in LC (Paxlovid, monoclonal antibodies, ensitrelvir, etc.)
- Frames LC as a **systemic infection**, not just post-viral aftermath
- Explains the multi-organ symptom picture (brain fog from CNS, GI from gut, dysautonomia from autonomic ganglia, etc.)

> "It would have been more wonderful for all of us if this was a mild virus that gives you a cold and you get better. The reality is it's not." — Al-Aly, ep #28

## On this podcast so far

Treated mostly as **mechanism named**, not yet a treatment route — antiviral RCTs in LC were not yet read out at the time of these episodes. Watch for ercipedia updates on:
- Paxlovid (Pfizer) RCT results — Khan #37 reports Paxlovid did not help him (n=1, third infection)
- Stanford Paxlovid LC trial
- Yale [[paxlovid-lc-trial]] (Krumholz/Iwasaki) — 15-day extended-dose RCT
- Monoclonal antibody arms
- Ensitrelvir trials
- **Truvada (HIV antiviral with EBV activity)** — PolyBio + Mt Sinai trial planned, generic-drug available
- **Combination antiviral therapy** (per #103 Proal — single-drug courses likely insufficient, mirroring HIV experience)

## Anchor evidence per #103 Proal

- **Tongue tissue** — virus found in patients with loss of taste up to 163 days
- **Olfactory bulb** — virus persistence in patients with smell loss
- **Gut tissue** — spike protein in colonoscopy biopsies up to **676 days** post-infection
- **UCSF PET imaging** (Henrich/Peluso) — T-cell activation maps show ongoing immune activity in **gut wall, spinal cord, lymph nodes** of LC patients; same patients had viral RNA in gut biopsies
- **Spike protein in blood up to 12–16 months** post-infection — argues active replication, not inert remnant; the body would clear free protein over months without a replicating source

## Why blood-PCR scepticism is looking in the wrong place

> "Pathogens hide. Blood is a stupid place for them to be — that's where the immune cells are. They go into tissue. So a negative blood PCR doesn't mean it's gone." — Proal, ep #103

This reframes how to read viral-persistence-sceptic positions on the wiki ([[robert-groysman]] #92's scepticism is blood-based). The microclots mechanism remains compatible: spike protein leaking from a tissue reservoir into blood can seed [[microclots]] consistent with [[douglas-kell]] / [[resia-pretorius]] (#33) findings.

## Adjacent receptor-blockade hypothesis (#130)

[[marco-leitzke]] (#130) brings a *complementary* hypothesis: SARS-CoV-2 spike protein has RNA-sequence similarity to **snake-venom toxins** (cobratoxin, bungarotoxin) that bind **nicotinic acetylcholine receptors (nAChR)**. Spike binds nAChR silently — blocks without producing a chemical signal, breaking autonomic and immune coordination across the body. Doesn't require active viral replication; just persistent receptor-occupation by spike protein consistent with the long-blood-spike-protein finding.

**Implication for treatment**: nicotine (~30× higher receptor affinity than spike) can compete spike off the receptors. See [[nicotine-patches]]. Hypothesis-of-record; no RCT yet.

## Therapeutic-trial pipeline (immunomodulation arm, #131)

[[wes-ely]] is now PI of the [[rvlc-trial]] — 550-patient placebo-controlled RCT of [[baricitinib]] (JAK1/2 inhibitor) for established LC. Hypothesis: persistent immune activation downstream of viral persistence can be dampened directly by JAK inhibition, regardless of whether viral material is still present. Parallel/complementary to the antiviral-suppression arm — both may eventually need to be combined.

## How patients clear vs don't (Proal three-factor model)

1. **Immune state at infection** — antibody neutralisation, T-cell readiness, prior pathogen load (e.g. EBV reactivation) modulating immune response
2. **Existing pathogen burden** — Karolinska CMV-twin study showed unmatched immune-system parameters; multiple prior immune hits compound
3. **Human genetic variation** — clotting genes (predispose to amyloid microclot formation), immune-system genes

## Newer angle (#38 Peers): gut SARS-CoV-2 persistence

Recent stool studies show SARS-CoV-2 may continue replicating in the gut. Antibiotic candidates from one such study: metronidazole, vancomycin, amoxicillin. Possible explanation for why Peers's MCAS-protocol patients improve but don't fully recover — and why apheresis gains often fade ("there's still something pressing the buttons keeping it going").

## Cross-mechanism note

#128's T-cell-exhaustion model and #103's tissue-reservoir model are not competitors. They describe different layers: tissue reservoir = where the virus hides; T-cell exhaustion = why the immune system can't finish clearing it. Together they support the **combination antiviral + immune-modulating** therapeutic frame.
