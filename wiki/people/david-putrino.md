# Dr David Putrino

Australian-born Mount Sinai (NYC) professor in Rehabilitation and Human Performance, Director of Rehabilitation Innovation. Implementation scientist by training — focus on getting interventions to patients quickly. Now runs **six centres** at Mount Sinai including a Center for Infection-Associated Chronic Illness (LC, ME-CFS, fibromyalgia, hyper-mobile EDS, chronic Lyme, vector-borne illness).

## What's distinctive

- **Reframed his own department.** Renamed Rehabilitation Medicine → **Rehabilitation and Human Performance** to capture the full spectrum from disability to elite performance under one stack.
- **Multi-driver framing for LC symptoms.** Putrino's ladder: stabilise (pacing, apps), reduce (vagus stim, ramped breathwork, antioxidants, nutritionist), then drill into drivers (persistent pathogens, autoimmunity, hormones).
- **Paxlovid LC trial co-author (failed).** Honest about the failure and explicit about the mechanistic reason — paxlovid doesn't penetrate tissue. The wiki position now: paxlovid is a "didn't work at population level, possibly works for circulating-spike subset" intervention pending the subgroup analysis.
- **Politically clear about systemic obstacles.** Names drug-company refusal to embrace complexity, decades of male-animal-only research, the historical psychogenic-illness misclassification of complex chronic illness.
- **Multidisciplinary clinic team** by design — physios, neuropsychologists doing cognitive remediation therapy, nutritionists, social workers matching patients by zip code. Zip-code social-work matching is novel.
- **Collaborators** he names in this episode: Akiko Iwasaki (hormones paper, Pax-LC trial); Harlan Krumholz (Pax-LC trial); David Joffe (slow-wave sleep); Rob Wüst (muscle physiology / energy generation).
