  - "Flipping the iceberg" — therapy needs to happen between activities, not in performance. Conventional rehab sees only the tip (what's possible in clinic); the disease lives below the waterline (in PEM + recovery).

# Amy Mooney

US occupational therapist (~25 years, paediatric / sensory-integration background). Primary caregiver since 2014 for her now-19-year-old daughter, who became unwell at 9 years old with what was eventually diagnosed as ME/CFS. The decade of caregiver-side experience — combined with the OT clinical background — reshaped her practice toward chronic-illness-aware OT for ME/CFS and LC. Increasingly engaged in educational outreach with [[bateman-horne-center]].

## Position on the wiki

- **First wiki anchor for the *occupational-therapist* perspective on LC** — distinct from PT (Powers #164, Brown #47/#51, Davenport #141), psychology (Gahan #78), and rehab medicine (Faghy #36, Ravindran #53).
- **Aggressive rest** as a named, operationalised practice — deliberate sensory deactivation, support-kit pre-arrangement, vibration tools (Sensate), weighted blanket, eye masks, joint-compression-without-exertion. Extends [[radical-rest]] from "rest a lot" to "rest *deliberately*."
- **Exertion buckets** model with explicit cognitive + sensory + social-emotional components — anchors what Cass MacDonald (#19) and Jay Wiles (#82) flagged less formally.
- **Caregiver-as-practitioner lineage** — pairs with [[156-nadyne-mckie-caregivers-children]] and [[011-iain-partner-with-chronic-illness]].

Her early experience taking her daughter to PT/OT clinics that prescribed graded loading (treadmill, catch, balance work) and watching her crash for weeks → grounds her practice in patient-side outcomes not clinic-tip performance.
